# THEORY-141: In a major evolutionary transition, where formerly free-living units come to form a higher-level unit, the collective is stable only if selection between collectives comes to trump selection among the units within them, typically through mechanisms such as policing, kinship and division of labour that align the interests of the units; the levels at which selection acts are then a product of evolution, to be explained rather than assumed

## Source

Okasha (2022), [LIT-749](../literature.d/LIT-749.md), read in [NOTE-586](../notes.d/NOTE-586.md). His 2005 paper,
[LIT-721](../literature.d/LIT-721.md), which the 2022 text cites for the two-level view and the
point about the hierarchy, is unread.

## What was actually shown

Okasha states and argues these points in [LIT-749](../literature.d/LIT-749.md); there is no model
or data.

- **The principles.** A collective needs a short-term advantage to form,
  "would not necessarily be stable owing to defectors pursuing their own
  interests", can be stabilised by "kinship, policing, division-of-labor",
  and needs higher-level selection to "trump" lower-level selection to
  evolve adaptations of its own. Okasha attributes the last to Buss and
  Michod and calls these principles relevant to all transitions.
- **Policing.** Success "typically" requires mechanisms "such as
  policing, to regulate the selfish tendencies of the lower-level units"
  or to align their interests.
- **The hierarchy explained.** The traditional levels-of-selection debate
  treated the hierarchy as given; in the transitions, hierarchical
  organization "moves from being part of the explanans to being part of
  the explanandum".

## What this does not say

- **Not that general principles explain transitions well.** "Relevant" is
  not "useful" nor "explanatorily fundamental"; whether a general theory
  can be specific enough is a question on which "the jury is still out".
- **Not that every transition is a collective action problem.** Okasha
  endorses Rainey et al.'s complaint that "cheating" and "public good"
  have been misapplied, from an a priori commitment to that assumption.
- **Not that transitions are inevitable.** Intermediate cooperation may be
  stable, and transitions may be rare singularities.
- **Not that only one level matters.** Genic and hierarchical
  explanations are compatible, though formally equivalent models need not
  be causally equivalent.

## Connections

- **Aktipis et al. ([THEORY-135](THEORY-135.md))** document the principle at one
  transition: multicellularity requires suppressing cell-level fitness,
  and complex multicellularity is a cheater detection and suppression
  system. Okasha's caution bears on their use of "cheating", which they
  define functionally.
- **The owner's essay.** §6.3's capture from below is what a transition
  must overcome, with policing as one remedy. The record reads the
  endogenized hierarchy as bearing on §6.2: the level members depend on is
  produced, not given. Okasha does not discuss organizations, and his
  caution against presupposing a collective action problem carries over to
  them.
