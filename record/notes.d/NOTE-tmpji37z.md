# NOTE-tmpji37z: Harsey and Freyd (2023) — The Influence of DARVO and Insincere Apologies on Perceptions of Sexual Assault

## Contribution

An experimental test of Freyd's (1997) hypothesis that DARVO works on third parties, in a sexual-assault scenario,
replicating the authors' 2020 dating-violence experiment and adding punishment judgments and insincere apologies.

## Key results

1. Abstract: "participants who were exposed to perpetrator DARVO rated the perpetrator as less abusive", less
   responsible and more believable, and rated the victim as more abusive and less believable.
2. Results: of the six ratings, "only victim responsibility did not vary by condition."
3. Discussion, punishment: "Endorsement of perpetrator punishment was 82% among individuals not exposed to DARVO but only 60.7%".
4. Abstract: "Insincere apologies had minimal impact on ratings."
5. Introduction, the earlier study: in the 2020 dating-violence experiment, "Contrary to predictions, DARVO penalized"
   the perpetrator's believability; here it raised it.
6. Limitations: the perpetrator-responsibility difference was "a small effect size (Aron et al., 2009)".

## Assumptions

The accused is guilty by stipulation: participants read about "an incident of sexual abuse". So the study measures
how DARVO moves observers given a guilty speaker, and cannot say whether DARVO-like statements are evidence of guilt.
Undergraduate observers; single 0-3 items; one written stimulus.

## Concepts

DARVO; insincere apology ("ifpology"); observer judgments of believability, responsibility, abusiveness and
punishment; rape myth acceptance; floor effect.

## Bearing on the record

- **The exchange** (A5 §4) reports the study accurately in direction. Corrections: 235 participants (230 in the
  analysed data the abstract reports), one scenario in four variants, small effects. A's own caveat, that such
  studies "do not provide an independent test for identifying which party is lying", is borne out by the design.
- **A design point the authors do not raise:** the pooled No DARVO arm includes a cell in which "no statement from
  Professor Smith was included at" all, so part of the DARVO effect may be the effect of making any statement.
- **[THEORY-327](../theory.d/THEORY-327.md)** (Fricker): DARVO's attack on the accuser's credibility is a deliberate manipulation of credibility,
  which Fricker distinguishes from testimonial injustice as "a related but distinct wrong"; this study is
  experimental evidence that such manipulation moves third-party credibility judgments.
- **[THEORY-317](../theory.d/THEORY-317.md)**: men in the sample rated the victim less believable than women did, the kind of deflated credibility
  judgement [THEORY-317](../theory.d/THEORY-317.md) describes; the study does not show that prejudice is the cause.
- **[CLAIM-tmpp8na7](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpp8na7.md) and [CASE-tmpwrhkd](../cases.d/CASE-tmpwrhkd.md)**, which the registration named: no bearing; the study is about accusation and
  response, not the distribution of control in a relationship.

## Limitations

Undergraduate, mostly women and white sample; short single-paragraph stimulus; high-status perpetrator only, not
varied; vague punishment item; tables not in this copy; the no-statement confound above.
