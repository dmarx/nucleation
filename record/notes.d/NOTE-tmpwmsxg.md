# NOTE-tmpwmsxg: Hessel, Tan and Lee — Science, AskScience, and BadScience: On the Coexistence of Highly Related Communities

## Contribution

The first large study of affix-defined "highly related" subreddit pairs:
a taxonomy of affixes, an early-participant fraction for whether a newer
community was born from the older one, and an exploration effect against
matched controls.

## Key results

1. **Order and activity (pp. 4–5).** The affixed community was the newer
   one in most pairs, "This is indeed the case in 86% of" them; newer
   communities are usually far less active, "The mean log ratio is
   -2.0", though "(25.7%) are more active."
2. **Founders (pp. 5–6).** "Almost half of highly related communities do
   not really" share early participants: "only 7 of the first 100
   participants in makinghiphop were members of hiphop". The fraction
   varies widely by affix: "meta (65.8% from the original".
3. **Governance can differ (p. 4).** wallpaper and wallpapers have
   indistinguishable content "yet the moderator sets are disjoint";
   "Politic was created because some users" disliked politics' rules.
4. **Exploration (p. 6).** In spinoff pairs (more than "10% of the first
   100 early participants in the newer community are members of the older
   community"), explorers "generally become more active in the original
   community", larger for "action", "better" and "parody", "smaller in"
   medium "and negative in “nsfw”". "“action” explorers are around 10%
   more likely to increase their activity"; place explorers "roughly 2%
   less likely". "All 5 bottom pairs present significantly negative
   exploration effect" (p. 8).
5. **No causal claim (pp. 6, 8).** "Disclaimer: we do not make any claims
   of causality given"; "exploration does not necessarily cause increased
   activity"; "exploration appears to be a strong signal of interest
   level".
6. **Hedged reading (p. 2).** "Our observations may suggest that spinoff
   communities generally" "serve a complementary rather than competitive
   role in multicommunity settings."

## Assumptions

Name affixes as a proxy for relatedness, with a topical filter; membership
as any post or comment in the prior 30 days; a 30-day window; controls
matched on prior activity count; pairwise and observational.

## Concepts

Highly related communities; affix taxonomy; early-participant fraction;
spinoff; explorer and non-explorer; exploration effect.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CASE-tmp6idy5](../cases.d/CASE-tmp6idy5.md).** Measures for three of the case's outcomes
  (formation by the early-participant fraction, displacement by the
  activity ratio, complementarity by the exploration effect), and support
  for its caution that a parent can supply participants without causing
  the child: content twins often share almost no founders. ChatGPT's
  gloss, that differentiation "need not imply fragmentation", goes beyond
  the paper, which measures short-term individual activity, not community
  fragmentation, and finds the sign reversed for some kinds of spinoff.
- **[THEORY-tmp5zxkk](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp5zxkk.md).** On the same platform, nesting is not descent: in
  14% of pairs the specific community is the older, and about half share
  few founders.

## Limitations

Observational; 30-day windows; affix-named pairs only; data end in 2014;
per-category values only as the text describes them.
