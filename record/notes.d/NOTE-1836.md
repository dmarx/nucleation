# NOTE-1836: Bansal et al. — Does the Whole Exceed its Parts? The Effect of AI Explanations on Complementary Team Performance

## Contribution

An end-to-end test of explanations in the complementary zone, the finding that explanations increase reliance rather than appropriate reliance, and design implications (informative rather than persuasive explanations, devil's-advocate doubts, timing to avoid anchoring).

## Key results

- Abstract: "explanations increased the chance that humans will accept the AI’s recommendation, regardless of its correctness".
- §1: "with 1626 users on three tasks"; complementary performance seen in every human-AI condition.
- §7: "none of the explanation conditions produced an accuracy significantly higher than the simple baseline of showing the AI’s confidence".
- §5.1 / Fig. 4B: "explaining the top prediction lead to better accuracy when the AI recommendation was correct but worse when the AI was incorrect".
- §6.2: "explanations increased blind trust rather than appropriate reliance on AI".
- §6.2 (Explaining AI for appropriate reliance): "Explanations should be informative, instead of just convincing."; the AI "might play a devil’s advocate role, explaining its doubts".
- §6.3 (Rethinking AI’s role) on timing: showing the AI's answer and justification first: "This approach risks invoking the anchor effect".

## Assumptions

Accuracy is the team metric; crowd workers on easy tasks; AI accuracy fixed by selecting examples; explanation formats limited to highlights and short arguments; the AI's answer is shown before the human decides.

## Concepts

Complementary team performance; appropriate reliance vs blind trust; Explain-Top-1, Explain-Top-2, adaptive explanation; confidence baseline; expert-generated explanations; unknown unknowns; anchoring; devil's-advocate AI.

## Bearing on the record

A (A39 §4) cites it for explanations raised acceptance of AI recommendations whether or not they were correct. **Accurate**, almost verbatim from the abstract. Add: the net effect on team accuracy was nil against the confidence baseline, because gains when the AI was right were cancelled by losses when it was wrong.

- **[CASE-188](../cases.d/CASE-188.md)** (a proposed study separating corrigible resolve from entrenchment). **Supports the case's design concern; does not test it.** The paper is a clean empirical instance of the failure [CASE-188](../cases.d/CASE-188.md) must avoid scoring as success: a mechanism (explanations) that raises uptake of a recommendation regardless of whether it is right. It also gives [CASE-188](../cases.d/CASE-188.md) a measurement pattern: split outcomes by whether the incoming advice is correct, as Fig. 4B does, which is the analogue of [CASE-188](../cases.d/CASE-188.md)'s runs with and without a real defeater. It concerns human reliance on AI advice, not an agent's commitments, so it is an analogy, not evidence about entrenchment.
- **[CLAIM-349](../claims.d/CLAIM-349.md)** (access to a signal, its authority over action, and action on it are separable; adaptive regulation keeps access and makes authority conditional on evidence). **Corroborates by analogy.** In the study, explanations changed the *authority* of the AI's signal (more agreement) without making that authority conditional on the signal's correctness; confidence display and adaptive explanations are attempts to make it conditional (Fig. 5: lower agreement on low-confidence items).- **[LIT-2283](../literature.d/LIT-2283.md)** (Buçinca et al., cognitive forcing; unread): Bansal et al. cite Buçinca et al. 2020 ("Proxy tasks and subjective measures can be misleading", ref. [12]), a different paper by the same first author; the cognitive-forcing paper is the follow-up answer to the overreliance shown here. Context only.
- **[CLAIM-184](../claims.d/CLAIM-184.md)** (anchors entrench): §6.3's anchoring point (AI answer shown first) is a human-side instance. Mention only.

## Limitations

Crowd workers and easy tasks (authors' own caveat); results may not transfer to experts. Effects in Fig. 4B per condition were not all individually significant; The reader did not check each test. Published version not compared.
