# THEORY-tmpcxzxx: Teo and Kane show that a defect in a non-interacting gapped insulator or superconductor can be classified by the homotopy class of the Bloch or Bogoliubov–de Gennes Hamiltonian H(k, r) on a surface enclosing it, that this classification depends only on the symmetry class and on δ = d − D, and that in their examples a nontrivial class coincides with protected gapless modes bound to the defect, a correspondence they observe rather than prove

## The account

Teo and Kane (2010) classify defects in non-interacting insulators and superconductors by the topology of the Hamiltonian around them. They do not use the defect's own order-parameter charge. The Hamiltonian H(k, r) varies slowly with position r on a D-dimensional surface around the defect. Its classes, under deformations that keep the gap open and respect the ten Altland–Zirnbauer symmetries, form a periodic table that depends only on δ = d − D. A generalized bulk-boundary correspondence pairs each nontrivial class with protected gapless modes at the defect: chiral or helical Dirac or Majorana modes on line defects, and chiral or Majorana zero modes at point defects, where the invariant counts N+ − N−. In the Jackiw–Rebbi kink, the Jackiw–Rossi vortex and the dislocation, the defect's structural charge fixes this invariant. In a disordered weak topological insulator the winding survives "even though the defect has no obvious structural origin".

## What it does not show

- A proof of the index theorem. The authors say they "will not attempt to prove" it.
- Anything about the defect's own persistence, energy or dynamics. The defect is a fixed background, and the protection is of the bound modes while the bulk gap stays open.
- Results for interacting, disordered (beyond the remark on weak topological insulators) or non-adiabatic systems, all of which the conclusion leaves as future work.
- Any back-action of the bound modes on the defect.
