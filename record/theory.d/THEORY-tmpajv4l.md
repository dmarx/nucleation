# THEORY-tmpajv4l: Lohmann and colleagues' meta-analysis finds exposure to coercive control moderately associated with PTSD and depression symptoms, comparably to broader psychological intimate partner violence in the same studies and not, as they predicted, more strongly

## Source

[LIT-tmpaio5u](../literature.d/LIT-tmpaio5u.md), read in [NOTE-tmp9acbc](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp9acbc.md) (Europe PMC full text, PMC10666508).

## What was actually shown

Across 45 observational studies, pooled correlations of coercive-control exposure with PTSD symptoms (r = .32) and depression symptoms (r = .27); in the same studies psychological IPV correlated r = .34 and r = .33. "It was unexpected that associations of coercive control with PTSD and depression would not be clearly stronger than associations involving other types of IPV". The PTSD association was larger in shelter and DV-service samples (r = .40) than in community samples (r = .26).

## What this does not say

- That coercive control causes these symptoms: "a direct causal link between coercive control and mental health could not be established".
- That control harms beyond, or independently of, physical violence: physical IPV was not controlled for, and the comparison with it is with other meta-analyses.
- Anything about complex PTSD (one study), about men or gender-diverse people (few studies), or about responsibility.
