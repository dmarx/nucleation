# THEORY-tmp4fo7b: Lewis and colleagues report that, in families with a child conceived by assisted reproduction, mothers' and fathers' depression symptoms went with the child's depression and anxiety symptoms as strongly when the parent was genetically unrelated to the child as when related, and read this as environmental transmission not explained by shared adversity

## The account

Lewis, Rice, Harold, Collishaw and Thapar (2011), LIT-tmpdsu42. 852 IVF
families. Mother–child correlations of depression symptoms were 0.32 in
unrelated pairs (egg or embryo donation) and 0.31 in related ones.
Father–child correlations were 0.17 and 0.23. They were not attenuated by
negative life events, income or class, held at a second wave, and were
stronger for girls in unrelated pairs.

## Evidence

Questionnaires at two waves, with child symptoms rated by each parent. The
test that could have failed: no association in unrelated pairs, which
would have made transmission genetic.

## What it does not show

- Not trauma. The exposure is current depression symptoms.
- Not the direction. Children's symptoms may raise parents'.
- Not a germline route, which this design puts with genes on the related
  side and does not estimate apart.

## What it bears on

QUESTION-297's parenting route. It is a ground of CLAIM-tmp3qsdg, which
maps what each design separates.
