# CLAIM-184: Anchors entrench: a structure that persists early can lock in its framing, so that later structure grows on it and alternatives become hard to reach, and retirement by status is the record's remedy only if superseded anchors stop attracting new structure

## The claim

If persistent structures are what more complex structure grows on
([CLAIM-185](CLAIM-185.md)), the first structures to persist get built on first. An
early framing, given a code, gathers claims that rest on it, objections to
it and terms defined against it. Replacing it later means re-anchoring all
of that.

The physics read for this line has the extreme case. Dunitz & Bernstein
([LIT-917](../literature.d/LIT-917.md), unread) are cited for crystal forms that could no longer be
made once a more stable form had appeared, because seeds of the new form
were everywhere. Ostwald ([LIT-920](../literature.d/LIT-920.md), [NOTE-709](../notes.d/NOTE-709.md)) shows the mechanism
at the bench: once a room has been used for alum, a supersaturated drop
cannot be kept free of seeds, and a single stray seed revives a sterilized
batch (pp. 291, 324). Luria names the same danger on the record side. It is
named for a mnemonist whose total recall crowded out meaning ([LU-ADR-011](https://github.com/dmarx/luria/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-011.md)).

The record's remedy is retirement by status. A Superseded claim stays,
with its successor named, so that what pointed at it still resolves, and a
reader is sent on. That works only if new structure stops growing on the
retired anchor.

## What would answer it

Two findings would. The first: Superseded entries draw almost no new
citations after retirement, other than citations of them as history, so
supersession does turn growth off. The second: a framing replaced
late in an argument costs no more to replace than one replaced early.

The check can be run on this record now. Count the citations of each
Superseded CLAIM and TERM made after its retirement date, and separate
those acknowledged as history (`inactive-ok`) from the rest.

## Note of 2026-10-11 (later): constancy against resilience

[THEORY-132](../theory.d/THEORY-132.md) (Holling) is added as a ground. Managing a system for constancy
can erode its resilience, its capacity to absorb change and persist within
its domain. That is a stronger form of this counter. A record that enforces
identity strictly maximizes constancy, and may reduce the argument's
capacity to reframe. [CLAIM-195](CLAIM-195.md) draws the same point from the
constraint-set side.

## Note of 2026-10-12: the bar for lock-in

The will-organization line's [TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md) (protective lock-in) sets the bar
this counter has to clear: a regime that "generates the conditions that
justify its own continuation and reduces the capacity to leave it". That
needs a loop, and [TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md) excludes inertia and habit. Path dependence
alone, Ostwald's ubiquitous seeds, does not qualify. [THEORY-178](../theory.d/THEORY-178.md) adds that,
in the one regulatory network studied, a lasting state needs a positive
circuit. [THEORY-136](../theory.d/THEORY-136.md) says what would show one: hysteresis or a lasting shift
after a temporary push. This counter has not shown a loop. A candidate
would be that forcing moves into the grammar miscodes them, the miscodes
produce correction entries, and those entries feed more filing. That is
unshown.
