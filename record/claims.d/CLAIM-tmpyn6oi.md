# CLAIM-tmpyn6oi: The alternatives against which a governing relationship or principle is assessed can be products of that relationship, so the present distribution of options is not a neutral baseline, and an actor cannot justify its terms by the narrowness of options it helped narrow without justification; the assessment must separate the existence of a dependence, the legitimacy of its creation, the authority exercised through it and the responsibilities of maintaining it

## The claim

A106 §9 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b):

> **The alternatives against which the principle is evaluated may themselves have been produced by the governing relationship.**

A102 §6's example: a company that "has eliminated competitors, structured
contracts to discourage exit, and acquired control over the infrastructure
necessary for alternative providers to operate" offers customers its terms
or no access, and they accept. A principle letting it enforce those
contracts "might appear relatively defensible if we take the resulting
market structure as an unquestioned background fact. But the market
structure is part of what may need justification." The consequence A
draws: "An actor cannot necessarily justify its conduct by pointing to the
limited options currently available to those affected if the actor helped
create those limitations through unjustified conduct."

A gives the converse the same weight: "Not every dependence was wrongfully
produced. Some dependencies arise through legitimate specialization,
voluntary cooperation, technological change, or unavoidable scarcity." So
the assessment must separate "**The existence of dependence**, **the
legitimacy of its creation**, **the authority exercised within it**, and
**the responsibilities associated with maintaining it**" (A102 §6). A106 §9
lists five loci: the legitimacy of the original allocation of powers,
whether the arrangement legitimately created the present dependencies,
whether the actor has authority to use them as leverage, whether the
arrangement keeps the protections and alternatives owed, and whether it
stays open to revision. "These are not necessarily five independent wrongs.
They are different possible loci of normative assessment."

The claim's point is about the baseline. Whatever the principle of
assessment, contractualist or not, it compares a relationship's terms
with some alternatives. If those alternatives were narrowed by the
relationship itself, comparing against them builds the narrowing into the
verdict. A: "The point is to avoid treating the present distribution of
resources, options, and bargaining power as morally self-validating."

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-670](CLAIM-670.md)** (Proposed): an arrangement can produce the dependence
  through which it exploits. This claim rests on it: it is the mechanism
  by which the baseline becomes endogenous.
- **[CLAIM-464](CLAIM-464.md)** (Active): an intervention can change what it would take to
  correct it, while the standards stay the same. This claim rests on it
  for the parallel point about assessment: an actor's earlier moves can
  change the alternatives against which its terms are judged.
- **[CLAIM-677](CLAIM-677.md)** (Proposed) measures the freedom an arrangement leaves by
  what remains open to a non-adopter; **[CLAIM-690](CLAIM-690.md)** (Proposed) sorts
  dependences by who controls their terms; **[CLAIM-682](CLAIM-682.md)** (Proposed) says
  when a dependence is presumptively dominating. Each takes the present
  alternatives as an input. This claim says that input can itself need
  justification.
- **CLAIM-tmp8wki5**, filed from the part before, is a special case with a
  loop: a restriction that lowers the competence it tests, and so produces
  the evidence for its own continuation.
- It bears on **[QUESTION-tmpk7u3e](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpk7u3e.md)**: A calls it "probably our sharpest
  potential criticism" of contractualism, and also says that Scanlon's
  framework "is capable of considering such consequences". Which of the
  two is right is that question.

## What it does not say

- Not that dependence is coercive. A: "This is much more discriminating
  than simply declaring dependent relationships coercive."
- Not which baseline to use instead. A102 §6 suggests "the vulnerabilities
  that would be produced, sustained, or relieved by general adoption of each
  principle"; that is a direction, not a measure.
- Not that present agreement is worthless. A106 §9: "A dependency may arise
  wrongfully while the dependent party's present agreement remains
  informed."
