# CLAIM-tmpuzk5o: Collective identity may be a sloppy mess of governed perspectives, that is, a pseudo-self, playing a question–answer game

## The claim

The owner's words, at U33b: "collective identity as a (sloppy?) mess of (governed) perspectives (i.e. a pseudo-self?) playing a question-answer game".

What is new beside the owner's earlier turns is the join. U6 made a self "a literal sloppy mess of connected perspectives" (CLAIM-tmpy4mba). U10 made a team a pseudo-self (TERM-tmpxwa7g). U26 proposed a game between queries and targets (CLAIM-tmp61i14). U33b carries the first to a collective, names it with the second, says its perspectives are governed, and says what holds the mess together: the question–answer game it plays.

## The question marks

- **"(sloppy?)"** asks whether the word fits. The record holds two readings of it from U6, filed as alternatives: colloquial (CLAIM-tmpy4mba) and technical (CLAIM-tmptsbhv). The owner's choice there carries here. A64b §1 gives it a third, precise meaning of its own: "The decomposition into perspectives is not necessarily unique, stable, or equally resolvable under every observational regime." That is the regime-relative decomposition the record holds as CLAIM-tmp7pe33 and, for teams, CLAIM-tmp7580l.
- **"(i.e. a pseudo-self?)"** asks whether a collective identity is a pseudo-self. A64b §6 answers with five criteria for a stronger candidate (CLAIM-tmp812ex), and wonders whether the name "undersells the possibility".

## Standing

Proposed, the owner's. A64b develops it without objecting (CLAIM-tmprkpq5), and so the claim stays open on the record's own pseudo-self question, QUESTION-tmp4fkkx: a collective that plays such a game may still be an ecosystem of pseudo-selves and not one.
