# ARG-tmpuhkwv: Northstar-A and Northstar-B differ only in escalation authority and act differently, so escalation authority helps govern efficacy

## The inference

Let R be the escalation and suspension relation. Holding attitudes, information
and policy fixed, alter R; if regime transitions change systematically, R
helps explain efficacy (Woodward's interventionism, [THEORY-138](../theory.d/THEORY-138.md), though the
manuscript does not cite it).

## Critical questions

- **Is everything said to be held fixed really fixed?** Stipulated, since the
  case is fictional. Answered only by stipulation.
- **Does changing R change anything else?** Not answered. Changes to authority
  usually change incentives, and incentives change attitudes.
- **Is the case realistic, or only coherent?** Not answered in the final text;
  the first draft conceded it does not show "that real organizations would
  behave so cleanly". That concession belongs here.
- **Causation or constitution?** An intervention shows R makes a difference; it
  does not by itself show R is part of the will-organization rather than a
  cause of it. The manuscript cites Craver ([LIT-tmpsf1ce](../literature.d/LIT-tmpsf1ce.md)) for the distinction and does not
  apply it to its own test.
