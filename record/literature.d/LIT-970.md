# LIT-970: Symmetry in Neural Network Parameter Spaces

Bo Zhao, Robin Walters and Rose Yu (2025), *Transactions on Machine Learning Research* — [ARXIV-2506.13018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13018)

## Key takeaways

- **A catalogue.** Linear layers have a GL(h) symmetry. Homogeneous
  activations such as ReLU have positive rescaling, tanh has sign flips,
  and batchnorm and other normalizations have scaling. Softmax has
  translation, and pointwise activations have permutation. Attention has
  GL(d_k) per head, plus head permutation and GL(d_v). A network inherits
  the symmetries of all its subnetworks (Table 1, §2.2).
- **Completeness.** For tanh and Gaussian RBF networks the known
  symmetries are complete. ReLU networks can have hidden symmetries
  beyond permutation and scaling, and functional dimension can vary
  within one fiber (§2.4).
- **Landscape.** Continuous symmetries make minima connected manifolds,
  which is one explanation of mode connectivity. The survey notes that
  "not all points in a minimum are related by a symmetry transformation"
  and that connectivity may have other sources (§3.1). Discrete symmetries
  replicate minima instead (§3.2).
- **Dynamics.** Continuous symmetries give gradient flow conserved
  quantities, layer imbalance being the standard one. These label
  trajectories and their endpoints, which formalizes part of implicit
  bias. Finite steps, noise and weight decay break them, and the cited
  results find SGD drifting toward balanced solutions (§5).
