# NOTE-tmppa0w2: Dijkema (2015) — The Ethics of Attention in an Age of Distraction

## Contribution

Second-hand, but it carries the book's positive account, with several verbatim passages from it, which the first-hand interview (LIT-tmp0gt3l) lacks.

## Key results

1. The thesis, Dijkema's statement (para. 1): Crawford argues that "our inability to pay attention does carry such moral weight".
2. The book quoted (para. 4): "The standard for truth is relocated: it is no longer found out in the world, but inside our own head."
3. Dijkema's gloss, not Crawford's words (para. 5): "monopolized attention, like a monopoly in the economy, leads to sameness and the loss of individuality."
4. The organ shop (para. 6): Taylor and Boody's organ makers show "a type of rationality he calls “an ecology of attention.”"
5. The book quoted through Dijkema (para. 8): communities of skilled practice, where "competence rests on an apprehension of real features of the world, as refracted through some set of human needs/desires and corresponding technologies"; and "we think through the body."
6. The attentional commons named (para. 12).
7. Dijkema's own argument: "Being educated requires submitting to authoritative structures—to competent authorities—which serve as a check on your subjectivity"; without them "we’re likely to end up with relativism again."

## Assumptions

The reviewer's: an ethics of attention needs authoritative guidance, and religion supplies it. Not Crawford's.

## Concepts

- **Ecology of attention**: the rationality of a craft tradition that attends to material constraints and to the purpose of the work.
- **Communities of skilled practice**: where competence answers to real features of the world.

## Connections

A second stand-in for LIT-tmpchx6j. With the interview it covers the book's diagnosis (first-hand, in the interview) and its positive account (second-hand, here): the Enlightenment self, monopolized attention, the ecology of attention, communities of skilled practice, the organ makers, thinking through the body, the attentional commons. It does not restate jigs, affordances, the casino and machine-gambling chapter in detail, or the book's policy proposals. It extends Crawford's 2006 essay (THEORY-tmpiihve) on skilled manual work as a ground of agency.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-tmp6wuql**: support in spirit, second-hand. Competence formed by engagement with real features of the world is what substitutive environments remove; no argument about trajectories. No relation.
- **CLAIM-tmpkjv7u**: leaves it open. "Monopolized attention" is market power over attention, not discretion over dependents.

## Limitations

A review with a religious argument of its own. The book's passages are the reviewer's selections and could not be checked against the book.
