# NOTE-1641: Schneider, Möritz and Dietz — The sequence of events during folding of a DNA origami

## Contribution

An experimental reference dataset, strand by strand and terminus by
terminus, of one origami's isothermal folding pathway, offered for
calibrating coarse-grained models; the authors name the domain-level
Markov chain "as described by Turberfield and co-workers" (refs 17 and 19:
Dunn et al. and Dannenberg et al., 2015). Its result: the order of folding
is set by "cooperative, nonlocal effects" of the topology of connectivity
more than by local sequence strength, and folding runs through a
nucleated, compacted intermediate whose removal reroutes folding rather
than forbidding it. It withdraws the authors' earlier conjecture (Sobczak
et al. 2012) that nuclei are the most thermally stable domains.

## Key insight

What folds first is decided by which scaffold regions crossovers tie
together, because tying distant regions shortens every later loop; it is
not decided by which segments bind most strongly. Remove the early
domains and the target is still reached, hours later by another route.

## Assumptions

- **One design, one condition.** A 42-helix honeycomb brick, 140 staples,
  1132 staple and 166 scaffold crossovers; Table 1: 25 °C for 2.25 min,
  65 °C for 120 min, 50 °C for 840 min; 20 nM scaffold, each staple
  100 nM. The text calls the 65 °C step a "denaturation phase", the Table 1
  caption "strand annealing". Generality is hoped for: the dataset
  "through its sheer detail, should achieve general importance".
- **Isothermal premise (Introduction).** Origami "also self-assemble at
  constant temperature (18) (so far, we have found no exception from this
  rule)".
- **Normalized progress.** Each reaction's fluorescence is set to 0 at the
  start and 1 at the end, so it says when a label saturates, not what
  fraction of objects fold.
- **Labels and inference.** Labelled and unlabelled copies at 1:1 assumed
  not to perturb the pathway; proximity under FRET taken as incorporation;
  single-terminus kinetics fit by assuming independence (below).

## Key results

- **Object folds well at one temperature (Results).** "The object
  self-assembles with satisfactory yield and few by-products when subjected
  to constant temperature incubation at 50°C in the presence of 20 mM
  MgCl2"; yield judged by gel and TEM, not given as a number.
- **Folding times (Fig. 2G-H).** Per-staple completion "varied from ~20 to
  ~200 min"; each curve fits two exponentials; interstrand time constants
  cluster at about 0.5, 30 and 300 min.
- **Termini independent (Fig. 3B-D).** "Whereas the 5′ dataset shows a
  clear gradient from early to late incorporating termini, the 3′ dataset
  has no apparent order"; "strand termini incorporate independently of
  each other"; so "folding occurs via individual double-stranded DNA
  (dsDNA) domain formation, rather than entire staple strand binding".
- **Inference flaw admitted (Methods).** Fitting assumes the pair signal is
  the product of single-terminus probabilities; "Since DNA origami folding
  globally shows signs of cooperativity, this description represents an
  oversimplification", though the fit is "highly overdetermined" (280
  unknowns against about 2800 pairs per time point).
- **Truncations (Fig. 3F).** Normally "the unfolded state disappears
  immediately" "and converts into an intermediate, compacted species".
  Without the 20 fastest 5' segments, "the intermediate no longer occurs.
  The unfolded state prevails for hours, and the fully folded state emerges
  only with substantial hour-long delay". Without the 20 slowest, the
  intermediate forms and "persists for much longer times". Discussion: "If
  these cannot be formed", "folding may still occur, but it proceeds via
  another, slower pathway."
- **Strength does not set the order (Fig. 4B, 4C, 4E).** "little
  correlation between the free energy of hybridization of entire staple
  strands with their mean folding time"; truncating the strongest or the
  weakest segments has "very similar effects"; "the folding pathway does
  not strongly depend on the strand segment binding strength."
- **Same connectivity, new sequences (Fig. 4D, 4F).** In a circular
  permutation "all the staple strands had completely different sequences"
  "but the internal topology of connectivity remained identical"; mean
  folding times correlate, and knocking out early strands abolishes the
  intermediate in both. Results: "both the local sequences and internal
  topology of connectivity are two important factors"; Discussion, more
  strongly: "the folding pathway does not depend so much on the local
  sequence but on cooperative effects induced by the global topology of
  connectivity".
- **No spatial order, a scaffold-topology order (Fig. 5A-B).** "The
  “exterior” or the “interior” of the object does not really exist during
  much of the folding." On a ring graph of the scaffold, "certain distant
  regions on the scaffold that, once connected by one or more crossovers,
  fold cooperatively or at least in close succession", explained by the
  "entropic cost of forming scaffold loops"; the slower 5' termini close
  longer loops, a difference that "arises coincidentally from the
  particular staple and scaffold routing".
- **Compaction (Fig. 5C, computed).** Mean scaffold distance falls "from
  initially >2000 bases to ~50 to 100 bases", so the entropic penalties of
  loops "vanish rapidly". Computed from the mean times, not measured.
- **Nucleation (Discussion).** "nucleating staple segment clusters
  connected by long-distance crossovers"; "a compacted intermediate formed
  by a set of nucleating double-helical DNA domains"; the earlier
  thermal-stability view of nuclei "is too simplistic".
- **Design consequence (Discussion).** The order "cannot be easily tied to
  (and, thus, also not controlled by) simple, local design rules."

## Concepts

- **Mean folding time**: when a staple's or terminus's normalized signal
  saturates, used to rank the sequence of events.
- **Compacted intermediate**: a gel-mobility species between unfolded and
  folded, formed at once in the default design.
- **Topology of connectivity**: which scaffold positions the staples'
  crossovers join, independent of the sequences that join them.
- **Nucleating clusters**: segments joined by long-range crossovers that
  incorporate early and together.
- **Surgical operations**: segment truncations and strand knockouts used to
  test which domains the pathway needs.

## What measurement told which states apart

- **Intrastrand beacon**: a whole staple's change; not which domain bound.
- **Interstrand FRET**: two termini colocalized on a scaffold; order of
  events, but single-terminus kinetics need the product model.
- **Quenched agarose gels**: unfolded, compacted, folded and aggregated
  species by mobility; not which domains are bound.
- **Negative-stain TEM**: the final shape only.
- **Cost**: 140 beacon reactions in triplicate and 12,688 FRET mixtures,
  five labelled variants of all 140 staples, for one object at one
  temperature.

## Connections

It names refs 17 and 19, Dunn et al. (2015), [LIT-2135](../literature.d/LIT-2135.md), and Dannenberg
et al. (2015), [LIT-2129](../literature.d/LIT-2129.md), as a starting point for modelling; their loop
term is the mechanism it invokes, and where Dunn et al. steer the fold by
staple strength, this paper finds strength a poor predictor of order. It
revises the nucleation reading of Sobczak et al. (2012), the same lab's
annealing study filed in this batch. The held simulation of DeLuca et al.
([LIT-2081](../literature.d/LIT-2081.md), [NOTE-1561](NOTE-1561.md), [THEORY-1398](../theory.d/THEORY-1398.md)) predicts a collapsed precursor before
global order; this paper's compacted intermediate is an experimental
counterpart, though gels show compaction, not local order. Its nucleating
clusters are the molecular counterpart of the placed actuators of
[THEORY-1418](../theory.d/THEORY-1418.md) (Stern, Pinson and Murugan): placement matters more than
strength.

## Limitations

- **One object, one temperature, one buffer**; no yields as numbers.
- **Normalized progress** hides the fraction that never folds.
- **The inference model** assumes the independence the paper's own
  cooperativity contradicts.
- **The compaction curve is computed**, not measured.
- **Correlations described qualitatively** in the text ("little",
  "more"); values in the figures not read; Supplementary Materials not
  read.
- No reconfiguration, strand displacement, misfold identification or
  melting data.

## Open questions

- Does the topology-over-sequence finding hold for another multilayer
  design or another temperature? A second object, or this one at 45 and
  55 °C, would tell.
- What fraction of objects take the slower pathway when the nuclei are
  removed, rather than how late the signal saturates? Gel or AFM yield per
  truncation would show it.
