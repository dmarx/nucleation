# CASE-tmpl995z: A proposed study: one network of locally described units in which physical coupling, the correspondence maps that say which local states agree, and the apparatus that revises those maps are manipulated independently, to find which of compatibility, attraction, effective boundary constraint, persistent individuation and reliable revision come apart, and how much each costs to establish

## The design

A network of units, each with a local state. The units are coupled
physically, or simulated as such. Each edge carries a correspondence map saying
which pairs of local states count as agreeing. An apparatus measures the units
and may be allowed to revise the maps. Three things are varied independently:

1. **Physical coupling:**
   - weak or strong;
   - dissipative or conservative;
   - with or without a common external drive.
2. **Correspondence maps:**
   - compatible around every cycle, or frustrated (nonzero holonomy);
   - scalar or of higher-dimensional stalk, so that reduction can need
     higher-order relations ([THEORY-tmpvecoh](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpvecoh.md)).
3. **Apparatus revision:**
   - none;
   - by consistency alone, as in discourse sheaves ([THEORY-tmptkmt9](../theory.d/THEORY-tmptkmt9.md));
   - by consistency checked against held-out interventions the apparatus
     did not choose ([CLAIM-342](../claims.d/CLAIM-342.md)).

Regimes from A22 §10, extended by the owner's review:

| regime | isolates |
|---|---|
| compatible maps, weak coupling | coherence without integration |
| compatible maps, strong dissipative coupling that enforces those maps | attraction to the specified compatibility space, and whether the gap in relaxation rates is large enough to separate time scales usefully ([CLAIM-tmpywecm](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpywecm.md)) |
| compatible maps, strong dissipative coupling that enforces other relations | strong coupling without attraction to the specified compatibility space |
| compatible maps, conservative coupling | coherent oscillation without relaxation |
| frustrated maps | persistent incompatibility and compromise organization |
| common drive, no reciprocal coupling | apparent synchrony without mutual integration ([QUESTION-063](../questions.d/QUESTION-063.md)) |
| higher-dimensional stalks with interior units | boundary constraints the parts' relations cannot state ([QUESTION-tmprr9yy](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmprr9yy.md)) |
| revision by consistency alone | coherence without knowledge |
| revision checked against held-out interventions | revision that can improve prediction |

### Coupling assumptions

The experiment is informative because coupling and correspondence can be set
independently. So the regimes must not assume that one delivers the other.

- **Strong coupling alone does not attract to the specified compatibility
  space.** Dissipative coupling drives the states toward the kernel of
  whatever operator it actually realizes. That is the specified space only
  when the coupling enforces the specified correspondence maps: in the
  linear case, when its Laplacian is δ*δ for the apparatus's own
  coboundary δ. A strongly dissipative coupling that enforces other
  relations attracts to a different space. That is why it is a regime of
  its own above.
- **A positive gap is not a useful separation.** For a connected, enforcing
  Laplacian flow, every incompatible mode decays, so there is always some
  gap above the kernel. A separation of time scales that supports a
  reduced description needs a gap large relative to the slower dynamics
  and to the observation horizon ([CLAIM-tmpywecm](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpywecm.md)'s window,
  1/(κK) ≪ t ≪ 1/(κε)). The readout is the size of the gap against those
  scales, not whether it is positive.

## Readouts

For each regime:
- whether an exact or approximate global section exists;
- whether the dynamics reach it, and at what rates;
- which normal modes emerge;
- what effective boundary law an observer confined to some units can fit,
  and whether it is pairwise, higher-order or history-dependent
  ([CLAIM-tmpose3y](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpose3y.md));
- whether the organization survives localized interventions;
- how much communication and computation it takes to recover the
  correspondences from measurements.

In the revising regimes, the key readout is whether revision improves
predictions on interventions the apparatus did not choose. A22 §10: this tests
"the difference between **becoming more coherent** and **becoming more
knowledgeable**."

## What it would show

The transitions [CLAIM-tmpgubhp](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpgubhp.md) says are distinct can be made to come apart on
purpose. Where they come apart in the study, the claim gains a constructed
case. Where they never come apart across the regimes, the claim's defeater is
in view. The revising regimes also bear on [QUESTION-tmplxq7l](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmplxq7l.md). They do so only
as adaptation of an apparatus, unless the revised maps feed back into the
physical coupling. A variant in which they do would be the study's closest
approach to interaction changing the operative correspondence structure.

## Standing
