# LIT-tmpxuqbt: The Major Transitions in Evolution—A Philosophy-of-Science Perspective

Samir Okasha (2022), *Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution* 10:793824 —
DOI-10.3389/fevo.2022.793824

## Key takeaways

- **What a transition is** (p. 2). Okasha defines it as "the evolution of
  a higher-level biological unit out of formerly-free living units",
  setting aside the genetic code, DNA, sex and language from Maynard Smith
  and Szathmáry's list.
- **Not inevitable** (p. 3). Transitions may be "rare singularities", and
  intermediate levels of cooperation may be stable rather than staging
  posts.
- **General principles, coarse explanations** (pp. 3–4). A collective
  needs a short-term advantage to form; it may be destabilised by
  defectors; kinship, policing and division of labour can align the
  units' interests; and higher-level selection must trump lower-level
  selection. These are relevant to all transitions, but "relevant" is not
  "explanatorily fundamental", and over-applying social-evolution concepts
  such as "cheating" has done harm (Rainey et al. 2014).
- **Genic and hierarchical approaches** (pp. 4–5) are "something of a
  false dichotomy", and formal equivalence between multilevel and
  inclusive-fitness models is not causal-explanatory equivalence.
- **The hierarchy is to be explained** (pp. 5–6). The traditional levels
  of selection debate took the hierarchy as given (citing his 2005 paper);
  in the transitions it "moves from being part of the explanans to being
  part of the explanandum". Given that transitions occurred, the
  importance of multi-level selection "can hardly be denied".
- **A privileged level** (pp. 7–9). A transition shifts the level of
  organismality, and the highest occupied level is privileged as where
  organisms are. Okasha weighs three ways of handling organisms within
  organisms and finds none decisive.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request, as a supplement to the essay on
organizational will, and as the substitute for Okasha's 2005 paper
([LIT-tmp6llhm](LIT-tmp6llhm.md)), which it cites for the two-level view of transitions and
for the point that the traditional debate set aside the origin of the
hierarchy. It restates that point and builds on it, so it is declared as
extending the 2005 paper. Aktipis et al. ([LIT-tmpo461k](LIT-tmpo461k.md)) apply its general
principle to multicellularity. The account is [THEORY-tmpevx7w](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpevx7w.md).

It bears on the essay's §6.3: capture from below is what a transition must
overcome, and policing is one way. The record also reads the
hierarchy-as-explanandum point as bearing on §6.2: the levels an
organization's members depend on are produced, not given. Okasha does not
discuss organizations.
