# NOTE-tmp24z3z: Kübler & Birbaumer — BCI and extinction of goal-directed thinking in CLIS

## Contribution

A retrospective pooled analysis ("meta-analysis") of every patient the
Tübingen group had trained with a brain–computer interface since 1995:
35 patients, 29 with ALS, trained with slow-cortical-potential, sensorimotor
rhythm or P300 BCIs. It shows that BCI performance does not decline
continuously with paralysis: it is flat from minor impairment to the
locked-in state and collapses in the completely locked-in state (CLIS). It
then states a hypothesis to explain the collapse, extinction of
goal-directed thinking through lost response–outcome contingency, and the
observation that would refute it.

## Key insight

The failure in CLIS is cast as operant extinction, not as loss of
perception or of cognition. Evoked potentials show the CLIS brain still
discriminates and processes heard speech; what goes is the voluntary,
reward-driven production of a response. The mechanism the authors name is
the patient losing "the perception of the contingency between the required
physiological behaviour ... and its consequences", compounded by a reward
whose value is blunted because artificial feeding and ventilation keep the
body from ever being out of balance. On this view, the inbound channel is
intact and the outbound loop is what has died.

## Assumptions

- **Defined by the absence of a channel.** CLIS is "a state in which no
  communication and thus, no social interaction is possible"; LIS patients
  keep "residual voluntary control over few muscles, such as eye movement,
  eye blinks, or twitches with the lip".
- **Categorical outcome.** Performance is coded into four ordinal levels
  (chance, above chance, criterion ≥ 70%, independence), not correct
  response rates, and the best level across BCI types is taken for each
  patient.
- **Heterogeneous training.** Session counts, BCI versions and
  interruptions differ across patients; number of sessions is not
  modelled. Eleven patients used two or three BCI types.
- **Cognition read from passive ERPs.** Cognitive function in CLIS is
  inferred from oddball P300 and semantic N400 responses to auditory
  stimuli, since the patients cannot respond.
- **The curarized-rat analogy.** The hypothesis rests on Dworkin and
  Miller's failure (1986) to replicate operant control of autonomic
  responses in chronically curarized rats, and on the claim that such rats
  and CLIS patients "share many similarities".

## Key results

- **Sample.** 35 patients: 29 ALS, 1 chronic Guillain–Barré, 1 muscular
  dystrophy, 1 cerebral paresis, 1 hypoxic brain damage after cardiac
  arrest, 2 stroke. Impairment: 2 minor, 8 moderate, 8 major, 10 LIS,
  7 CLIS (Table 2; Table 4 has N = 34 because one patient's data were lost).
  SCP-BCI: 25 patients; SMR-BCI: 5; P300-BCI: 11.
- **Mean correct responses.** SCP 66% (N = 23), SMR 71% (N = 5), P300 66%
  (N = 11) (Table 3).
- **Impairment and performance.** Kruskal–Wallis χ² = 13.4, df = 4,
  p < .01; η² = .40 (F4/29 = 4.78, p < .01). Without the CLIS group:
  χ² = 3.5, df = 3, p = .32; η² = .12 (F3/23 = 1.0, p = .41). Adding other
  groups' patients (Table 5), still without CLIS: χ² = 3.9, df = 3,
  p = .27; η² = .07.
- **The CLIS group.** "in the CLIS group, 5 of 7 patients performed at
  chance level and only two achieved above chance level control, which was
  with 59 and 62%, far below criterion level control." They were in CLIS
  from the start: "we were unable to restore basic communication (yes/no)
  in patients who were in the complete locked-in state at the beginning of
  training".
- **Ventilation is not the cause.** Among non-CLIS patients, ventilated and
  non-ventilated did not differ (U-test Z = −.87, p = .43).
- **Cognition in CLIS.** "all patients had the N1-P2 complex indicating
  functionality of primary auditory cortex"; in the oddball paradigm "all
  but one patients' response to the target stimuli was significantly
  different from that to the standard stimuli"; "Responses to semantic
  mismatch were absent in two CLIS patients". In the Discussion: "With one
  exception, all CLIS patients of the sample reported here had
  ERP-responses to one or more of the complex cognitive tasks", which "do
  not prove or disprove normal information processing in CLIS but suggest
  some intact 'processing modules' in most ALS patients with CLIS despite a
  reduced general arousal".
- **The hypothesis, in the authors' words** (Discussion): "Chronically
  curarized rats and people with longer time periods in CLIS may loose the
  perception of the contingency between the required physiological
  behaviour (SMR decrease, changes of the SCP amplitude or heart rate
  increase) and its consequences (brain stimulation reward in the
  curarized rat and cursor control or letter selection in the patient)."
  Extinction occurs "because experimental trials with a clear contingency
  between the physiological response (e.g., heart rate) and the rewards are
  too rare in a 24 hour day ... the remaining 22 hours without any
  contingency provide enough time for extinction. Together with the
  proposed blunting of the reward value due to the lack of homeostatic
  imbalances, acquisition of voluntary control of the desired physiological
  response becomes impossible".
- **The prevention it predicts.** "We propose that learning BCI-control or
  any other contingency before onset of CLIS, which can be transferred from
  LIS to CLIS should prevent extinction in CLIS."
- **The conclusion and its refutation condition.** "we may conclude that
  passive sensory information processing is intact in CLIS even at the
  most complex semantic processing levels. It is the complete lack of
  motor control and feedback which might be responsible for the cessation
  of voluntary cognitive activity, goal directed thinking and imagery
  supporting a 'motor theory of thinking' already discussed by William
  James ... A single CLIS patient who learns to communicate with a BCI or
  any other communication method will disprove our hypothesis."

## Concepts

- **LIS / CLIS.** LIS: almost completely paralysed "with residual
  voluntary control over few muscles"; CLIS: "all motor control is lost",
  equivalently "no communication and thus, no social interaction is
  possible". CLIS can also arise from disorders of consciousness; one of the
  seven (PR, hypoxia after heart attack) "was probably in such an altered
  state of consciousness but has never been diagnosed as such".
- **Criterion level control.** ≥ 70% correct, the accuracy the authors
  take as needed for binary communication.
- **Extinction.** In the learning-theory sense: a learned response fades
  when it is no longer followed by its consequence.
- **Motor theory of thinking.** Attributed to James (1890): voluntary
  thought depends on motor control and its feedback.

## Connections

It pools the group's earlier patient reports (Kübler et al. 1998, 1999,
2004, 2005; Birbaumer et al. 1999; Neumann and Birbaumer 2003; Nijboer et
al.; Hill et al. 2006) and compares them with other groups' (Wolpaw,
Pfurtscheller, Neuper, Sellers and Donchin, Piccione, Hoffmann). The
extinction hypothesis is Birbaumer's (2006, "Breaking the silence"),
resting on Miller (1969) and Dworkin and Miller (1986). The ERP battery is
Kotchoubey et al. (2002–2005). Chaudhary et al. (LIT-tmpzuemc, read in
NOTE-tmp4xxvp), with Birbaumer as senior author, cite it as the hypothesis
their patient tests.

## Bearing on the record

- **QUESTION-tmpr5tck: which of the two things does the hypothesis
  concern?** Contingency between intention and outcome, as the question
  says, and the paper is explicit that the other one survives. What is
  lost is "the perception of the contingency between the required
  physiological behaviour ... and its consequences"; what is kept is
  "passive sensory information processing ... even at the most complex
  semantic processing levels". The extinction mechanism is operant: too few
  trials in which a response is followed by its consequence, and a reward
  that no longer restores a homeostatic imbalance. So the question's
  separation is the authors' own, and its inference holds: if the
  hypothesis is true, what extinguishes goal-directed thinking is not a
  shortage of evidence the loop did not select. One qualification: the
  conclusion names "the complete lack of motor control and feedback", and
  "feedback" there plausibly includes the somatic and proprioceptive
  feedback of movement (the paper does not say). That is still feedback
  contingent on the patient's own output, not unselected input.
- **CLAIM-tmpki5gp.** Its quotation is accurate, but it gives the
  hypothesis as "the judgement loop, on this view, runs down when action is
  gone". The paper says less and something more specific. Less: it is
  "might be responsible", from seven patients, by analogy with a rat result
  that itself failed to replicate. More specific: what runs down is
  voluntary, reward-driven response production ("voluntary cognitive
  activity, goal directed thinking and imagery"), not perception or
  semantic processing. The paper therefore supports the claim's own
  concession ("What it does not say", second bullet) more than its
  empirical paragraph: it is evidence that action carries the contingency
  that sustains goal-directed activity, not evidence that action is the
  route by which the loop receives unselected evidence.
- **CLAIM-tmpq9do8, defeater (b).** The paper offers a candidate for (b):
  lucid goal-directed judgement failing when every contingency between
  intention and outcome is removed. But it does not establish it. The
  patients' consciousness was not shown to be otherwise preserved (one was
  probably in a disorder of consciousness, two lacked the N400, arousal
  was "reduced", and "It is often difficult to decide whether the patient
  is awake or in sleep stage 1 or 2"), and the failure was a failure to
  acquire a new operant skill, not a demonstrated loss of judgement. The
  thesis's distinction also bites: what the paper says is extinguished is
  production of a response that acts on something (a cursor, a letter);
  whether judgement about what is heard survives is exactly what the
  paradigm cannot see. So the paper leaves (b) untested, which is the
  CASE's asymmetry.
- **CLAIM-tmpt568z.** No direct bearing. The paper's scoring of patient
  performance is fixed (chance thresholds computed in advance), but that
  is the observers' anchor, not the patient's.
- **CASE-tmpxgcq2.**
  - *Hearing intact; vision not tested.* All CLIS patients had the N1-P2
    auditory response, so primary auditory cortex was working. All stimuli
    were auditory; vision is not assessed. The SCP and SMR BCIs gave visual
    cursor feedback, which matters for CLIS patients whose eyes may be
    closed or dry; the paper does not discuss this.
  - *"None of seven CLIS patients regained yes/no communication".* Correct
    in substance, but "regained" should read that they entered training
    already in CLIS. None had learned a BCI in LIS and carried it over,
    which is precisely the route the authors predict would prevent
    extinction. Chaudhary et al.'s patient took that route (he used an
    EOG-based BCI with the same speller before CLIS), so his success fits
    the hypothesis's own proviso rather than simply refuting it.
  - *"Not every contingency goes."* The paper sharpens this against the
    CASE. Its second mechanism is that artificial feeding and ventilation
    keep bodily functions "artificially constant", so "imbalances of the
    equilibrium do not occur" and reward loses its homeostatic value. The
    visceral regulation the CASE counts as surviving paralysis is, in the
    ventilated and tube-fed CLIS patient, partly clamped from outside. That
    bears on QUESTION-tmpr5tck's candidate 2 (the body) as well.
- **For the anthology.** No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- Seven CLIS patients, heterogeneous in cause (one hypoxic, one GBS), BCI
  type and training length; several were trained only briefly ("days",
  "one session").
- The hypothesis is an analogy; the authors give no direct measure of
  perceived contingency or of goal-directed thinking in any patient.
- Ordinal coding of performance and multiple entries per patient; the
  authors "refrain from inferential statistics" across BCI types.
- The count of other groups' patients is given as 27 in the Results and
  Table 5 and as 23 in the Discussion.
- Visual feedback BCIs were used with patients whose vision was not
  assessed.

## Open questions

- Does a contingency learned in LIS survive the transition into CLIS?
  The paper predicts yes; Chaudhary et al. is the closest test.
- Is the CLIS failure extinction, arousal (sleep-like EEG slowing),
  cognitive decline or a failure of the feedback channel? The paper cannot
  separate them.
- For the record: is judgement about heard material, which needs no
  response contingency, preserved in CLIS? Only a paradigm whose readout
  does not depend on operant learning could say.
