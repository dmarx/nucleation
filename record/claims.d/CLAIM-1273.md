# CLAIM-1273: Credit should attach to the work that was done, at the level where the contribution occurred, so that a claim, method, critique or revision held as an identifiable object carries its own provenance and a citation can name the inference and the version it relied on rather than the paper that contained it

## The claim

A5: "If a claim, method, dataset, critique, or revision is an identifiable
scholarly object, it can carry its own provenance." Someone who finds an
error "can be credited for a correction, rather than appearing only as a
footnote in somebody else's intellectual history". A5 cites the CRediT
taxonomy and the FORCE11 software citation principles as partial steps;
the record holds neither.

A8 keeps it from becoming a metric: "We don't need to pretend there's a
simple scalar function that converts every intellectual contribution into
an objective measure of scientific importance", and "What we need is
provenance."

## Where the record meets it

- [THEORY-1758](../theory.d/THEORY-1758.md): Clark, Ciccarese and Goble represent a publication as a
  principal claim with its attribution, and challenges as attributed
  micropublications of their own.
- [CLAIM-668](CLAIM-668.md): a shared record can represent who contributed a proposition
  and what standing it gives it, not what anyone believes. This record
  does attach credit at the level of the entry: each is filed in the
  voice of the owner, another assistant or this one, quoted from the turn.
- [QUESTION-072](../questions.d/QUESTION-072.md) asks whether that distributes credibility unevenly: what
  gets a code gets cited, and what does not is re-derived or lost. Credit
  at the level of the contribution reaches only contributions that are
  filed.
- The second half, citing "the version they used", is not met by a code
  in a luria record. See [CLAIM-1290](CLAIM-1290.md).

## What it does not say

Not that the byline goes, and not how credit is weighed.
