# CLAIM-024: Nothing here is new: planning theory, control theory, executive control and institutional theory already study every mechanism the account names

## The claim

"Planning theory already studies temporally extended intentions. Control
theory studies regulation. Cognitive science studies executive control and
metacontrol. Institutional theory studies authority, procedure, and
exception."

## Why it is still open

The paper's own standard: "If no such explanatory gain exists, the concept
should not be retained merely as a new label." The comparison with
control/metacontrol literature is on the submission worklist and not done.

## Note of 2026-10-27: a partial reply

The status review of this date ([ADR-057](../decisions.d/ADR-057.md)) recorded [CLAIM-159](CLAIM-159.md) as objecting
to this claim. [CLAIM-159](CLAIM-159.md) places will-organization beside the expected value of
control and Ainslie's interests as a different claim: about what governs how
the drive profile changes, where they are about who drives. The comparison
with planning theory and with institutional theory is still not made.

## Note of 2026-10-28: retargeted

The status review of this date found that this objection does not dispute
[CLAIM-027](CLAIM-027.md)'s title, which says what decides efficacy and makes no claim to
novelty. It disputes the gain the paper stakes itself on, which was
carried by [CLAIM-027](CLAIM-027.md)'s standing without being one of its words. That gain
is now [CLAIM-tmpnvpfe](CLAIM-tmpnvpfe.md), and this objection is to it. Its first half, that
the mechanisms are not new, the paper grants ([CLAIM-021](CLAIM-021.md)), which is why
[CLAIM-021](CLAIM-021.md) no longer files as an objection to this one.
