# THEORY-1468: On Kadowaki and colleagues, restoring the pressure-free conditions of a degraded ecosystem can fail because the degraded phase leaves a reinforcing legacy of its own, such as hardwoods that stop fire spreading, sludge that favours a floating competitor, or eroded beaver sites, so recovery needs direct biotic manipulation, by removal or by reintroduction in the right order, besides abiotic restoration

## Source

Kadowaki et al. (2018), [LIT-2165](../literature.d/LIT-2165.md); read in [NOTE-1677](../notes.d/NOTE-1677.md). A stand-in
for Suding, Gross & Houseman (2004), [LIT-2169](../literature.d/LIT-2169.md), which it cites for the
framing and which was not read.

## What was actually shown

- **Wetlands** (p. 992, from Martin & Kirkman 2009): "re-introduction of
  fire alone does not restore previous herbaceous communities because
  hardwood species impede the spread of fire, which further facilitates
  hardwood dominance"; restoration adds hardwood removal.
- **Japanese lakes** (p. 994): after water quality improved, "the
  submerged macrophytes have been outcompeted by water chestnut (genus
  Trapa)", on sludge from the eutrophic phase; local sludge removal
  "succeeded in reducing the floating-leaved plants and recovering the
  submerged plants". No numbers.
- **Yellowstone** (p. 995, from Wolf et al. 2007): "Although wolves were
  experimentally reintroduced in 1995, the willows did not initially
  recover", because beaver loss had eroded the sites willows establish on.
- **The model** (p. 994): hysteresis in total abundance under rising
  mortality, and restoration that fails or succeeds by which species is
  reintroduced first (Fig. 2e, f). The authors' own words: "The model
  itself is not novel".

Each case could have come out the other way, with restored conditions
alone bringing recovery; the paper reports that they did not, on the
cited studies' authority.

## What this does not say

- That any of these systems has alternative stable states. No case is
  tested for hysteresis, initial-state dependence or a lasting shift with
  slow recovery excluded, which [THEORY-136](THEORY-136.md) requires; "irreversible" is the
  cited authors' word.
- That the biotic manipulation can then stop. The wetland is "maintained
  with prescribed fires"; no case is followed after the intervention ends.
- How often restoration by conditions fails. Three cases chosen to
  illustrate say nothing of the rate; Schröder, Persson & De Roos (2005)
  found that removing a pressure left the system unrecovered in 3 of 15
  tests (5 recovered; 7 were not conclusive), [LIT-2167](../literature.d/LIT-2167.md).
- Anything Suding, Gross & Houseman (2004) showed.
