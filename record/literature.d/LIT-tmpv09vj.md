# LIT-tmpv09vj: Observation of branched flow of light

Patsyk, A., Sivan, U., Segev, M. & Bandres, M. A. (2020). Observation of branched flow of light. *Nature* 583(7814): 60–65. doi:10.1038/s41586-020-2376-8.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpg83d3; the account is THEORY-tmp4wies.*

- **The mechanism** (p. 60): "When waves propagate through a weak
  disordered potential with correlation length larger than the wavelength,
  they form channels (branches) of enhanced intensity that keep dividing as
  the waves propagate." The branches come from caustics.
- **The platform** (p. 61): "smooth thickness variations in the film act
  as a correlated disordered potential" for the guided light.
- **The scaling, tested, not derived** (pp. 61–62, Fig. 3): the
  scintillation index "grows sharply at a distance proportional to"
  lc v0^−2/3, the law the paper takes from earlier theory.
- **Statistics and width** (Fig. 4): branch intensities have a heavy tail
  against the Rayleigh fit, and "The branched channel maintains the same
  width for at least ten diffraction lengths (Rayleigh lengths)".

## Standing in the record

Filed with the optics and holography batch of 2026-11-15 from A's turn
proposing that wave mathematics helps explain learning. A's description of
the phenomenon holds, almost verbatim from the abstract, but omits its
defining condition (correlation length longer than the wavelength). A
credits the scintillation index and the first-branch scaling to "the 2026
paper"; both are already here, in the isotropic form, and Chang et al.
(2026), LIT-tmpn3i8n, cite this paper for them. Its own author list is four
names, not four and "et al.".

The paper is wave transport in a linear medium. It says nothing of memory,
storage, capacity or computation, so it gives no evidence for any claim
of the record's about learning; a link from branched flow to how a network
routes information would be the record's own analogy, to be filed as such
if at all.
