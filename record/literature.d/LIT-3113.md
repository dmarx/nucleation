# LIT-3113: Where there is a way, is there a will? The effect of future choices on self-control

Uzma Khan and Ravi Dhar (2007). *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* 136(2): 277-288. DOI-10.1037/0096-3445.136.2.277

## Key takeaways

- **Awareness of a recurring choice lowers self-control now.** The share
  choosing the vice rises when people know the same choice comes again
  (Experiments 1-4), and so does the share taking smaller sooner money
  (Experiment 5, high-self-control participants only).
- **Optimism, not consistency.** People predict a more virtuous next
  choice than they make (67% against 36%), and anticipated guilt
  mediates the effect (Experiment 3).
- **Perceived precedent reverses most of it.** In a study reported in the
  General Discussion, telling people that their present choice is the best
  indicator of how they will choose next week lowered the vice share from
  90% to 53%; the isolated choice gave 46.6%.
