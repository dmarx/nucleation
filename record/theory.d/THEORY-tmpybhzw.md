# THEORY-tmpybhzw: Gaboriaud, Gautheron, Quinton and Smeding propose that intent, outcome and causality feed both wrongness and blame judgments with different weights, and find in one preregistered experiment that all three affect both judgments, intent most, while only causality weighs measurably more in blame

Gaboriaud et al. (2024), [LIT-tmp5poa8](../literature.d/LIT-tmp5poa8.md).

## The result

145 psychology undergraduates (144 analysed) judged 64 scenarios for
wrongness or blame, learning intent first and the outcome second. Main
effects: intent d = 1.68, outcome d = 0.53, causality d = 0.61. Causality
counted more for blame (b = 0.98) than for wrongness (b = 0.82), d = 0.05.
The predicted intent and outcome differences between the judgment types
were not found.

## What it does not show

- **A robust wrongness/blame distinction.** The authors say it "must not
  be overestimated".
- **How observers weigh a third party who structured the agent's choice.**
  Causality is the agent's against "another independent source".
- **Generality.** One French student sample, mostly women, care/harm
  scenarios.
