# CLAIM-329: A collective that replaces all its members persists, when it does, by causal transmission of the organization that produces it, not by resemblance: an organization of the same kind that arises independently is not its continuation, and a lapse in its coherence need not end it

## The claim

A9 §9 separated three persistences:

- **State persistence.** A characteristic variable changes slowly,
  Z_{t+Δ} ≈ Z_t.
- **Dynamical persistence.** "The organization continues generating
  characteristic responses even as its internal states change", so
  P(Y_{t+h} | Z_t) stays stable for a stated range of horizons.
- **Historical persistence.** "The processes responsible for producing
  the organization are themselves causally transmitted and reconstituted
  across successive populations of constituents."

"A reputation may persist even though the processes generating the actual
behavior have changed. A program may maintain its characteristic
capabilities while its present reputation changes. A fandom may preserve
its historical lineage even while losing its characteristic norms."

This claim is that the third is what persistence through total turnover
consists in. Two consequences mark it off from its neighbours:

- **Against resemblance.** A5: "Two unrelated universities might
  independently develop almost identical sporting programs. Whatever
  distinguishes those cases must involve a history of causal
  transmission, not just similarity of structure or output." Reproducing
  a kind is not persisting as an individual.
- **Against conservation alone.** A5 §2: a program that loses its whole
  roster and staff, falls apart and rebuilds its capacity over several
  seasons may fail [CLAIM-161](CLAIM-161.md)'s conserved-coherence test at the trough and
  still be the same program, because what persisted was "a capacity to
  restore or regenerate a characteristic organization". [CLAIM-161](CLAIM-161.md) is
  about persisting as the same self and says nothing against this; the
  two are tests of different persistences.

[CLAIM-280](CLAIM-280.md) separated a persistent product from a persistent producer.
This claim says what the producer's own persistence is when its members
are all replaced. [CLAIM-280](CLAIM-280.md) recorded A10 §4.3's "causal-organizational
continuity" of 2026-10-13 as "a candidate of the same shape" and did not
file it. This is its second appearance, from a different case.

A6 §3 found the organizational-studies form: Feldman and Pentland's
ostensive and performative routines ([LIT-1124](../literature.d/LIT-1124.md), unread), where
enactments reproduce and modify a pattern that organizes later
enactments. Whether that literature already separates "the continuity of
a *particular organization* from reproduction of an *organizational
kind*" was A6's first question for it.

## What it does not say

- **What must be transmitted.** That is [QUESTION-118](../questions.d/QUESTION-118.md). Without an answer
  the claim risks A6's warning that it "risks making any causal descendant
  a continuation of the original entity".
- **How to decide between several descendants.** [CASE-105](../cases.d/CASE-105.md) has three,
  each continuous with the original in a different respect.
- **That transmission suffices for being a self, an agent or one
  system.** It is a persistence condition only, as [CLAIM-161](CLAIM-161.md) is.

## Note of 2026-10-21: the persistences re-derived, and reconstitution

A10 of the exchange of 2026-10-20 (§4, §10) re-derived this claim's three
persistences as "structural", "generative", "viability", "interventional"
and "historical" continuity, without citing it. A13 and A14 then proposed
a TERM, "organizational reconstitution", for processes that "restore,
reproduce, or transform" an organization's relations. In this record
reconstitution is what this claim says is not continuation: "an
organization of the same kind that arises independently" ([QUESTION-118](../questions.d/QUESTION-118.md),
[THEORY-418](../theory.d/THEORY-418.md), [CASE-105](../cases.d/CASE-105.md)). The proposed TERM would merge the two. The
persistences are filed instead as a disambiguation TERM under another
name, [TERM-071](../terms.d/TERM-071.md).

## Note of 2026-10-22 (later): transmitted organization, untransmitted perspective

The exchange "Graph Learning Applications" brought two kinds of case
against this claim's criterion, causal transmission of the organization
that produces the collective.

- **Party realignment** ([CASE-133](../cases.d/CASE-133.md)). The parties' organizations were
  transmitted continuously, names, institutions and all, while their
  coalitions and stances on civil rights changed. By this claim each party
  persisted. Organizational continuity did not guarantee continuity of the
  party's operative perspective: some orientations and constituencies
  persisted, others migrated or transformed, and the parties' internal
  perspectives were heterogeneous throughout. (Narrowed the same day after
  a review the owner relayed; the first version said flatly that the
  perspective did not persist.) So the claim's criterion is one for a
  carrier's persistence, and a perspective carried needs its own ([CLAIM-417](CLAIM-417.md)). A29: "Different political
  coalitions developed, migrated, split, and recombined through
  institutions that retained their historical names."
- **Descent through opposition** ([CLAIM-420](CLAIM-420.md)). A movement founded to
  oppose a figure exists because of that figure's activity, a causal
  connection, without the figure's organization being transmitted to it.
  A38: "Causal descent ⇏ Perspectival inheritance." That fits this claim,
  which asks for transmission of the producing organization and not mere
  causal connection, and it gives [QUESTION-118](../questions.d/QUESTION-118.md) a case where something is
  causally continuous and the answer is no.

Taylor's women's movement in abeyance ([LIT-1381](../literature.d/LIT-1381.md), unread) is the
dormant form, close to [CLAIM-287](CLAIM-287.md)'s reinstatable capacity.

## Note of 2026-10-26: a case in which every successor is causally descended

<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-263 — Proposed; the quotient protocol, cited as open, nothing here rests on it -->

T24 of the exchange "Experimental Infrastructure Review" cited this claim
for "distinct forms of organizational persistence" of features across a
transformer's training checkpoints ([QUESTION-275](../questions.d/QUESTION-275.md)). It split
"Representational continuity: The same distinction remains recoverable"
from "Computational continuity: A causally effective computation continues
to operate, perhaps through different physical components", and said
"These can come apart". Those are [TERM-071](../terms.d/TERM-071.md)'s state and dynamical senses.
The historical sense, which is this claim's thesis, is missing: this claim
is not a list of persistences but the thesis that persistence through
turnover is by causal transmission, not resemblance.

The reply's §6 then asks this claim's question without citing it: "Can we
distinguish a continuing computation from a newly learned replacement with
similar output behavior?" Training sharpens the question in one way. Every
checkpoint is causally descended from the last through the update rule,
so causal connection is total and cannot by itself separate continuation
from replacement. The verdict falls to [QUESTION-118](../questions.d/QUESTION-118.md), on what must be
transmitted. [CLAIM-420](CLAIM-420.md), descent through opposition, is the record's other
case of causal connection without transmission. Implementation turnover,
"perhaps through different physical components", is this claim's member
replacement, and matching features across checkpoints needs [CLAIM-263](CLAIM-263.md)'s
quotient by the architecture's full symmetry group before resemblance can
count for anything.

The training-dynamics works T24 drew on, read the same day, judge
persistence by resemblance throughout, so the cited prior art does not
answer §6's question either.

- Cross-checkpoint crosscoders ([NOTE-1186](../notes.d/NOTE-1186.md)) call a feature "maintained"
  when one index of a jointly trained dictionary has weight at both
  checkpoints: continuity by a shared index, which is resemblance.
- The refined-LLC study ([NOTE-1188](../notes.d/NOTE-1188.md), [THEORY-967](../theory.d/THEORY-967.md)) has an n-gram
  "move" from one head to another when the same tokens are affected at two
  checkpoints.
- The fine-tuning circuit study ([NOTE-1183](../notes.d/NOTE-1183.md), [THEORY-997](../theory.d/THEORY-997.md)) judges a
  circuit the same before and after by the overlap of its nodes and edges.

The induction-heads paper ([NOTE-1173](../notes.d/NOTE-1173.md)) draws [TERM-071](../terms.d/TERM-071.md)'s line itself: a
constant in-context learning score "doesn't necessarily mean that the
underlying mechanisms of in-context learning are constant". The claim is
unchanged.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the organizational literature A6 hoped for, read

Feldman and Pentland, [LIT-1124](../literature.d/LIT-1124.md), which "The claim" cites as unread, is now
read ([NOTE-1386](../notes.d/NOTE-1386.md)), so that no longer holds. A6's first question for it
was whether it separates the continuity of a particular organization from
the reproduction of an organizational kind. It does not. Its analysis is
of one routine in one organization, and its persistence story is
maintenance: an ostensive aspect survives only while it is performed, and
fades when it is not. That is a form of this claim's historical
persistence, carried by the chain of performances and not by similarity
to a type. Its one move toward the question is negative: it doubts that
routines can serve as unchanging genealogical carriers between
organizations (p. 114), and leaves the matter as empirical.

Two more works read on this date give the claim vocabulary, not its
criterion.

- **Marquis and Tilcsik**, [LIT-1144](../literature.d/LIT-1144.md) ([NOTE-1373](../notes.d/NOTE-1373.md)). Secondhand
  imprinting is causal transmission of a characteristic by emulation; a
  cohort effect is shared resemblance from shared conditions; and the
  review insists the two differ (pp. 206–207, 228–229). It gives a case of
  a feature persisting across total turnover in one position: Burton and
  Beckman's position imprint constrains the third and fourth successors.
  But every case it discusses fixes the bearer's identity by its legal or
  institutional existence and asks only which features persist. It
  supports the half of this claim that transmission and resemblance
  differ, and says nothing on which transmission makes the same
  collective.
- **Gioia, Schultz and Corley**, [LIT-1135](../literature.d/LIT-1135.md) ([NOTE-1389](../notes.d/NOTE-1389.md)). Their
  continuity of labels with changing meanings challenges any persistence
  criterion that requires identity content to stay fixed, as A6 expected.
  It does not supply causal transmission: their continuity is members'
  interpretive work and outsiders' demand for recognizable partners, and
  their examples, the Marlins' gutted roster among them, are posed as
  puzzles, not answered.

So the distinction remains the record's own. The claim stays Proposed.

## Note of 2026-10-30: what holds it back

Kept Proposed by the eighteenth status review. Without an answer to
QUESTION-118, on what must be transmitted, the claim cannot tell
continuation from causal descent where every candidate is causally
continuous, as in training (note of 2026-10-26) and CASE-105's three
successors. CASE-105's 2005 Nationals, continuity by name and place with
no causal path from the original club, is the test its defeat condition
names, and nothing in the record says whether the league, the club or its
supporters count it as continuation. The note of 2026-10-22 limited the
criterion to a carrier's persistence, and the title does not say so.
