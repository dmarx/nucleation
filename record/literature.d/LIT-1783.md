summary: >-
  A conceptual paper. It takes Cialdini et al.'s claim that empathy-induced helping is due to self-other merging and asks what "merging" could coherently mean. May argued that every reading either attributes absurd beliefs or fails to explain helping. The authors answer that merging is group identification: in empathizing with Y, X comes to conceive of Y as part of X's social self, so Y's welfare becomes constitutive of "our" welfare. They argue that this reading is behaviourally adequate, because it explains Batson's results in the same way as minimal-group favouritism, and psychologically plausible, because it posits a non-doxastic self-representation rather than a belief in being two bodies. Helping so motivated is then altruistic at the individual level and egoistic at the group level. The empirical dispute with Batson et al. (1997) is set aside.
