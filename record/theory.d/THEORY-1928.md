# THEORY-1928: Elish argues that when control over an automated system is distributed across people and machines, responsibility for its failures can be misattributed to the nearest human operator, who had limited control, so that the operator absorbs moral and legal blame while the technological system is kept faultless, and she reads the reception of Three Mile Island and Air France 447 as instances

## Source

Elish (2019), [LIT-2665](../literature.d/LIT-2665.md), read in full from the open-access version of record; the reading is
[NOTE-2019](../notes.d/NOTE-2019.md).

## What was actually shown

A concept and its illustration, not a measured pattern. The human "may become simply a
component—accidentally or intentionally—that bears the brunt of the moral and legal
responsibilities when the overall system malfunctions", and "the moral crumple zone protects the
integrity of the technological system, at the expense of the nearest human operator" (p. 40). At
Three Mile Island the operators really did err ("Without a doubt, actions taken by the plant
operators led to the accident", p. 44), yet "Even as their actions were constrained, their
culpability was focused on and singled out" (p. 46). On Air France 447, "While control has been
effectively distributed, responsibility has not scaled accordingly" (p. 49), and the certification
regime leaves the human pilot as "the only possible malfunction, systemically" (p. 50). The
evidence is headlines, official report wording and commentary.

Corroborates [CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md) in its after-failure half only: blame placed where the failure is
visible, the system kept faultless.

## What this does not say

- Not that every automated system produces one: "moral crumple zones are not a property of every
  complex and automated system" (p. 50).
- Not why the gaps occur: "The concept of a moral crumple zone is not a way to explain why these
  gaps occur" (p. 50).
- Nothing about credit for outcomes produced by human compensation, nor about the upstream
  allocation of obligations; those parts of [CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md) are not Elish's.
- Not legal liability: the paper is about "cultural perceptions of blame and responsibility,
  particularly in an American context" (p. 52).
