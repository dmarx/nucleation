## Summary

The paper proposes the simplest local nonlinear Langevin equation for a
coarse-grained growing interface h(x, t), Eq. (1):

∂h/∂t = ν∇²h + (λ/2)(∇h)² + η(x, t),

with Gaussian noise ⟨ηη⟩ = 2Dδ^d(x − x′)δ(t − t′). The (∇h)² term comes
from growth along the local normal (the Eden argument, p. 890). Two
transformations follow. W = exp[(λ/2ν)h] gives a diffusion equation in a
random potential, which is the partition function of a directed polymer,
Eq. (2). v = −∇h gives Burgers's equation with noise, Eq. (3).

Without noise, Eq. (4) solves the equation exactly through the Cole–Hopf
form. A rough initial interface relaxes into paraboloid segments joined
at kinks, the counterpart of Burgers shocks, whose size grows as t^(2/3)
in d = 1 (Fig. 1).

With noise, a one-loop dynamic RG (Eqs. 5a, 5b) gives the flow of the
effective coupling λ̄² = λ²D/ν³. The exponents are defined by
w(L, t) = L^χ w₀(t/L^z). The outcome depends on dimension:

- **d = 1.** λ̄ is relevant. A fluctuation-dissipation theorem makes
  χ = ½ and z = 3/2 exact.
- **d = 2.** λ̄ is marginally relevant and flows to strong coupling, out
  of reach of perturbation theory. Directed-polymer numerics suggest
  z ≈ 3/2 and χ ≈ ½ "may be regained".
- **d ≥ 3.** λ̄ is irrelevant when it is small, so the interface is
  asymptotically smooth with z = 2. A transition at strong coupling is
  possible.

The paper sorts growth into three classes: random deposition
(λ = ν = 0), the "ideal" Edwards–Wilkinson interface (λ = 0), and a new
class for λ ≠ 0.

## Contribution

- The KPZ equation itself, argued from symmetry and locality as the
  lowest-order nonlinearity: "the lowest-order nonlinear term that can
  appear in the interface growth equation" (p. 889). It is the first
  universality-class account of the dynamics of a growing interface.
- Its two mappings, to Burgers and to the directed polymer, and the use
  of the zero-temperature polymer as a fast numerical probe of the
  strong-coupling regime.
- Exact 1D exponents, which explain why earlier simulations had seen the
  "ideal" static exponent ½.

## Key results

- **R1, Eq. (1), p. 889.** "Higher-order terms can also be present, but
  they are irrelevant, and will not modify the universal scaling
  properties." The form of the noise distribution "is irrelevant".
- **R2, symmetries, p. 889.** "Note that Eq. (1) is invariant under
  translations h → h + const, and obeys the infinitesimal
  reparametrization h → h + ε·x, x → x + λεt, which describes the
  tilting of the interface by a small angle." On the mean growth
  velocity: "In principle there is also a velocity term, which is
  removed by choice of an appropriate moving coordinate system."
- **R3, deterministic growth, p. 890.** "the average size of these
  paraboloids grows in time as t^(2/3) in one dimension". Burgers shocks
  correspond to kinks in the profile.
- **R4, RG, p. 891, Eq. (5a).** dν/dl = [z − 2 + K_d λ̄²(2 − d)/4d]ν;
  dD/dl = [z − d − 2χ + K_d λ̄²/4]D; dλ/dl = [χ + z − 2]λ. "(The diagrams
  contributing to dλ/dl cancel at this order.)" Eq. (5b):
  dλ̄/dl = ((2 − d)/2)λ̄ + K_d((2d − 3)/4d)λ̄³. The paper notes this flow
  is "identical to the one for Burgers's equation" (Forster, Nelson and
  Stephen).
- **R5, d = 1, p. 891.** "The expansion results z = 3/2 and χ = ½ are
  actually exact. This is a consequence of a fluctuation-dissipation
  theorem that holds in d = 1 only". Simulation checks: Eden,
  z = 1.55 ± 0.15 (Plischke and Rácz); ballistic deposition, width
  ∝ t^(0.30 ± 0.02) against "our prediction of ν′ = χ/z = 1/3", and
  χ = 0.42 ± 0.02 (Family and Vicsek).
- **R6, the static exponent misleads, p. 891.** "Another consequence of
  this theorem is that χ … takes the same value as for an 'ideal'
  interface." Of the strip simulations giving χ = ½: "This was
  interpreted as indicating an 'ideal' interface, but as discussed above,
  it is a result of a peculiarity of one-dimensional interfaces."
- **R7, d = 2, pp. 891–892.** "λ is marginally relevant, and grows under
  rescaling. The fixed point … is not accessible by perturbation theory."
  Polymer numerics give 1/z = 0.62 ± 0.04 and χ/z = 0.33 ± 0.03:
  "Although the results are not quite conclusive, it appears as though
  z ∼ 3/2 and χ ∼ ½ may be regained, in which case these exponents will
  be superuniversal (independent of dimension)."
- **R8, d ≥ 3, p. 892.** "the coupling λ is irrelevant, and an
  asymptotically ideal smooth surface is expected with z = 2. In
  principle, Eq. (5b) allows for the possibility of a phase transition to
  different scaling for strong enough couplings. This is probably not
  relevant to the growth problem".
- **R9, conclusion, p. 892.** "Eq. (1) embodies three different
  universality classes … With λ = 0, it corresponds to an ideal interface
  with χ = (2 − d)/2 and z = 2. For λ ≠ 0 we obtained a new universality
  class with χ = ½ and z = ⅔ (possibly independent of d)."
  **Misprint:** the page image shows z = ⅔, against z = 3/2 everywhere
  else in the paper (abstract body p. 889, p. 891).
- **R10, note 14(b), p. 892.** "as the results obtained in this paper are
  independent of the sign of λ, they should also describe a corroding
  interface (an 'acid rain model')."

**Arithmetic rechecked.** In d = 1, K₁ = S₁/2π = 1/π. Setting
dν/dl = dD/dl = 0 gives 2χ − 1 = 0, so χ = ½. The fixed point of (5b) is
λ̄*² = 2/K₁ = 2π, and then z = 2 − K₁λ̄*²/4 = 3/2. This matches
χ + z = 2, the condition for dλ/dl = 0 with λ ≠ 0. And ν′ = χ/z = 1/3.

## Assumptions

- The interface is single-valued, with no overhangs, and coarse-grained.
- Growth is local and short-ranged, and the noise is uncorrelated in
  space and time.
- The interface relaxes by surface tension: ν > 0. The paper's note 14(a)
  says Eden and ballistic models have no relaxation (ν = 0), "a rather
  singular limit", though comparison with simulation "suggests that this
  is not a serious problem (at least in d = 1)".
- The RG is one-loop. Its exactness is claimed only for d = 1.

## Concepts

- KPZ equation; lateral growth nonlinearity (∇h)².
- Edwards–Wilkinson ("ideal") interface; random deposition.
- Dynamic scaling w(L, t) = L^χ w₀(t/L^z): the roughness exponent χ and
  the dynamic exponent z. The growth exponent χ/z = 1/3 is called ν′
  here; it is today's β.
- Cole–Hopf transformation; directed polymer in a random medium;
  Burgers equation and shocks.
- Dynamic renormalization group; marginal relevance at d = 2.
- Fluctuation-dissipation theorem in d = 1.
- Tilt (reparametrization) invariance, which later literature calls
  Galilean invariance or statistical tilt symmetry (Le Doussal, p. 4).
- Superuniversality, conjectured here.

## Verdict on the exchange's characterization

The record has three characterizations, all fair.

- **LIT-2978's summary.** "a nonlinear stochastic PDE whose scaling
  exponents define a universality class far from equilibrium": correct.
  The paper's words are "a new universality class" and "a classification
  of growth processes by their universality". It never says "far from
  equilibrium", but its PACS heading is 05.70.Ln, nonequilibrium
  thermodynamics.
- **CLAIM-841's KPZ row.** "W(L, t) = L^α F(t/L^z), α = ½, z = 3/2 in
  1D": correct. It matches w(L, t) = L^χ w₀(t/L^z) on p. 891. "z = α/β =
  (½)/(⅓)" matches the paper's ν′ = χ/z = 1/3. The paper calls the
  exponent χ, not α.
- **"generically".** Correct for d ≤ 2: any λ ≠ 0 flows away. It does
  not hold as stated for d ≥ 3, where small λ is irrelevant and the
  interface is EW-smooth (R8).
- **LEAD-209's KPZ column.** "z = 3/2 in 1D" and "W ∝ L^α" are correct.
  The rows "nonzero skewness", "nonzero currents" and "cutoff" are not
  addressed by the paper.
- **The brief's "1/3 and 3/2".** These are the paper's ν′ = χ/z = 1/3
  and z = 3/2.

## Bearing on the named codes

- **CLAIM-841 (Proposed): supports the KPZ row, qualifies "generically".**
  - *Supports.* R5 and R9: for any λ ≠ 0 in d = 1, the scaling
    w = L^χ w₀(t/L^z) holds with exact exponents, and no parameter is set
    to a special value. The only quantity removed is the mean velocity,
    taken out by a change of frame (R2), not tuned.
  - *Qualifies.* R8: in d ≥ 3 the KPZ class is reached only past a
    strong-coupling threshold. The paper raises "the possibility of a
    phase transition", and Le Doussal (p. 4) confirms "an intermediate
    fixed point, at g = g* ≃ d − 2, which separates a weak noise phase
    where the nonlinearity is irrelevant, and the strong noise phase".
    That transition is a tuned point. Even so, both phases are scale
    invariant: EW has z = 2, χ = (2 − d)/2. So the claim's thesis stands,
    but the row should read "in d ≤ 2".
  - *Supports the bullet "A histogram cannot name its mechanism"* (A24
    §1). R6 is the paper's own instance: in d = 1 the stationary
    interface has the "ideal" (EW) static exponent, so the earlier strip
    simulations read it as ideal. The dynamics (z = 3/2 against 2) tell
    the classes apart. This also bears on CLAIM-803, which the first
    search missed.
- **QUESTION-508 (Open): qualifies, a partial answer for one case.**
  The paper does not ask the question. It does state, on p. 889, the two
  symmetries that matter: invariance under h → h + const, and the tilt
  invariance (R2).
  - *My inference, not the paper's words:* translation invariance
    forbids a restoring term ∝ h, which is A27's "symmetry removes the
    restoring term" route applied to an interface.
  - *Also inference:* the vanishing of the λ renormalization at one loop
    (R4), later read as a consequence of the tilt symmetry (Le Doussal
    p. 4, "Galilean invariance (i.e. the STS symmetry) ensures the
    relation χ = 2 − z"), is what fixes the exponents.
  - *What the paper itself says:* whether λ is relevant (d < 2) or
    irrelevant (d > 2) decides which scale-free class is reached, and in
    d > 2 there may be a threshold (R8).

  So for interfaces the answer to QUESTION-508 is "a symmetry plus the
  relevance of the nonlinearity", and above d = 2 the threshold
  reintroduces a tuned point between two untuned phases.
- **LIT-2982 (Takeuchi and Sano), background.** The paper predicts the
  exponents that LIT-2982 tests. It has no fluctuation distributions:
  Tracy–Widom is not in it.

## Limitations

- It is a four-page letter. The one-loop RG is only sketched, the
  fluctuation-dissipation theorem is cited to Deker and Haake rather than
  shown, and the directed-polymer numerics are "preliminary".
- The d = 2 superuniversality conjecture failed. Le Doussal (p. 4): "The
  conjecture χ = 2/(d + 3) was put forward by Kim and Kosterlitz … More
  recent numerics show that it is not exact, with, in d = 2,
  χ = 0.3869(4)". By KPZ's own relation that gives z ≈ 1.61, not 3/2.
- The conclusion misprints z as ⅔ (R9).
- The Eden and ballistic models have ν = 0, a "singular limit" (note
  14a).
- Nothing on height distributions, skewness or probability currents.

## Candidate THEORY

**Sentence.** Kardar, Parisi and Zhang propose
∂h/∂t = ν∇²h + (λ/2)(∇h)² + η as the simplest local equation for a
growing interface. By a one-loop dynamic renormalization group they show
the following:

- In one dimension any nonzero λ is relevant and leads to a universality
  class distinct from random deposition and the linear Edwards–Wilkinson
  interface. Its width scales as w(L, t) = L^χ w₀(t/L^z), with χ = ½ and
  z = 3/2 exact by a fluctuation-dissipation theorem, so the width grows
  as t^(1/3).
- In two dimensions λ is marginally relevant.
- Above two dimensions a small λ is irrelevant and the surface is
  asymptotically smooth with z = 2.

**promote_when.** An independent derivation or measurement confirms the
one-dimensional exponents χ = ½ and z = 3/2 (growth exponent 1/3) for
λ ≠ 0, and their absence (z = 2) for λ = 0. Any of these would do:

- an open re-derivation of the RG or exact result, read and filed;
- the Takeuchi–Sano experiment (LIT-2982), read;
- a 1D discretization run under LEAD-209's trial.
