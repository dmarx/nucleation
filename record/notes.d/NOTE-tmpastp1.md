  Finn separates two questions in the Kingma / Smith–Brogaard debate on the
  metaphysics of pregnancy. The **Timing Question**: when does an organism
  of the relevant kind start to exist (During-Pregnancy models such as Smith
  and Brogaard's sixteen days; After-Pregnancy models such as Kingma's "Nine
  Months")? The **Relationship Question**: is the Foster a proper part of,
  or merely contained by, the Gravida? She argues the questions are
  independent unless connected by a **Maximality** principle ("Proper parts
  of an F are not themselves Fs"), which need not be held and may fail for
  pregnancy. She then maps the relationship options with mereology plus
  topology: Parthood, Containment (three topological variants by contact),
  Overlap (shared proper part, e.g. placenta) and Underlap (the Gravida is
  the sum of a niche container and the Foster, so the Foster is both part
  and merely contained by the rest). She argues Smith and Brogaard are
  circular, and that Kingma's "dilemma" for parthood is only a choice.

# NOTE-tmpastp1: Finn (2023) — The Mereotopology of Pregnancy

## Contribution

A taxonomy of positions, the independence thesis, Maximality as the explicit
bridge principle, the Underlap view as a new hybrid, and the case for adding
topology (contact, fiat vs bona fide boundaries) and dependence to the
debate.

## Key results

1. **Independence (Abstract).** "I take these questions to be independent of
   each other, such that an answer to one does not provide an answer to the
   other. I argue that the way to connect them is via a maximality principle".
2. **Maximality (§II).** "Maximality: Proper parts of an F are not themselves
   Fs."
3. **Counting does not settle relation (§II, Timing and Counting).** "without
   some explicit endorsement (and justification) of a Maximality condition,
   one cannot learn about the number of human beings from the way that the
   entities are mereologically related to each other".
4. **Kingma's dilemma is a choice (§III, Parthood View).** (A) Foster is an
   organism of the relevant kind, (B) Foster is a proper part of the Gravida,
   (C) Maximality: "only two can be consistently held"; "the dilemma is not
   much of a dilemma—it is more like a choice between two plausible options."
5. **Circularity charge (§V).** Smith and Brogaard "seem to use maximality to
   argue both from human-hood at sixteen days ... to non-part-hood ... and
   from non-part-hood to human-hood at sixteen days."
6. **Underlap (§III).** "This makes the Underlap view a hybrid whereby there
   is both mere-containment and proper parthood."
7. **Placenta as overlap (fn. 27).** "The placenta (and perhaps umbilical
   cord) could be considered either as a shared part of the Foster and
   Gravida or as a part of the Foster whilst being outside the external
   boundary of the Gravida".
8. **Dependence as a third coordinate (§V).** "dependence, which provides a
   third coordinate for investigating the different models of pregnancy".
9. **Open direction (Conclusion).** "it is unclear whether Maximality
   constrains what we say about pregnancy, or whether pregnancy constrains
   what we say about Maximality."
10. **Ethics (§V).** The models "alone do not determine ... the rights that a
    human Gravida has over her body ... nor the moral status of the Foster".

## Assumptions

Parthood is classical mereological proper parthood (biological parthood is
flagged as possibly different, §V); containment is within an external bona
fide boundary (after Kaiser; Smith and Varzi); "relevant kind" talk is usable
though "problematic" (fn. 4); answers may vary across trimesters but this is
not explored; the paper deliberately does not adjudicate.

## Concepts

Gravida / Foster; Timing, Counting and Relationship Questions; Maximality;
part-containment vs mere-containment; fiat vs bona fide boundaries; niche,
tenant, niche container; Parthood, Containment, Overlap, Improper-overlap,
Underlap views; During- vs After-Pregnancy models; mereotopology; applied
metaphysics.

## Bearing on the record

**On the LIT's and the exchange's characterization.** A13's account ("distinguishes when a new organism begins from what relation
of parthood, containment or overlap it has to the gestating organism") is
**accurate**. It omits the paper's two main theses: that Maximality is the
only bridge between the questions, and the Underlap view.

- **[THEORY-448](../theory.d/THEORY-448.md)** (Meincke: under substance ontology, Substances-as-Parts has
  "no consistent count"; the process view keeps parthood and identity at the
  price of a count between one and two): **qualifies**. Finn's analysis
  implies that Meincke's inconsistency charge holds only given a Maximality
  premise (an organism-substance cannot be a proper part of an organism of
  the same kind), which Finn argues needs independent justification and may
  be refuted by pregnancy itself. Rejecting Maximality, Substances-as-Parts
  counts two organisms one of which is part of the other, consistently. Finn
  also adds options Meincke's dichotomy does not consider (Overlap, Underlap).
  This is the record's inference from Finn's text: Finn does not discuss
  Meincke.
- **[QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md)** (when is a combined form one system, where is its
  boundary): **bears, leaves open**. Finn's independence thesis applies:
  counting systems and fixing their part–whole relation are separate
  questions, linked only by a Maximality-type principle; and her topological
  distinctions (contact, fiat vs bona fide boundary, shared parts) give a
  vocabulary for "where its boundary lies". She supplies no criterion.
