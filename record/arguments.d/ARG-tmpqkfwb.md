# ARG-tmpqkfwb: The persistence of race-based power in the United States after abolition, as evidence, supports that norms can outlast their original justification and end discontinuously, and does not show that the reliance loop of [CLAIM-tmpjg5gx](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjg5gx.md) is what maintained it

## The argument

From [CASE-tmp3euq0](../cases.d/CASE-tmp3euq0.md): the justification the owner names went with abolition
in 1865; the norm was upheld by precedent in 1896 and ended in law in
1954–1965 by overturned precedent and statute. So a norm can outlast its
original justification, and this one ended by discontinuous intervention
([CLAIM-tmp8l5s9](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp8l5s9.md)).

## Critical questions

- **Is the evidence what it is said to be?** The dates and holdings are the
  standard ones. They are not checked here, and the case says so.
- **Was it the same norm?** On Woodward's reading, segregation was largely
  built after Reconstruction. If so, the norm of race-based power persisted
  while its form was rebuilt. That still supports persistence after the
  original justification, but less directly.
- **Does the evidence separate the rival explanations?** Not for the
  mechanism. The norm was also kept up by violence, disfranchisement and the
  interests it served. [CLAIM-tmpjg5gx](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjg5gx.md)'s defeater asks for norms that differ
  in how much has been built on them and are otherwise alike. One national
  case cannot supply that comparison; studies across counties or states
  might ([LEAD-tmpuuj6e](../leads.d/LEAD-tmpuuj6e.md)).

## Verdict

Supports [CLAIM-tmp8l5s9](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp8l5s9.md). Corroborates [CLAIM-tmpjg5gx](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjg5gx.md) only for the
persistence it predicts, not for its mechanism.
