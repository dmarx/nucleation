# LIT-tmp3qow8: Group Duties: Their Existence and Their Implications for Individuals

Stephanie Collins (2019), *Oxford University Press* — DOI-10.1093/oso/9780198840275.001.0001

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read.* The abstract asks "(i) whether such groups are apt
to bear duties and (ii) what this implies for their members", and
defends a "Tripartite Model":

- **Combinations**, "collections of agents that do not have any goals or
  decision-making procedures in common", cannot bear duties; each member
  has a duty to "I-reason".
- **Coalitions**, groups "whose members share goals but lack
  decision-making procedures", cannot either; members have duties to
  "we-reason".
- **Collectives** "have group-level procedures for making decisions" and
  "can bear duties". Their duties "imply duties for collectives' members
  to use their role in the collective with a view to the collective doing
  its duty."

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 at the owner's request, with the works on corporate
psychopathy. French ([THEORY-131](../theory.d/THEORY-131.md)) and Pettit ([THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md)) say a
corporation can be responsible; Velasquez says it cannot. Collins's third
category says what follows for members if it can: a member's duty is to
use their role toward the corporation doing its duty. That bears on the
record's claim that a corporation registers the costs it imposes only
through what its procedures supply: on Collins's model, members' role
duties are one such supply. Her line between collectives and coalitions
also matches the record's line between an organization with a decision
structure and a mere perspective ([CLAIM-418](../claims.d/CLAIM-418.md)).
