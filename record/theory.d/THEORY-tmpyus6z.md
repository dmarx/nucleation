# THEORY-tmpyus6z: In the physical dualities examined, theoretically inequivalent duals turn out empirically inequivalent, so dualities yield no genuine in-principle underdetermination; where duals would contradict each other a cautious realist is warranted in committing only to their common core, and because the exact cases' common cores are rich rather than empty, dualities give no support to structural realism

## Source

De Haro & Butterfield (2025), [LIT-199](../literature.d/LIT-199.md) (*The Philosophy and Physics of
Duality*, OUP), read in full in [NOTE-178](../notes.d/NOTE-178.md) from the arXiv manuscript
(2508.01616v1). Page numbers are printed ones. The Part II derivations were
read for their claims, not checked line by line.

## What was actually shown

**The framework (§3.1, §12.1.2).** A duality is an isomorphism between the
model roots (states, quantities, dynamics) of two models of a common core,
which must itself be a bare theory, a triple ⟨S, Q, D⟩. Duals are
physically equivalent iff (A) they are duals and (B) their interpretation
maps commute with the duality, i1 = i2∘d. Kramers–Wannier is the worked
case of duals that are not physically equivalent: the heat bath is an
external standard of temperature.

**Underdetermination (§13.4).** "Strictly speaking, we do not find any
genuine cases of under-determination" (p. 459). In the examples,
theoretically inequivalent duals turn out empirically inequivalent, as the
Kramers–Wannier temperatures do. For a hypothetical genuine case
(§13.2.3, pp. 444–450), each dual entails the common core (M1 ⊨ T,
M2 ⊨ T) while contradicting the other on specific structure (M1 ⊨ ¬M2), so
the cautious realist commits to T. That makes the underdetermination
"benign", not absent.

**Structural realism (§13.2.4, pp. 450–454).** The evidence "does not
vindicate a structural realist view" (p. 454). Three arguments are given
against Matsubara's and Dawid's structural readings: the hole-argument
analogy, that a referential, intensional semantics for bare theories goes
beyond what a structural realist would say, and that common cores are rich:
- bosonization's contains both bosonic and fermionic states;
- the electric–magnetic one contains a Faraday-type tensor, with "purely
  electric" a relational fact;
- T-duality's has momentum and winding as non-independent properties.

**What could have come out otherwise.** A theoretically inequivalent pair
of duals could have proved empirically equivalent, and a common core could
have been too thin to interpret. In the examples neither happened.

## What this does not say

- **That underdetermination never arises.** The verdict is over the book's
  examples. Underdetermination "as usually stated is too strong", and the
  antirealist must show that empirically equivalent rivals lack a
  compatible common core (pp. 459–460, 528).
- **That the cautious realist always has something to commit to.** The
  entailment argument needs an interpreted common core. Not every common
  core is rich enough to be a theory, and the Schema is restricted to cases
  where it is.
- **That the rich-core verdict covers the cases structural realists cite.**
  For gauge–gravity duality the common core is "only a class of asymptotic
  operators and a conformal class of ... metrics" (p. 70). Whether Dawid's
  "virtually empty" worry returns there is open.
- **That the physics is settled.** Montonen–Olive has "very good evidence"
  but no proof, and "the main string dualities remain unproven" (p. 353).
  The Schema is illustrated "if gauge-gravity duality is true".
- **That isomorphism is the only defensible equivalence criterion.** The
  authors argue against categorical and definitional equivalence and "never
  saw a convincing case against" isomorphism, but that rests on the
  stipulated principle of univocality and on theories formulated in set
  theory (p. 393).
