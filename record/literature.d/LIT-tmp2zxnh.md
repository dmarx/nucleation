# LIT-tmp2zxnh: The Effects of Exposure to Better Neighborhoods on Children: New Evidence from the Moving to Opportunity Experiment

Chetty, R., Hendren, N. & Katz, L. F. (2016). The Effects of Exposure to Better Neighborhoods on Children: New Evidence from the Moving to Opportunity Experiment. *American Economic Review* 106(4): 855–902. doi:10.1257/aer.20150572. Working paper: NBER w21156 (doi:10.3386/w21156), May 2015, revised September 2015.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpqfxp3](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpqfxp3.md). Page numbers are the working paper's.*

- **Standing support, a transient restriction.** The vouchers "could
  initially (for the first year) only be used in census tracts with 1990
  poverty rates below 10%"; then "The experimental vouchers became regular
  Section 8 vouchers after a year" (§I, pp. 6–7), held indefinitely while
  income stayed low.
- **Young children gained.** For children under 13 at random assignment
  the TOT on adult earnings is "$3,477. This is a 31% increase relative to
  the control group mean" (p. 18).
- **Exposure, not a critical age.** The gain falls about $364 per year of
  age at move and "reaches zero for children who are 13.25 years at RA";
  "There is little evidence of a “critical age”" (pp. 28–29), though the
  experiment "did not randomize the age at which children moved".
- **Adults did not.** "it is the amount of exposure to better
  neighborhoods during childhood (rather than total lifetime exposure)
  that matters" (p. 32).
- **Not family income.** The results "do not arise from improvements in
  family income" (p. 32).
- **Fade-out, then re-emergence.** Early gains "largely faded away over
  the next decade", yet adult effects "re-emerge in adulthood" (p. 40).
- **The authors' own framing.** The neighbourhood change "was an on-going
  treatment throughout childhood rather than a one-time treatment"
  (footnote 48, p. 40).

## Standing in the record

Registered with the gap-filling readings for [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md)'s clause that the
unit whose persistence counts must be the one the support was for. Here
the return is carried by people, not places: children keep it into
adulthood after they leave the supported household, while their parents,
given the same places, gain nothing. It is the mirror of Collins and
Shester ([LIT-2161](LIT-2161.md), [THEORY-1470](../theory.d/THEORY-1470.md)) and LaVoice ([LIT-2142](LIT-2142.md), [THEORY-1476](../theory.d/THEORY-1476.md)), where
places gained and people were dispersed; LaVoice's reading, [NOTE-1680](../notes.d/NOTE-1680.md),
already cites it. It is not a withdrawal test: the subsidy continued, and
what ended was childhood, so it shows persistence after the end of
exposure. The closer case of truly transient support persisting in people
is Banerjee, Duflo and Sharma ([THEORY-1493](../theory.d/THEORY-1493.md)). Filed from it:
[THEORY-tmptpq58](../theory.d/THEORY-tmptpq58.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice.
