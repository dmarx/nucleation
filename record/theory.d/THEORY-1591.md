# THEORY-1591: Wade-Benzoni, Sondak and Galinsky report that framing an intergenerational decision as leaving burdens rather than using up benefits raised legacy concern, felt responsibility and affinity toward future generations, and that people kept more of a benefit than of a burden for themselves when the other party would receive it in the future, though no study measured legacy concern and allocation together

## The account

Intergenerational decisions join a time delay to a self–other trade-off.
Where the recipient is in the future and powerless, burdens made people less
selfish than benefits did. The authors explain the reversal by legacy:
"people care more about avoiding leaving a burdensome negative legacy than
about leaving a beneficial positive legacy" (p. 13).

## Evidence

- Experiment 1 (76 undergraduates): a nine-item modified generativity scale,
  its items about being remembered and living on, was higher after a burden
  framing (4.90 against 4.10), F(1, 74) = 12.44.
- Experiment 2 (101 MBA students): legacy concern, responsibility and
  affinity for future generations, and ethical concern were all higher after
  burdens, on single items or a short scale.
- Experiments 4–6: self-interest was higher with benefits than with burdens.
  In Experiment 5 only benefits with a future recipient differed from the
  other cells; in Experiment 6 removing the self from the trade-off lowered
  present favouritism for benefits and did nothing for burdens.

Experiments 1–3 measure attitudes and 4–6 allocations, so "legacy concern
drives allocation" is inferred across studies. Experiment 4's F(1, 28) =
10.58 is printed p < .001 (it is about .003), and Experiment 5's interaction
F(1, 127) = 2.89 is printed p = .05 (about .09 two-tailed) ([NOTE-1273](../notes.d/NOTE-1273.md),
Limitations).

## What it bears on

experiments ([NOTE-1273](../notes.d/NOTE-1273.md)) never measure legacy concern and generosity in the

question asks whether legacy priming raises beneficence only through the
wish to be remembered. The reading bears on its legacy-motivation evidence
