# CLAIM-tmp6r6t6: If a communicative kind can outrun every probe, human same-kind judgements, themselves one probe family, can neither confirm nor defeat the October thesis; if it cannot, the kind does in the tests only what an observational class does, which the record's term for a kind says it is not

## The objection

The record says a kind can outrun the probes that observe it. [TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md) says
a kind is "Not an observational class" ([TERM-014](../terms.d/TERM-014.md)), and quotes A218 Ch7's core
claim for it: "A structural kind may be real while remaining
indistinguishable under an insufficient family of empirical probes."
[CLAIM-135](CLAIM-135.md): "A kind can be real and still unseparated by an insufficient
family of probes."

Yet the defeat conditions take human judges as the ground truth for kinds.
[CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md): "Human judgements of shared communicative kind across
translations, recastings and media are predicted as well by …". [CLAIM-137](CLAIM-137.md):
two realizations "still reliably judged different acts". A judge's verdict is
a response of an interpreter to a realization, so the judges are a probe
family like any other.

**The dilemma.**

- *If a kind can outrun every probe family*, it can outrun the judges.
  Judges may call two realizations of one kind different (they attend to
  surface), or two of different kinds the same (their probing is
  insufficient). Then predicting their verdicts is neither necessary nor
  sufficient for tracking kinds, and the verdicts can neither confirm nor
  defeat what [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) says about kinds, unless a further premise says the
  judges' family is sufficient. [CLAIM-142](CLAIM-142.md)'s rule of evidence forbids
  assuming that of any family.
- *If some family, the judges' or another, cannot be outrun*, then in every
  test the kind's extension is the observational class relative to that
  family ([TERM-014](../terms.d/TERM-014.md)). A realist can say that coinciding in extension is not
  being identical. But the difference then lies only in the ontological
  clause, which [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s "What it does not say" grants the programme does
  not test, and [TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md)'s distinction does no work in any test.

Either way the dilemma reaches the epistemic and fidelity clauses that
[CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md) says are tested: "So the empirical programme tests the epistemic,
transport and fidelity clauses, not the ontological one."

**Two defeat conditions misfire.**

- [CLAIM-142](CLAIM-142.md) is defeated if "a finite probe family fixed in advance separates
  every pair of realizations that judges distinguish, on held-out
  realizations". Let the family contain one probe: ask judges whether the two
  realizations are the same act. That family is finite, can be fixed in
  advance, and separates every pair the judges distinguish, held out or not.
  Nothing in the defeat condition excludes it, so the condition is met
  trivially.
- [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s third disjunct is "no relational structure beyond the observed
  response profiles is needed to explain those judgements". [CLAIM-046](CLAIM-046.md)'s rule
  is that identity and fidelity "should be defined on observable responses",
  so every model the programme fits is a model of response profiles, and a
  relational structure estimated from them predicts nothing a model of the
  same profiles cannot. [CLAIM-150](CLAIM-150.md) grants that an ontology of intrinsically
  individuated objects "can reproduce the same predictions". Read as a claim
  about prediction, then, the disjunct is always met and the thesis is always
  defeated. Read as a claim about explanation, no data decide it.

## What it does not say

- It does not say kinds are unreal, or that the realist reading is wrong.
- It is not [CLAIM-150](CLAIM-150.md)'s point that the data underdetermine the ontology. That
  point concerns the ontological clause. This one concerns the clauses the
  programme claims to test.
- It does not say [CLAIM-142](CLAIM-142.md)'s relativity of observational equivalence to
  probes is wrong. It uses it.

## What would answer it

- A statement of what, besides the judges' verdicts, fixes a kind's extension
  in the tests, with the judges placed explicitly as one probe family among
  others and their verdicts excluded from the families that [CLAIM-142](CLAIM-142.md)'s
  defeat condition quantifies over.
- [CLAIM-139](CLAIM-139.md)'s third disjunct rewritten as an observable comparison: for
  instance, a relational model against a model of the response profiles
  alone, compared on transfer to held-out realizations, media or languages.
