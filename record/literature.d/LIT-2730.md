# LIT-2730: Rational snacking: Young children’s decision-making on the marshmallow task is moderated by beliefs about environmental reliability

Celeste Kidd, Holly Palmeri and Richard N. Aslin (2013). Rational snacking: Young children’s decision-making on the marshmallow task is moderated by beliefs about environmental reliability. *Cognition* 126(1): 109–114. DOI 10.1016/j.cognition.2012.08.004.

## Key takeaways

- "Waiting is only the rational choice if you believe that a second
  marshmallow is likely to actually appear after a reasonably short
  delay" (§1.2).
- Mean waits of 722.43 s (reliable) against 181.57 s (unreliable); 9 of 14
  against 1 of 14 waited the full 15 minutes (§3).
- The authors do not claim self-control is irrelevant: the effect of
  beliefs about reliability "is at least comparable to their capacity for
  self-control" (§4).
