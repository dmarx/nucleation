# LIT-718: The Conversable, Responsible Corporation

Philip Pettit (2017), "The Conversable, Responsible Corporation", in E. W.
Orts & N. C. Smith (eds), *The Moral Responsibility of Firms*, Oxford
University Press, pp. 15–35 — DOI-10.1093/oso/9780198738534.003.0002

## Key takeaways

- **Corporations are agents.** By "evidential and executive reliability" in
  normal conditions they maintain purposes, form representations and act
  on them. The "nexus of contracts" objection is invalid: being composed of
  contracts no more stops a corporation being an agent than being composed
  of cells stops an animal (§1).
- **And conversable.** Authorised spokespersons avow the corporation's
  attitudes and make promises in its name. Members have authorised them,
  and live up to their words on pain of the benefits of incorporation.
  Avowal forgoes the "misleading-mind" excuse; a promise forgoes the
  "changed-mind" excuse too (§1).
- **With a mind of its own.** Majority voting on "p", "q" and "p&q" can be
  inconsistent (the discursive dilemma), so a conversable corporation
  cannot follow any mechanical rule. It must check and correct its
  aggregate, for example by a straw-vote procedure. That makes its voice
  "relatively autonomous" from its members' (§1). Pettit thinks Hobbes was
  wrong to deny this.
- **Fitness to be held responsible** (§2) needs three things. The first is
  a significant choice. The second is the capacity to make a normative
  judgment about the options. The third is the capacity to choose as that
  judgment requires, *because* it requires it. Holding someone responsible
  is a regulative practice addressed to a conversable agent. It does not
  need contra-causal freedom.
- **Corporations meet them** (§2). A corporation claiming it cannot judge
  right and wrong commits a performative contradiction. A corporation that
  claims it cannot police its members can give itself that capacity, so
  over time it has it.
- **Not instead of individuals** (§3). Members can be responsible too. Where
  they have excuses, holding only them responsible leaves "a loophole for
  people to incorporate for socially harmful but selfishly rewarding ends".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request, as a supplement to the essay on
organizational will. It is the nearest thing in the literature to what the
essay's §8 does with Wolf: normative competence attributed to an
organization. It also offers §7.1 a non-regressive route to corporate
endorsement. What makes an attitude the corporation's own is the authority
of its spokespersons and the procedures that correct its aggregate, not a
higher-order attitude. The account is [THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md).
