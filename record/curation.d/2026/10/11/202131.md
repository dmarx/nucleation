After [CLAIM-tmpdj0qe](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdj0qe.md) (storage is not anchoring; addressability is), the
owner raised Hebbian learning and sparse distributed memory and asked
whether the record held either. It held one reading on SDM, Bricken &
Pehlevan ([LIT-269](../../record/literature.d/LIT-269.md), [NOTE-242](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-242.md)), and no work on Hebbian learning. The
anthology held Kanerva's book and review. The owner asked for all of them
to be filed here, with a new claim.

## What entered

- **Hebb 1949, *The Organization of Behavior*** ([LIT-tmpgwyow](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpgwyow.md),
  doi:10.4324/9781410612403, the 2005 reprint; first published by Wiley in
  1949). Neither record held it. The postulate is a persistence trigger
  keyed to recurrence, graded and strengthened by use.
- **Kanerva 1988, *Sparse Distributed Memory*** ([LIT-tmpz8twi](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpz8twi.md)) and
  **Kanerva 1992, "Sparse Distributed Memory and Related Models"**
  ([LIT-tmpcc2ij](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpcc2ij.md)). Both are held in the anthology, as [ANTH-LIT-666](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-666.md) and
  [ANTH-LIT-669](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-669.md), so they are held in both records under [ADR-013](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-013.md).
  - **The anthology's question:** what SDM says about attention.
  - **This record's question:** what retrieval by content, and its
    critical distance, says about anchoring an argument.

  Each LIT here names its anthology counterpart.
- **Hopfield 1982, "Neural networks and physical systems with emergent
  collective computational abilities"** ([LIT-tmp64dfb](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp64dfb.md),
  doi:10.1073/pnas.79.8.2554). Neither record held it. An anthology topic
  could hold it, beside its Kanerva entries, so it carries the
  anthology-candidate flag ([ADR-005](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-005.md)).

Each citation was checked against Crossref, the NASA Technical Reports
Server, or the anthology's own entry. All four are Deferred and unread.

## The claim

[CLAIM-tmp7vmnc](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmp7vmnc.md) reads drift as retrieval failure outside a critical
distance. A restatement close enough to an idea retrieves it, and one that
has drifted is taken for new. A code bypasses retrieval by content. It
rests on [CLAIM-tmp4qgbo](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmp4qgbo.md) and [CLAIM-tmpdj0qe](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdj0qe.md) and is grounded on [LIT-269](../../record/literature.d/LIT-269.md)
(read), Kanerva's book and Hopfield (both unread).

## What can be read next

Two of the four have lawful open full texts. Hopfield 1982 is free in
PubMed Central (PMC346238), and Kanerva 1992 is public at NASA NTRS
(19920021480), with a PDF and a text file. Hebb's book is in copyright,
and the 1949 scans at the Internet Archive were not used. Kanerva's book
has no open copy.
