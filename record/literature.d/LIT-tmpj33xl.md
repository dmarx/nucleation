  Evans, Silverberg & Santangelo (2015). Each degree-N origami vertex has
  N − 3 internal degrees of freedom, solved by spherical trigonometry,
  whose solutions are "generically multi-valued", giving several
  configuration branches. Vertices are joined by constraints on shared
  folds and a Bloch-wave ansatz gives a rigidity-like matrix. For the
  Miura-ori, with facet bending as two extra folds per vertex, the theory
  finds three uniform zero modes, an acoustic-like mode, and inhomogeneous
  soft modes decaying from the boundaries with an analytic decay length,
  tested on an 8 × 8 paper sheet under indentation and with reversible
  pop-through defects. The degrees of freedom of a finite tessellation
  scale with its perimeter, not its area.

# LIT-tmpj33xl: Lattice Mechanics of Origami Tessellations

Evans, A. A., Silverberg, J. L. & Santangelo, C. D. (2015). Lattice Mechanics of Origami Tessellations. *Physical Review E* 92: 013205. doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.92.013205. arXiv:1503.05756.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading of arXiv v2, NOTE-tmpghi05.*

- **Branches at every vertex.** "the associated solutions are generically
  multi-valued. These results imply that there are multiple branches of
  configuration space for any given spherical polygon." (Sec. I.)
- **Defects are local.** "in the presence of a “pop-through defect” (PTD)
  [7], the lattice distorts to accommodate the induced strain." (Fig. 6
  caption.)
- **Freedom at the edges.** "the number of degrees of freedom scale with
  the perimeter of a finite tessellation, rather than the area." (Sec. IV.)

## Standing in the record

Not cited in the pasted synthesis on constraint architectures. It was
registered as the stand-in for Silverberg et al. (2014), LIT-tmpa3uim,
which A cites as O9 and which could not be read. It stands in partly. It
confirms that Miura-ori sheets admit reversible pop-through defects with
local strain. It does not confirm A's claims that the 2014 paper's cells
are bistable and that its defects are analogous to vacancies, dislocations
and grain boundaries. In its own right it bears on CLAIM-478 and
CLAIM-480: whether facet bending counts as a constraint or a freedom
depends on the energy scale, and composed vertices leave their freedom at
the boundary. Filed from it: THEORY-tmpv4i3y.
