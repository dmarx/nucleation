# NOTE-1691: Zheng et al. — Nonlinear reshaping of Gaussian water wave packets

## Contribution

A systematic laboratory test of how a Gaussian envelope, not an exact
soliton, evolves under the focusing NLS dynamics of deep-water waves, with
the amplitude varied at fixed width: which initial packets broaden, which
hold their shape, and which focus.

## Key insight

A pulse that is not a soliton but is close to one in amplitude and width
travels almost as a soliton would; well below it the pulse spreads, well
above it it focuses as several solitons would.

## Assumptions

Deep water (depth 5 m); narrowband packets; a 1D flume of limited length;
the time-like NLS equation, and the modified NLS for strong focusing; a
fixed width equal to the envelope soliton's, with the amplitude scaled by
a real factor N (eq. 7).

## Key results

- **Regimes** (Results): the experiments are categorized as
  "dispersion-dominated (N = 0.36 and 0.6)", quasi-stationary or solitonic
  (N = 0.8 and 1.0), and "focusing/multi-soliton (N = 1.2, 1.4, 1.6, and
  1.8)". At N = 0.36 the amplitude falls about 50% and the width grows about
  fourfold over 3.45 L_D.
- **Near-stationary** (Discussion): "a Gaussian envelope can remain nearly
  stationary over a propagation distance of" 1.07 L_D when its width and
  amplitude match the soliton's; the authors' gloss: "Gaussian initial
  conditions may naturally evolve toward soliton-like attractors under
  suitable conditions."
- **Below the soliton** (Discussion): "gradual attenuation and an early
  stage of reshaping during propagation for 0.5 < N < 1.0".
- **Long-time fate** (Discussion), anticipated only: "the Gaussian envelope
  will either disperse completely into radiative wave components or evolve
  into one or multiple solitons", beyond the paper's scope.
- **Model agreement** (Discussion): NLS predicts the dispersion-dominated
  and balanced regimes quantitatively; strong focusing needs the modified
  NLS.

## Concepts

Envelope soliton; Gaussian against sech initial data; dispersion length;
dispersive broadening; nonlinear focusing; Satsuma–Yajima multi-soliton
initial conditions; radiation as the non-soliton remainder.

## Connections

- **Satsuma & Yajima (1974)**, [LIT-2194](../literature.d/LIT-2194.md), unread: cited for the
  multi-soliton initial condition N sech; the paper carries none of its
  formula. The sech threshold derived from Cui & Wang (2023), no soliton
  for A ≤ 1/2, is the reader's derivation and is not tested here, since the
  packets are Gaussian and the tank too short to see the outcome.
- **Zabusky & Porter (2010)**: a laboratory version of Russell's
  observation that a large enough initial mass forms more than one
  solitary wave.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md)**: weak. It shows a pulse near the soliton's size
  travelling as a soliton would, but not the separation of a formed
  soliton from its shed remainder.

## Limitations

Short tank: the formation outcome is anticipated, not seen. One width;
eight amplitudes. Experiment with simulation; no theory of the threshold for
Gaussian data.
