# CASE-tmpwrhkd: Two couples, stipulated: in one both partners fight and neither controls the other; in the other the partner who strikes more often is the one controlled, and the other partner holds the money, the messages, the friendships and the exit

## The case

A3 §1 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afa2a8d-2598-83ea-8d1a-d9ceb992a99c),
in A's words.

**Relationship A.** "Alice and Bob have a terrible relationship. They argue
constantly, insult each other, get jealous, and occasionally shove or slap
one another during heated confrontations. Neither is especially frightened
of the other outside those episodes. Neither systematically controls the
other's friendships, money, movements, or decisions. Both feel free to
leave".

**Relationship B.** "Carol and Dan also have physical fights. Perhaps Carol
has even struck Dan more times than Dan has struck Carol. But Dan controls
their finances, monitors Carol's messages, threatens to destroy her
reputation if she leaves, isolates her from friends, and punishes her
whenever she challenges him. Carol sometimes lashes out violently. Yet she
lives in continual fear of what Dan will do next, while Dan can generally
behave however he likes."

What is stipulated: in B, Carol commits as many wrongful violent acts as
Dan or more, and Dan alone holds control over the terms of Carol's life
(money, communication, friendships, exit). In A, both commit wrongful acts
and neither holds that control.

## What it can show

That the count of wrongful acts and the distribution of control can come
apart in either direction: A has mutual wrongdoing with no domination, and
B has the more frequent wrongdoer as the dominated party. A3 names the
pattern in Johnson's typology (situational couple violence, coercive
controlling violence, violent resistance, mutual violent control), which
the record registers unread ([LIT-tmpyfuuw](../literature.d/LIT-tmpyfuuw.md)), with Stark on coercive control ([LIT-tmp77bag](../literature.d/LIT-tmp77bag.md)).

## What it cannot show

- **That any real couple is B.** It is fictional, and A3 §6 lists the
  limits of applying the typology to real relationships: motives are
  opaque, power is domain-specific, relationships move between patterns,
  and the typology's empirical support is mixed.
- **That Carol's acts are excused.** The case fixes who did what and who
  controlled what; it says nothing about how much blame Carol's acts carry,
  which A3 keeps as a separate question ("Experiencing abuse does not give
  someone unlimited moral permission to hurt their partner").
- **Anything about the Depp and Heard dispute,** where the exchange began.
  The curation entry of 2026-11-15 records why no case is filed for it.

## Where it sits

[CASE-264](CASE-264.md) is the same contrast for an organization: two
workshops equal in dependence and different in who controls the terms. This
case varies the count of wrongful acts as well as control, and so separates
a thing [CASE-264](CASE-264.md) holds equal.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Cunha and colleagues, read

The case's own caution, "Counting physical assaults could make Carol appear to be the more violent partner", names a known property of the field's main instrument. This review documents it ("This limitation can lead to the misclassification of individuals who act in self-defense as engaged in BV"). The review shows the case's contrast is a live measurement problem, not only a thought experiment. It says nothing about how often Relationship B's pattern occurs. Relation: none.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Lohmann, read

The case stipulates control as a dimension separate from the count of assaults. The review treats coercive control the same way, as its own construct, but finds its measured associations with PTSD and depression "comparable to those involving psychological IPV". So the distinction the case draws is not yet one the field's instruments reliably separate. The exchange's gloss on this review (A5 §1, "sustained control can be profoundly harmful even when physical assaults aren't the central feature") is more than the review shows. It did not separate control from co-occurring physical violence. Relation: none.

## Note of 2026-11-16: Mazinani and O'Connor, read

Relationship B, in which the controlled partner strikes more often, is coherent as stipulated. The review reports one population-level finding against its being a common route: "Spencer et al. (2022) found that male partners’ controlling behaviors were not significantly associated with women’s IPV perpetration." This qualifies any use of the case as typical. It does not touch its use as an existence proof (ARG-tmpsyaz2). Relation: none.
