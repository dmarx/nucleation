# NOTE-tmp7ylue: Wilkins & Bourrat — Replication and Reproduction (SEP, 2022 revision)

## Contribution

A survey of the replicator concept and its critics. For this record,
its §5.4 is a short, recent statement of Griesemer's reproducer by an
author (Bourrat) who works on transitions, with the objections to it.

## Key insight

The reproducer puts development into the unit: "A reproducer is an
entity that develops and has a material overlap between the “parent”
and “progeny”" (§5.4), and "inheritance is a system property, not a
property of parts, unless they are also reproducing systems, and is
developmentally acquired".

## Key results

- **Reproduction** (§5.4): "a composite process of development and
  progeneration, leading to objects that are themselves capable of
  development and progeneration"; progeneration is the "increase in
  numerically distinct objects of a given kind". "Organisms do not
  arise already capable of reproduction in the main, but must undergo
  orderly transitions before they may reproduce."
- **Replication as an evolved form** (§5.4): Griesemer treats
  replication as "the terminal form of reproduction, once coding
  mechanisms have been evolved, which is itself an evolved form of
  multiplication", citing "Griesemer 2000a: 76"; "replication arises
  when material overlap evolves progeneration, and progeneration evolves
  a codical inheritance system" (Figure 4, from Wimsatt and Griesemer
  2007).
- **No fixed level** (§5.4): the reproducer "does not imply a particular
  level of reproduction or progeneration"; Szathmáry and Maynard Smith
  read each major transition as an evolved mode of progeneration.
- **Objections** (§5.4): Godfrey-Smith finds material overlap too narrow;
  Griesemer replies that "he is primarily concerned with the idea that
  parents cause progeny"; Godfrey-Smith's simple, collective and
  scaffolded reproducers; Veigl, Suárez and Stencel's "reconstitutor",
  which the authors find too liberal.
- **Developmental systems theory** (§5.1): if whole developmental cycles
  are replicators, the replicator/interactor distinction blurs. The
  section does not discuss Griesemer.

## Concepts

- **Reproducer**, **progeneration**, **material overlap**: as above.
- **Scaffolded reproducer** (Godfrey-Smith): reproduced as part of a
  larger unit or by another entity.

## Connections

It stands in for [LIT-1932](../literature.d/LIT-1932.md) and [LIT-1937](../literature.d/LIT-1937.md) for the reproducer concept only.
It restates: reproduction as development plus progeneration with
material overlap; inheritance as a developmentally acquired system
property; replication as an evolved form; no privileged level. It does
not restate: [LIT-1932](../literature.d/LIT-1932.md)'s argument that developmental systems theory
reunifies heredity and development only nominally, or its claim that the
reproducer keeps a distinction between cultural and biological
evolution; [LIT-1937](../literature.d/LIT-1937.md)'s elaboration of Maynard Smith's multiplication, its
revision of transition theory, or its "three-stage scenario". The
citation "Griesemer 2000a: 76" names the Philosophy of Science paper
(pp. S348-S368), which has no page 76; page 76 falls in the Selection
paper (pp. 67-80), the entry's 2000b. The claim is most likely from
[LIT-1937](../literature.d/LIT-1937.md); unverified until read. [LIT-188](../literature.d/LIT-188.md) applies the reproducer to
groups.

## Bearing on the record

- **[LIT-1937](../literature.d/LIT-1937.md)'s question** (whether a unit that reproduces can be
  identified before, and without assuming, the individual a transition
  produces): the entry's statement that the reproducer "does not imply a
  particular level" and that reproducing capacity is "developmentally
  acquired" fits a yes: reproducers are picked out by material overlap
  and progeneration, not by individuality. It is the survey's
  summary, not Griesemer's argument, and does not settle it.
- **[CLAIM-529](../claims.d/CLAIM-529.md)** (a parallel): material overlap and progeneration are
  criteria stated on a candidate's causal relations, not on members'
  attitudes, which is the claim's interaction route. No edge from a
  survey.
