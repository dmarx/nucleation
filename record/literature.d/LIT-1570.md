# LIT-1570: The Evolution of Dependence and Cohesion in Incipient Endosymbioses

Athreya, G. S., Czuppon, P. & Gokhale, C. S. (2025). The Evolution of Dependence and Cohesion in Incipient Endosymbioses. *The American Naturalist* 206(5): 435–451. doi:10.1086/737588.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1211](../notes.d/NOTE-1211.md).*

- **Two axes.** Dependence comes from each partner's "obligacy", its
  investment in collective over free growth. Cohesion comes from its
  "adhesion", its investment in synchronized reproduction. A transition
  is both axes at their maximum.
- **The faster partner goes first.** The symbiont, with a larger and
  faster population, becomes fully obligate first, a Red King effect
  aligned by shared fate. The host's obligacy is then neutral. It rises to
  full dependence if partners die inside the collective, and falls to
  none if they reproduce there ([THEORY-1051](../theory.d/THEORY-1051.md)).
- **Carrying capacity, not growth rate.** Traits leave zero only if the
  collective's carrying capacity exceeds about aK_HK_S/d.
- **Dependence before cohesion, conditionally.** This holds when free
  growth depends on a partner's own obligacy and dissociation on both
  adhesions. The appendix (Table S1) shows it vanishing or reversing
  under other mappings ([THEORY-1050](../theory.d/THEORY-1050.md)).
- **Untested.** The authors' table of ten real symbioses "does not allow
  us to confirm/refute" the bias.

## Why this is here

A2's highest priority: it "addresses almost exactly the conceptual gap", separating two properties the record discusses together. A2 crossed them into a two-by-two of regimes, which it called a conceptual classification, not stable endpoints. [QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md) rests its first two dimensions on it. A2 proposed connecting it to [THEORY-141](../theory.d/THEORY-141.md) and [THEORY-418](../theory.d/THEORY-418.md).
