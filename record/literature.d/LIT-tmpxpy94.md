# LIT-tmpxpy94: Fiat and Bona Fide Boundaries

Barry Smith and Achille C. Varzi (2000), *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 60(2), 401–420 — DOI-10.2307/2653492

## Key takeaways

*Filed, not read.* Known here by what it is standardly cited for.

- Boundaries are of two kinds: **bona fide**, where the world itself is
  discontinuous, and **fiat**, which owe their existence to demarcation,
  cognition or convention.
- Fiat boundaries bound fiat objects, and the two kinds are standardly said to
  behave differently topologically. What the paper's formal theory says about
  each is to be checked in a reading.
- It is the standard citation for the distinction itself, which later work in
  geographic and biomedical ontology builds on.

## Standing in the record

Filed for [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md), the counter that a gap or coupling criterion cannot tell
a boundary a system produces from one imposed on it (a cell membrane and a
beam joining two pendulums pass alike). The fiat/bona fide distinction is the
named, older form of a nearby distinction, and the survey of the Mathematics
of Meaning corpus pointed to it ([MOM-LIT-016](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-016.md)).

**The two distinctions are not the same, and a reading should say how they
differ.** Bona fide means a physical discontinuity is there. [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md)'s
"produced" means the system makes the boundary for itself. The beam is a bona
fide object, but the boundary around the coupled pair is drawn by the
analyst's target, which is fiat in Smith & Varzi's sense only if a target
counts as demarcation. Self-production is Weber & Varela's notion ([LIT-tmpbvcwm](LIT-tmpbvcwm.md)), filed beside this. [QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md), where a combined form's boundary lies, is the
question both bear on.

Varzi's SEP entry on mereology ([LIT-124](LIT-124.md)) is the neighbour already held.
