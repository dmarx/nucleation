# CLAIM-484: Internal conflict is the gap between the precedence a motive claims over action and the authority the rest of the organization grants it

## The claim

A4 §2 of the exchange of 2026-10-13 proposed the distinction between
*claimed precedence* and *endorsed authority* because it "may be essential
to modeling internal conflict". [CLAIM-283](CLAIM-283.md) version 1 joined two things: the
distinction itself, and the identification of internal conflict with the
gap. The audit of 2026-10-23 (workbench entry of 2026-10-24) suggested
separating them. This claim is the identification. A motive is in conflict
with the organization when it claims more precedence ([TERM-094](../terms.d/TERM-094.md)) than
it is granted, and the size of the conflict is the size of that gap. A34
§3 of the exchange of 2026-10-17 wrote it p_i − w_i, "when the quantities
have been defined on comparable scales".

## What it does not say

- **That conflict is dysfunction.** A34: "a good organization may
  correctly prevent an unreliable process from taking control." A gap
  can be the right outcome.
- **That the scales are comparable.** The subtraction needs claimed and
  granted precedence on one scale. Where they are only ordered, the gap is
  ordinal, as [CLAIM-347](CLAIM-347.md) treats frustration.

## Why it is Proposed

Nothing in the record shows a gap and conflict varying together. What
would settle it is an episode, documented with how the actors evaluated
the situation, where the claim, the grant and the marks of conflict are
each recorded.

## Note of 2026-10-29: what holds it back

The identification needs an episode in which the precedence a motive
claims, the authority it is granted and the marks of conflict
(interruption, oscillation, a costly override) are each recorded, so that
gap and conflict can be seen to vary together. The record holds none.
[CASE-151](../cases.d/CASE-151.md) and [CASE-127](../cases.d/CASE-127.md) separate access from authority, and their notes say
they do not show that a motive claimed precedence.
