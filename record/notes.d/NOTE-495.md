# NOTE-495: What Is Wrong with the No-Report Paradigm and How to Fix It

## Contribution

The paper identifies a flaw in the no-report paradigm, the main method for
deciding whether perceptual consciousness is prefrontal: it removes report
but not the thinking about the stimulus that report would have expressed.
It proposes the right target, a paradigm with no post-perceptual cognition
that differs between percepts, and finds an instance in an existing
binocular-rivalry experiment.

## Key insight

The confound was never report as such. It was cognition about the percept,
of which report is one product. A subject who is not asked to say which
way the grating moves can still notice, think and wonder which way it
moves, and that thought is correlated with the percept. The way to remove
it is to make the perceptual change one that the subject cannot single out,
so that nothing in thought follows it.

## Assumptions

- **Cognition** means thought-based mentality: reasoning, deciding,
  evaluating, reporting and working memory, with transitions between
  propositional states that depend on their contents (Glossary).
- **Consciousness** means phenomenal consciousness throughout; the access
  distinction is noted in the Glossary.
- **Correlational evidence** only; Block cautions that causal interventions
  are better where available.
- **Prefrontal regions at issue**: dorsolateral, medial prefrontal, anterior
  cingulate and orbitofrontal. Both sides grant parietal involvement.

## Key results

- **The opposing sides.** Prefrontalists: global workspace and higher-order
  theories, and the authors of the 2017 *Science* manifesto on machine
  consciousness. The back: IIT and recurrent processing. Noncognitivists
  can allow that the phenomenology of thought itself is prefrontal.
- **Rivalry and replay.** Early fMRI found stronger frontal differences at
  transitions; replay subtraction isolates transitions, not contents.
- **No-report evidence on both sides.** Frässle et al. 2014: with nystagmus as
  the readout and no task in the scanner, prefrontal differences were minor
  ("Frontal activity relates to introspection and action but not to
  perception"). Safavi et al. 2014 and later Utah-array work decoded
  rivalry contents from monkey prefrontal cortex without report, in monkeys
  never trained on a discrimination. Wang et al. 2013 decoded contents
  prefrontally, with report.
- **The bored monkey problem.** Hours of fixation with nothing to do invite
  thoughts aligned with the alternating percept. The same applies to the
  older human no-report studies (Lumer and Rees 1999).
- **Inconspicuous rivalry (Brascamp et al. 2015, Fig. 2).** Random-dot motion
  changing every 300 ms; rivalry switches with same-colour dots were
  detected at chance by d′. Density reports showed one eye's array, not a
  merger, confirming real rivalry. Prefrontal differences appeared for
  different-colour dots and were "altogether undetectable" for same-colour
  ones.
- **Box 1, rivalry versus merger.** Attending to local features of a male and
  a female face gives rivalry; attending to gender gives a fused
  androgynous face. Locally compatible stimuli fuse even if globally
  different (Carlson and He).
- **Box 2, predictive processing.** Clark's account of rivalry as competition
  between high-level hypotheses, because a face and a house cannot occupy
  one place, is undercut: low-level local features largely decide rivalry.

## Concepts

- **no-report paradigm**: reports calibrate another indicator of
  consciousness (here nystagmus), then experimental subjects make none.
- **no-(differential-)post-perceptual cognition paradigm**: one in which no
  cognition that differs between the competing percepts follows them.
- **prefrontalists**: those who hold certain prefrontal circuits necessary
  for perceptual consciousness.
- **optokinetic nystagmus (OKN)**: slow pursuit in the direction of
  perceived motion with fast returns, used as a no-report readout.
- **bored monkey problem**: uninstructed subjects think about what they see.

## Limitations

- **The positive case is one fMRI null result**, from a design built for a
  different purpose (avoiding attention to switches), and Block concedes the
  changes may be too fine for fMRI.
- **The method is specific to rivalry**; Block lists its extension as an
  outstanding question.
- **The higher-order theory** in which the higher-order state is
  post-perceptual is ruled out of court by the paradigm, as Block notes;
  versions in which it is a simultaneous pointer are not.

## Open questions

- How could the no-post-perceptual-cognition principle be extended beyond
  binocular rivalry?
- If consciousness and cognition have entirely distinct bases, could a
  creature be radically wrong about its own conscious perception?
- Could some vegetative patients be conscious without even the cognition
  needed to imagine playing tennis?
