# THEORY-554: On the grounds of moral standing now commonly accepted, together with scientific premises that are not unreasonable, the aggregate interests of insects or the interests of near-future AI could outweigh humanity's; no capacity yet proposed secures human priority, and the books that state those grounds retreat to moderate advice without a principle that supplies the stopping point

## Source

- Schwitzgebel & Sinnott-Armstrong (2026), [LIT-166](../literature.d/LIT-166.md), read in full in [NOTE-116](../notes.d/NOTE-116.md)
  from the September 2025 draft. This is the primary source.
- Gruen & Monsó, SEP "The Moral Status of Animals" (2024 revision), [LIT-146](../literature.d/LIT-146.md),
  read in full in [NOTE-181](../notes.d/NOTE-181.md), for the survey of grounds of considerability.

## What was actually shown

**The two arms ([LIT-166](../literature.d/LIT-166.md), §1).** For insects: a sentientist premise (E1) and
the scientific premise (S1) that there are about 10¹⁹ insects, each with at
least one millionth of a human's capacity for valenced consciousness (p. 4).
For AI: "(E2.) Entities with human-level or superior capacities for
conscious practical deliberation deserve at least equal rights with humans",
"commonly accepted by deontologists, contract theorists, and others", and
"(S2.) Near future AI systems will have human-level or superior capacities
for conscious practical deliberation", "not unreasonable as a scientific
prediction" (p. 5). The premise pairs are illustrative, not endorsed.

**Parity (§2.3, p. 20; §3.1, p. 26).** It is "suspiciously self-serving" to
set humans above a threshold of status that animals fail, while denying any
higher threshold that superior AI could cross.

**No capacity secures human priority.** The review's survey (§5) finds that
none of consciousness, agency, intellect, creativity or appreciation of
ethics secures human specialness against animal science and AI. The SEP
entry reaches the same result for animals by another route: every proposed
uniquely human capacity has been observed, "often in less elaborate form", in
other animals, so capacity-based exceptionalism is "not the most promising"
([LIT-146](../literature.d/LIT-146.md), §1.2), and the problem of marginal cases defeats personhood as the
criterion (§1.3.1).

**The retreats (§§2.3–2.4, 3.1–3.2).** Birch's proportionality runs through
citizens' panels of 150–450 people, which the reviewers expect to be
human-centred; weighting representation by likelihood × degree × population
would give "about a hundred representatives of insect interests for every
one representative of human interests" (p. 19). Sebo's "relational" and
"co-beneficial" considerations are contingent, and his 2023 "rebugnant
conclusion" article resists ant-over-human replacement only on contingent
grounds. Keane defers to slowly adapting cultures.

What could have come out otherwise: a ground of standing on which humans
come out first without special pleading, or a principle internal to the
books that stops short of the radical conclusion. The reviewers find
neither.

## What this does not say

- **That human priority is false.** The reviewers defend the conditional and
  leave the choice between its consequent and modus tollens to "further
  reflection" (§5). "We are not endorsing this radical conclusion" (p. 38).
- **That the scientific premises are true.** S1 and S2 are called "not
  unreasonable" and get no argument ([NOTE-116](../notes.d/NOTE-116.md), limitations). If AI
  consciousness is unlikely on current trajectories, as Seth ([LIT-135](../literature.d/LIT-135.md))
  argues, the AI arm loses its antecedent.
- **That no such ground exists.** §5 finds none among the usual candidates.
  It does not argue that none could exist.
- **That the books are fairly represented.** The record has not read Birch,
  Sebo or Keane. Much of the case against Sebo reads his chapter titles
  literally, which the reviewers concede he does not intend (§3.1). The
  liveness of each of Sebo's views does not make their conjunction live,
  as the reviewers note.
- **That the SEP entry endorses the conclusion.** [LIT-146](../literature.d/LIT-146.md) covers animals
  only, never AI, and its agency grounds all presuppose experience, so they
  do not extend status to non-experiencing systems as stated ([NOTE-181](../notes.d/NOTE-181.md)).
  It is cited for the failure of human exceptionalism, not for aggregation
  or for AI. It is also not neutral on weighing: §2.2 argues for a
  multi-factor utilitarian view, and §3 ends on Gruen's own entangled
  empathy.
- **That considerability fixes weight.** The SEP's radar-screen distinction
  (§2.2) separates being on the screen from how strong the signal is. The
  conditional needs premises about weight and aggregation as well as about
  standing.
