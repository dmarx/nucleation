# LIT-3004: Emergence of Scaling in Random Networks

Barabási & Albert (1999). Science 286, 509. DOI 10.1126/science.286.5439.509;
arXiv cond-mat/9910332.

## Standing in the record

Registered on 2026-11-21 from the exchange "Rich Get Richer Power Laws" (2026-11-20 to 21), where A cites it at A1, A3 for preferential attachment as the canonical rich-get-richer route to a power-law degree distribution. It bears on the base case of the exchange's opening question, how rich-get-richer processes produce power laws. Not read when registered.

Read on 2026-11-22 in [NOTE-tmpwgw8e](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpwgw8e.md) (the arXiv preprint; the print
version was not reached); the reading is [THEORY-tmpfrz0k](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpfrz0k.md). The first
summary is accurate; 3 is the mean-field exponent and the simulated one
2.9 ± 0.1. It grounds [CLAIM-817](../claims.d/CLAIM-817.md)'s base case and A3's "attachment alone
does not guarantee" clause ([ARG-tmp3q9cy](../arguments.d/ARG-tmp3q9cy.md)), and [CLAIM-830](../claims.d/CLAIM-830.md)'s premise
([ARG-tmpmylwc](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpmylwc.md)), where Model B's transient power law qualifies the
Pólya-urn contrast.

## Note of 2026-11-22: Broido and Clauset, read

Broido and Clauset ([LIT-2963](LIT-2963.md), read in [NOTE-tmpleyjh](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpleyjh.md); the reading is
[THEORY-tmphcnj3](../theory.d/THEORY-tmphcnj3.md)) **qualify this work's empirical premise** that real
networks are generally scale free: 4% of 928 network data sets are
Strongest, and median exponents concentrate near 2, not the model's 3.
They keep directed preferential attachment as a synthetic control, which
their test classes Strongest. Newman ([LIT-2976](LIT-2976.md), [NOTE-tmpa7yd2](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpa7yd2.md)) notes that
the model's c = m, which gives 3, has no good reason behind it (p. 19).
