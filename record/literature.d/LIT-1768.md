summary: >-
  Parfit (2017), his last paper, submitted unfinished the day before his death. Most of our choices of policy change who will exist, so a policy that makes later lives much worse may be worse for no one who ever lives: the non-identity problem. Narrow person-affecting principles, on which nothing can be worse, or wrong, if it is worse for no one, then imply that such policies, and even ending human history by all choosing childlessness, are not wrong. Parfit rejects them, and rejects solutions that appeal to rights, wronging or Boonin's acceptance of the implausible conclusions. He also retracts his own earlier appeal to an impersonal principle. Instead, being caused to exist with a life worth living is an intrinsic, existential benefit, and on a Wide Person-Affecting Principle one outcome is worse if it benefits people less. That solves the non-identity problem in person-affecting terms and keeps "better than" transitive, where the Weak Narrow Principle and the Two-Tier View do not. For different numbers he proposes a Wide Dual Principle, counting both the benefits to each and the benefits to all. It does not imply the Repugnant Conclusion, though Parfit grants that this alone does not let us avoid it. He allows that we may be permitted, or required, to favour present people over greater benefits to future people, but warns that done by every generation this could "wreck the Earth".
