# CLAIM-1306: Two views of the record should be separate scholarly contributions only when they have different central claims needing different evidence; that audiences want different presentations is a reason for different views, not different contributions, so an atlas of the record is organized around a few primary arguments with focused, cross-cutting and frontier views beside them rather than as a list of paper titles

## The claim

A57: "Two articles should remain separate when they have different
central claims and require different evidence—not simply because they
have different audiences. Conversely, two audiences wanting different
presentations is a reason to create different views, not necessarily
different scholarly contributions." And A56, before it: "a hyperarticle
should exist when it offers a useful, independently navigable
perspective on a body of inquiry—not merely because we can divide the
record into another topic."

A57's four kinds of entry in an atlas: "Primary hyperarticles:
substantial, independently motivated arguments. Focused views: narrower
expositions, technical derivations, case studies, or alternative
presentations. Cross-cutting views: presentations that relate several
regions without establishing a new canonical body of research. Research
frontiers: promising article scopes whose central arguments remain
incomplete." A59 applies it: thirty candidates become fifteen primary
scopes, each with "a question and a boundary", and the rest focused views
or frontiers.

## Where it stands on the record

[QUESTION-258](../questions.d/QUESTION-258.md) asks how a manuscript drawn from the record should be chosen:
as a well-supported connected part of the argument, or as a smallest set
of claims that changes what it can conclude. This claim answers a
different part of it: when two candidate manuscripts are one contribution
or two. Its test is the central claim and its evidence, which fits
[CLAIM-1224](CLAIM-1224.md) (a contribution is a change in the state of knowledge, not
a publication). The record's own practice already folds works this way:
four proposed essays were folded into organization-of-perspective when
one manuscript superseded them (the `works` vocabulary).

## What it does not say

How to tell two central claims apart when one refines the other. That is
the same judgment as whether a revised claim is a new claim, which is
[QUESTION-541](../questions.d/QUESTION-541.md)'s.
