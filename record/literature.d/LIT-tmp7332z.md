# LIT-tmp7332z: Formality Considered Harmful: Experiences, Emerging Themes, and Directions on the Use of Formal Representations in Interactive Systems

Frank M. Shipman III and Catherine C. Marshall (1999), *Computer Supported Cooperative Work* 8(4):333–352 — DOI-10.1023/A:1008716330212

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read.* From the abstract and an outline of the authors' web
version: the paper draws on experience with many interactive systems,
argumentation and hypermedia among them. It locates the cause of their
unexpected difficulties in users' unwillingness or inability to make
structure, content or procedures explicit. The reasons it gives are the
cognitive overhead of a formal language, the tacit knowledge that resists
being stated, reluctance to commit early to a structure that may be wrong,
and structure that differs by person and task. It proposes incremental and
system-assisted formalization, and recognising structure users have not
declared.
