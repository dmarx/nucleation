# LIT-230: Riesz representation theorem (Wikipedia)

Wikipedia contributors (2026), *Wikipedia* — <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riesz_representation_theorem>

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmprvqbi](../notes.d/NOTE-tmprvqbi.md).*

- **The theorem.** For every continuous linear functional φ on a real or
  complex Hilbert space there is a unique f_φ with φ(x) = ⟨x, f_φ⟩ and
  ‖f_φ‖ = ‖φ‖. It is the vector in (ker φ)^⊥ with φ(f_φ) = ‖φ‖², and the
  minimum-norm point of φ^{-1}(‖φ‖²). The map y ↦ ⟨·, y⟩ is an
  antilinear isometric bijection H → H*; over ℝ it is linear.
- **Where completeness enters.** Only in H = ker φ ⊕ (ker φ)^⊥. The norm
  formula ‖⟨·, y⟩‖ = ‖y‖ holds in any inner-product space.
- **Constructions.** f_φ = conj φ(u) u/‖u‖² for any nonzero u ⊥ ker φ;
  f_φ = Σ_i conj φ(e_i) e_i over any orthonormal basis, with
  ‖φ‖² = Σ_i |φ(e_i)|²; on ℂⁿ, conjugate transposition.
- **Adjoints.** For bounded A : H → Z the adjoint A* is defined by Riesz
  from the functionals h ↦ ⟨z | Ah⟩, and A* = Φ_H^{-1} ∘ ᵗA ∘ Φ_Z: the
  transpose, read through the two Riesz maps. Self-adjoint, normal and
  unitary operators are restated in terms of functionals.
- **What it does not cover.** Unbounded operators, kernels and RKHS (the
  word "reproducing" does not occur), and spectral theory.
