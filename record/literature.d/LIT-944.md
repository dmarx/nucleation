# LIT-944: Questioning Representational Optimism in Deep Learning: The Fractured Entangled Representation Hypothesis

Akarsh Kumar, Jeff Clune, Joel Lehman and Kenneth O. Stanley (2025), *arXiv:2505.11581*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp8icvu](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp8icvu.md).*

- **Same function, same architecture.** A CPPN's image is its whole
  input–output function on the grid. The evolved skull, butterfly and
  apple are rewritten exactly as dense MLPs, and SGD from random weights
  reproduces each image in that architecture. On the sparse evolved
  architecture itself SGD fails (Fig. 12).
- **Different internals.** The evolved skull is symmetric from early
  layers, with 24 novel feature maps and separate neurons for its parts.
  The SGD skull is symmetric only at the output, with many high-frequency
  maps. The evolved apple separates background, body and stem; the SGD
  apple mixes them.
- **Different perturbations.** Single-weight sweeps of the evolved networks
  open a mouth, wink an eye or swing a stem while keeping the image's
  regularities. Those of the SGD networks distort it. The same holds in a
  PCA basis and along random weight directions (App. F), and for ReLU
  networks (App. E).
- **What is argued, not shown.** That the order of discovery in open-ended
  search (symmetry, then a face, then a skull) is the cause. That large
  models have FER, on the strength of anecdotes about GPT-3, GPT-4o and
  cited interpretability work. That FER harms generalization, creativity
  and continual learning.
