# CLAIM-687: The accountability that those who control an arrangement owe to those who depend on it should rise with how indispensable the arrangement has become to them, so that an arrangement that has become effectively compulsory owes public justification of its purposes and governing rules, and those affected owe no deference to expertise or ownership as such

## The claim

A13 §VI.3 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6af6b47c-8b0c-83ea-ae04-3e3eb2649597), after six protections for "essential technological
systems":

> The goal is to impose a burden of justification on arrangements that
> concentrate power, eliminate alternatives, or make essential
> participation conditional on submission to opaque authority.
>
> The more indispensable a system becomes, the stronger its obligations of
> accountability should ordinarily be.

and the sixth protection, "Public justification": "When a technological
arrangement becomes effectively compulsory, its purposes and governing
rules should be open to public scrutiny." A8 §VII gives the aim: "to
prevent technical expertise or economic ownership from becoming a license
for unaccountable social authority", and A11 §24 the formula:
"Specialized competence in operation, public accountability in purpose,
and enforceable rights for those affected."

The claim generalizes "technological system" to any arrangement, which A's
examples (a public utility, a hospital, an AI system) already range over.
"Arrangement" is [TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md)'s sense 1; who owes is "those who control" it,
the agents [CLAIM-682](CLAIM-682.md)'s second condition names.

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-682](CLAIM-682.md)** is a threshold: essential dependence, controllers'
  discretion and too little accountability together make domination by the
  controllers. This claim is a slope below and above that threshold: the
  accountability owed grows with the first condition, essentiality. It
  does not say that a dependence meeting the threshold is wrong for that
  reason alone, only what it owes.
- **[CLAIM-684](CLAIM-684.md)** raises the burden of justifying a choice with the size
  and irreversibility of the narrowing it brings. This raises the
  accountability owed for an arrangement with the dependence it has
  brought. The two meet at an arrangement that became indispensable by a
  choice no one justified.
- **[CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)**: a second-order duty to keep violations knowable,
  contestable and correctable. Contestability and accountability in A's
  list are that duty; this claim says its strength scales.
- **[CLAIM-686](CLAIM-686.md)** says which part of [CLAIM-682](CLAIM-682.md)'s third condition the
  record's three achievements of normative efficacy cover. The protections
  A lists here fall on both sides of that line: contestability and
  accountability are voice, portability and interoperability are exit.

## A's riders, carried here

- **Governance at the appropriate scale** (A11 §25, A13 §VI.4): authority
  distributed "according to effectiveness, accountability, and the
  preservation of substantive freedom", and "not decentralization as an
  unconditional principle", since "A local authority can exercise
  arbitrary domination just as a national government can". This is a
  caution on where the accountability is owed, not a further claim.
- **"A right not to be optimized"** (A8 §VII): "The problem arises when
  measurable characteristics become substitutes for the persons they
  describe." That is [TERM-108](../terms.d/TERM-108.md)'s proxy objective with persons as the
  outcome measured, and [CLAIM-683](CLAIM-683.md)'s mechanism outside meaning; it is
  noted here, not filed.

## What it does not say

- Not that every technical decision belongs to a public. A8 §VII: "The aim
  would not be to place every engineering decision under popular vote."
- Not that the protections are absolute. A13 §VI.3: "These are not absolute
  requirements without exceptions."
- Not how indispensability is measured. A uses "essential",
  "indispensable" and "effectively compulsory" without a measure; the
  claim inherits that looseness, and [CLAIM-677](CLAIM-677.md)'s measure, what remains
  open to a non-adopter, is the nearest the record has.

## Note of 2026-11-17: intensifiers, not grounds; a protected private domain; and a limit on challengers, from "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-tmpnhesz CLAIM-tmpyqfrf — filed from the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" on 2026-11-17, Proposed -->

A83 §3D of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b, 2026-11-17) relays this claim faithfully and calls it "an important candidate principle, but it requires care". A83 §6 then orders it. With Φ_N(x) the forms of answerability a power x requires, partially ordered, A's "proportionality hypothesis" lists four intensifiers, discretion, dependence, irreversibility and control over recourse: "These aren't four independent moral foundations. They are features that can make existing claims more vulnerable to interference." And: "**The grounds of answerability determine why something is owed; vulnerability and power asymmetry help determine what protections are required for it to be adequately honored.**" So indispensability is an intensifier, not a ground: it raises what is owed to those who already have a claim ([CLAIM-tmpnhesz](CLAIM-tmpnhesz.md)), and A warns "we shouldn't expect a simple monotone equation". The claim's "should rise with" is best read as an ordering with other features held fixed.

A boundary case, A93 §4: a homeowner does not owe "every interested stranger a public justification", "an important counterexample to any theory claiming that consequential power always entails universal answerability." It is consistent with this claim, which scales with dependence: a stranger does not depend on the household. A83 §6's contrast is the infrastructure owner, whose obligations rise "Even if it owns the infrastructure". The limit is filed as [CLAIM-tmpyqfrf](CLAIM-tmpyqfrf.md).

A limit from the other side, A100 §11: "The claimant's authority to demand explanations cannot simply be taken as unlimited", given interests "in privacy, finality, protection against harassment, due process, or freedom from unreasonable burdens"; "The required form of answerability must therefore be justified to the parties on both sides of the relationship." The accountability that rises with indispensability must itself be justifiable to those who answer.

No status changes.
