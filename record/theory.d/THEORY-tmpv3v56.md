# THEORY-tmpv3v56: Whether a self at one time is the same as a self at another has an answer only relative to a level of abstraction fixed by the purpose of asking, and asked at no such level it has none; the question with a substantive answer is what makes a self one thing at a time

## Source

Floridi (2011), "The Informational Nature of Personal Identity", *Minds and
Machines* 21(4): 549–566, [LIT-136](../literature.d/LIT-136.md), read in full in [NOTE-130](../notes.d/NOTE-130.md).

## What was actually shown

**Two branches (pp. 552–554).** Diachronic "egology" asks about
identification through time or across worlds; synchronic egology about
individualisation in time. Identity ("a is this") and sameness ("this is
the same a as that a") depend on the observables a question's purpose
fixes. The hospital that became a school is the same by location and
different by social function. Saul and Paul, the caterpillar and the
butterfly, and Theseus's ship "are and are not" the same, depending on the
level of abstraction (p. 553). Diachronic identity questions "are really
teleological questions", and "insofar as they are dealt with
metaphysically (modally or not, it does not matter), they do not deserve to
be taken seriously" (pp. 553–554). "This is not relativism: given a
particular goal, one LoA is better than another" (p. 554).

**Individuation first (pp. 554–556).** "Individualisation logically precedes
identification" (p. 554), on an appeal to Plato in three sentences. Lockean
and narrative accounts both individuate through information processing,
and both inherit the problem Hume's Appendix states: what binds the
information into a unity. Kant's transcendental unity answers it only
partially and non-constructively (p. 556).

**The synchronic answer (pp. 557–560).** The three-membranes model: a self
individuated by successive encapsulations, corporeal, cognitive and
conscious, bonded by chemical bonds, mutual information and semantics
(p. 560). Its author calls it "biologically and informationally plausible,
but … admittedly, somewhat figurative" (p. 557). Because semantics cannot be
private, a self needs a community (p. 560).

**The premises.** The method of levels of abstraction: any system "is always
accessed and hence modelled at a given level of abstraction" (p. 553),
assumed from Floridi 2008. Endurantism and perdurantism are said to
presuppose "direct realism", a non-mediated access to the system in itself
(p. 553), which is asserted.

**What could have come out otherwise.** Some sameness question about a self
could have resisted relativisation to a purpose. The paper's examples are
all ones where it does not, which is why they cannot test it.

## What this does not say

- **That it gives a criterion of personal identity.** The paper sets the
  persistence question aside and deflates it. [NOTE-130](../notes.d/NOTE-130.md) corrects the seeded
  description on exactly this point.
- **How to choose between purposes.** "One LoA is better than another"
  given a goal, but nothing adjudicates between goals, such as
  responsibility against prudential concern.
- **That individuation comes first.** It is asserted.
- **That the membrane model decides cases.** It gives no criterion for when
  a membrane or a bonding force is present, so it cannot say whether an AI
  system has a consciousness membrane.
- **That the paper keeps to its own deflation.** Having set diachronic
  identity aside, it still says the self can run on another platform and
  that recorded memories fix it over time (pp. 561–562).
- **That selves are where information becomes conscious.** That closing
  claim is conditional on informational structural realism ([LIT-152](../literature.d/LIT-152.md)), whose
  support here is nil.

## Connections

- **Earlier versions of the move.** Locke ([THEORY-tmpidotl](THEORY-tmpidotl.md)) says the
  difficulty "rather rises from the names ill-used" (§28). Hume
  ([THEORY-tmpyf0hd](THEORY-tmpyf0hd.md)) says hard cases are "rather grammatical than …
  philosophical difficulties" (1.4.6.21), for borderline cases only. Parfit
  ([THEORY-tmp60gbs](THEORY-tmp60gbs.md)) proposes that whether to call a later self "I" is a
  matter of choice once the facts of continuity and connectedness are fixed.
  Floridi makes the relativity cover every diachronic question.
- **Against it.** Williams ([THEORY-tmpgxi0r](THEORY-tmpgxi0r.md)) argues that a conventional
  answer, fit for lawyers, "seems to have no sense at all" as a guide to a
  person's fears about his own future. Olson's map ([THEORY-tmp2b3do](THEORY-tmp2b3do.md)) rejects
  the view that the persistence question is answered by conceptual means.
- **The LLM "I".** Shanahan ([THEORY-tmpb08ha](THEORY-tmpb08ha.md)) lets context select which of
  several sites an LLM's "I" picks out, and makes his conversation-bound
  self literally text and a program. [NOTE-376](../notes.d/NOTE-376.md) calls this Floridi's move made
  for an LLM; the paper does not cite Floridi.
- **Formal individuality.** Bourrat's information-theoretic and
  coarse-graining accounts ([LIT-005](../literature.d/LIT-005.md), [LIT-049](../literature.d/LIT-049.md)) are formal versions of the
  idea that mutual information marks off an individual, which the cognitive
  membrane asserts informally.
- **[THEORY-034](THEORY-034.md)** finds that among the record's structural realisms only
  Floridi's informational structural realism is of the kind Chakravartty's
  dilemma reaches. That is the ontology this paper assumes for its closing
  claim.
