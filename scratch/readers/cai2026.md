## Summary

The paper treats InfoNCE as an energy over representation *measures* on a
fixed compact embedding manifold Z (the sphere is the canonical case; any
compact homogeneous manifold with an isotropic kernel qualifies, Rem. 2.1).
In the large-batch limit the softmax denominator becomes a "population
partition field", a kernel-smoothed density of the current representation
law. With this the finite-N objective converges, in value (up to a constant)
and gradient, to a closed-form deterministic energy (Thm. 3.1, 4.1). Lifted
to densities, the energy for unimodal InfoNCE is a free energy, alignment
potential over τ minus entropy, which is strictly convex with a unique Gibbs
minimizer ρ* ∝ exp(−U/τ) (Prop. 3.1). For symmetric (CLIP-style) multimodal
InfoNCE it is the average of two such free energies *minus* a symmetric KL
divergence between the two modalities' densities (Def. 4.3). The minus sign
makes each modality's density a barrier in the other's effective potential, so
the two marginals can stay apart, giving a modality gap, while positive pairs
stay well aligned (Prop. 4.1, Takeaway 2). Synthetic experiments on S¹/S² and
on frozen OpenCLIP models on MS-COCO back this up.

## Contribution

(i) Large-batch value and gradient consistency from stochastic InfoNCE to
deterministic population energies; (ii) a reading of unimodal InfoNCE as a
strictly convex free energy with a Gibbs equilibrium, and of Wang–Isola
"uniformity" as entropy acting *within* the alignment basin rather than as a
global force against alignment; (iii) a "bifurcation", meaning a structural
difference between the unimodal and the symmetric multimodal objective, not a
dynamical bifurcation in a parameter: the multimodal one carries a negative
symmetric-KL coupling that can keep a modality gap open; (iv) evidence from
synthetic and CLIP experiments.

## Key results

- **Thm. 3.1 (p. 4), large-batch consistency.** As N → ∞, "L_NCE,N(θ) −
  J_τ(θ) − log(N V_κ(τ)) → 0, ∇θL_NCE,N(θ) − ∇θJ_τ(θ) → 0". J_τ(θ) =
  (1/τ)∫U_θ dq_θ − H×(q_θ, ρ̃_θ,τ), the alignment potential plus a
  cross-entropy against the model's own smoothed density.
- **Prop. 3.1 (p. 5), unique Gibbs equilibrium.** "F_τ,U is strictly convex on
  its effective domain and has the unique Gibbs-form minimizer ρ*(z) =
  (1/Z_τ) exp(−U(z)/τ)". Prop. 3.2: as τ → 0+ it concentrates on
  near-minimizers of U.
- **Uniformity reread (§1, p. 2).** "This reframes 'uniformity' (Wang & Isola,
  2020) not as a global force opposing alignment, but as entropy-driven
  spreading inside the alignment basin." And §3.2: "the entropy term in Eq. (7)
  selects the most internally dispersed near-optimal configuration. This is the
  sense in which 'uniformity' emerges."
- **Thm. 3.2 (p. 5).** For a sharp kernel, |J_τ(θ) − F_τ,Uθ(ρθ)| ≤
  2ε_kde(τ)/ρ_θ, so the parametric energy approaches the intrinsic free energy
  as τ → 0+.
- **Def. 4.3 and Prop. 4.1 (pp. 6–7), multimodal barrier.** F^Sym = ½(F_τ,U12(ρ1)
  + F_τ,U21(ρ2)) − D^Sym_KL(ρ1, ρ2). "the coordinate map ρ1 ↦ F^Sym(ρ1, ρ2) is
  concave", and its infimum is approached by placing excess mass on minimizers
  of V_1|2 = U12/τ + log ρ2. Rem. 4.5: "the multimodal functional does not admit
  Gibbs-type coordinate minimization", which yields "a winner-take-all-like
  extremal geometry".
- **Thm. 4.2 (p. 7).** τ → 0+ removes the KDE mismatch "it does not remove the
  divergence term D^Sym_KL(ρθ, ρϕ)... it remains an explicit
  separation-inducing contribution".
- **Takeaway 2 (p. 7).** "Symmetric multimodal InfoNCE is pairwise attractive
  yet population-level repulsive."
- **Experiments (§5, pp. 7–9).** Finite-N gradients approach the reference as N
  grows (Fig. 4c–d). Trained particles track the Gibbs law across τ on S²
  (Fig. 5). The marginal gap grows with misalignment σ_mis (Fig. 4b). On
  OpenCLIP, "stronger retrieval does not necessarily imply a smaller modality
  gap", and replacing captions with same-category ones "consistently degrades
  retrieval and enlarges the centroid gap" (Fig. 7).

## Assumptions

Negatives drawn i.i.d. (in-batch dependence is excluded, fn. 1). Compact Z with
constant kernel volume (Asm. 2.1). Bounded C¹ encoder Jacobians and similarity
gradient (Asm. 3.1). A sharp diagonal kernel peak for the low-τ results
(Asm. 3.2). Strictly positive continuous densities, and density floors in the
multimodal intrinsic problem (Def. 4.3, App. A.3; without them "the
variational problem becomes ill-posed"). Expressivity at the best attainable
level for parametric inheritance (Asm. E.1). The limits are taken in order:
N → ∞ at fixed τ, then τ → 0+.

## Verdict on the exchange's characterization

The LIT summary has the paper as "an alignment potential pulling positives
together and entropic dispersion spreading embeddings, with a divergence
between modalities in the cross-modal case". That is **accurate**. CLAIM-798
has A29 cite it "for geometry-dependent changes between unimodal and multimodal
regimes", which is also accurate, provided "bifurcation" is read as a
difference between two objectives and not as a transition in a control
parameter.

Three qualifications:
1. A29 §3 writes "an attraction–repulsion energy on the sphere". The paper
   explicitly declines that reading for the unimodal case: what spreads the
   embeddings is entropy inside the aligned basin, "not ... a global force
   opposing alignment". Population-level repulsion appears only between
   modalities, through the −D_KL term.
2. The "collapse, dispersion and structured regimes" are not the paper's. It
   has diffuse (high τ) and concentrated (low τ) Gibbs states. It excludes
   collapse: singular measures are set aside (App. G.1, "distributions that
   collapse onto lower-dimensional manifolds or discrete sets").
3. "Hypersphere" is the canonical case, not a restriction (Rem. 2.1). The
   gradient experiment also runs a compact Euclidean RBF regime.

The exchange's "ordering process" framing is the exchange's own. The paper is
about the geometry of the objective, not about dynamics (App. G.1: "Our theory
describes the geometry of the objective rather than the trajectory of a
specific optimization algorithm").

## Bearing on named codes

- **CLAIM-798 (weak vs strong active-matter reading): supports the weak
  reading and gives nothing toward the strong one.** The paper's population
  picture is variational and passive. Its unimodal state is a Gibbs
  equilibrium of a strictly convex free energy (Prop. 3.1), the kind of
  stationary state the claim's strong reading must get past. Its "entropic
  dispersion" is a deterministic term of the objective, coming from the
  softmax denominator: the cross-entropy against the model's own smoothed
  density. It is not stochastic forcing, and τ is the softmax temperature,
  not a noise level. So the dispersion is not evidence of noise-driven or
  active motion. The multimodal case is a single scalar energy F^Sym over
  both modalities, so the coupling is reciprocal. It is gradient-like at the
  joint level, and its novelty (coordinate concavity, barriers,
  winner-take-all extremals) is about the shape of the landscape, not about
  currents. The paper leaves dynamics open (App. G.1, "how stochastic
  optimization explores the resulting barrier-shaped landscape ... remains an
  important open problem"). Note: the two modalities' alternating best
  responses against each other's barrier are a place where
  non-gradient-looking behaviour could in principle arise. The paper does not
  study it, and since a joint energy exists, CLAIM-818's Lyapunov argument
  still covers joint gradient flow.
- **QUESTION-515: background; leaves open.** It gives the "forces" half of
  A29 §2's particle picture an explicit population form for contrastive
  objectives: the alignment field U and the partition field Γ, which is a
  mean field through the representation's own density. It says nothing on
  activity or criticality. For sense 2 it points to where irreversibility
  would *not* come from, namely the objective; App. G.1 names finite-batch
  and SGD noise as uncharacterized.
- **CLAIM-818: consistent (parallel).** It reaches the same structural fact
  at the population level: a single energy functional with an equilibrium
  (unimodal) or a joint energy (multimodal). Nonequilibrium behaviour would
  have to come from the optimizer or the noise, as CLAIM-818 says.
- **THEORY-2033 (Wang–Isola alignment and uniformity): qualifies.** The paper
  proves a closed-form large-batch limit at finite τ, beyond the asymptotic
  decomposition, and rereads uniformity as entropy within the alignment
  basin. That reconciles Wang–Isola's finite-dataset tension: alignment picks
  the basin and entropy spreads mass inside it.
- **THEORY-305 (InfoNCE as a Laplacian form plus log normalizers): parallel.**
  Both rewrite InfoNCE's log-normalizer as a functional of the representation
  law: a kernel-smoothed density / cross-entropy here, a finite-n log-sum
  there.
- **THEORY-2012 (VICReg variance hinge): contrast.** There a collapse
  preventer is added by hand. Here the entropy term implicit in the InfoNCE
  denominator plays that role.

## Limitations

The theory is for the objective, not for training trajectories. The
large-batch limit is at fixed τ, followed by a low-τ limit, while practice
uses finite batches and moderate τ ("may soften the barrier-like geometry",
G.1). Negatives are i.i.d. The multimodal results rest on density floors and
on coordinate-wise (one encoder fixed) inheritance, not on a joint minimizer
analysis. The CLIP evidence is observational, plus one caption-corruption
intervention. The modality-gap mechanism is "a natural geometric mechanism",
not shown to be the only one (cf. Yi et al. 2025 on cone constraints, cited
§6).
