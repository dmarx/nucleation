# NOTE-tmpwwoeb: Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being

## Contribution

A compact review, not a new result. It puts SDT's two founding mini-theories
in one place. Cognitive evaluation theory (CET) accounts for what raises and
lowers intrinsic motivation. Organismic integration theory (OIT) accounts
for how extrinsic regulations are internalized and how far. The paper then
adds the step that unifies them: both sets of findings are read as effects
of satisfying or thwarting three basic needs, and need satisfaction is tied
directly to well-being, including within-person, day-to-day.

## Key insight

The question about a motive is not how strong it is but whose it is. Two
students can both do their homework, one "because they personally grasp its
value for their chosen career", the other "only because they are adhering to
their parents' control" (p. 71). Both are extrinsically motivated and both
are intentional, but they differ in relative autonomy, and the difference
predicts persistence, quality of performance and well-being. Autonomy for a
non-interesting act is achieved by internalization, and how far
internalization goes depends on whether the context supports autonomy.

## Assumptions

- **An organismic metatheory.** People have "evolved inner resources" and
  "inherent growth tendencies" (p. 68). The theory does not explain why
  intrinsic motivation exists ("an evolved propensity", p. 70), only what
  sustains or diminishes it.
- **Intrinsic motivation applies only to activities that hold intrinsic
  interest.** "For activities that do not hold such appeal, the principles of
  CET do not apply" (p. 71). Cameron, Banko and Pierce's 2001 reply
  ([LIT-tmpyhay6](../literature.d/LIT-tmpyhay6.md), unread) reports, by its abstract, that rewards enhance free
  choice on low-interest tasks, so the scope condition matters to the
  dispute.
- **Self-reported reasons measure regulation.** The continuum's evidence is
  Ryan and Connell's (1989) self-report reasons, whose subscales correlate in
  a "quasi-simplex pattern" (p. 73).
- **Universality.** The three needs are "innate, essential, and universal"
  (p. 74). Their modes of satisfaction are not.

## Key results

The paper reports others' results. Its main claims, with its pages:

- **CET (pp. 70–71).** Competence-supporting events (optimal challenge,
  positive feedback) enhance intrinsic motivation only with "a sense of
  autonomy", an internal perceived locus of causality. Tangible rewards,
  threats, deadlines, directives, pressured evaluations and imposed goals
  undermine it. Choice and acknowledgement of feelings enhance it. The reward
  meta-analysis of Deci, Koestner and Ryan ([LIT-tmp58ot3](../literature.d/LIT-tmp58ot3.md)) is said to have
  "confirmed, in spite of claims to the contrary by Eisenberger and Cameron
  (1996), that all expected tangible rewards made contingent on task
  performance do reliably undermine intrinsic motivation" (p. 70).
- **Relatedness (p. 71).** Its role in intrinsic motivation is "distal": a
  secure base, not a proximal requirement.
- **OIT and the continuum (pp. 71–73).** Amotivation, external regulation,
  introjection, identification, integration, intrinsic motivation. External
  and introjected regulation are sometimes combined as "controlled", and
  identified, integrated and intrinsic as "autonomous". Internalization is
  not a stage sequence: a new regulation can be internalized "at any point
  along this continuum" (p. 73).
- **Outcomes of internalization (p. 73).** Introjection goes with effort but
  also anxiety and poor coping with failure. Identification goes with
  interest, enjoyment and positive coping. In health care, more internalized
  regulation goes with medication adherence, maintained weight loss, glucose
  control and attendance in addiction treatment.
- **What fosters integration (pp. 73–74).** Relatedness and competence
  support internalization. Contexts "can yield autonomous regulation only if
  they are autonomy supportive". In Deci, Eghrari, Patrick and Leone (1994),
  controlling contexts yielded internalization "only introjected".
- **Autonomy is not independence (p. 74).** Autonomy "refers ... to the
  feeling of volition that can accompany any act, whether dependent or
  independent, collectivist or individualist".
- **Needs and well-being (pp. 74–76).** Intrinsic aspirations (growth,
  relationships, community) predict well-being; extrinsic aspirations
  (wealth, fame, image) predict lower well-being, and attaining them does not
  help (T. Kasser & Ryan; replicated in Russia). Daily fluctuations in each
  need's satisfaction independently predict daily well-being (Reis et al.).
  Contexts that set needs against each other, "as when a child is required by
  parents to give up autonomy in order to feel loved", set up "alienation and
  psychopathology" (p. 75).

## Concepts

- **intrinsic motivation**: "the inherent tendency to seek out novelty and
  challenges, to extend and exercise one's capacities, to explore, and to
  learn" (p. 70); doing an activity "for the inherent satisfaction of the
  activity itself" (p. 71).
- **extrinsic motivation**: "the performance of an activity in order to
  attain some separable outcome" (p. 71).
- **internalization / integration**: "Internalization refers to people's
  'taking in' a value or regulation, and integration refers to the further
  transformation of that regulation into their own so that, subsequently, it
  will emanate from their sense of self" (p. 71).
- **introjected regulation**: regulation "taken in" but "not fully accepted
  as one's own"; "regulation by contingent self-esteem" (p. 72).
- **identified regulation**: "a conscious valuing of a behavioral goal or
  regulation, such that the action is accepted or owned as personally
  important" (p. 72).
- **integrated regulation**: identified regulations "fully assimilated to the
  self", i.e. "evaluated and brought into congruence with one's other values
  and needs" (p. 73).
- **amotivation**: "lacking the intention to act" (p. 72).
- **perceived locus of causality**: deCharms's internal versus external
  attribution of the cause of one's action (pp. 70, 72).
- **basic need**: an energizing state whose satisfaction conduces to health
  and whose frustration contributes to pathology (p. 74).

## Connections

- **Deci & Ryan (2000), [LIT-tmpc4275](../literature.d/LIT-tmpc4275.md).** The long version of the same theory,
  published the same year, with the definition of needs argued against Hull
  and Murray, the self, need thwarting and evolution.
- **Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999), [LIT-tmp58ot3](../literature.d/LIT-tmp58ot3.md).** The meta-analysis this
  paper cites as settling the reward question. The dispute it settles is
  with Eisenberger and Cameron (1996), the reworked form of Cameron and
  Pierce (1994), [LIT-tmp3x51d](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3x51d.md).
- **Bandura (1977), [LIT-545](../literature.d/LIT-545.md), unread.** The paper names self-efficacy twice:
  autonomous motivation predicts better outcomes even at equal self-efficacy
  (p. 69), and not feeling competent is one source of amotivation (p. 72,
  citing Bandura 1986). Self-efficacy is SDT's perceived competence, and is
  treated as necessary, not sufficient.
- **The record's Frankfurt holdings ([LIT-293](../literature.d/LIT-293.md), [NOTE-264](NOTE-264.md); [THEORY-029](../theory.d/THEORY-029.md),
  [THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md)).** No philosopher is cited here, but the continuum answers a
  question they ask. Frankfurt distinguishes desires a person identifies
  with from those that are "ours in a gross literal sense" but externalized
  ([NOTE-264](NOTE-264.md), §§5–6). SDT's introjection is regulation "within the person"
  that has not become part of "the self", and it is placed by degree on a
  continuum, not by the order of an endorsing attitude. Ryan and Deci draw
  the comparison themselves in 2006 ([LIT-tmpictnr](../literature.d/LIT-tmpictnr.md)).

## Bearing on the record

- **The empirical counterpart of [THEORY-029](../theory.d/THEORY-029.md).** That account says no
  higher-order attitude makes a motive one's own by its order alone. SDT
  places ownership in integration, the congruence of a regulation "with one's
  other values and needs" (p. 73), and makes it graded. It says nothing about
  order. It does not escape Velleman's dilemma either: "the self" with which
  a regulation is integrated is specified, in this paper, only through the
  integrating process. The 2000 target article ([LIT-tmpc4275](../literature.d/LIT-tmpc4275.md)) supplies the
  organismic account of the self that would have to carry that weight.
- **A historical condition for [THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md).** SDT makes the social history of
  internalization matter: the same behaviour, internalized under control, is
  introjected; under autonomy support, it can be integrated (pp. 73–74). That
  is a condition on how an attitude came to be held. The paper does not
  consider manipulation, and its measures are present self-reports.
- **No ML instruction.** Nothing here belongs in the anthology.

## Limitations

- A review: every empirical claim is cited to other work, and no effect
  sizes are given except by reference.
- The continuum rests largely on self-reported reasons. The paper does not
  discuss whether people can report the source of their regulation.
- Integrated regulation is defined but rarely measured separately: "In some
  studies, identified, integrated, and intrinsic forms of regulation have been
  combined" (p. 73).
- "Universal" needs are supported here by samples from the United States,
  Russia and Korea. The cultural argument is that modes of satisfaction vary
  while the needs do not, which makes the universality claim hard to falsify
  without a measure of need satisfaction independent of well-being.

## Open questions

- Can integrated regulation be measured apart from identified regulation and
  intrinsic motivation? If not, the top of the continuum is a definition.
- Is the need for relatedness proximal or distal for intrinsic motivation?
  The paper calls it distal (p. 71) but central for internalization (p. 73).
- What evidence would show that a need is not basic? The 2020 review
  ([LIT-tmplhywo](../literature.d/LIT-tmplhywo.md)) states criteria.
