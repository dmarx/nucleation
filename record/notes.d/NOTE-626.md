# NOTE-626: Fantastically Ordered Prompts

## Contribution

Before it, prompt sensitivity had been studied for templates and for which
examples to include. This paper isolates the order of a fixed set of
examples, shows its effect is as large as a template's, establishes that
it is present across model sizes, tasks, templates and numbers of
examples, and that the good orders are idiosyncratic to a model. It then
gives a label-free procedure to pick orders.

## Key insight

The same evidence, presented in a different sequence, is a different
context for the model, and the difference is not small or systematic: a
causal LM's prediction depends on the order of its examples enough to
decide whether it performs the task at all, and which orders work is a
property of the particular model, not of the examples.

## Assumptions

- Text classification (eleven datasets, from sentiment to entailment),
  answered by the most probable label word after a template.
- k = 4 examples (1 for DBPedia, 2 for AGNews) because of GPT-2's
  1024-token window; five random example sets per task, all 24 orders
  (two sets and 12 orders for GPT-3 175B); evaluation on 256 sampled
  validation items.
- The probing set is generated at temperature 2 with n-gram blocking, up
  to 128 tokens per generation; its generated labels are discarded.
- "True few-shot": no labelled development set is used for selection.

## Key results

- **Figure 1.** SST-2 and Subj, four-shot, 24 orders, eight model sizes:
  some orders above 85% (SST-2), others near 50%; spread shrinks with size
  on SST-2, not on Subj.
- **Figure 3.** Variance remains high from 1 to 32 examples (GPT-2).
- **Figure 4.** Spearman correlations of permutation accuracy between
  sizes lie between −0.35 and 0.27 off the diagonal; 175B vs 2.7B is 0.05.
- **Figure 5.** For the six label patterns (NNPP … PPNN), correlations
  range from −0.66 to 0.77: no pattern is consistently good.
- **Figure 6.** Failing prompts mostly predict one label; calibration
  raises 2-shot GPT-2 XL accuracy but the spread of orders stays.
- **Table 2.** GlobalE and LocalE select top-4 orders by entropy; GlobalE
  averages 13% relative improvement over all orders, LocalE 9.6%, with
  lower variance, and an oracle top-4 above both. Gains are large for
  high-variance models and marginal for GPT-3 175B, except CB (+4.9).
- **Table 3.** The method helps under four SST-2 templates.
- **Table 5.** Entropy probing beats selecting orders on half of the four
  training examples held out as dev, at every GPT-2 size.

| C4 | Bad orders mostly fail by collapsing onto one label | moderate | Figure 6 error analysis |
| C5 | Entropy on a self-generated probing set identifies better orders without labels | moderate | Tables 2, 3, 5 |
| C6 | Order sensitivity is a fundamental property of in-context learning | weak | the authors' conclusion from C1–C2, not tested beyond causal GPT models and classification |

## Method

For a set of n training examples, form all n! orders (24 here). Under each
order as context, sample a continuation and parse it into probe inputs
(discarding the generated labels); pool these into a probing set. For each
order, predict labels for all probe inputs and score the order by the
entropy of the predicted label distribution (GlobalE) or the mean per-item
prediction entropy (LocalE). Keep the top four orders.

## Concepts

- **order sensitivity**: variation of accuracy across permutations of the
  same examples.
- **probing set**: an unlabelled artificial development set generated by
  the model from the candidate prompts.
- **GlobalE / LocalE**: entropy of the predicted-label histogram over the
  probing set / mean entropy of each probe's predictive distribution.

## Connections

Works in the frame of GPT-3's few-shot prompting. Against Liu et al.
(2021), who retrieve nearest-neighbour examples from a full training set
and find order barely matters, it finds the opposite in the true few-shot
setting. Zhao et al. (2021) is the calibration it tests. Xie et al.
([LIT-797](../literature.d/LIT-797.md)) reproduce a 10–40 point order effect in GINC, where it
follows from the prompt's mismatch with pretraining.

## Bearing on the record

- It establishes, as a measurement, that a model's response to a context
  is not invariant to the order of the context's parts, even when the
  parts and the task are unchanged, and that which orders work is specific
  to a model. No THEORY in the record holds this; I file none, because it
  is a single robust measurement better cited from the LIT than restated.
- The paper's method is an instruction for practice; it belongs to the
  anthology's question, not this record's (`anthology-candidate`).

## Limitations

- Classification only, small k, causal GPT-family models.
- The transfer analysis (Figures 4–5) rests on one fixed set of four SST-2
  examples.
- The text cites "Figure 5" for the comparison with split training data,
  which is Table 5.
- The method scales as n!; it is shown only for n ≤ 4 with all
  permutations.
- No account of why orders differ; the label-collapse observation
  describes the failure, not its cause.

## Open questions

- What property of an order predicts its effect for a given model? The
  paper finds no common denominator.
- Does the sensitivity survive instruction tuning and models trained with
  in-context objectives?
