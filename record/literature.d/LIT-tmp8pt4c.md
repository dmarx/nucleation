# LIT-tmp8pt4c: The Nature of Epistemic Injustice

Maitra, I. (2010). The Nature of Epistemic Injustice. *Philosophical
Books*, 51(4), 195–211. doi:10.1111/j.1468-0149.2010.00511.x.

Read from the author's draft of 14 July 2010, marked "please do not cite
without author's permission". The record cites the published version
above.

## Key takeaways

- Fricker's testimonial injustice, as Maitra reports it, requires a
  credibility deficit caused by the hearer's identity prejudice, which
  Fricker defines as resistance to counter-evidence owing to an affective
  investment (book p. 35). Epistemic bad luck with a reliable,
  non-prejudicial stereotype is excluded (book p. 42).
- Fricker's hermeneutical injustice, as reported, is a matter of
  structural identity prejudice (book p. 155): a significant area of
  social experience obscured from collective understanding by
  hermeneutical marginalization (book p. 158), which may be systematic or
  incidental. The central case is systematic.
- Maitra argues a credibility deficit wrongs a speaker only where the
  hearer owes it to her to judge her credibility correctly, as a juror or
  a near relative does, and that structural prejudice can produce
  testimonial injustice with no individual prejudice in the hearer.

## Why this is here

THEORY-317 states Fricker's two definitions from her précis alone and
waits on a reading of the book (LIT-1004), which is Unavailable. This
