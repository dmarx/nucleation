# LIT-tmps2dqw: Parts and Places: The Structures of Spatial Representation

Roberto Casati and Achille C. Varzi (1999), *The MIT Press* — DOI-10.7551/mitpress/5253.001.0001

## Key takeaways

*Filed, not read.* Known here by what it is standardly cited for.

- Mereology gives parthood and nothing else. It cannot tell two halves of one
  ball from two separate balls, because both have the same mereological
  description; connection has to be added as a primitive.
- With connection, interior, boundary and self-connectedness become definable,
  which is what makes boundary talk possible.
- Its contents beyond this, including how it treats holes, location and fiat
  boundaries, are not checked here.

## Standing in the record

Filed with Smith & Varzi (2000; [LIT-tmpxpy94](LIT-tmpxpy94.md)), beside it, as the reference theory behind the
fiat/bona fide distinction, for [QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md) and [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md). The survey of the
Mathematics of Meaning corpus pointed to both ([MOM-LIT-016](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-016.md)).

**What a reading should check.** The record's boundary criterion is dynamical:
a block whose spectral gap beats the Occam factor ([CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md)). Mereotopology's
connection is a primitive of space. Whether connection can be read off a
coupling structure, so that the two meet, is the question to bring to the
book.
