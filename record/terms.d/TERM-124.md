# TERM-124: domination, of a party within an arrangement, relative to a declared standard of answerability: an agent's or role's capacity, used or not, to set or change the terms on which the party can act, exit, contest or renegotiate, where the capacity is not answerable to the party under a standard declared with the judgment

## Definition

A judgment that a party is dominated within an arrangement has four
relata, and is well-defined only when all four are stated ([CLAIM-363](../claims.d/CLAIM-363.md)):

1. **The arrangement**, with its sense declared ([TERM-074](TERM-074.md)). Sense 1 is
   the default.
2. **The holder**: an agent or a role that has the capacity. It must be
   something that can choose, because domination is a capacity to set
   terms.
3. **The party dominated**, with standing ([TERM-083](TERM-083.md)).
4. **The standard of answerability**, declared with the judgment: what
   would make the holder's capacity answerable to the party. Examples are
   a channel of contest the party can use, a rule the holder cannot change
   alone, an outside enforcer, or a duty of answerability such as
   [CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md)'s.

The party is dominated, relative to that standard, when the holder has
the capacity to set or change the terms on which the party can act, exit,
contest or renegotiate, and that capacity is not answerable to the party
under the standard. The capacity counts whether or not it is used:
[CLAIM-545](../claims.d/CLAIM-545.md)'s "used or not", and [CLAIM-354](../claims.d/CLAIM-354.md)'s channel "it never uses".

## What it is for

The record's argument about dependence needs a word for the condition in
which one party's terms are at another's discretion. [CLAIM-690](../claims.d/CLAIM-690.md) says
that this condition, and not the magnitude of dependence, sorts a
whole-part dependence in both directions. [CASE-264](../cases.d/CASE-264.md)'s workshop B is
domination in this sense, under the standard "P can withdraw or
renegotiate". Its workshop A is not.

## What it is not

- **Not exploitation.** Exploitation ([TERM-123](TERM-123.md)) is benefit on terms
  that violate a declared standard. A holder can dominate without
  benefiting, and a beneficiary can exploit without holding a capacity
  over the party's later choices.
- **Not dependence, nor integration.** Equal dependence can be dominated
  or not ([CASE-264](../cases.d/CASE-264.md)), and a capability given up to a supplier
  ([CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md)) need not put the terms in the supplier's hands. The owner's
  review of [#488](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/488) asked that vulnerability to another's control be kept
  apart from integration. The term is stated in control and does not
  mention integration.
- **Not interference.** A holder that never interferes still dominates if
  it has the capacity. A party constrained by an arrangement that nobody
  controls is constrained, not dominated.
- **Not a verdict on the holder's obligations.** That a party is
  dominated says where control lies. Whether it grounds an obligation,
  and on whom, is [CLAIM-671](../claims.d/CLAIM-671.md)'s supposition and [QUESTION-460](../questions.d/QUESTION-460.md)'s question. The
  term names a likely bearer, the holder, and settles nothing about the
  ground.
- **Not freedom's opposite in full.** [CLAIM-545](../claims.d/CLAIM-545.md)'s freedom of will is an
  agent's governance of its own governability. Domination is one way it
  fails, from outside. An agent can lack that freedom with no one
  dominating it.

## What would unsettle it

- A case the record needs to call domination in which no agent or role
  holds the capacity: an arrangement whose terms no one sets, which still
  dominates its parts. Then relatum 2 is too narrow, and [CASE-256](../cases.d/CASE-256.md)'s third
  result, constraint without a duty-bearer, would need a word of its own.
- A use in which the standard of answerability is idle, so that every
  unanswerable capacity counts whatever is declared.
- Pettit's own account ([LIT-1614](../literature.d/LIT-1614.md), Unavailable), once read, if its
  arbitrariness condition cannot be stated as a declared standard of
  answerability without loss.

## Note of 2026-11-17: control over when action becomes irreversible, and first-order contestability against answerability, from "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-723 CLAIM-737 TERM-126 — filed from the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" on 2026-11-17, Proposed -->

**Timing as a term.** A39 §6 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b, 2026-11-16) compares two institutions with identical rules and the same recognized objections, in one of which objections can suspend action before it becomes hard to reverse and in the other are heard only afterward: "the objection becomes a retrospective commentary on a foreclosed alternative." It proposes "a possible organizational characteristic of domination: **One party exercises relatively unilateral control over the temporal conditions under which another party's agency can become effective.**" "The issue is not speed itself. It's whose interests and standing determine when action becomes irreversible." The term's "terms on which the party can act, exit, contest or renegotiate" do not name timing. Read with A39 §6, when a contest can still take effect is one of those terms, and a holder who sets it unanswerably dominates in the term's sense. The owner's U27 turned this into a wrong ([CLAIM-723](../claims.d/CLAIM-723.md)).

**First-order contestability and answerability.** A71 §10 asks whether legitimate authority requires that it be contestable, and answers no in that form: a parent's authority, emergency action and self-binding commitments can rightly limit a party's power to block or revise a decision. "Instead, I would distinguish **first-order contestability** from **second-order answerability**": the first "concerns whether an affected party can prevent or revise a particular decision"; the second "concerns whether the governing authority is constrained by justified standards concerning its purposes, jurisdiction, exercise, and continued operation." "**Legitimate authority need not be symmetrical, but asymmetrical authority demands justification appropriate to the interests and standing of those subject to it.**" The term's "standard of answerability" is the second; its examples (a channel of contest the party can use, a rule the holder cannot change alone, an outside enforcer, a duty of answerability) include first-order contest as one way of meeting it, not as a requirement. So a holder whose capacity a party cannot contest case by case is not thereby dominating, if the capacity is answerable in the second sense. That agrees with the term as written and makes explicit what its examples leave implicit. [CLAIM-737](../claims.d/CLAIM-737.md) relies on it; [TERM-126](TERM-126.md) (F4) proposes a definition of answerability itself.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-17: second-order domination and a counterfactual test, from "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
<!-- inactive-ok-file: CLAIM-732 CLAIM-724 — filed from the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" on 2026-11-17, Proposed -->

A81 §4 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse" (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b, 2026-11-17): "**Second-order domination arises when an actor possesses inadequately answerable power to impair the conditions through which another party's protected claims, decisions, or objections can become effective.**" It is this term with "terms on which the party can act, exit, contest or renegotiate" narrowed to the conditions of effective contest, and A keeps the term's "used or not": "the distribution of capabilities can matter independently of their observed exercise." A76 XVI and A90 XVI restate it as the exchange's thesis.

A adds a test for the capacity: Δ_A α_J(u_A) = α_J(u_A) − α_J(u_A^0), B's corrective capability under A's strategy against "an independently justified comparison strategy", with J "a *warranted corrective objective*, rather than merely the outcome B desires", so that "A defendant's legitimate introduction of decisive contrary evidence" does not count. A negative Δ shows impairment, "The quantity does not establish wrongdoing." The stages it can act on are filed as [CLAIM-732](../claims.d/CLAIM-732.md), the robust measure as [CLAIM-724](../claims.d/CLAIM-724.md).

The term's "declared standard of answerability" had no definition on the record. [TERM-126](TERM-126.md), filed from the same exchange, proposes one.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-18: what holds it back

Not adopted: one use departs from the definition. [CLAIM-730](../claims.d/CLAIM-730.md)'s title says "the rule dominates them without anyone actively intervening", making a rule the holder where relatum 2 requires an agent or role, which is this term's first condition under "What would unsettle it". Either [CLAIM-730](../claims.d/CLAIM-730.md) names the holder, whoever controls the slow level's revision, or the term widens relatum 2.
