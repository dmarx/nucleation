# CLAIM-tmpb47ul: In an arrangement, control over the conditions of action, the practical obligation to make it succeed and blame when it fails can come to rest with different parties, upstream, downstream and where failure becomes visible; where they diverge, the conscientiousness of those downstream both keeps the arrangement running and supplies the terms in which its failure is told as theirs, credit for success goes to the arrangement, and those upstream also set the standards by which responsibility is judged

## The claim

A17's opening (exchange at
https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b): "**An
institution can concentrate decision-making power upstream, concentrate
moral obligations downstream, and then assign blame at the point where the
consequences become visible.**" "Neither concept requires a conscious
mastermind."

Four parts:

- **Control and duty part.** A17 §1, on a supervisor formally responsible
  for 10,000 automated decisions a day: "**authority on paper is not the
  same as actionable control in practice**". A17 §7: "**Delegating
  responsibility to someone does not necessarily discharge your own
  responsibility for making their task feasible.**" A15 §6, after Tronto as
  A relays her: "**Power and felt obligation can become inversely
  distributed.**"
- **Conscience does double duty.** A17 §10: "First, it supplies extra effort
  that helps the institution function. Then, it supplies a moral language
  in which the institution can explain failure as a lapse in individual
  duty." The first half is the moral subsidy this claim rests on.
- **Prevention is invisible, failure is not.** A17 §6: "**The system's
  resilience is attributed to the system. The system's failure is
  attributed to the person who stopped compensating for it.**" Two
  organizations with the same policies can differ by the compensating
  labour of their staff, so "Credit and blame become distributed according
  to different principles."
- **Who sets the categories.** A17 §10: "**Power includes the ability to
  establish the categories through which responsibility is understood.**"
  Its third question: "**Who has the authority to define and enforce the
  standards by which the first two questions are judged?**" A15 §9 calls
  the power over "who receives credit, blame, and public recognition for
  moral outcomes" attributional power.

**A's later restatement.** A49 §11.1, a day later, puts the wrong at the
point of restriction: "A restriction can be wrongful because it prevents an
actor from fulfilling obligations the surrounding institution continues to
assign to them", with the principle "**Responsibility for an outcome should
be evaluated alongside responsibility for the institutional conditions
under which the outcome became possible, likely, or preventable.**" On
that reading responsibility is displaced onto the party least able to
correct the outcome.

**A test.** A17 §9's audit asks who controls the conditions, who must
ensure the outcome, who can refuse or escalate, who receives credit, who
absorbs blame and who can redesign the system. The claim's divergence is a
pattern of answers to those six.

## How it sits with the record

- **CLAIM-687** raises the accountability owed by an arrangement's
  controllers with how indispensable it is; **CLAIM-624** names a duty to
  keep violations knowable, contestable and correctable. This says where
  blame lands when neither is met, and why: the party that sets the terms
  of evaluation is the one the evaluation spares.
- **CASE-151** (Wells Fargo, Active) has the shape in the board's own
  findings: signals of harm "held by investigators, regional leaders and
  control functions without authority to act", and leadership that "blamed
  individual employees". **CASE-261** (a stipulated hospital AI) has
  clinicians who lose discretion.
- **CLAIM-614** and **CLAIM-657** hold how harm and need stop being legible
  to an organization; the third part above is that mechanism applied to
  the record of who prevented what.
- From the same exchange: CLAIM-tmp238zy (verification prompts that become
  evidence the user owned the decision), CLAIM-tmpsk25g and CLAIM-tmp4o53g
  (blame and correction kept apart). The senses of responsibility are
  TERM-tmp8pnnr's: the claim needs accountability without culpability,
  A17 §9's "A person can be required to explain their decisions and
  participate in correcting a failure without necessarily being morally
  culpable for everything that happened."
- A17 §4's Uber case (Tempe, 18 March 2018), relayed from the NTSB, is not
  filed until the report is read. A's point from it: "**A moral crumple
  zone can contain someone who is partly responsible for the failure.**"

## What it does not say

A17 §8 names three dangers, which the claim keeps:

- Not that every frontline error is systemic: "The existence of upstream
  authority doesn't itself establish a moral crumple zone."
- Not that every unequal division of duty is unjust: "Unequal duties are
  not automatically unjust duties."
- Not that structure dissolves individual accountability: "having an
  explanation does not mean lacking responsibility."
