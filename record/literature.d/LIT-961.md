# LIT-961: A Reply to Makelov et al. (2023)'s "Interpretability Illusion" Arguments

Zhengxuan Wu, Atticus Geiger, Jing Huang, Aryaman Arora, Thomas Icard, Christopher Potts, Noah D. Goodman (2024), *arXiv:2401.12631*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-809](../notes.d/NOTE-809.md).*

- **The illusion restated (§2, Eq. 7–8).** The effect of a patch splits
  into a null-space term and a rowspace term, and the "illusion effect" is
  the difference between patching v and patching its rowspace part.
- **The toy counterexample (§3.2) does not hold as the test is
  specified.** The reply finds that h₃, Makelov et al.'s faithful
  direction, keeps only 20% of its effect when patched along the
  unnormalized rowspace vector. Patching the subspace that vector spans,
  which is Makelov et al.'s test and, by the reply's footnote 1, their
  code, gives the full effect. The toy cannot discriminate either way,
  because its readout is one-dimensional.
- **The general point (§3.3) stands.** A faithful direction need not be
  orthogonal to the null space of downstream weights, because the
  data-induced submanifold generally is not.
- **Multiple abstractions (App. A).** A rotation makes (h₁ + h₂)/√2 carry
  the input as h₃ did. Under the interventions performed the two
  alignments tie; aligning further variables separates them.
- **Metrics and set-up (§4).** A 0.505 → 0.5025 change gives about 50%
  FLDD with no behavioural change. Low IIA (4.2%) is read as no relevant
  structure. The factual-recall DAS was trained and tested on single fact
  pairs.
- **Their GPT-2 results (§5).** Name position is written by the layer-8
  attention block and spread over heads (no single head above 0% IIA;
  heads 6 and 10 matter most). The IO name appears at layer 9, with head
  9.9 most important but about 11 heads needed.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 for [QUESTION-030](../questions.d/QUESTION-030.md), and read on 2026-10-14 after
Makelov et al. ([LIT-968](LIT-968.md)), in [NOTE-809](../notes.d/NOTE-809.md). The registration said the
dispute "turns on whether a subspace interchange intervention finds the
variable that constitutes a behaviour or one that only correlates with
it". It does not. Both sides agree on the facts at MLP8 and in the
residual stream, and neither speaks of constitution or correlation. They
disagree over whether an effect running through components the next layer
cannot read discredits a found subspace, over the metric, and over how DAS
should be trained. What bears on [QUESTION-030](../questions.d/QUESTION-030.md) is the point both papers
grant, filed as [THEORY-243](../theory.d/THEORY-243.md): interventions on a fixed set of variables
do not by themselves pick the mediating variable from alignments related
by a change of basis. That is the network-level form of the concession
the question records, that causal tests do not settle constitution
without a theory of the act. It supports the premise of [CLAIM-150](../claims.d/CLAIM-150.md).

The reply does not undercut [THEORY-282](../theory.d/THEORY-282.md), filed from [LIT-968](LIT-968.md): it
contests the label and the toy, not the dependence of the MLP8 effect on
the null space. Its advice on training and evaluating DAS is for the
anthology. An anthology topic on interpretability could hold it, so it
keeps the `anthology-candidate` flag, and it is held here for its own
question under [ADR-013](../decisions.d/ADR-013.md).
