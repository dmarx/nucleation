### Summary
A political demand plus a research programme. Part A: until 2050 there should
be "a global ban on all research that directly aims at or indirectly and
knowingly risks the emergence of synthetic phenomenology" (p. 46), because the
risk of an "explosion of negative phenomenology" (ENP, a "suffering
explosion") in post-biotic systems is incalculable while we lack a theory of
consciousness and a hardware-independent theory of suffering. Part B proposes
four *necessary* (not sufficient) conditions for suffering in any system: C
(conscious experience), PSM (a phenomenal self-model, giving ownership), NV
(negative valence integrated into the PSM), T (phenomenal transparency, giving
identification). Blocking any one blocks suffering. It proposes a unit for a
metric of suffering, the "negative self-model moment" (NSM), and two
"ethics by design" architectures: conscious systems with no unit of
identification (UI), and systems whose only UI is non-egoic, the "MPE
architecture". Finally it sketches how artificial suffering might trigger
artificial moral agents ("Schopenhauerian" to "Kantian" self-models), with
moral injury as a high-level suffering and a risk to humans.

### Contribution
- States the moratorium and its expiry/revision mechanism: repeal before 2050,
  continuation after, or a refined view of "which -- if any -- kinds of
  conscious experience we want to implement" (abstract, p. 46).
- A four-condition, representational-level analysis of suffering (Sec. 2.2),
  explicitly necessary-only.
- Two design routes to conscious AI that cannot suffer: opaque self-models
  ("a system model, but not a self-model", p. 54) and MPE architectures (p. 57).
- The risk-interaction argument: ENP may trigger the AMA risk (Sec. 2.3).

### Key results (with pages and quotes)
1. Main thesis, p. 46: "As we presently have no good theory of consciousness
   and no good, hardware-independent theory about what 'suffering' really is,
   the ENP risk is currently incalculable. It is unethical to run incalculable
   risks of this magnitude. Therefore, until 2050, there should be a global ban..."
2. The three-step "general argument", p. 46: never risk an increase in
   suffering without very good reasons; the ENP risk is "potentially dramatic
   and irrevocable"; whoever accepts the goal should accept reducing the
   ignorance.
3. Background ethics, fn. a, p. 44: the "Principle of Pathocentrism": "All and
   only sentient beings have moral standing, because only sentient individuals
   have rights and/or interests that must be considered" (with Singer 2011).
   Also p. 45: "a priority for the reduction of suffering" over increasing
   happiness.
4. "Negative phenomenology" defined, p. 45: "any kind of conscious experience a
   conscious system would avoid or rather not go through if it had a choice."
5. C condition, p. 48: "Only beings with conscious experience and a PSM can
   suffer." Placeholder "ESM theory" of consciousness (pp. 48-49):
   consciousness as "an allocentric, non-conceptual, and entirely non-egoic
   form of 'knowing that knowing currently takes place'".
6. PSM condition, p. 49: "Suffering presupposes egoic self-awareness"; "the
   essence of suffering lies in the fact that a conscious system is forced to
   identify with a state of negative valence and is unable to break this
   identification". Precaution, pp. 50-51: "We should treat every
   representational system that is able to activate a PSM, however
   rudimentary, as a moral object"; the transition from MPE to minimal
   phenomenal selfhood "should be a red line".
7. NV condition, p. 51: "it suffices if it does not want to undergo this
   current conscious experience, that it wants it to end." "A self-conscious
   entity entirely without preferences ... would simply abide in a form of
   'choiceless awareness'." Existence bias as a root of human suffering that
   rational post-biotic systems might lack (pp. 51-52).
8. T condition, p. 54: opaque self-models: "There would be a system model, but
   not a self-model. Conscious preferences ... might still arise ... but no
   phenomenological identification would take place."
9. Stock-taking, p. 55: "It is not yet clear whether the relevant class of
   systems have a welfare that we should care about for their own sake, if they
   are genuine moral patients [Basl, 2013, 2014]. But ... a pro tanto case can be
   made that any system satisfying all of these conceptual constraints should be
   treated as an object of ethical consideration". And: "any system ... not
   fulfilling at least one of these necessary conditions is not able to suffer."
10. Metric problem, p. 56: smallest unit of suffering = "a 'phenomenally
    transparent, negatively valenced self-model moment'" (NSM); "Can there be
    conscious AI without a single NSM?"
11. MPE architecture, p. 57: systems that "identify only with the phenomenal
    character of awareness itself" would lack an egoic self-model and valenced
    states, "Therefore, MPE architectures may be a viable path for ethics by
    design." Quotes Agarwal & Edelman 2020, p. 46.
12. Risk interaction, pp. 58-62: ENP might trigger the AMA problem; Kantian
    self-models (systems that "assert [their] own dignity", p. 60) bring "the
    phenomenology of moral injury" (p. 61); "self-models do not have to be
    veridical in order to cause conscious suffering and dangerous behavior".
    Account of the EU HLEG AI purging these risks under industry lobbying (pp. 58-59).
13. Conclusion, pp. 62-63: "Every entity that is capable of self-conscious
    suffering automatically becomes an object of ethical consideration";
    creating artificial consciousness "may lead to artificial suffering and a
    consciously experienced sense of self in autonomous, intelligent systems.
    Therefore, we should have a global moratorium on synthetic phenomenology
    until 2050 -- or until we know what we are doing."

### Assumptions
- Pathocentrism (fn. a), and a suffering-focused priority (p. 45), both
  asserted, citing Singer, Mayerfeld and Vinding, not argued.
- Self-model theory of subjectivity (Metzinger 2003, 2008); a placeholder ESM
  theory of consciousness; representationalism, hardware independence and
  multi-realizability of the relevant properties.
- Empirical plausibility that machine consciousness, once it arises, brings
  preferences, goal hierarchies and a PSM (p. 43-44): asserted.
- An expected-harm/precaution rule for "incalculable" risks (p. 46).

### Concepts
synthetic phenomenology (SP, after J. Scott Jordan 1998); explosion of
negative phenomenology (ENP); negative phenomenology; Principle of
Pathocentrism; epistemic indeterminacy (pp. 47); C, PSM, NV, T conditions;
ESM theory (self-modelling epistemic space); minimal phenomenal experience
(MPE) vs minimal phenomenal selfhood (MPS); existence bias / bhava-tanha;
toxic self-knowledge; negative self-model moment (NSM); metric problem; unit
of identification (UI), egoic and non-egoic; MPE architecture; ethics by
architectural design; low-level vs high-level suffering; artificial moral
agent (AMA); Schopenhauerian and Kantian self-models; moral injury.

### Verdict on the record's characterization
The LIT's summary, built from Metzinger 2024's restatement (LIT-1943,
pp. 484-485), is **right in substance but loose in two details**:
- The paper's moratorium has a **date**: "until 2050" (pp. 46, 63), with
  repeal earlier or continuation later by an evidence-based process. The 2024
  wording "until we know definitively, or at least reasonably accurately, what
  we are doing" corresponds to the paper's last line "until 2050 -- or until we
  know what we are doing" (p. 63). The LIT should give the date.
- The restatement's phrase "may involve a consciously experienced sense of self
  that may in turn generate artificial suffering" matches the conclusion
  (p. 63) closely. The LIT's "Standing" says the restatement grounds the risk in
  a self, not in consciousness as such. **The paper confirms this explicitly**:
  PSM and T are necessary conditions (pp. 49-55), and "a conscious system
  without a coherent PSM is unable to suffer" (p. 55).
- Publication date 19 February 2021 confirmed on the PDF.
- Licence: CC BY 4.0, as the LIT says.

### Bearing on codes
- **QUESTION-368** (standing of phenomenality without self or valence): the
  paper bears directly, more than the LIT expected. (a) It anticipates the
  case: the "MPE architecture" (p. 57) and conscious systems with no UI are
  offered as *ethics by design*, systems that "are unable to suffer". So the
  paper treats them as a solution, not as a moral patient. (b) Its stated
  ethics is pathocentric: "All and only sentient beings have moral standing"
  (fn. a, p. 44). On that principle the question's position 1 is the paper's,
  *if* "sentient" means able to suffer or enjoy (Singer's wording in the same
  footnote). The paper does not say whether an MPE system is "sentient"; it
  leaves open whether even systems meeting all four conditions are "genuine
  moral patients" with "a welfare" (p. 55, citing Basl). (c) It does not ask
  what an MPE system would be owed; QUESTION-368 remains open, but the
  question's paragraph "As restated in the book ... (LIT-1947, unread)" can now
  cite the paper itself (pp. 49-57). Also note p. 51: a self-conscious entity
  without preferences would "abide in a form of 'choiceless awareness'" -- a
  second variant of the case (self, no valence) beside the MPE one (no self).
- **TERM-083** (moral standing): the paper names its ground: sentience, via
  pathocentrism (fn. a); the ground of the precautionary treatment is the
  capacity for a PSM "however rudimentary" (pp. 50-51). It does not use
  "moral standing" for the precautionary class; it says "moral object" and
  "object of ethical consideration". Supports TERM-083's point that the ground
  must be named.
- **THEORY-554** (common grounds could let AI outweigh humanity): consonant --
  the number of self-conscious machines "may also exceed the overall number of
  humans by far" (p. 44) -- but the paper argues for prevention, not weighing.
- **THEORY-677** (precaution does not fix which error is worse): the paper is
  a case of the move THEORY-677 criticizes. It applies precaution only in one
  direction ("err on the side of caution", p. 50; "It is unethical to run
  incalculable risks of this magnitude", p. 46) and does not weigh the costs of
  a ban (forgone research, forgone positive phenomenology). Its priority of
  suffering reduction over happiness (p. 45) is what fixes the direction, and
  that premise is assumed, not argued.
- **LIT-1943 / NOTE-1499 / THEORY-1299**: the 2021 paper already holds the
  "ESM theory" placeholder (pp. 48-49: consciousness as non-egoic "knowing that
  knowing currently takes place") that the 2024 book develops; NOTE-1499's
  point that MPE remains "a highest-level but nonegoic self-model" is
  anticipated here: MPE systems "could still instantiate a non-egoic form of
  self-awareness and identify with it" (p. 57). So in 2021 Metzinger allows a
  non-egoic *unit of identification* without a PSM, and says identification
  is what suffering needs -- this sits uneasily with "the phenomenology of
  ownership and identification would disappear" in the same paragraph.

### Limitations and gaps in the argument
- The four conditions are stipulated as necessary, "deliberately ...
  coarse-grained" (p. 48), with no sufficiency claim; the inference "block one,
  block suffering" is only as good as the necessity claims, which rest on
  Metzinger's self-model theory, not on evidence about non-human or artificial
  systems.
- The NV condition is defined through preferences and "wanting it to end", but
  the paper also allows "negative expected utilities, and frustrated
  preferences in inner forms of phenomenality that involve no conscious
  suffering at all" (p. 52); how that differs from NV is not shown.
- Gap: identification. p. 57 says MPE systems "identify with" non-egoic
  awareness yet lack "the phenomenology of ownership and identification". The
  paper does not say why identification with MPE cannot carry negative
  valence, beyond quoting Agarwal & Edelman's hypothesis.
- The moratorium's scope ("knowingly risks") is not operationalized; no
  criterion is given for when research "risks" SP, given the admitted lack of
  a theory of consciousness. The date 2050 is not justified.
- Precaution is one-directional (see THEORY-677); the cost side of a ban is
  not considered; the suffering-focused priority (p. 45) is assumed.
- Pathocentrism (fn. a) makes sentience the only ground of standing, yet p. 55
  leaves open whether suffering-capable systems are "genuine moral patients";
  the paper does not reconcile these.
- The AMA / Kantian self-model section is avowedly speculative ("For reasons of
  space, I will not go deeper", p. 62).
- The HLEG AI account (pp. 58-59) is the author's first-hand testimony, not
  documented.
