# LIT-tmp71qsd: Powerlessness and Social Interpretation

Fricker, M. (2006). "Powerlessness and Social Interpretation". *Episteme*
3(1–2), 96–108. DOI 10.3366/epi.2006.3.1-2.96. Author-posted manuscript.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpgy7n9](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpgy7n9.md).*

- **Not every hermeneutical disadvantage is an injustice.** Carmita
  Wood's harasser shares her lack of the concept "sexual harassment", but
  it costs him nothing. Someone with a not-yet-diagnosed condition is
  disadvantaged by a collective gap, and that is "circumstantial epistemic
  bad luck". What makes Wood's case an injustice is the unequal
  hermeneutical participation behind the gap.
- **Hermeneutical marginalisation** is unequal participation in the
  practices that make social meanings, for "some significant area(s) of
  social experience". It is "always socially coerced" and "always a form
  of powerlessness, whether structural or one-off". A hermit who opts out
  is not marginalised.
- **The generic definition.** Hermeneutical injustice is "the injustice of
  having some significant area of one's social experience obscured from
  collective understanding owing to hermeneutical marginalization".
- **Systematic and incidental.** The systematic case, with persistent and
  wide-ranging marginalisation and a "structural prejudice in the
  collective hermeneutical resource", is central. The incidental case
  involves marginalisation "only fleetingly and/or in respect of a highly
  localized patch". Joe, in *Enduring Love*, is a white, educated,
  straight man whose stalking has to be "poured into the available
  bureaucratic mould" of a police flow chart.
- **No culprit.** "Whether the injustice is of the systematic or the
  incidental kind, it involves no perpetrator."
- **The harms.** The primary harm is "situated hermeneutical inequality",
  like a gap in free dental care that harms only those who cannot pay.
  Secondary harms include practical losses, lost epistemic confidence and
  so lost knowledge, and the constitutive construction of a social
  identity.
