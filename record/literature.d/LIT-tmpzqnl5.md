# LIT-tmpzqnl5: Blunting concepts: The double-edged effect of popularizing psychotherapy language

Manuel Almagro, Carme Isern-Mas (2025), *Philosophical Psychology* — DOI-10.1080/09515089.2025.2573763

## Key takeaways

- The spread of mental-health vocabulary into everyday talk comes from two distinct linguistic practices. "Therapy-speak", which reframes interpersonal or moral situations as psychological needs, blunts the concepts that people with mental health conditions need to describe their experience, and so produces hermeneutical injustice.

*Seeded from the abstract alone, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Mental-health language is increasingly common in ordinary conversation. This creates a tension: it can improve mental-health literacy and give people interpretive resources for their experiences, but it can also distort or trivialise those same resources. Using a practice-focused approach to meaning, the authors separate two practices. "Mental health-speak" aims at awareness and at resisting hermeneutical injustice. "Therapy-speak" recasts interpersonal or morally complex situations in psychological terms, often without real concern for mental health. They argue that therapy-speak blunts key mental-health concepts and so deprives people with mental-health conditions of the tools they need to express their experiences accurately.
