# QUESTION-tmpzdn8b: When an organization allocates authority over related propositions to different processes by endorsement, what keeps its collective judgments consistent, and must that be a separate integration step as in judgment aggregation?

## Why it is a question

A4 §4: "The equation allocates authority through local, independent
endorsement. But it does not guarantee that the resulting decisions satisfy
global coherence constraints. If different agents receive authority over
semantically related propositions, their outputs can be mutually
inconsistent." And: "Authority aggregation ≠ Judgment aggregation ≠
Collective rationality." Its proposal: "let endorsement allocate *procedural
jurisdiction*, while a separate integration operator enforces constraints
among decisions. For instance, authority over premises may be allocated
differently from authority over conclusions."

The record reads List and Pettit ([THEORY-127](../theory.d/THEORY-127.md), [LIT-705](../literature.d/LIT-705.md)). A group whose
judgments are aggregated proposition by proposition cannot in general be
both responsive and consistent. [CLAIM-030](../claims.d/CLAIM-030.md) says a whole's operative intention
is produced by systemic mechanisms. [CLAIM-271](../claims.d/CLAIM-271.md), filed the same day as this
exchange, says aggregating members' conclusions can yield verdicts the
collective's own reasons do not support. Those concern aggregating
judgments. This question concerns allocating jurisdiction, which is what
[CLAIM-tmpxuigm](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpxuigm.md)'s endorsement scheme does. List and Pettit's own escape,
having different members settle different premises and deriving the
conclusions, is a case of allocating jurisdiction. It is not held here as a
reading.

An answer says whether jurisdiction allocated by endorsement can keep
judgments over a connected agenda consistent, and what does the integrating
if not the allocation itself. If a separate integrator is needed, it is the
residue [QUESTION-054](QUESTION-054.md) asks about.
