# QUESTION-tmpihjeu: When what a learner retains is identifiable but not localized, or localized but not identifiable, which of the two, if either, fixes the boundary of the learner and its individuation by its learning history?

## Why it is a question

TERM-131 version 2 makes learning relative to a declared system and
horizon, so that in-context learning counts as learning of the model
running in an episode. That move treats retention in the context and
retention in the weights alike. They are not alike in two respects, which
the owner separated.

Identifiability: whether one can establish, from observation and
intervention, that a particular retained change exists and what it
contributes, up to stated ambiguities (CLAIM-135's second achievement).
Localization: whether the change sits in a place one can point to, such as
a component, an address or a token position.

Learning in a context is both: the demonstration has positions, and
deleting it is the intervention that identifies its effect. Learning in
the weights is identifiable, estimated by retraining or attribution,
without being localized. A located circuit can fail to be identified,
since it may be one of several sufficient ones. CLAIM-tmpvnjh2 states the
four combinations with the record's instances.

The two bear on individuation differently. What is localized is separable:
it can be detached, copied, or put on either side of a boundary (the
context, the record, a notebook). What is identifiable is attributable: it
can be credited to an experience, which individuation by history needs. A
learner's learning can be attributable without being separable (its
weights), or separable without being uniquely attributable (a copied
context, a redundant circuit). QUESTION-035 asks where a combined form's
boundary lies; this asks which of the two properties the boundary of a
learner follows, and what happens to individuation by learning history
when they diverge.

## What would count as an answer

A criterion stated for a declared kind of system that says, for each of
the four combinations, whether the retained change belongs to the learner,
and that survives the cases: an in-context demonstration copied into a
second model; weight learning identified only by attribution; a redundant
located circuit; and competent behaviour with no fixed internal model
(CLAIM-519). Or an argument that neither property fixes the boundary, with
the property that does. An answer may be relative to a declared interface,
since what is localized depends on the tools available (located
subnetworks and lesions).
