## 15. The paper's joins, its gaps, and conclusion

### 15.1 What this paper adds: its joins

Each section marks where the paper joins entries that the record keeps apart. The record does not hold these joins. They are this paper's, and they are collected here so that a reader can see at once how much of the argument is the paper's own. None of them has been put to the record's admission test (ADR-075, step 5) yet. Some will turn out to be already stated in an account's "Not stated on the record" section, and some will fail as recaps. Under ADR-131 a failed join stays in the paper as the paper's own; it does not enter the record as a claim.

Thirty-two joins over fifteen sections. Three kinds recur:

- **carrying** a result from the case the record proves it in to industrial ecosystems: JB2, JC4, JD2, JD6, JD7;
- **combining** two entries that answer different parts of one question: JA2, JB3, JB7, JB8, JB10, JC1, JC3, JD5;
- **locating** a phenomenon the record names but does not place: JB5, JC7, JC8, JC9.

The carried joins are the weakest. Each assumes that what holds for a self, a sensor network or a coupled meaning–influence model holds for firms, regulators and infrastructure. The record holds no industrial case on which to check that (§14).

### 15.2 What the argument needs and the record lacks: its gaps

The drafting found twenty-nine places where the argument needs a step the record does not hold. They are not settled in the text. Each is marked where it arises, by a label beginning with G, and each is to be filed in the record as a Proposed entry or listed for the owner (ADR-tmpbdfmk, invariant 7). The README lists them with what would fill each. The ones the paper's central claims lean on are these:

- **Is the separating case of §8 an artefact of the closed loop?** (GA2.) The construction separates reorganization readiness from adaptive viability only for the kernel under the policy the regime actually follows. If the admissible evolutions include a drill or a change of budget rule, the Regime I state is viable. What the paper calls a loss of adaptive viability is then a loss under one policy, and the difference between the two kernels is what a change of governance can save.
- **The tangent and transverse argument's conditions** (GB1). That strong stabilization of some modes can enable extensive adaptation in others needs the tangential directions to be actuated and the blocks to be decoupled. Neither is stated.
- **The trade between a filter's speed and its noise** (GB4). For a first-order filter, the time to meet a step demand within δ times the variance it passes from white noise is σ² ln(1/δ)/2, whatever the filter's rate. This ties §4's filtering to §6's readiness. The derivation is the paper's, and is checked; the record does not hold it.
- **The study itself** (GC1). §9 cites a script, not an entry. Its results bear on CLAIM-tmpg8yl2, CLAIM-tmpf71gk, CLAIM-878, CLAIM-361, QUESTION-395 and LEAD-tmpnblsf, and belong in the record as an executed-study CASE.
- **Who legitimately sets a demand specification** (GD1), and **how a capability is identified apart from the organizations that hold it** (GD3). §2 and §12 both presuppose answers. The record holds open questions on the first (QUESTION-485, QUESTION-480) and none on the second beyond QUESTION-tmpn9t5a's note.
- **No industrial case** (GD5). Every result the paper uses was proved or measured for something other than an industrial ecosystem.

### 15.3 Conclusion

Regulation is usually described as a set of constraints on conduct within an economy whose possibilities are already given. The record supports a narrower claim than "regulation is the governance of possibility", and a firmer one.

The firmer part is formal. A constraint added to a fixed model cannot enlarge what an ecosystem can jointly achieve (CLAIM-582, CLAIM-tmpk4bou; Active, demonstrated). Demands that are jointly satisfiable can fail to be within their deadlines, and a change in the response architecture can restore them without weakening any (CLAIM-tmpdvgrf; Active, demonstrated). A filter that keeps an organization's slow variables through disturbance pays for its stability in lag, at a rate fixed by its own speed (CLAIM-tmppqdnv; Active, demonstrated). And a budget set to last period's use forecloses any transition costlier than holding, so two regimes with the same present output can differ completely in whether they can reorganize (CLAIM-577; Active, demonstrated). §9's model runs this. It shows that redesigning a transition lowers the reserve it needs without replacing it (CLAIM-tmpg8yl2; Proposed, demonstrated). It also shows that which regime is most ready depends on the deadline.

The less firm part is the integration. The thesis that identity is selective conservation, tuned to the timescales of the demands, and the thesis that adaptive governance is the maintained viability of a demand-appropriate response repertoire, are ChatGPT's, and Proposed. They answer the owner's own claim that stabilizing identity and keeping it adaptable are in inherent tension (CLAIM-tmpntzjo), on one of its two readings, which the owner has not chosen. The paper's own contribution is in its joins (§15.1). The firmest of them combine results the record holds. The weakest carry those results to industrial ecosystems, where the record has no case.

What the paper does establish is a way of asking the question. Present output does not settle what a regime can become. A regime can be judged by what it keeps reachable, in time, at a cost, and correctably: by its readiness curves, by its reserve and its redesigns, and by whether its classification of noise, its specification of demands and its protected sources of evidence can themselves be corrected. Those are separate requirements. The record holds most of them separately. Putting them together for a real regulator is the work that remains.
