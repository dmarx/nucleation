# QUESTION-tmpx8hzz: Is there a map from an empirical model's signed global sections to a quasiprobability or frame representation of the corresponding GPT fragment, and if so does it preserve positivity both ways?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-014](../theory.d/THEORY-014.md) holds three proved results, from [LIT-016](../literature.d/LIT-016.md), [LIT-003](../literature.d/LIT-003.md) and [LIT-777](../literature.d/LIT-777.md).
In each formalism a real-linear representation always exists, and
classicality is the existence of a nonnegative one. Its title states them
together. Its "What this does not say" denies that the sheaf section and the
GPT representation are the same object, and that a sheaf-noncontextual
model yields a diagram-preserving ontological model of the corresponding GPT
fragment, or the reverse. Until 2026-10-19, its promote_when asked for
exactly that bridge, which the title does not claim. At the owner's
direction, from the promotion audit's batch 010 ([ADR-047](../decisions.d/ADR-047.md)), the THEORY was
promoted on its proved halves, and the condition moved here unchanged.

It is open because [NOTE-017](../notes.d/NOTE-017.md) records that [LIT-003](../literature.d/LIT-003.md) attempts no formal
comparison with [LIT-016](../literature.d/LIT-016.md), and no other source the record holds makes one.
[QUESTION-067](QUESTION-067.md) asks the related question of which noncontextuality assumption
a test of pragmatic judgements would refute.

## What would answer it

The condition formerly on [THEORY-014](../theory.d/THEORY-014.md), unchanged: a published result mapping
an empirical model's signed global sections onto a quasiprobability or frame
representation of the corresponding GPT fragment, or proving that no such
map preserves positivity. A further example of negativity in either
formalism alone cannot settle it.
