# LIT-tmpxpy94: Fiat and Bona Fide Boundaries

Barry Smith and Achille C. Varzi (2000), *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 60(2), 401–420 — DOI-10.2307/2653492

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-tmpu3hq9](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpu3hq9.md)).

- A boundary is **bona fide** when it follows a spatial discontinuity or a
  qualitative heterogeneity of material, and **fiat** when it follows none
  and exists by demarcation: perception, language, law or a mathematical
  definition. The distinction is "absolute": building a fence along a
  border, or cutting along a fiat line, makes a new bona fide boundary; it
  does not transform the fiat one.
- Bona fide boundaries obey ordinary closure topology: a boundary belongs
  to one side, so two closed bodies cannot touch. Fiat boundaries are parts
  of what they bound; each side has its own, and the two coincide (the
  equator, the Mason–Dixon line).
- Distinct bodies are never in genuine contact, so any object made of
  separate bodies is a fiat object, possibly scattered. Fiat objects are
  still not arbitrary: real properties of the material enable and
  constrain where fiat borders can be drawn.
- Self-connectedness has to be defined by fiat connection, so the bona
  fide theory presupposes the fiat one. The formal part is a sketch, with
  no models or metatheory, and the theory is spatial and synchronic.

## Standing in the record

Filed for [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md), the counter that a gap or coupling criterion cannot
tell a boundary a system produces from one imposed on it (a cell membrane
and a beam joining two pendulums pass alike), and for [QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md), where a
combined form's boundary lies. The survey of the Mathematics of Meaning
corpus pointed to it ([MOM-LIT-016](https://github.com/dmarx/mathematics-of-meaning/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-016.md)).

**Fiat/bona fide does not map onto produced/imposed.** Bona fide asks
whether a discontinuity is there; produced asks who makes and keeps the
boundary. The membrane is bona fide and produced, the beam bona fide and
imposed, the equator fiat and imposed, and the paper has no category for a
boundary the bounded thing draws for itself. It sorts the membrane and the
beam together, as the gap criterion does, which is indirect support for
[CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md)'s point and no answer to it. The paper's "imposition" of
boundaries is an observer's demarcation, not [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md)'s externally
maintained constraint.

**The coupled pair's boundary is fiat on the paper's terms**, and not only
because a target is chosen: separate bodies are never in bona fide contact,
so the pendulums with their beam, and the dancing couple ([CASE-046](../cases.d/CASE-046.md)), are
fiat objects. The distinction therefore classifies every answer to
[QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md) as fiat and cannot choose among them. What it offers the
record is the vocabulary of a fiat boundary that real coupling strengths
enable and constrain, which fits a target-relative block ([CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md)).

**Where the resemblance fails.** The theory is spatial and has no time or
process; its heterogeneity is of material, not of interaction strength.
Reading a near-decomposability gap as bona fide would extend the paper.
Its dependence of a boundary on a host is ontological, not the causal
maintenance at issue in self-production (Weber & Varela, [LIT-tmpbvcwm](LIT-tmpbvcwm.md),
filed beside this).

Varzi's SEP entry on mereology ([LIT-124](LIT-124.md)) is the neighbour already held.
