# LIT-tmp0l1qi: Many, but almost one

David Lewis (1993), in *Ontology, Causality and Mind* (Cambridge University Press); reprinted in *Papers in Metaphysics and Epistemology* (Cambridge University Press, 1999), 164–182 — DOI-10.1017/CBO9780511625343.010

## Key takeaways

*Filed, not read.* Known here by what it is standardly cited for.

- **Almost one.** The many candidates differ only at the edges, so they are
  almost identical, and counting by almost-identity gives one.
- **Supervaluation.** The word is vague among the candidates; on each way of
  making it precise there is exactly one, so "there is one cat" is true on all
  of them.
- Lewis offers the two as working together, not as rivals.

## Standing in the record

Filed with Unger (1980; [LIT-tmpddovi](LIT-tmpddovi.md)), beside it, for [QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md). Lewis's first reply has
the shape of a claim the record already holds: [CLAIM-164](../claims.d/CLAIM-164.md) says that what a
spectral gap makes definite is a subspace, not a single direction. Many nearly
equal partitions, all inside one gap-isolated subspace, would be many but
almost one. A reading should check whether that is a fair use of Lewis, or
only a resemblance.
