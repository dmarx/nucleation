# THEORY-tmpf67xs: On McCutcheon and colleagues' survey, bacterial endosymbionts lose their own cell-envelope and envelope-transport genes together below about 400 kb, and begin to lose translation genes (ribosomal proteins, tRNAs, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases) below about 200 kb, so that the smallest-genome symbionts cannot be self-sufficient and their membranes, transport and protein synthesis are presumably supplied by the host, as for organelles

## Source

McCutcheon, Garber, Spencer & Warren (2024), *PLOS Biology* 22(4):
e3002577, LIT-1571, read in full in NOTE-tmpx2qkq from the PubMed
Central copy. Figure 2 (the gene matrix) and its caption; the sections
on envelopes, transport and translation.

## What was actually shown

- **A gene matrix (Figure 2).** It covers representative bacteria under
  1 Mb: all such genomes, but only the largest and smallest per genus,
  some in draft form.
  - Genes for fatty acids, phospholipids, cell wall, the BAM complex, the
    sec translocon and ATP synthase are "generally lost in concert".
  - Below about 400 kb they are "almost universally" gone.
  - Below about 200 kb, many ribosomal-protein, tRNA and aaRS genes are
    also gone. One cicada endosymbiont lacks tRNAs for eight amino acids
    whose codons it still uses, and up to 20 ribosomal proteins can be
    missing.
- **Composition (Figure 3).** Among 98,966 proteomes, tipping-point
  endosymbionts sit outside the main cloud, beyond the extremophiles.
  This is "preliminary".
- **Indirect evidence of host supply.**
  - Electron micrographs show three membranes, one host-derived.
  - Host amino-acid transporters are enriched at the symbiont vacuole.
  - A host-encoded protein has been seen in an aphid endosymbiont, and
    another in a mealybug endosymbiont (one each).

What could have failed: the losses could have been gradual, with no
threshold, or scattered across functions, not concerted.

## What this does not say

- **Not where the thresholds are, precisely.** The 200 kb cutoff is
  "somewhat arbitrary", and no statistical method locates either
  boundary.
- **Not how the host supplies anything.** Membrane contact sites, imported
  tRNAs, imported aaRSs and host ribosomal proteins are all hypotheses.
  The paper names four mysteries and solves none.
- **Not that gene transfer to the host is how control arises.** No
  bacterial tRNA gene moved to a nucleus has been seen imported, and no
  new aaRS transfer has been found. Supply, if it happens, may draw on the
  host's existing machinery.
- **Not that the symbionts are organelles.** The paper calls them
  "organelle-adjacent". Large-scale import is established only in
  protist endosymbionts above the tipping point.

## Connections

- **QUESTION-277, regulatory control (C) and gene distribution (G).**
  Here host control is forced by the symbiont's losses, not gained by
  gene transfer. Losses that move G past a threshold make C necessary.
  So the two dimensions are coupled, and in the insect cases G may come
  first.
- **CASE-174 and CASE-167.** *Paulinella* and the nitroplast are the
  pre-tipping-point systems with large-scale import. The insect symbionts
  here reach dependence on the host by a different road, with less
  evidence of import.
- **CASE-173 and CASE-171.** Aphid and mealybug endosymbionts are where
  single host proteins have been seen inside the symbiont. *Tremblaya*,
  in the mealybug case, is a tipping-point endosymbiont.
