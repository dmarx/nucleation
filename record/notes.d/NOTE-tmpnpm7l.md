# NOTE-tmpnpm7l: Zeiser — Owning Decisions

## Contribution

Separates attributability from accountability for AI decision support,
following Watson's and Shoemaker's faces of responsibility, and argues
that the attributability gap reaches descriptive as well as prescriptive
tools.

## Key results

- **Three faces** ("The Faces of Responsibility"): accountability is "the
  practice of holding agents responsible in reacting to misconduct by
  assigning blame or imposing legal sanctions"; attributability is "rooted
  in the idea that actions express an agent's character"; answerability is
  an agent's ability "to explain and justify their conduct to others".
- **They come apart** (same section): "Sometimes, for example, the right
  person to blame didn't actually make the decision, such as when a
  manager takes the blame for her employee".
- **The gap** ("The Attributability-Gap"): it occurs "when a human
  decision-maker uses a decision-tool that presupposes values that have a
  significant influence on the outcome of the decision and that they
  haven't endorsed as their own values". Of Susan, a recruiter: "Despite
  being technically made by a human the decision doesn't reflect
  characteristics of Susan's judgement." "This is not merely a problem of
  Susan over-relying on the DSS's recommendation".
- **The standard** (same section): "A decision-support tool should enable a
  human operator to make decisions in accordance with their goals and
  values (attributability), in a way that allows them to normatively
  justify and defend their decision (answerability)."
- **On meaningful human control**: Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven's
  framework "does not specifically focus on attributability, which
  emphasises the agency of decision-makers and their contribution through
  value-judgements."
- **Descriptive tools** ("Descriptive Decision-Support"): "descriptive AI
  systems also don't provide neutral, value-free information"; so
  "attributability might not be a binary property, but a gradual one."
- **Responsibility without attributability** (Conclusion): "Where we care
  primarily about having structures for blame and liability in place, we
  could still aim for responsibility without attributability, for example
  through 'blank check responsibility'". Attributability gives "a more
  robust basis for accountability".

## Assumptions

- Watson's self-disclosure view of attributability, read as
  value-judgements.
- Endorsement as what makes a value one's own.
- That decision-makers' own values should govern, not the organization's.

## Concepts

- **Attributability gap**.
- **Decision ownership**.
- **Prescriptive and descriptive** decision support.
- **Endorsement, customisation and description-only** strategies.
- **Blank check responsibility**.

## Bearing on the record

Filed as [THEORY-tmpzdtc8](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpzdtc8.md).

- **[CLAIM-022](../claims.d/CLAIM-022.md)** (explaining practical efficacy does not settle whose the
  will is) gets an instance in a new domain: a decision "technically made
  by a human" that does not express her judgement. Zeiser's remedy is an
  endorsement criterion, which he does not test against manipulated
  endorsement, so [CLAIM-022](../claims.d/CLAIM-022.md)'s point stands. Corroborates.
- **[QUESTION-068](../questions.d/QUESTION-068.md)** (would settling whose a will-organization is settle
  accountability?): for AI decision support, no. Accountability can be
  had without attributability: a manager blamed for an employee's
  decision, and blame and liability kept by "blank check responsibility",
  though attributability is "a more robust basis" for them.
- **[TERM-tmp8pnnr](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8pnnr.md)**: its attributability sense ("Whose action, judgment,
  or institutional process was it?") is broader than Zeiser's (whose
  value-judgements a decision expresses). A source for the narrower sense.
- **[CLAIM-tmpb47ul](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpb47ul.md)**: a user can be accountable for a decision not
  attributable to her, one way blame and ownership diverge; Zeiser does
  not discuss blame landing downstream. No relation written.
- **Santoni de Sio and van den Hoven ([LIT-tmpqyz50](../literature.d/LIT-tmpqyz50.md))**: Zeiser places his
  account beside theirs, as the face of responsibility theirs leaves out.

## Limitations

- Conceptual; no study of whether users' values diverge from tools'.
- The ethical weight of the gap is left beyond the paper's scope.
