# THEORY-579: If belief is individuated by functional role, an external store that is constantly consulted, directly available and automatically endorsed can carry a standing belief, so the material vehicles of some mental states extend beyond the skin; the step from coupling to parthood rests on that premise and a parity principle the authors state rather than argue

## Source

- Clark & Chalmers (1998), *The Extended Mind*, [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md), read in full in
  [NOTE-180](../notes.d/NOTE-180.md) from the authors' web version; agreement with the typeset
  *Analysis* text is unverified.
- Clark (2005), *Intrinsic content, active memory and the extended mind*,
  [LIT-403](../literature.d/LIT-403.md), read in full in [NOTE-363](../notes.d/NOTE-363.md): the first reply by either author to
  the published criticism.

## What was actually shown

**The cases.** In the three Tetris cases (§2) rotation is done mentally, by
a button on screen, or by a neural implant. The paper argues that the only
thing separating the button from the implant is the skin, which is
"precisely what is at issue". Otto has Alzheimer's disease and carries a
notebook that "plays the role usually played by a biological memory" (§4).
He believed the museum was on 53rd Street before he looked it up, as Inga
did before she recalled it. Twin Otto, a physical duplicate "from the skin
in" whose notebook says 51st Street, believes 51st Street: "a belief is
simply not in the head". Unlike the Putnam–Burge twins, the two Ottos walk
to different streets, which is the paper's answer to the worry that
externally fixed content is explanatorily idle (§3).

**What carries the conclusion.** [NOTE-180](../notes.d/NOTE-180.md) finds three moves and no argument
from causation to parthood: role-functionalism about belief ("What makes
some information count as a belief is the role it plays", §4), assumed; the
parity sentence of §2, stated with the hedge "(so we claim)"; and
explanatory simplicity with a revisionary proposal about the concept ("the
notion of belief ought to be used so that Otto qualifies", §4). The
evidence for coupling is causal: two-way interaction, and a drop in
competence when the resource is removed (§3). The note's judgement is that
the constitutive step is "stipulated given functionalism, not argued".
Clark's 2005 reply confirms it in his own words: "We did not argue for this
principle, except by providing illustrations and defences against
counter-arguments" ([NOTE-363](../notes.d/NOTE-363.md), p. 2).

**The coupling features.** Constancy (Otto "will rarely take action without
consulting it"), direct availability, automatic endorsement, and past
conscious endorsement, which the paper calls "arguable" (§5). They are
"features of our central case that make the notion so clearly
applicable", and applicability "gradually falls off" as cases lack them.
The Internet "is likely to fail on multiple counts, unless I am unusually
computer-reliant, facile with the technology, and trusting"; "certain
files on my computer may qualify".

**The defence ([LIT-403](../literature.d/LIT-403.md)).** The thesis concerns material vehicles, not
contents (n. 1). Against Adams and Aizawa's intrinsic-content objection:
the notion is unclear; silicon could carry it; and their own concession
that not every cognitive state need involve non-derived content removes
its force, since the thesis never claimed a cognizer made wholly of
notebooks. Against the active-memory objection: the notebook is conceded to
be "unlike biological memory", and the claim is weakened to Otto-plus-notebook
exhibiting "enough of the central features and dynamics" of a believer,
with the differences said to lie below the level a functionalist should
attend to.

**What could have come out otherwise.** Very little here could, because
the support is argument from imagined cases. What could have gone the
other way is the critics' search for a relevant difference. Each candidate
offered so far (intrinsic content, active updating) has been answered by a
parity case, and the active-updating difference has been conceded rather
than denied.

## What this does not say

- **That the four features are sufficient conditions.** The paper refuses
  that framing (§5). A record document that cites [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md) for conditions
  under which an external store becomes a belief is citing it for more
  than it says ([NOTE-180](../notes.d/NOTE-180.md), Corrections).
- **That consciousness extends.** The paper allows that "some mental
  states, such as experiences, may be determined internally" (§4). Its
  thesis is about standing, dispositional belief and non-conscious
  cognitive processes.
- **That coupling is shown to be constitution.** The causal-coupling
  reading, on which the notebook is only an input to an internal belief,
  is the rival the paper treats as "one step too many", not one it
  refutes. Adams and Aizawa ([LIT-438](../literature.d/LIT-438.md), [LIT-455](../literature.d/LIT-455.md)) press exactly this point.
- **That ordinary tools, or AI assistants, carry their users' beliefs.**
  The paper expects the Internet to fail for typical users. Whether an
  assistant meets automatic endorsement is a question the account frames
  and does not answer.
- **That the self extends.** The paper infers this in one paragraph (§5),
  and the moral consequence ("interfering with someone's environment" as
  interfering with their person) in one hedged sentence. This account does
  not include either.
- **That "Parity Principle" is the 1998 paper's term.** The name is from
  [LIT-403](../literature.d/LIT-403.md). [NOTE-363](../notes.d/NOTE-363.md) also finds that the 2005 paper's quotation of the
  parity sentence differs from the 1998 web text ("for that time" for "so
  we claim"), so it should not be cited as the 1998 wording.

## Connections

- **[QUESTION-035](../questions.d/QUESTION-035.md)** asks when a combined form is one system and where its
  boundary lies. This account gives one answer for the cognitive kind: the
  boundary is set by reliable functional coupling of the kind the four
  features mark, not by the skin. It constrains the question; it does not
  settle the general case, since its criterion is offered for one kind and
  read off one case. [CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md) and [CLAIM-172](../claims.d/CLAIM-172.md) offer other criteria
  (near-decomposability, mutual entrainment).
- **[CLAIM-175](../claims.d/CLAIM-175.md)** argues that a dancing couple is a combined cognitive form
  "on the extended-mind thesis's own terms". It relies on the statement of those
  terms, which is [THEORY-tmplyryk](THEORY-tmplyryk.md).
- **The critics.** Adams and Aizawa ([LIT-438](../literature.d/LIT-438.md), [LIT-455](../literature.d/LIT-455.md)) argue that coupling
  is not constitution and that cognition needs non-derived content. Rupert
  ([LIT-422](../literature.d/LIT-422.md)) argues that the functional differences Clark sets "below the
  level" do explanatory work. Chalmers's later chapters ([LIT-415](../literature.d/LIT-415.md), [LIT-421](../literature.d/LIT-421.md))
  revisit the thesis.
- **Offloading.** [THEORY-343](THEORY-343.md) and [THEORY-346](THEORY-346.md) describe when people rely on
  external stores rather than memory. They bear on whether the constancy
  and availability features are met in practice, not on whether meeting
  them makes a store part of a mind.
- **Implementation.** Chalmers's implementation conditions
  ([THEORY-609](THEORY-609.md)) are indifferent to whether a component lies inside the
  skull, so they are compatible with this account, though neither paper
  draws the link.
- **Fictionalism.** Toon's pretence account uses the same notebook image
  for the opposite purpose; Drayson's critique of it is [THEORY-646](THEORY-646.md).

## Note of 2026-10-29: the proposal split off

What Clark and Chalmers argue, as distinct from whether it is true, is now
[THEORY-tmplyryk](THEORY-tmplyryk.md), Active, so that [CLAIM-175](../claims.d/CLAIM-175.md), which argues on the thesis's
own terms, can stand on the statement alone. This entry keeps the question
whether the conditional holds, and its promote_when.

## Note of 2026-10-29: what holds it back

Kept Proposed by the sixteenth status review. Both papers are read in full,
but promote_when's result is not in the record: no account of constitution,
stated apart from the Otto case, that puts the notebook inside and an
ordinary reliably consulted tool outside by one procedure. Adams and
Aizawa's coupling–constitution objection ([LIT-438](../literature.d/LIT-438.md), [LIT-455](../literature.d/LIT-455.md)) and Rupert's
objection that the differences "below the level" do explanatory work
([LIT-422](../literature.d/LIT-422.md)) are unanswered here.
