# LIT-999: Naïve Learning in Social Networks and the Wisdom of Crowds

Benjamin Golub, Matthew O. Jackson (2010), *American Economic Journal: Microeconomics 2(1), 112–149*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-840](../notes.d/NOTE-840.md).*

- **Consensus as an eigenvector average.** For a convergent interaction
  matrix T, every agent in a closed group ends at s·p(0), s the unit left
  eigenvector of T; an agent's influence is the trust-weighted influence
  of those who listen to it.
- **Wisdom ⇔ no dominant influence (Proposition 2).** A growing society's
  limit beliefs converge in probability to the truth iff the largest
  influence weight → 0, for all independent signal distributions with
  mean μ and variance bounded below, or for none.
- **Equal reciprocal weights (Corollary 1).** Wisdom ⇔ max degree / total
  degree → 0.
- **Obstruction (Proposition 3).** A finite group that everyone attends
  to, directly or within t steps, with weight bounded below, prevents
  wisdom. Indirect position can give an agent near-total influence
  (Example 4).
- **Sufficient conditions (Theorems 1, 4).** Balance plus minimal out- or
  in-dispersion.
- **Speed is separate (§V).** Fast or slow consensus can be right or
  wrong.
- **The agents are naïve.** They ignore repetition and correlation of what
  they hear; only the first round is Bayesian.
