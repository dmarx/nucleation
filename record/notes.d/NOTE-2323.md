# NOTE-2323: Strand, Coffman and Burke — Large DNA viruses that parasitoid wasps transmit to hosts

## Contribution

A map of the range of wasp–virus relations, from mechanical carriage to
full domestication, with the claim that parasitoids likely vector many
more viruses than are known.

## Key results

- **The range** (Abstract). "Some viruses are mechanically transmitted
  to hosts, which can reduce the fitness of wasp offspring. Others have
  evolved into beneficial symbionts or reproductive parasites that
  replicate in wasps and hosts. Some large dsDNA viruses have also been
  coopted into domesticated endogenized viruses that are vertically
  transmitted to offspring but still produce virions or virus-like
  particles that wasps use to parasitize hosts."
- **DEVs** (Introduction; section 3). "DEVs are permanently integrated
  into the germline of wasps but produce virions or virus-like
  particles (VLPs) that adult females inject into hosts for functions
  that benefit offspring." Unlike most endogenous viral elements, they
  "consist of many genes that functionally interact". Five DEV
  lineages are known.
- **Bracoviruses** (6.1). "BVs evolved ∼100 million years ago from a
  nudivirus that integrated into a braconid wasp"; the microgastroid
  complex holds an estimated 50,000 species. Virions form only in ovary
  calyx cells. The viral DNA polymerase is missing, and its early loss
  "likely prevented self-replication of the BV ancestor, which may have
  been a key event in domestication"; a wasp polymerase is inferred to
  amplify the proviral segments. "Some of the virulence genes on
  proviral segments originate from wasps, while others derive from
  transposable elements or potentially the nudivirus ancestor."
  "Thus, wasps have domesticated BV virions into agents that transfer
  virulence genes from adult wasps to hosts."
- **In the host** (box on *Microplitis demolitor*). The bracovirus's
  genes suppress the caterpillar's immune defences and induce
  hyperglycemia, reduce nutrient stores and suppress ecdysteroid
  production, "preventing C. includens larvae from pupating".
- **Outlook** (section 7). "we hypothesize that parasitoids play a much
  more significant role in vectoring a diverse and largely undiscovered
  range of RNA and DNA viruses among insects than is currently known."

## Concepts

- **DEV** — domesticated endogenized virus: a virus integrated into the
  wasp germline whose many genes interact to make particles that serve
  the wasp.
- **EVE** — endogenous viral element, usually a single fragment with no
  sign of domestication, "defined as providing an evolutionary benefit
  to a eukaryote".

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-661](../claims.d/CLAIM-661.md)** cites this work for wasps "whose domesticated viral
  genes, once a separate lineage, now let them alter a third organism".
  It holds: the bracovirus descends from a nudivirus that integrated
  into a wasp about 100 million years ago, is now inherited in the
  wasp germline, cannot replicate alone, and its particles carry genes
  that change the caterpillar's immunity and development. One
  qualification bears on the claim's own distinction of origin from
  governance: the delivered virulence genes are partly of wasp origin,
  and the replication that makes the particles depends on a wasp
  polymerase. The machinery is viral in origin; what it carries and how
  it is copied are mixed. The claim's dated note says so.
- **[QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md)** (a parallel, no edge): a former separate lineage
  reduced until it cannot replicate alone, its function kept by the
  host's genome and machinery, is the integration route the endosymbiont
  cases are compared on, in a virus.
