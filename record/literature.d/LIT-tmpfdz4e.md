# LIT-tmpfdz4e: The indeterminacy of word segmentation and the nature of morphology and syntax

Martin Haspelmath (2011), *Folia Linguistica 45(1), 31–80 (2011). Reprinted in Folia Linguistica 51(s1000), 2017, as DOI 10.1515/flin-2017-1005, which is the identifier the survey gave* — DOI-10.1515/flin.2011.002

## Key takeaways

- None of the ten standard criteria for a cross-linguistic "word" (pauses, free occurrence, mobility, uninterruptibility, non-selectivity, non-coordinatability, anaphoric islandhood, nonextractability, morphophonological idiosyncrasy, deviations from biuniqueness) is necessary and sufficient, and no combination reproduces orthographic practice. So linguistics has no principled basis for a universal morphology–syntax division, and morphosyntax should be treated as one domain.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The morphology–syntax distinction is usually taken for granted, but it depends on a cross-linguistically valid notion of the morphosyntactic word. Haspelmath argues no good criteria for such a notion exist. He examines ten criteria and shows none is necessary and sufficient alone, and no combination yields a definition matching linguists' spelling conventions. "Word" can be defined per language or as a fuzzy concept, but the fuzzy version only helps if the affix–word–phrase continuum shows clustering, and there is no systematic evidence of that. So there is currently no good basis for splitting morphosyntax into morphology and syntax.
