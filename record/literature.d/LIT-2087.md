# LIT-2087: Rigid Origami Theory

Hull, T. C. (2020). Rigid Origami Theory. Chapter 14 of *Origametry: Mathematical Methods in Paper Folding*, Cambridge University Press, pp. 290–318. doi:10.1017/9781108778633.019.

Not read: the chapter is behind Cambridge Core's paywall (see the status note). What follows is the publisher's summary, quoted from the chapter page.

## Key takeaways

*From the publisher's summary only; unverified until read.*

- **Complexity**: the first section "outlines a proof that deciding whether
  or not an origami crease pattern can be rigidly folded from the unfolded
  state using some subset of the creases is NP-hard".
- **Local structure**: "the germ of single-vertex rigid origami
  configuration spaces is isomorphic to the germ of a quadratic form".
  "Examples of disconnected rigid origami configuration spaces are also
  included."
- **Self-folding**: "we wish for these actuators to fold the crease pattern
  to a target state and not to some other rigid origami state"; "we
  present the current theory behind this as well as its limitations".

## Standing in the record

The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures cites it for branching,
disconnected configuration spaces and selecting the intended branch, which
matches the summary's configuration-space and self-folding parts; A omits
the NP-hardness section, which bears directly on A's own point that a
feasible target need not be reachable. Two neighbours were read in its
place. Akitaya et al. (2020), [LIT-2071](LIT-2071.md), co-authored by Hull, is the
NP-hardness result the first section names (strongly NP-hard with optional
creases); whether the chapter outlines the same reduction is unknown.
Hull and Tachi (2018), [LIT-2033](LIT-2033.md), stands in for the self-folding
theory, with its main theorems. For the quadratic-form germ, Chen and
Santangelo (2018), [LIT-2021](LIT-2021.md), show the same fact by another route, a
single vertex's second-order configurations being the null cone of a
quadratic form with one negative eigenvalue; whether that is the chapter's
statement or proof is unknown. For disconnected configuration spaces,
Foschi, Hull and Ku ([LIT-2055](LIT-2055.md)) and Berry et al. ([LIT-2024](LIT-2024.md)) give
examples; which the chapter uses is unknown. The chapter's account of the
theory's limitations is covered by nothing read.
