# NOTE-1467: Hennick & De Baerdemacker — Almost Bayesian

## Contribution

It proposes a description of SGD's "long runtime behaviour" (abstract) in
which three quantities meet: the local learning coefficient (LLC) λ(w) of
singular learning theory, read as a mass fractal dimension of low-loss
regions (§3.2.1, App. B.1); a spectral dimension d_s, the exponent of the
volume of visited states, V_s(t) ∝ t^(d_s/2) (Definition 3.1); and a walk
dimension d_walk = 2λ/d_s, the Alexander–Orbach relation (Theorem 3.1),
which sets displacement R(t) ∝ t^(1/d_walk). From a time-fractional
Fokker–Planck equation it derives a local steady state exp(−γL_m/D_ξ)
(Lemma 3.3) and reads it as a tempered posterior (Corollary 3.2). The
experiments fit d_s from displacement and compare it with LLC estimates,
and in one toy setting compare cluster masses of SGD solutions with an
SGLD posterior.

## Key insight

Late in training SGD does not diffuse like Brownian motion. The
displacement grows more slowly, as if the weights moved through a medium
whose open volume scales with the LLC. If a region's effective diffusion
is a constant D_ξ, SGD's stationary law there is a Gibbs distribution at
temperature D_ξ, which is the posterior at sample size 1/D_ξ. On this
account SGD is "almost Bayesian": Bayesian within a region, at a
temperature set by the dynamics.

## Assumptions

- **Large batch** "so that the estimation noise ... doesn't dominate the
  dynamics" (§3.1).
- **A steady state exists.** The key lemma assumes "there exists
  steady-state solutions" (Lemma 3.3); the main text adds "(if it
  exists)". §5.1 concedes "In general, SGD iterates do not converge to
  exact equilibria" and that a non-equilibrium steady state is possible.
  Proposition D.1 shows there is none if displacement has even a small
  linear component.
- **Diffusion tensor ∝ Hessian, and nearly all Hessian eigenvalues ≈ 0**
  (App. D.2, citing Xie et al. and Sagun et al.).
- **An effective scalar diffusion coefficient**, D_ξ = ξ^(2 − d_walk), at a
  chosen length scale ξ (Lemma 3.2, Corollary 3.1), constant on the region
  considered.
- **The Alexander–Orbach relation holds locally near critical points**
  (Theorem 3.1, stated from Hambly et al. and not proved here); "in cases
  where there are no nearby saddle points (which is more common in early
  training), this relation does not need to hold" (p. 5).
- **A single spectral dimension over training** for vanilla SGD (§3.3.1).

## Key results

- **Theorem 3.1.** d_walk(t) = 2λ(w_t)/d_s near critical points.
- **Theorem 3.2 / D.2.** With isotropic noise D along degenerate
  directions, the first-passage time through a ball around w* scales as
  ε^λ(w*)/(DC), C the capacity of the escape set. The proof replaces |Ω|
  by V(ε) in a cited mean-first-passage formula for porous media; it is a
  one-line substitution.
- **Lemma 3.3.** Where D_ξ is constant on W ⊂ 𝒲 and a steady state
  exists, p_s(w) ∝ exp(−γL_m(w)/D_ξ).
- **Corollary 3.2.** With γ = 1 and log loss, p_s(w)^(mD_ξ) ∝ p(X_m|w), so
  p(w|X_m) ∝ ρ(w)p_s(w)^(mD_ξ). This is the identity
  (e^(−L_m/D_ξ))^(mD_ξ) = e^(−mL_m). Equivalently, p_s is the posterior
  with a flat prior at effective sample size 1/D_ξ.
- **Lemma 3.4 and Corollary 3.3.** d_s ≤ λ(w(t)) as t → ∞, and
  d_s ≤ the time-averaged λ. The proof argues d_walk > 2 from the loss
  being non-constant, then d_s = 2λ/d_walk < λ.
- **Table 1.** λ, d_s, α = d_s/(2λ) (the displacement exponent; I checked
  the column is this ratio) and r²: TinyStories-1M 32, 21.4, 0.33, 0.98;
  TinyLlama-15M 76.1, 48.3, 0.32, 0.98; TinyStories-33M 39.3, 38.7, 0.49,
  0.98; ResNet18 72.05, 0.57, 0.004, ≈1; ResNet34 73.5, 0.62, 0.004, ≈1;
  VGG16 159.7, 0.14, 0.001, ≈1. The vision models' α ≈ 0.004 means their
  displacement barely grows; the fit is then near-flat and r² ≈ 1 says
  little.
- **Posterior concentration (§4.2, Table 2).** 500 two-hidden-layer ReLU
  networks on 512 moons samples, SGD at rate 0.01 for 200 epochs; the
  "Bayesian posterior" is SGLD seeded at the SGD solutions with lowest loss
  and LLC (App. G.4), 1000 draws at step 10⁻⁵. After tempering with
  ξ = 0.5, chosen by the KL between empirical and theoretical SGD
  distributions (Fig. 3), the cluster masses agree: KL 0.009, Wasserstein
  0.002, JS 0.003. Untempered, SGD prefers a different cluster (C1) from
  the posterior (Fig. 4b).
- **Appendices H–J.** Ablations on MNIST: Adam has much smaller d_s than
  SGD (0.41 against 7.8 mean) and its LLC tracks displacement less well;
  CIFAR-10 convnets and resnets show early super-diffusion growing with
  model size; Table 8's 50 identical MNIST runs give d_s from −0.33 to
  15.5 at LLC 18.4–23.8.

## Claims

| id | claim | strength | support |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Late-training SGD displacement is sub-diffusive, R ∝ t^α with α < ½ | moderate (experiment) | Table 1, Figs. 1, 14–20; fits of a power law to displacement |
| C2 | α = d_s/(2λ) with d_s ≤ λ | weak | d_s is defined by fitting this relation (Eq. 17), so the fit cannot test it; Lemma 3.4's proof is heuristic |
| C3 | Within a region of constant D_ξ the steady state is a tempered posterior | strong for the algebra, conditional | Lemma 3.3, Corollary 3.2, given that a steady state exists and D_ξ is constant |
| C4 | SGD's solution distribution is the posterior "tempered" by accessibility | weak | one toy experiment; cross-region weights not derived; ξ fitted; the posterior is SGLD seeded at SGD's own solutions |
| C5 | The diffusion tensor is well approximated by a scalar for small γ/n | weak, and vacuous as stated | Lemma D.2 bounds ‖D − aI‖ absolutely; relative to ‖D‖ the error does not shrink (my check) |

## Method

Fit the LLC by the SGLD estimator of Lau et al. (devinterp, Table 3
settings), record weight displacement from initialisation every 100 steps,
regress log R(t) on log t with slope d_s/(2λ) (Eq. 17) to get d_s, and
compare d_s with λ. For the posterior test, cluster SGD solutions, sample
SGLD from the best of them, and compare cluster masses before and after
raising the SGD distribution to the power set by ξ.

## Concepts

- **Spectral dimension d_s** — the exponent of the volume of visited
  states, V_s(t) ∝ t^(d_s/2); here estimated from displacement given λ.
- **Walk dimension** — R(t) ∝ t^(1/d_walk); > 2 means sub-diffusion.
- **Accessibility constraints** — the paper's phrase (abstract) for the
  difference between where SGD's steady state puts mass and where the
  posterior does, "since SGD simply cannot reasonably reach those areas"
  (p. 7). It is not given a formula beyond D_ξ.
- **Characteristic length ξ** — the homogenisation scale at which D_ξ is
  defined (App. E); in the experiment, a fitted parameter.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-363](../questions.d/QUESTION-363.md).** The question reads this paper as "a tempered
  posterior that accounts for local accessibility constraints", the third
  candidate in its title. Two corrections.
  - **The tempering is a temperature, and it maps to sample size, not to
    training time.** Corollary 3.2 says SGD's local steady state is the
    posterior at effective sample size 1/D_ξ. That is the same kind of
    bridge as Mandt et al.'s ε/S against 1/N: a noise scale standing in for
    n. Nothing in the paper relates training time t to n; time enters only
    through displacement and the fractional derivative, and the theory is
    of "late stage" dynamics.
  - **"Accessibility" is not derived where it would matter.** Within a
    region of constant D_ξ there is no accessibility correction at all: the
    steady state there is exactly a tempered posterior. Between regions,
    the paper's own Fokker–Planck equation with a spatially varying D gives
    a zero-flux steady state ∝ exp(−∫∇L/D), which depends on D along the
    paths between regions, not on each region's own D ([THEORY-1258](../theory.d/THEORY-1258.md)).
    The relative mass of regions is therefore not given by per-region
    tempering, and in §4.2 it is matched after fitting ξ. The paper does
    not give a case of the kind the question asks for, a region dominant in
    the posterior that SGD cannot reach.
  - **Phase transitions are outside the theory.** The paper ties its
    steady state to regions where "λ(w) be locally constant ... away from
    phase transitions" (p. 7), and treats LLC jumps in grokking as needing
    a space-fractional term it does not develop (App. F). So it says
    nothing about the order of transitions over training.
- **[QUESTION-039](../questions.d/QUESTION-039.md).** The sub-diffusion results bear on how fast SGD moves
  late in training, not on what sets the order of acquisition.
- **[THEORY-209](../theory.d/THEORY-209.md).** Both describe a converged network that keeps moving.
  Kunin's model is regular and quadratic and explains super-diffusive
  drift by overdamped modes, with larger rates lowering the exponent; this
  paper reports sub-diffusion and attributes it to degeneracy. The settings differ (resumed ImageNet
  training at the original schedule there, low-rate fine-tuning here), and
  neither paper addresses the other.
- **New:** [THEORY-1258](../theory.d/THEORY-1258.md), Active, and [THEORY-1272](../theory.d/THEORY-1272.md), Proposed.
- **ML practice.** Appendix C proposes uses for transfer learning,
  schedulers and optimiser evaluation; the LIT keeps `anthology-candidate`.

## Limitations

- **The existence of a steady state is assumed**, and the authors say it
  may fail (§5.1, Prop. D.1).
- **The scalar diffusion coefficient is not justified.** Lemma D.2 shows
  ‖D − aI‖ is small in absolute terms when D is small, which any small
  matrix satisfies; relative to ‖D‖ the error does not shrink.
- **d_s is defined by fitting the relation it is meant to test** (Eq. 17).
  The check of Lemma 3.4 (Fig. 2a, Table 1) compares a fitted d_s with λ;
  d_s ≤ λ holds wherever the fitted exponent is below ½, which is what
  sub-diffusion means.
- **The posterior test is one toy problem**, with a fitted ξ, and an SGLD
  "posterior" seeded at SGD's own lowest-loss, lowest-LLC solutions and run
  at step 10⁻⁵ for 1000 draws, which is unlikely to mix between clusters.
- **Negative spectral dimensions** appear in Table 8, which the authors
  attribute to early super-diffusion and keep "for transparency".
- **The vision rows of Table 1 have α ≈ 0.001–0.004**, displacement that
  barely moves, so their r² ≈ 1 is weak evidence.
- **Theorem 3.1** is the Alexander–Orbach relation carried over by
  analogy; its conditions are not checked for loss landscapes.

## Open questions

- What does the paper's own Fokker–Planck equation give for the relative
  mass of two regions with different D, and does it match SGD's cluster
  frequencies without fitting ξ?
- Is the late-training sub-diffusion a property of degeneracy, as claimed,
  or of a small learning rate near a minimum, as Kunin et al.'s
  rate-dependent drift would suggest? A sweep crossing both regimes in one
  architecture would separate them.
