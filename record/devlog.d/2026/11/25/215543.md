A wrong theory, and how it was found out. During the THEORY retitle
(pull request 827), a heuristic (same source, 45% or more word overlap)
flagged 67 pairs of THEORYs, almost all one Active and one Proposed. The
assistant called them "twin readings", the same reading filed twice, and
proposed retiring the Proposed half of each.

Tracing where each was first added showed otherwise. The Active halves
came from ordinary readings; the Proposed halves came from status reviews
and the [ADR-047](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-047.md) sweeps, whose commit messages say what they did:
"attribution titles promoted under [ADR-065](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-065.md), their theses filed beside
them". That is [ADR-065](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-065.md) §2–3 working as written. A reported title ("X
reports…") goes Active on its reading, and the wider thesis it asserts
moves to a Proposed THEORY that `extends` it. 65 of the 67 pairs carry that
`extends`; one is a supersession; the last ([THEORY-1011](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-1011.md), Rejected, and
[THEORY-1039](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-1039.md)) is a strong claim and its narrowing to what the authors
conjecture.

Nothing is retired. Two lessons:
- Check a relation between entries before calling them duplicates: the
  `extends` edge said what they were.
- A shallow clone makes every older file look added by its oldest commit.
  The first trace blamed one commit for 118 files until the history was
  fetched in full.

What the titles cost: retitling each pair separately (pull request 827)
gave the report and its thesis labels that read as two findings. On a
graph the `extends` edge between them says which is which.
