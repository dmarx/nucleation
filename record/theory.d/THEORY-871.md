# THEORY-871: Instructed reappraisal lowers reported negative affect to aversive pictures while the responses of the amygdala, periaqueductal gray, hypothalamus and other subcortical regions to the same pictures show evidence of no change; the regions it reduces are cortical, mainly higher visual and parietal

## Source

Bo, Kraynak, Kwon, Sun, Gianaros & Wager, [LIT-1359](../literature.d/LIT-1359.md), read in full in
[NOTE-1061](../notes.d/NOTE-1061.md) from the bioRxiv preprint, version 2 (10 May 2023): pp. 7,
9–11 and 20–22, with Figures 1E, 3 and 4. The version of record is closed
and was not read.

## What was actually shown

- **Reported affect falls.** Reappraisal lowered negative ratings against
  looking (t = −17.5, d = 0.92), and "81% of individual participants showed
  regulation effects in the same direction as the group findings" (p. 7).
- **Subcortical responses do not.** Regions engaged by negative pictures
  were tested for change under reappraisal with Bayes factors. "Most
  regions responsive to emotional stimuli were non-modifiable, including
  all subcortical emotion-related regions" (p. 10): amygdala, PAG,
  parabrachial nucleus, hypothalamus, ventral striatum and pallidum. In
  anatomically defined regions, every amygdala subregion and the PAG
  responded to negative pictures (all p < 0.0001), and none was reduced by
  reappraisal (all BF < 0.1); basolateral and superficial amygdala moved the
  other way (p. 11).
- **What falls is cortical.** The eight regions reduced were "located
  exclusively in the cortex", mainly higher retinotopic visual areas, the
  intraparietal sulcus, postcentral gyrus and inferior frontal junction
  (p. 10). Their reduction, not the subcortical response, predicted how much
  reported affect fell (pp. 11–12).

What could have come out otherwise: the amygdala could have fallen with
the ratings, as the meta-analyses the paper cites lead one to expect. With
samples large enough to support a null, it did not.

## What this does not say

- **That reappraisal leaves the emotion unchanged underneath.** That is
  the reading the result invites. It treats the subcortical response as
  the emotion and the rating as its feeling, the split Barrett et al.
  ([LIT-1353](../literature.d/LIT-1353.md), [NOTE-1067](../notes.d/NOTE-1067.md)) say is a premise of basic emotion theory.
  The authors' closing phrase, that reappraisal cannot modulate
  "subcortical-level affective experience" (p. 22), makes the same move. No
  measure here ties the subcortical response to what was felt: it did not
  correlate with reappraisal success (r = 0.015).
- **That subcortical processing cannot be reinterpreted.** One instructed
  strategy, mostly reinterpretation, in a midlife sample. The authors
  themselves give strategy and age as possible reasons why earlier studies
  found amygdala reduction (pp. 20–21).
- **That nothing changes in those regions.** A BOLD null on the mean
  response at 2 mm voxels. Fast, small or patterned changes would not show.
- **That the non-modifiable system is subcortical.** Much of it is
  cortical: lateral OFC, medial prefrontal, midcingulate, parietal. The
  subcortical regions are the ones where nothing was modifiable.
- **That reappraisal acts on perception only.** The authors argue the
  visual reductions reflect semantic and conceptual representation, not
  shifted attention, because the areas are higher-order (p. 21). That is
  argued from location, not tested.

## Connections

It supports [CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md) narrowly, and limits it narrowly.

- **[CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md).** Changing the interpretation changed the reported
  experience, and the change followed reorganized cortical representation
  of the picture. That is the claim's CBT and reappraisal case, now with
  data. The exchange cited the study as a limit: not every component of an
  emotion is open to reinterpretation. The limit holds only if the
  unchanged subcortical response is a constituent of the experience. The
  study does not show that, and the claim is free to deny it. It is not its
  defeater: that needs the organization held fixed while experience
  differs, and here experience changed with the organization.
- **[THEORY-056](THEORY-056.md) and [THEORY-680](THEORY-680.md).** Either can absorb the result. On
  [THEORY-680](THEORY-680.md) reappraisal acts early on meaning and leaves later or faster
  generators alone. On [THEORY-056](THEORY-056.md) a competing appraisal reshapes the
  cortical representation without a separate regulator acting on the
  subcortical response. [THEORY-878](THEORY-878.md) holds the half of the study that
  bears more directly on them.
- **[THEORY-057](THEORY-057.md).** Not tested. One category of affect, no variation across
  instances.
