summary: >-
  Narveson (2017). Returns to his 1967 and 1973 theme, that utilitarianism commits one to making people happy and not to making happy people, after abandoning utilitarianism. He holds that creating a person neither benefits nor harms them: before conception there is no one to confer life on, and without a prior person harm and benefit lack a comparator. Yet, "contrary, perhaps" to how he was read, the existence of people with good lives is normally a good thing and a reason to create them. It matters to us because parents and the benevolent like it, and because more people benefit those around them. Morality's enforceable part, though, licenses interfering with procreation only when it would harm actual persons, "alive, breathing, and acting now (or at any rate in their lifetimes)", never for possible ones. Not helping the poor instead of having a child is not harming them. Against Benatar, a life's goods can outweigh its harms. The second half argues, with little evidence, that resources are created rather than fixed, that current climate policy is inefficient, and that no general conflict between present and future people exists. Parents may have as many children as they want and can afford.
