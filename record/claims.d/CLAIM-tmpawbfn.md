# CLAIM-tmpawbfn: Originalism, strong executive unitarism and preservationist conservatism locate authority in different places, the historical specification, the present center of direction and established practice, which can conflict, so they cannot be treated as expressions of one coherent philosophy of conservation and their commitments must be examined separately

## The claim

A40 §5's table: originalism emphasizes authority located in the
"Historical specification", executive unitarism in the "Present center of
direction", preservationist conservatism in "Established organizational
practice". A38 §4: "A strong centralizing interpretation of executive
authority might dismantle historically developed institutional
relationships that a Burkean conservative would regard as repositories of
practical knowledge. An originalist interpretation might require departing
from a longstanding institutional convention. A tradition-oriented
interpretation might grant significance to historical developments that a
stricter originalist regards as lacking constitutional authority." A40 §5:
an originalist reading "may support either the executive's claim or the
historically developed institutional arrangement, depending on its account
of constitutional meaning and lawful construction."

A38 §4 also pairs each with what it privileges and underrepresents:
historical specification against continuing constitution, hierarchical
authority against independent regulatory capacity, structural persistence
against regeneration. A40 §6 gives each a real risk it addresses
(arbitrary reinterpretation, fragmented responsibility, destruction of
accumulated knowledge) and one it can introduce (specification staleness,
common-mode failure, lock-in): "These are paired design problems."

## What the exchange omits

- **The debate is known.** Within conservative legal thought, Burkean
  critiques of originalism argue the second of A38's conflicts, and
  Burkean minimalism (Sunstein) sets deference to practice against
  originalist correction. From the assistant's knowledge; the turns cite
  neither, so they are not registered.
- **Two of the three are not independent.** Unitary executive theory is
  usually argued on originalist grounds ([CLAIM-tmpqhxsz](CLAIM-tmpqhxsz.md)), so where they
  conflict with conservatism they tend to conflict together.
- **A candidate shared principle.** Originalism and executive unity are
  often defended together as limits on unaccountable discretion, of judges
  and of insulated officials. That principle does not decide their
  conflicts with Burkean deference to practice, so the claim stands; its
  novelty is low.

## Reflexive closure

A40 §5 adds a "combined risk": historical authority, concentrated governing
authority, entrenchment and restricted corrective inquiry reinforcing one
another, so that an arrangement "governs access to the activities through
which its inadequacy might be discovered". A40 marks it a "Hypothetical
conjunction, not an empirical finding" and says it "applies equally to any
institutional arrangement, regardless of its ideological orientation". It
is held as a mechanism by [CLAIM-614](CLAIM-614.md) (an organization reproducing the
projections that make its harm illegible) and [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md), and the exchange's
A36 §24 names it "Epistemic closure"; it is not filed again here.
