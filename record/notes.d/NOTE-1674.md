# NOTE-1674: Brown et al. — Kirtland's Warbler viability after delisting, on supplied habitat

## Contribution

It puts numbers on how far a recovered, conservation-reliant species'
viability depends on continued supplied habitat when that supply is cut
by stated fractions, and shows that in this model a partial cut moves the
population to a lower plateau rather than to extinction. It does not
model stopping the supply.

## Key insight

The supply here is carrying capacity: plantations replace a habitat-making
process, fire, that is still suppressed. The population tracks what is
supplied, so reliance does not end with recovery; the species "will
continue to be reliant on humans to maintain the current quantity of
breeding habitat" (§4).

## Assumptions

- **Ceiling density dependence**: carrying capacity is habitat area times
  breeding density.
- **Males only**, with survival and productivity from cited field
  studies; the modelled area holds over 95 % of breeders.
- **No management response and no range shift** in the scenarios;
  changes are applied as steps, not ramps.
- **Baseline supply**: "14,593 ha as the target for annual breeding
  habitat availability" (§2.2.3).

## Key results

1. **Recovery under management (§1).** "recovered from ca. 200 breeding
   males in 1971 to over 2,000 breeding males today", through "aggressive
   and collaborative management actions, coupled with the pivotal
   >24,000‐ha Mack Lake wildfire".
2. **Two supplies (§1).** Recruitment raised by removal of cowbirds, "an
   obligate nest parasite", and "increasing carrying capacity through
   creation of breeding habitat". "Funding has been secured to support
   postdelisting continuation of the Brown‐headed Cowbird removal
   program".
3. **Why the habitat supply goes on (§1).** The habitat "was historically
   generated naturally through large wildfires with ca. 60 year return
   intervals", but "due to broad‐scale fire suppression, an extensive
   network of plantations is currently required".
4. **Pressure to cut the supply (§1).** The post-delisting plan proposes
   "experimental planting techniques for up to 25% of future created
   habitat", to "reduce planting costs and increase timber value".
5. **Current conditions (§3, Table 2).** λ = 1.0022 (95 % CI
   0.9998–1.0045), about 2,500 males; "4.9% of simulations resulted in a
   population that fell below the management target of 1,000 males, but
   no simulations fell below the critical threshold of 200 males".
6. **Partial withdrawal (§3).** With habitat reduced by climate the
   population "initially declined, but stabilized at ca. 1,300 males"; by
   management changes, "stabilized at ca. 1,900 males".
7. **No collapse from habitat alone (§4).** "loss of breeding habitat
   alone (up to 48.7%) did not cause extinction".
8. **Combined stress (§3).** With the lower 10th percentile of projected
   winter precipitation and climate-reduced habitat, "98.1% of
   simulations fell below 200 males, and 9.9% went extinct".
9. **No self-rescue by moving (§4).** "without additional human
   intervention, there is little opportunity for a major distributional
   change on the breeding grounds due to strict habitat requirements".
10. **Conclusion (§4).** The species "will continue to be reliant on
    humans to maintain the current quantity of breeding habitat to
    minimize the risk of the population falling below specified
    management thresholds".
11. **Validation (§2.2.5).** Adding winter precipitation cut the sum of
    squared deviations from observed abundance from 496,237 to 189,940.

## Concepts

- **Conservation reliance after delisting**: recovery under management
  that must continue once legal protection ends.
- **Supplied carrying capacity** (plantations standing in for wildfire)
  and **supplied recruitment** (cowbird removal).
- **Management target** (1,000 males) and **critical threshold** (200
  males).
- **Full annual cycle**: breeding and wintering grounds modelled
  together.

## Limitations

- A simulation, not an observation; parameters come from cited field
  studies, and only males are modelled.
- No scenario removes the plantations or cowbird control entirely, so it
  says nothing quantitative about full withdrawal.
- Ceiling density dependence builds in the result that population tracks
  habitat area; the plateaus follow from that assumption.
- Managers are assumed not to respond to habitat loss, and birds not to
  shift range.
