summary: >-
  In-group identification has five components on two correlated dimensions. Group-level self-definition is individual self-stereotyping (seeing oneself as like the average member) and in-group homogeneity; group-level self-investment is solidarity, satisfaction and centrality. In confirmatory factor analyses of Dutch, European and university identification in two cohorts (N = 464, 363), this model fit better than one-factor, two-factor and cognitive-versus-affective alternatives; the two dimensions correlated .61 to .77. Prospectively, the components predicted different things: centrality predicted perceived threat, homogeneity perceived intergroup difference, and, facing evidence of their group's misdeeds, self-stereotyping predicted group-based guilt while satisfaction and centrality predicted legitimizing the misdeeds.
