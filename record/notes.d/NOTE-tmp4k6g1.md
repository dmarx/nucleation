summary: >-
  Millum argues that there is no distinctive "right to an open future"
  (Feinberg's rights-in-trust). As a negative right it would rest either on
  future autonomy or on analogy. The first fails because autonomy as demand
  (Darwall) protects an agent's current decisions, grounded in a current
  capacity that young children lack. The second fails because Feinberg's and
  Davis's clear cases (cutting off a child's legs, sterilization) are
  explained by bodily integrity plus harm and do not carry over to contested
  cases (religious upbringing, schooling). As a positive right it is
  autonomy as benefit, grounded in children's interests, and nothing gives
  those interests priority over the child's other interests (pain relief,
  nutrition, attachment, play). Two applications show the right is
  unhelpful: in genetic testing for adult-onset conditions it points both
  ways; in Davis's argument against selecting for deafness it does no work
  beyond the child's interests. Talk of the right obscures the weighing of
  interests.

# NOTE-tmp4k6g1: Millum (2025) — No Right to an Open Future

## Contribution

A reduction: everything the open-future right is invoked for is either an
existing right of the child (bodily integrity) or an interest to be weighed; the
right adds no ground and no priority.

## Key results

- Abstract and p. 2: "there is no compelling argument in favor of a distinctive ‘right to an open future’".
- p. 4 (against the future-autonomy argument): "Last week’s autonomous decision is no longer authoritative." Reason given: "autonomy rights are grounded in the agent’s existing capacity for autonomous decision-making."
- p. 5 (against the analogy argument): "the available analogies prove too little".
- p. 7 (positive version): talk of the right "may be not only otiose but misleading", since it presents several rights of differing weight as one.
- p. 10 (genetic testing): "Avoidable ignorance would close off future options." The open-future verdict there is ambiguous.
- p. 11 (Davis on deafness): "Whatever the merits of Davis’s argument, the ‘right to an open future’ does no work in it."
- Note 24: his earlier reconstruction; the step from "impermissible to make protected choices on their behalf at that time" to "now" is "unwarranted" (paraphrase of the note; check wording before quoting).

## Assumptions

- Children have negative rights (bodily integrity) and positive rights; some rights have thresholds.
- Darwall's distinction between autonomy as benefit and autonomy as demand.
- Autonomy as demand is grounded in present capacity and applies to present decisions.

## Concepts

Right to an open future; rights-in-trust; A-, C- and A-C-rights (Feinberg);
autonomy as benefit vs autonomy as demand (Darwall); dependency rights; parental
discretion; weighing of children's interests; incidental findings.

## Bearing on the record

**On the LIT's and the exchange's characterization.** "There is no separate right to an open future, the protections it is invoked for
being grounded in children's existing rights and interests": **accurate.**

- **[QUESTION-tmp2lvhi](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmp2lvhi.md)** (what legitimates authority over another's body when they
  have standing but cannot decide): **answers one sub-question and sharpens the
  asymmetry.** The question asks whether "the future adult's interest in deciding"
  is a ground of its own; Millum argues it is not: what it protects is bodily
  integrity plus the child's interests. Two further points bear on the question's
  advance-directive half. (1) His ground for autonomy as demand is present
  capacity, and he holds that a competent person's earlier consent does not bind
  against her present refusal; that gives earlier wishes no authority over a
  competent later self. (2) He does not address an earlier competent self
  governing a later incompetent one, so the advance-directive side is left open.
  He lists pediatric circumcision only as a topic where the right has been cited
  (note 4, Darby); he does not analyse it.

Searched for open future, Feinberg, rights-in-trust, Darwall, autonomy as demand,
parental discretion, advance directive.
- **[THEORY-464](../theory.d/THEORY-464.md)** (in identity-involving ethics the deciding relation is usually
  not numerical identity): consistent; Millum's argument never turns on whether
  child and adult are the same person, only on present capacity and interests.
  Leaves the theory's claim untouched; a mild illustration.
- **[QUESTION-364](../questions.d/QUESTION-364.md)** (continuation of a will under revision of its standards):
  Millum's "Last week’s autonomous decision is no longer authoritative" is a
  position on the question's authorization-by-history: for a competent agent, the
  present decision governs. Bears on it as one answer for the competent case.
- **[CASE-128](../cases.d/CASE-128.md)** (voluntary delegation against imposed restriction): tension worth
  noting for the filer: Millum's principle, taken strictly, would deny the earlier
  self's delegation authority over the later competent self, which is what [CASE-128](../cases.d/CASE-128.md)
  turns on. Millum does not discuss precommitment, so this is a question for the
  record, not his claim.
- **[NOTE-166](NOTE-166.md) / [THEORY-464](../theory.d/THEORY-464.md) (Dufner SEP)** names advance directives (Dworkin,
  Jaworska, Dresser) as identity-adjacent; Millum adds nothing on them.

## Limitations

A philosophical argument against a concept's usefulness; it does not supply the
weighing it recommends. Its treatment of autonomy as demand is brief, and it does
not engage precommitment or advance directives.
