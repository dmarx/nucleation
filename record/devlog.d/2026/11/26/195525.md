**What went wrong.** Filing von Oswald et al. 2023 (LIT-tmpuv16r), the
assistant searched `record/literature.d` and THEORY entries for the paper
and its neighbours, briefed the reader on readings only, and filed dated
notes on readings and one question. It never searched claims, cases and
terms for the source's ideas, which is the second bullet of step 2 of the
read-and-file skill. The owner caught it. The record's most relevant
hypothesis, CLAIM-118 (Active), already carried a section of
in-context-learning readings that the paper's fixed-teacher control bears
on directly.

**Why.** The first search was keyed to the work: its id, its title, its
predecessor. Everything it found was literature, so the brief's "why it is
being read" named literature, and the reader's recheck, told to search
"with what the reading surfaced", followed the brief. Each step was
faithful to the one before it, so nothing broke the chain.

**The pattern.** This is the second search scoped too narrowly today. The
first was the case-sensitive grep that missed Sen (the devlog entry of
181308). Both times the search was shaped by the first thing found.

**For the retro.**
- Before briefing a reader, run step 2's idea search over the argument
  schemes (claims, questions, cases, terms, leads) by the work's key words.
- Put what it finds into the brief's "why it is being read".
- The reader's recheck should name those schemes explicitly.

A one-line change to the skill's brief would do this. It is offered to the
owner and not made here.
