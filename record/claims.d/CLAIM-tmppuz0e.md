# CLAIM-tmppuz0e: Axiological and constitutional frontiers move apart

## The claim

A21 §8:

| Frontier | Governing question |
|---|---|
| Operational | What can I do? |
| Developmental | What can I learn to do? |
| Inquiry | What can I discover or formulate? |
| Axiological | What can I become capable of appreciating, caring about, or recognizing as a reason? |
| Constitutional | What can acquire legitimate authority over my organization? |

"Someone could acquire extensive knowledge of an artistic practice
without developing an appreciation for it. Someone could develop a
concern without possessing the language to articulate it adequately. An
institution could recognize the value of an activity while being
incapable of giving it practical standing."

A21 §11 gives the corresponding modalities, ◇_act, ◇_learn, ◇_recognize,
◇_value and ◇_commit, "candidate semantic distinctions, not yet a formal
logical system".

## How it stands to the record

- CLAIM-754 holds the first three and that no scalar measure ranks
  learners across them; this claim adds two frontiers with the same
  independence.
- The constitutional frontier is TERM-tmpmdrmj's constitutional reach for
  an institution, and A19 §VI's constitutional power.
- An institution that recognizes a value and cannot give it standing is
  QUESTION-270's evaluation that is right and not consequential, and
  CLAIM-403's stages of inclusion, where recognition does not bring
  uptake.
- CLAIM-tmpk8knm says what moving the axiological frontier is: making a
  difference evaluatively consequential.

## What it does not say

How the axiological frontier is measured. A21: "The axiological frontier
would be particularly difficult to measure"; a test would ask whether an
intervention makes new distinctions consequential to behaviour across
circumstances, kept apart from "changed beliefs, capabilities,
incentives, or authority" (LEAD-tmp18reb).
