# QUESTION-417: Do the primary studies behind Araki, Berejikian, Ford and Blouin's Table 1 bear out hatchery salmonids' relative fitness in the wild generally below one and lowest, at lifetime 0.02–0.37, for nonlocal segregated stocks, and does a reproduction of their Eqn (3) and Figs 2–3 show that on one trait a decline of more than 30% per generation needs strong truncation, high heritability and strong wild selection together?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-1495](../theory.d/THEORY-1495.md) reports Araki and colleagues' review of 14 studies and their
single-trait quantitative-genetic model as the paper's findings. Neither
the primary studies behind Table 1 nor a reproduction of the model has
been checked.

## What would answer it

A check of Table 1 against its sources, and a reproduction of Eqn (3) with
Figs 2–3 finding that LT ≤ 0.5, ω2 > 5 or h2 ≤ 0.2 each fails to give a
decline of more than 30% per generation, would answer yes; a single trait
under modest selection sufficing would answer no.
