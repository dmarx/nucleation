# THEORY-606: If consciousness words have meaning only through what is public, an entity is a candidate for consciousness only if an encounter with it in a shared world can be engineered, at least in principle; then disembodied chat agents, even with tools and memory, are not candidates, robots and virtually embodied agents could be, and beyond what an informed community comes to say there is no further fact about their consciousness

## Source

Shanahan (2024), "Simulacra as Conscious Exotica", *Inquiry* 69(6),
2982–3010, [LIT-465](../literature.d/LIT-465.md), read in full in [NOTE-382](../notes.d/NOTE-382.md) from arXiv:2402.12422 v2;
the version of record was not reached, so sections and quotations are the
preprint's.

## What was actually shown

**The method (§§4–5).** Following the private-language remarks,
consciousness words "have meaning only insofar as they relate to what is
public": bodies, brains and behaviour. For an exotic entity, the question
becomes whether we can "engineer an encounter" with it in a world we
share, and how a "society-wide conversation" would then come to speak of
it. There is "no more to the truth of the matter" than the consensus
reached, and where there is none, "no residual philosophical mystery".

**Candidature (§§5.2–5.4).** The ability to engineer an encounter, "even if
only in principle, establishes an exotic entity's candidature". The
octopus and an alien cube containing a simulated world pass. If an
entity's behaviour is wholly unintelligible, the language of
consciousness does not apply: the "void of inscrutability" is empty.

**Chat agents (§§6.1–6.2).** "It is not possible to engineer an encounter
with a simple conversational agent, even in principle", since "we cannot
be with them in a shared world". Tools, multimodality, memory and
planning legitimise talk of belief and intention, but "we still cannot
engineer an encounter", and "nothing has changed" for consciousness.

**Embodied agents (§§6.2–7.2).** Robots and virtually embodied agents can be
encountered and so are candidates. In an extended scenario, a community
might come to treat embodied simulacra as conscious, retract on learning
the mechanism, find the gap closed, or invent a "consciousness adjacent"
vocabulary. The paper describes these language games without taking a
side.

**What could have come out otherwise.** Given the method, little: the
verdict on chat agents follows from the candidature test and the fact
that they share no world with us. What could fail is the method, and the
paper does not test it.

## What this does not say

- **That chat agents lack experience as a matter of fact.** On the method,
  there is no hidden fact for them to lack. The verdict is about whether
  the words apply, not about an inner state.
- **That the method is established.** It is summarised from Wittgenstein,
  with "no attempt here to offer a definitive interpretation" (fn 8).
  Chalmers's pure-thinker view is named and set aside (fn 18), and readers
  who do not share the method get no argument against it ([NOTE-382](../notes.d/NOTE-382.md),
  Limitations).
- **That embodied simulacra are conscious.** They are candidates, and the
  outcomes of the scenario are listed, not weighed.
- **That a minority view can be right.** If there is no truth beyond
  convergence, holding a view the community rejects is "hard to reconcile"
  with the account, as the paper concedes (§5.5).
- **Anything settled about the self.** The two self passages, that a chat
  agent has "nothing that would qualify as a self worth preserving",
  lacking body, history and autobiographical memory (§3), and that a role
  player has no "person behind the mask" (§7.3), are asserted. The second
  takes a stable human core self for granted, which the author's 2025
  paper gives up ([THEORY-490](THEORY-490.md)). Neither is part of this account.
