# THEORY-135: Multicellular cooperation depends on suppressing cells that gain fitness at the expense of the organism, across five foundations (proliferation inhibition, controlled cell death, division of labour, resource allocation and maintenance of the extracellular environment); cancer-like cheating on them is found across the independent origins of complex multicellularity, so complex multicellularity can be understood as a system for detecting and suppressing cheaters

## Source

Aktipis et al. (2015), [LIT-740](../literature.d/LIT-740.md), read in [NOTE-562](../notes.d/NOTE-562.md).

## What was actually shown

Aktipis and colleagues review the literature in [LIT-740](../literature.d/LIT-740.md); they
classify reports and add no data.

- **The framework.** Effective multicellularity "requires not just
  cooperation among cells but also mechanisms for suppressing conflict
  that results from mutations that can enhance cell-level fitness at the
  expense of the organism". Five kinds of cooperation are named, and their
  breakdown gives the phenotypes of cancer.
- **The survey.** Complex multicellularity evolved independently at least
  seven times, so cheater suppression did too. Cancer-like phenomena,
  broadly defined, are reported across these lineages, with gaps the
  authors attribute partly to lack of study. Cheating on division of
  labour appears in all forms of multicellularity; resource and
  environment cheating only in animals and plants so far.
- **The interpretation.** "Complex multicellularity represents a highly
  sophisticated cheater detection and suppression system", with
  mechanisms inside cells, in tissue architecture and across the body.
  Larger bodies likely need more of it.

## What this does not say

- **Not that cheating is a strategy.** It is a functional description of
  a breakdown, not a claim that cells evolved to defect.
- **Not that clonality is required.** Development from one cell reduces
  conflict, but aggregative biofilms also cooperate at scale.
- **Not that animals are uniquely prone.** They appear so, "although we
  cannot completely eliminate the possibility that this conclusion is
  influenced by sampling bias".
- **Not a model of multilevel selection.** It is invoked by citation; the
  idea that proliferative units are an intermediate level of selection is
  offered as a possibility.

## Connections

- **Okasha ([THEORY-141](THEORY-141.md)).** The general principle of the major
  transitions, that higher-level selection must come to trump selection
  among the units, typically through policing, is what this account
  documents at one transition. Okasha also cautions against assuming that
  every transition turns on a collective action problem; this account
  assumes it for multicellularity and supports it with phenotypes, not
  with measured fitness conflicts.
- **The owner's essay.** §6.3's capture from below has its biological case
  here: units gaining at the collective's expense, and a collective that
  persists by detecting and suppressing them. The paper's remark that
  cancer is also "a matter of being in an environment that has poor
  cheater detection and suppression" puts part of the failure in the
  collective, not only in the defecting units.
