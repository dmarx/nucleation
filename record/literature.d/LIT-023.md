# LIT-023: Russian Hybrid Warfare

Mason Clark (Institute for the Study of War) (2020), *Institute for the Study of War (ISW), Military Learning and the Future of War Series* — <https://www.understandingwar.org/sites/default/files/Russian%20Hybrid%20Warfare%20ISW%20Report%202020.pdf>

## Key takeaways

- The report argues that the Russian military defines "hybrid war" as a coherent type of war, not a toolkit below the threshold of conflict. In that type of war every action, including the use of conventional force, is subordinate to an information campaign, so US strategy aimed at deterring major conventional war leaves the US exposed to strategic defeats.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The report argues that the US misreads the Russian approach to war. Washington treats hybrid war as activity below the level of armed conflict. Russian military writing, by contrast, treats it as a strategic-level effort to reorient a target state's governance and alignment, with conventional forces included and all instruments serving an information campaign. Drawing on Russian military journals, the author says the Kremlin believes hybrid wars dominate twenty-first-century conflict, sees itself as the target of one, and is adapting its institutions accordingly. The report ends with recommendations for US policy.

**Priority for a deeper reading: medium — the owner spent substantial time on it (t=1115 s) and it offers a clear framework, but it is a single-author think-tank report whose central claims are dated by events since 2022.**

What a deeper reading should check:

- It is an interpretive framework built largely on Russian open-source military journals. A deeper reading should check how representative the cited theorists are of official doctrine.
- It was published in September 2020, before the 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine. That invasion is a natural test of the report's claim that Russia seeks to avoid major conventional war.
- ISW is a think tank with a stated policy perspective. The recommendations are advocacy and should be read separately from the descriptive analysis.
