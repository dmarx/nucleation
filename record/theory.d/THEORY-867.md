# THEORY-867: The geometry of the colour similarity space does not mark the four unique hues, nor the difference between the black–white axis, along which every grey is a mixture of both ends, and the opponent axes, along which each half shows only one end; so a structuralism on which phenomenal character is fixed by similarity relations alone cannot account for how colour experiences are composed

## Source

Aldé (2026), [LIT-1348](../literature.d/LIT-1348.md), *Synthese* 207:204, read in full in
[NOTE-1055](../notes.d/NOTE-1055.md): the colour composition claims (§3, pp. 7–10), the unique
hues objection (§4.1, pp. 11–13), the opposing dimensions objection (§4.2,
pp. 13–15) and the reply to introspective doubt (§5, pp. 15–18).

## What was actually shown

- **The target.** "The phenomenal character of a mental quality is fully
  determined by its similarity relations with the other phenomenal
  characters in the similarity space" (p. 4). If so, everything about
  colour character must be a geometric feature of the colour solid: once
  colours and axes are removed, the solid "encodes only its own shape"
  (p. 11).
- **The facts.** Exactly four hues are experienced as unmixed and the rest
  as weighted combinations of two adjacent ones, with red–green and
  blue–yellow combinations "prohibited"; with white and black these give
  six pure colours (p. 7). The evidence is reported from colour science:
  Hering, hue scaling, colour matching, and cross-cultural and infant
  studies (pp. 7–9). The critics' studies are answered, not re-run
  (pp. 9–10).
- **The unique hues objection.** No geometric property of CIELAB
  distinguishes the unique hues from composite ones; better metrics are
  unlikely to; and colours missing from current models (chimerical
  colours, "olo") would add mainly saturated teals and light blues (pp.
  11–13, Figure 3).
- **The opposing dimensions objection.** The black–white axis is one
  gradient; axes through grey are two half-gradients meeting at grey.
  Current models are asymmetric, so every axis is geometrically unique,
  "yet only the black-white axis is so peculiar in its phenomenology, and
  it is not equally peculiar in its geometrical uniqueness" (p. 15). This
  argument does not need the unique hues.
- **Introspection.** Similarity structuralism is built on introspected
  similarity, so it cannot dismiss introspected composition without
  reason, and judgements that a colour contains others are component
  analyses of an ordinary, reliable kind (pp. 15–18).

What could have come out otherwise: the unique-hue loci might have been
geometrically special, as extremal points or singularities. The paper
argues they are not, by inspection.

## What this does not say

- **It does not show premise 3 by computation.** No invariant search over a
  uniform colour space is reported; the paper says one "could" be done
  (p. 12). The promotion test asks for it.
- **It does not refute structuralisms with other relations.** Rosenthal's
  discrimination spaces are set aside (pp. 4–5), and relations of
  composition, causation or neural organization are not considered. The
  alternative it points to, the opponent-process mechanism, is itself a
  structure, though not a similarity structure.
- **It is about colour only.** Other modalities are left open, with the
  conjecture that they would need the same non-structuralist treatment.
- **The unique hues are not settled science.** The paper grants that their
  stimulus loci vary widely across subjects and are not opposite in colour
  space, and that their neural basis is "elusive" (p. 9).
