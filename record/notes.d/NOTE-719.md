# NOTE-719: Di Paolo — Autopoiesis, Adaptivity, Teleology, Agency

## Contribution

A critique of Weber and Varela's (2002) proposal that autopoiesis grounds
both intrinsic teleology and sense-making. Di Paolo grants the first and
denies the second. Autopoiesis in its original formulation allows no
degrees, so the only norm it yields is live-or-die, and that distinction is
available to an observer, not to the system. He names the missing property,
adaptivity, defines it, and uses it to give new accounts of breakdown,
meaning-construction, behaviour, agency, health and the time-structure of
acts. The definition of agency here is the one Barandiaran, Di Paolo &
Rohde ([LIT-566](../literature.d/LIT-566.md)) later formalise.

## Key insight

Staying alive is not the same as being able to tell that one is getting
worse. A system that merely conserves its organisation is equally well off
in every viable state until it dies. Only a system that senses tendencies
toward the edge of its viability and acts on them has states that are
better or worse for it while all of them are viable, and only then can
anything mean anything to it.

## Assumptions

- **Autopoiesis in its original formulation.** Note 1: "throughout this
  paper the term autopoiesis will be used in its original formulation and
  interpretation which are given in the primary literature". Later,
  looser readings of autopoiesis (Varela's own included) may already
  contain adaptivity; Di Paolo says that if so, "it still needs to be made
  explicit in operational terms".
- **Structural determinism.** Changes of state "always operate in the
  present as a result of its current structure". This is why the system
  cannot respond to a future event, only to present tendencies.
- **A viability set** of "finite measure, bounded, and possibly
  time-varying", the ranges of perturbation and structural change the
  system tolerates, "defined by the organization and current state of the
  system". It is not given formally beyond this sentence.
- **Physicality.** Production is material, in physical space, with a
  membrane (note 3, which calls the demand "at first ad hoc and
  arbitrary" and leaves it unresolved).
- **Method.** "pragmatic circulation" between science and phenomenology:
  Jonas's insights are signposts to be explained, not explanations.

## Key results

- **Conservation, not homeostasis (Autopoiesis as conservation).** Maturana
  and Varela use both words. Homeostasis implies active mechanisms;
  conservation is "a set-theoretic condition that may or may not be
  realized in an active manner". Autopoiesis, "a concept allowing no
  degrees, can only entail the more general case of conservation (thus
  admitting the possibility of fortuitous self-maintenance)".
  - The norm conservation suggests is all-or-nothing: "organisms live as
    long as they don't die". It lets the observer tell lethal from
    non-lethal encounters, and so satisfies Weber and Varela's step A (a
    unity and a norm), but not step B, which "would require the
    autopoietic system itself to be able to make the same distinction."
  - The cliff: "balancing at the edge of a cliff is a perfectly viable
    behaviour, so is falling over the edge – both are interactions that
    conserve autopoiesis."
  - The bacterium: "Even if the current rate of nutrient intake is lower
    than the rate of consumption (leading to certain loss of autopoiesis in
    the near future), bacteria will not seek higher concentrations just
    because they are autopoietic".
- **Robustness against adaptivity.** Robustness is tolerance of a range of
  perturbations and structural changes; it "implies endurance but not
  necessarily adaptivity".
- **The definition (Adaptivity).** "A system's capacity, in some
  circumstances, to regulate its states and its relation to the
  environment with the result that, if the states are sufficiently close to
  the boundary of viability, 1. Tendencies are distinguished and acted upon
  depending on whether the states will approach or recede from the boundary
  and, as a consequence, 2. Tendencies of the first kind are moved closer to
  or transformed into tendencies of the second and so future states are
  prevented from reaching the boundary with an outward velocity."
  - It may come from dedicated mechanisms or be emergent; it "is not a
    direct consequence of the definition of autopoiesis".
  - A breakdown is "the severity of a negative tendency distinguished and
    measured by the amount of regulative resources that it demands to
    compensate for it".
  - Both self-monitoring and regulation are needed: "Events that provoke
    the same regulative response are not meaningfully distinguishable."
  - Adaptivity without self-production is cybernetics, and Jonas's
    criticism holds: a feedback loop "may instantiate a purpose, but this
    purpose is external to it".
- **Properties of adaptivity.**
  - No optimum: "it does not imply any kind of optimal or ideal state for
    the organism", and there may be neutral regions. "Nevertheless,
    adaptivity establishes an inner norm, a distinction between good,
    neutral, and bad ways of realizing autopoiesis."
  - Graded: "Regulation may function over a large range of conditions or
    only in selected extreme situations."
  - Recursive in two senses. Adaptive processes may regulate by distance
    from triggering more basic adaptive responses (preferring nutritious
    food to avoid drawing on reserves). And "regulative processes may
    themselves be plastically adjusted or transformed depending on their
    effectiveness."
  - Pathology becomes definable as failure of adaptivity while still
    autopoietic: "exhaustion of adaptive resources, malfunction of
    regulation, loss of adaptive buffering provoking the activation of
    extreme regulation, disharmonious activation of conflicting adaptive
    mechanisms". After Canguilhem and Goldstein, "a successful cure may
    well re-define rather than simply restore the organism's own
    normativity".
- **Two senses of sense-making.** Uncovering established meaning
  corresponds to regulation by existing adaptive processes; constructing
  new meaning to their "plastic modification, attunement or generation".
  Only the second explains why a proxy (the smell of food, snowprints)
  can mean anything.
- **Behaviour and agency (Natural agency).** Structural coupling is
  undergone; behaviour is its regulation: "Unregulated coupling is better
  described as suffering an exchange while behaviour is the control and
  selection of what exchanges to suffer." An agent is "a self-constructed
  unity that engages the world by actively regulating its exchanges with it
  for adaptive purposes that are meant to serve its continued viability."
  Note 8: agency is not implied by autopoiesis and adaptivity together
  (internal-only regulation, as in the Chemoton), and comes in degrees.
- **Robots.** "Being functionally scared is not the same as being scared."
  The test is the system's organisation, its "self-generated structure of
  sense-making (if any) and its relation to the system's viability". He
  leaves open whether artificial intentionality is possible "short of
  reproducing a fully metabolizing robot".
- **Temporality.** An adaptive act has onset, acceleration, consummation
  and cadence (after Langer). It has a direction that autopoiesis lacks:
  run backwards, autopoiesis is still conserved, but adaptivity becomes
  dysfunctional. There is a minimum grain below which an act decomposes
  into "raw processes". Adaptive responses can leave the organism "viable
  but changed", and so history begins.
- **Non-metabolic values (The road ahead, declared speculative).**
  Self-sustaining forms within metabolic viability but "underdetermined by
  it" (habits, sensorimotor patterns, social values, human projects) can,
  "via a process of adaptive closure analogous to metabolism", generate
  their own values, so that "different identities" coexist in one
  organism. Prismatic-goggle adaptation happens only when the disrupted
  patterns are exercised. Addiction is a self-generated value that "may
  even work against metabolic viability". Without such forms "inner
  conflict would never arise".

## Concepts

- **Conservation.** Changes of state without loss of organisation; it may
  happen fortuitously.
- **Homeostasis.** Conservation by active compensating mechanisms.
- **Robustness.** Tolerance of a range of perturbations and internal
  changes before autopoiesis is lost.
- **Viability set.** Those ranges, bounded, of finite measure, possibly
  time-varying, fixed by the organisation and current state.
- **Adaptivity.** The capacity defined above: regulating states and
  relations to the environment so that tendencies toward the viability
  boundary are detected and reversed.
- **Breakdown.** A negative tendency, measured by the regulative resources
  needed to compensate it.
- **Behaviour.** Regulation of structural coupling, as distinct from the
  coupling.
- **Agent.** A self-constructed unity that regulates its exchanges for
  adaptive purposes serving its viability.
- **Sense-making.** A concerned perspective on the world. Split here into
  uncovering meaning (established regulation) and constructing it
  (plastic change of the regulation).

## Connections

It corrects Weber & Varela (2002; [LIT-1018](../literature.d/LIT-1018.md), read the same day in [NOTE-720](NOTE-720.md)) and builds on
Jonas's philosophy of the organism, Ashby (1960) for how adaptivity might
arise, Canguilhem and Goldstein ([LIT-167](../literature.d/LIT-167.md)) on health, and Ruiz-Mirazo &
Moreno (2000) for agency. Its definition of agency, its asymmetry argument
and its four-phase act are taken up by Barandiaran, Di Paolo & Rohde

sense-making to social interaction. Its note 6 links adaptive autopoiesis to
Boden's "strongest" sense of metabolism.

## Bearing on the record

**Is adaptivity a capacity in the sense of [CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md)'s transformation
capacity? Mostly not, but its second-order part comes close.**

- First-order adaptivity, as defined, is regulation against one fixed
  boundary: tendencies toward the edge of the viability set are detected
  and turned back. That is closer to what [CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md) calls the operative
  regime, or to its governing organisation, than to the capacity to
  transform that organisation. Nothing in the definition requires the
  system to be able to change what it regulates or how.
- Two places in the paper reach further. The second recursion, in which
  "regulative processes may themselves be plastically adjusted or
  transformed depending on their effectiveness", is a capacity to change
  the regulation itself; and meaning-construction is "plastic
  modification, attunement or generation of novel adaptive processes". The
  cure that may "re-define rather than simply restore the organism's own
  normativity" is the Canguilhem point that [CLAIM-018](../claims.d/CLAIM-018.md) makes, and here it
  is stated for organisms by an author the record reads. But Di Paolo does
  not separate these layers as things that can persist or fail apart. He
  treats them as aspects of one graded property. [CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md)'s thesis is
  that they come apart, so this paper is not evidence for it. It supplies
  the vocabulary in which the thesis could be stated for organisms.
- What the paper does separate is autopoiesis from adaptivity: a system can
  remain autopoietic while its adaptivity fails (exhaustion, lost
  buffering, malfunction of regulation). That is a separation of the same
  shape as [CLAIM-099](../claims.d/CLAIM-099.md)'s (persisting is not keeping the capacity to govern
  one's persistence), one level down: maintaining organisation and
  maintaining the capacity to regulate it are different achievements. The
  bacterium that is still autopoietic but whose intake is below
  consumption, "leading to certain loss of autopoiesis in the near
  future", is [CLAIM-079](../claims.d/CLAIM-079.md)'s case in organic dress: currently satisfying the
  constraints with no trajectory that keeps satisfying them. The mapping
  is the record's. Di Paolo does not distinguish the viability set (K)
  from the viability kernel ([THEORY-134](../theory.d/THEORY-134.md)). His "viability set" is
  the set of tolerated states, so states inside it with no viable future
  are, on his terms, still in it, which is exactly the gap [CLAIM-079](../claims.d/CLAIM-079.md)
  names.
- **On [ARG-007](../arguments.d/ARG-007.md)'s mapping premise.** [ARG-007](../arguments.d/ARG-007.md)'s weakest step is choosing the
  constraint set for an agent's regulatory capacities. Di Paolo's
  speculative last section offers one answer: a self-sustaining
  behavioural or social form, by "adaptive closure analogous to
  metabolism", fixes its own viability conditions, distinct from the
  organism's. If that held, K for a regime would be set by the regime's
  own closure, not chosen by an observer. He gives it no criterion and calls
  it speculation, so it is a lead, not an answer.
- **Addiction and protective lock-in.** His addiction case, a
  self-sustaining form whose value "may even work against metabolic
  viability", resembles [TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md) in that a regime maintains itself at the
  cost of the larger system. The resemblance fails on the mechanism. [TERM-020](../terms.d/TERM-020.md)
  is protective, begins in a disturbance and works through the conditions
  that justify it; Di Paolo's form needs none of that.

**What norms does it yield, relative to [CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md)? Norms for the system
only, and on the point [CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md) makes about viability, Di Paolo agrees.**

- He argues that viability alone (conservation) yields no norm the system
  has, only the observer's lethal/non-lethal distinction, "the rather
  useless a posteriori realization by the external observer that the
  organism should have avoided that very last encounter". That is a
  stronger version of [CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md)'s disclaimer, not a rival to it.
- What adaptivity adds is "an inner norm, a distinction between good,
  neutral, and bad ways of realizing autopoiesis", a "normativity within
  their current set of viability conditions and for themselves". It is
  better or worse *for the organism*. Nothing in the paper says that the
  organism, or anything, ought to persist, or that its persistence has
  value for anyone else. [CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md) does not deny norms of this kind.
- One real difference. [CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md)'s summary says "Viability licenses conditional functional
  judgment only", an observer's if-then. Di Paolo's norm is
  meant to be non-conditional in one respect: it is generated by and
  accessible to the system, not imputed from outside. If the record's
  *conditional* means conditional on the system's persisting, Di Paolo's
  norm is still conditional in that sense; if it means ascribed by an
  observer, his is not. [CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md)'s wording does not say which, and this
  paper is a reason to say.
- His own account undercuts any slide from viability to value: health "may
  well re-define rather than simply restore" norms, there are "many ways
  of being healthy", and non-metabolic values can conflict with
  metabolism. Persistence is not even the system's only value.
- **Enoch ([LIT-117](../literature.d/LIT-117.md)).** The shmagency objection targets constitutivist
  arguments that derive reasons for action from what is constitutive of
  agency. Di Paolo makes no such argument. He does not claim the
  organism has reasons, or that anyone should care about its norm. So the
  objection, as the record describes it, does not bite here. [LIT-117](../literature.d/LIT-117.md) is
  unread; this is the record's judgement from its summary.

**Elsewhere.** [THEORY-071](../theory.d/THEORY-071.md) takes its agency conditions from [LIT-566](../literature.d/LIT-566.md), and
this paper is their source: asymmetry by regulation of coupling, and
agency in degrees (note 8). The robot section bears on [LIT-135](../literature.d/LIT-135.md)'s
biological naturalism: Di Paolo's test is organisational, not substrate,
and he leaves the metabolism question open.

## Limitations

- Conceptual. There is no formal model; the only operational example is
  the Chemoton, in a note. The viability set is defined in one sentence.
- C1 is a reading of autopoietic theory. Di Paolo concedes (note 1) that
  later formulations may contain adaptivity, and the critique of Weber and
  Varela is of their reliance on what the original definition does not
  supply.
- *Tendency*, "sufficiently close" and "outward velocity" assume a state
  space with a metric and a boundary; the paper does not supply one.
- The time-reversal argument is a thought experiment; reversing a reaction
  network's flows is not shown to be physically meaningful.
- Agency requires regulation that "in general" improves viability; how
  general is not said.
- The section on non-metabolic values is declared speculative by the
  author, and nothing there is argued beyond examples.

## Open questions

- What counts as a separate failure of the plastic, second-order layer of
  adaptivity, as distinct from failure of first-order regulation? A case
  of an organism with intact regulation and lost plasticity would carry
  [CLAIM-025](../claims.d/CLAIM-025.md) into biology.
- Can a self-sustaining non-metabolic form's viability conditions be stated
  independently of an observer? That would answer [ARG-007](../arguments.d/ARG-007.md)'s mapping
  premise.
- Is Di Paolo's viability set the constraint set K or the viability
  kernel? A formal restatement in viability theory ([THEORY-134](../theory.d/THEORY-134.md)) would
  settle it.
