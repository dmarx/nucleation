# QUESTION-tmpzje42: Is the unity of an experience the existence of a global section over its interpretive contexts, or the unity of one process that can hold local interpretations that do not glue, as when someone wants to approach and avoid the same thing at once?

## The question

Take interpretive contexts (vision, touch, interoception, memory, action
readiness) as a cover. Each context carries local interpretive states,
which may or may not agree on overlaps (A7 §10). One answer: an
experience is unified when the local states glue into a global section.
A7 rejected it: "a system can have unified experience while maintaining
incompatible interpretations. A person might simultaneously want to
approach and avoid something", and "the conflict itself can be a unified
experience". Its alternative: "Unity = A realized organization of local
interpretations and their interactions", "a process that *maintains,
negotiates, and responds to* incompatible interpretations."

The two come apart both ways. A7: "A globally consistent assignment could
exist in a merely descriptive model without any corresponding unified
process. Conversely, a genuinely unified process could contain locally
incompatible states." A6, of Lee-Youngzie et al. ([LIT-tmphb8p5](../literature.d/LIT-tmphb8p5.md)):
"compatible local sections in a specified mathematical model need not
establish that there exists a conscious subject corresponding to their
union. Nor does failure of a global section automatically imply the
absence of experience."

## Why it is a question

The record already uses the global-section reading for a self.
[CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md) (Active): "A global section, one coherent self, exists exactly
when the monodromy Φ around a cycle vanishes." [CLAIM-196](../claims.d/CLAIM-196.md), [CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md)
and [CLAIM-198](../claims.d/CLAIM-198.md) place disunity on overlaps: preference reversal "is not yet
disunity" until a self-prediction creates an overlap. Those claims are
about whether a self holds together across contexts, not about the unity
of one experience. A7's conflict case is exactly an overlap whose
sections disagree, felt as one state. If a gluing failure on an overlap
can be a unified experience, then [CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md)'s coherent self and
phenomenal unity are different unities. The tag vocabulary already keeps
four unities apart ([ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)), but no claim says where a gluing failure
falls among them.

It also bears on [CLAIM-tmpdga80](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpdga80.md), whose unity is a property of a
process, and on Metzinger's "single functional cluster"
([THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md)), which is a dynamic criterion, not a sheaf condition.

A7's decomposition of binding names what is at stake. Functional
binding: how distributed processes coordinate. Perspectival binding: when
coordinated processes constitute one frame. Phenomenal binding: "Why, or
whether, does that organization constitute the unity of conscious
experience?" A gluing condition is a candidate for the second, and the
question is whether it can be the third. [CLAIM-355](../claims.d/CLAIM-355.md) already records the
exchange of 2026-10-17's five bindings and says that it "does not solve
phenomenal binding".

## What would answer it

Either a showing that a unified experience of conflict is, under the
right cover, a global section after all (for instance, the conflict as one
section of a sheaf of action-readiness states, not two sections that
disagree); or a formal object, beside the sheaf, for a process that holds
non-gluing sections together, with a reason to call it the unity of an
experience and not only a functional coupling. Isaac's dynamical
structures ([LIT-tmpwhbpm](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwhbpm.md)) and the record's frustration of maintained demands
([CLAIM-368](../claims.d/CLAIM-368.md)) are candidate materials for the second.
