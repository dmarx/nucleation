# NOTE-tmpv0pib: Damasio — The somatic marker hypothesis and the possible functions of the prefrontal cortex

## Contribution

A compact statement, by its author, of a hypothesis already set out in
*Descartes' Error* (1994) and earlier reviews (the text "follows closely, in
form and substance, several reviews", p. 1413). What it adds as a paper is
the explicit list of assumptions and operations (§2), a named network (§3),
an evolutionary argument for why the markers are somatic (§4), and a summary
of the first three lines of evidence (§5). There is no new experiment here.

## Key insight

Good decisions under uncertainty need something besides knowledge and logic.
Patients with ventromedial prefrontal damage keep their IQ, factual and social
knowledge, language, attention, working memory and performance on the
Wisconsin Card Sorting Test, and still decide badly in personal and social
life. What they lose along with good decisions is emotional responsiveness.
Damasio's account is that past experience leaves a link between a kind of
situation and the body state it produced. Re-enacting that body state when an
option is imagined "marks" the option as good or bad. The mark either is felt
(an alarm or incentive signal) or acts covertly as a bias, and so cuts the
option space down before cost-benefit reasoning starts. Without the marks,
"options and outcomes become virtually equalized" (p. 1415), and choosing is
slow, error-prone, or random and impulsive.

## Assumptions

- **Scope.** The hypothesis is about ventromedial prefrontal patients and the
  normal function their deficit reveals. It is "not ... a general theory for
  how prefrontal cortices work" (p. 1414). Patients with other frontal lesions
  "may or not be accountable by" it.
- **Taken for granted.** "An operative self and consciousness, the basis for
  neither of which I will discuss" (p. 1414).
- **Four background assumptions (§2a):**
  1. Reasoning and decision depend on many levels of operation, some conscious
     ("minded") and some not. Minded operations depend on sensory images built
     in early sensory cortices.
  2. All mind operations depend on support processes such as attention and
     working memory.
  3. Knowledge of situations, actors, options and outcomes is stored in
     "dispositional" form (coded, implicit, non-topographic) in higher-order
     cortices and some subcortical nuclei, per the convergence-zone framework
     (Damasio 1989a, b; [LIT-tmpll80w](../literature.d/LIT-tmpll80w.md) is 1989b). It is made explicit in motor
     responses (some of which constitute emotions) and in images.
  4. Knowledge divides into (A) knowledge about bioregulatory processes and
     body states, including emotions; (B) knowledge about entities, facts,
     actions and stories; (C) the links between B items and A items formed in
     individual experience; and (D) categorisations of A, B and C.
- **Emotion is body-state representation.** Emotion is "most importantly,
  though not solely" expressed "through changes in the representation of body
  state", and represented in the brain as "transient changes in the activity
  pattern of somatosensory structures" (p. 1414). "Somatic" covers
  musculoskeletal, visceral and internal-milieu components, and a somatic
  signal need not originate in the body.

## Key results

The paper's results are its proposed operations, and the evidence it reports
from earlier studies.

- **Proposed operations (§2b).**
  - (i) Ventromedial prefrontal cortex holds dispositional links between a
    class of situation and the emotional state that went with it. The link
    holds neither the facts nor the emotion, only "the potential to
    reactivate an emotion" (p. 1414).
  - (ii) When a situation recurs, facts are recalled as images and, "nearly
    so" simultaneously, the ventromedial link activates the emotional
    apparatus (e.g. the amygdala). Reactivation goes by a "body loop" (the
    body changes and the change is sensed) or an "as if body loop"
    (somatosensory cortex adopts the pattern directly, the body bypassed).
    Damasio believes the body loop came first but that the "as if" loop is
    now used more often (p. 1415). Either may be overt or covert.
  - (iii) The somatosensory pattern, co-displayed with the imagined
    scenario, "marks the scenario as good or bad". Overt, it is an alarm or
    incentive signal. Covert, it is a biasing signal, for instance through
    dopamine (p. 1415).
  - (iv) Markers also boost attention and working memory and may assist
    response inhibition.
  - (v) Markers make logical reasoning tractable by letting some
    option-outcome pairs be "rapidly rejected or endorsed".
- **Network (§3).** Ventromedial convergence zones; central autonomic
  effectors (amygdala, hypothalamus, brainstem nuclei); somatosensory
  cortices (insula, SII, SI), "especially in the non-dominant hemisphere";
  possibly basal ganglia. The anatomical case is that ventromedial cortex
  receives all sensory modalities, is "the only known source" of frontal
  projections to central autonomic control (Nauta 1971), and is reciprocally
  linked to hippocampus and amygdala. Basic unconditioned stimuli use a
  separate thalamus-to-amygdala route, and the formulation "predicts a
  dissociation" between responses to complex and to basic stimuli (p. 1416).
- **Why somatic (§4).** Personal and social situations are bound to reward,
  punishment and homeostatic regulation, which are represented somatically. A
  survival-guidance system was "pre-adapted" for abstract decisions (p.
  1417). Only the situation-to-body-state links need to be stored, because
  body states "can be re-enacted on demand".
- **Evidence (§5), all summarised from other papers, without statistics.**
  - *Emotive pictures* (Damasio et al. 1990, 1991; Tranel 1994; Tranel et al.
    1995). Normal controls and non-frontal lesion patients give skin
    conductance responses (SCRs) to emotionally charged pictures and few to
    neutral ones. Bilateral ventromedial patients give none to the charged
    pictures, though their SCRs to startle and deep breaths are normal.
  - *Gambling task* (Bechara et al. 1994). Four decks; A and B pay $100 a card
    with large occasional penalties (net −$250 per ten cards); C and D pay $50
    with small ones (net +$250 per ten cards); 100 selections, a $2000 loan.
    44 controls (21 women, 23 men, aged 20–79) against 6 ventromedial
    patients (4 men, 2 women, aged 43–84). Controls drift to C and D and,
    "about halfway through", keep to them. Patients keep choosing A and B and
    lose the loan. High-IQ controls asked to track the penalties aloud could
    not compute the decks' net yields. A reversed task, with punishment up
    front and unpredictable reward, leaves the patients unchanged, which the
    paper takes against both hypersensitivity to reward and insensitivity to
    punishment (Anderson et al. 1996, an abstract).
  - *SCRs during the task* (Bechara et al. 1996). In the roughly four seconds
    before a choice, controls develop large anticipatory SCRs before choosing
    from the bad decks, rising over the task, and not before the good decks.
    Ventromedial patients show none, though their SCRs to actual losses are
    normal.
  - *Timing of knowledge.* "Recent studies in our laboratory suggest" that
    controls produce the anticipatory SCRs "long before they have ... any
    notion whatsoever" of which decks are good (p. 1419). This is the result
    published in the 1997 report ([LIT-tmp8vfsi](../literature.d/LIT-tmp8vfsi.md)); here it is one sentence.
- **Discussion.** To Everitt: in adults the "as if" loop is the usual mode,
  so changes in the periphery should have relatively little effect on
  emotion, feeling and decision (paraphrased: the line is cut off in the scan); the evidence from cord damage and peripheral neuropathy is left
  open. To Bishop: a failure to inhibit an early-rewarded response is
  "conceivable", but the striking fact is that the patients do not correct
  when they realise their strategy is losing; most patients who fail the task
  "acquire classical conditioning normally".

## Concepts

- **Somatic state.** An umbrella term for emotional changes, read as
  representations of body state (musculoskeletal, visceral, internal milieu),
  which need not originate in the body.
- **Somatic marker.** A somatic state, re-enacted from a learned link,
  co-displayed with an imagined option-outcome scenario so that it qualifies
  it as good or bad.
- **Dispositional.** "Coded, implicit and non-topographically organized"
  (p. 1414): a record that can reconstruct a pattern elsewhere but does not
  hold it. From the convergence-zone framework.
- **Body loop / "as if body loop".** Re-enactment through an actual change in
  the body, sensed back; or directly in somatosensory cortex, with the body
  bypassed.
- **Overt / covert.** A marker that is attended and felt (a feeling), or one
  that biases processing without awareness. Feelings are body states or their
  surrogates "made conscious"; unattended ones still act (p. 1416).
- **Ventromedial patients.** Bilateral lesions of the ventral and medial
  prefrontal cortex with the "index condition": acquired defects of real-life
  decision making.

## Connections

- **Damasio's 1989 convergence-zone proposal ([LIT-tmpll80w](../literature.d/LIT-tmpll80w.md)).** Cited as
  1989b for the dispositional and convergence-zone framework. The
  ventromedial links here are convergence zones that record conjunctions of
  situation and body state. This paper is the clearest statement I could
  read of what that framework is for; the 1989 paper itself could not be
  reached.
- **The 1997 Science report ([LIT-tmp8vfsi](../literature.d/LIT-tmp8vfsi.md)).** The "recent studies" of the
  last paragraph of §5. Its note checks how far its statistics carry the
  claim this paper previews.
- **Damasio's later work.** His later papers on feeling, homeostasis and
  consciousness are being filed in this record at the same time; how far
  they keep the body-state reading of emotion and the "as if" loop stated
  here is for their readings to say. This paper mentions homeostasis only
  in passing, in §4, where personal and social situations are said to be
  linked to reward, punishment and homeostatic regulation.
- **Seth ([LIT-135](../literature.d/LIT-135.md), [NOTE-169](NOTE-169.md)).** Seth's interoceptive inference in the service
  of allostasis puts body-state signals at the centre of consciousness; this
  paper puts them at the centre of decision and sets consciousness aside. The
  two meet in the claim that body-state representations, not the body,
  carry the signal: the "as if" loop is a representation-level mechanism of
  the kind a predictive-processing account would give.
- **Roberts ([LIT-207](../literature.d/LIT-207.md), [NOTE-157](NOTE-157.md)).** Roberts lists "affect with somatic
  expression" among states out of bounds to systems without a body. The "as
  if body loop" is a case where the somatic state is produced with the body
  bypassed, so an argument from this hypothesis to "affect needs a body"
  would have to rest on the developmental claim (the body loop came first),
  not on the adult mechanism.
- **Cross-modal fluency ([LIT-190](../literature.d/LIT-190.md), [NOTE-146](NOTE-146.md)).** A felt processing quality
  enters judgements about its source. Overt somatic markers are the same
  shape of claim with a body state as the feeling.
- **Not held in either record.** *Descartes' Error* (1994); Bechara et al.
  1994 (Cognition 50:7–12) and 1996 (Cerebral Cortex 6:215–225), the primary
  reports of the gambling task and its SCRs; Shallice & Burgess (1993), whose
  marker proposal §4 compares. The later critiques of the hypothesis and of
  the gambling-task evidence, Maia & McClelland (2004, PNAS,
  DOI-10.1073/pnas.0406666101) among them, are not held either and were not
  read here.

## Bearing on the record

- **No THEORY bears on it.** The record holds no account of emotion or
  decision. A THEORY drawn from this work would be "body-state signals
  re-enacted from ventromedial prefrontal links bias decisions under
  uncertainty before and alongside reasoning". It should not be sourced from
  this paper alone: this paper reports the evidence secondhand, and the
  timing claim it most needs is in the 1997 report, whose statistics are
  weaker than its abstract. A THEORY would also have to read the critiques
  named above.
- **Topic.** The work's primary subject is emotion and its role in decision,
  which the new `emotion-and-affect` topic now holds. `cognition` covers
  reasoning and decision; `neuroscience` the lesion and autonomic evidence.
  Nothing else is left unsaid.
- **No ML instruction.** The paper says nothing about machines or learning
  systems. Nothing here belongs in the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Evidence secondhand.** Every result is summarised from other papers, with
  no group statistics, effect sizes or tests.
- **Small samples.** The gambling comparison is six patients against 44
  controls, and the patients are older (43–84 against 20–79).
- **Alternatives treated briefly.** Response inhibition is set aside as
  "implausible as the sole source of the defect" for reasons of task
  complexity, not by an experiment reported here. Reward hypersensitivity and
  punishment insensitivity are set aside on a conference abstract. The
  alternative that controls first reason and then respond somatically is
  found "less plausible" on evolutionary grounds and on the unpublished
  timing data (p. 1419).
- **Mechanism unobserved.** No experiment here measures the ventromedial
  link, the "as if" loop or the dopamine route. SCRs index a somatic state;
  they do not show that it marks options or that it causes the choice.
- **Body loop untested.** The author's reply to Everitt leaves the role of
  peripheral signals open.

## Open questions

- Is the anticipatory SCR a cause of advantageous choice, a correlate, or a
  consequence of knowledge participants have but are not asked about? The
  1997 report is the test the paper points to; see its note.
- Do body-loop and "as if" loop separate experimentally, for instance in
  spinal-cord injury or peripheral autonomic failure, as the reply to
  Everitt suggests?
- Is the predicted dissociation between complex (cortical) and basic
  (thalamo-amygdala) triggers of markers found?
- Is the deficit specific to uncertain, experience-dependent choice, or does
  it reduce to reversal learning, as Bishop's question implies?
