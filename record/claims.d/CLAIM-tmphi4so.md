# CLAIM-tmphi4so: No human study the record has read separates a germline route of inherited trauma from the womb, parenting and cultural inheritance, and none of the designs now in use can: so which route inherited trauma travels by cannot now be settled in humans, and that no clean separation is achievable holds as a statement about present methods

## The claim

[QUESTION-297](../questions.d/QUESTION-297.md) asks, among other things, by which route the effects of
trauma reach descendants who did not live through it: germline
epigenetics, the womb, parenting, or a group's narrative and identity. Its
answer criterion for the routes is "a human study that separates germline
transmission from uterine, parenting and cultural routes … Or evidence
that no such separation is achievable."

This claim holds the second, in a scoped form: with the designs now in
use, no human study separates them, and none the record has read does.

## Its grounds

- **Horsthemke** ([THEORY-1618](../theory.d/THEORY-1618.md)) argues that human studies can exclude
  genetic inheritance but not ecological or cultural inheritance, and
  proposes a three-step standard of proof that he grants can hardly be met
  in humans. That is the claim's main ground, and the reason for its
  scope: he speaks of what can now be done.
- **Costa, Yetter and DeSomer** ([THEORY-1594](../theory.d/THEORY-1594.md)) have the strongest paternal
  design the record holds, sons born after a father's captivity, and still
  favour an epigenetic route by elimination, without measuring one. The
  father raised the sons, so parenting is not excluded.
- **Youssef and colleagues** ([THEORY-1612](../theory.d/THEORY-1612.md)) review the Holocaust FKBP5
  studies and stress in pregnancy; the parent–child studies are small and
  cross-sectional, and the authors name unmeasured confounds among their
  weaknesses.

## What it does not say

- **Not that separation is impossible.** Adoptee, donor-gamete and
  paternal-only designs weaken the confounds without removing them
  ([QUESTION-297](../questions.d/QUESTION-297.md)'s note of 2026-10-29). A design that removed them would
  defeat the claim.
- **Not that the germline route is absent.** Animal work (Dias and
  Ressler, [THEORY-1602](../theory.d/THEORY-1602.md); Jawaid and Mansuy, [THEORY-1587](../theory.d/THEORY-1587.md)) shows germline
  effects in rodents. The claim is about what human evidence can show.
- **Not that the sceptics settle it.** Horsthemke, Bird ([THEORY-1614](../theory.d/THEORY-1614.md)) and
  Heard ([THEORY-1576](../theory.d/THEORY-1576.md)) are, as [QUESTION-297](../questions.d/QUESTION-297.md)'s note says, "one school, not
  three judgements"; and their chief confound, families passing on habits,
  is itself one of the question's routes. The claim leans on Horsthemke
  for the methodological point only, which the two empirical grounds
  illustrate independently of the school's view that the mechanism is
  unlikely.

## What follows for the question

On the routes, the social routes gain share by default, not by evidence,
as [QUESTION-297](../questions.d/QUESTION-297.md)'s note already says. The question's identity route, and
its test of whether distress follows identification measured apart from
knowledge and family, is untouched by this claim.
