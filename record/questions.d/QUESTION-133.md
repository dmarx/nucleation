# QUESTION-133: Can a population of operators whose demands are jointly infeasible under one functional organization develop a differentiated or compositional repertoire that makes them jointly feasible, without suppressing, weakening or deleting any of those demands?

## Why it is a question

[CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md) separates two ways an organization can deal with incompatible
demands. It can choose among them, which leaves the complex unchanged. Or
it can change what it can attain, which enlarges the complex.
[CASE-119](../cases.d/CASE-119.md) shows the second is possible. [CASE-115](../cases.d/CASE-115.md) shows a continuous
version: splitting a shared parameter removes a two-demand frustration.
[CLAIM-360](../claims.d/CLAIM-360.md) separates a larger realizable set from better choice within a
fixed one in the deep-against-shallow results.

The synthesis offered a candidate thesis, not filed as a claim:

> Some organizational conflicts are not best understood as problems of
> choosing among competing demands. They are problems of
> representational or functional organization: the existing repertoire
> cannot realize certain jointly demanded outcomes, while a
> differentiated or recomposed repertoire can.

That is stronger than saying specialization makes a decision process more
efficient. It says an organization can change its own attainable set.
Ecological niche differentiation is the analogy. Two organisms competing
for one scarce resource may be unable to grow at the same time, and
differentiated acquisition strategies can make joint growth feasible.
Differentiation can also fail, by adding costs or dependencies.

## What would answer it

A model that:

- fixes the acceptance sets of the operators' demands throughout
  ([CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md));
- lets learning, differentiation or recomposition change the attainable
  outcome set ℛ(𝒪);
- shows a strict enlargement of 𝒦 with no compatible collection lost, or
  shows why the class of models considered cannot produce one.

Higher-order structure should be checked, not assumed. Pairwise
compatibility does not fix collective compatibility ([CASE-119](../cases.d/CASE-119.md)), but
some apparent higher-order effects reduce to pairwise ones ([THEORY-598](../theory.d/THEORY-598.md)).
An answer should show when the complex carries information its
1-skeleton does not.

## What would not answer it

Apparent relief that comes from deleting a demand, lowering its weight
or redefining success ([CLAIM-361](../claims.d/CLAIM-361.md)). An improvement in aggregate loss that
does not change 𝒦.

## Note of 2026-11-03: differentiation driven by frustration

A33's H2 in the exchange "Assess SDT Coverage" (2026-11-02), integration through adaptive differentiation, is [CASE-115](../cases.d/CASE-115.md)'s
construction (one shared parameter split into task-routed ones) without
citing it. What it adds is a hypothesis: recurrent frustration between
demands is the signal that drives the split, and maintenance keeps the new
channel after the conflict that occasioned it ends. [CASE-117](../cases.d/CASE-117.md) is the study
that would test it, and [CLAIM-532](../claims.d/CLAIM-532.md) states it as part of a positive
account of self-formation.

The question stays open.

## Note of 2026-11-22: joint persistence reached by adaptation, in a restricted class

LEAD-049 lists this question among those it advances. What it gives
applies only on one reading. Take each operator's demand to be its own
persistence with positive recovery when rare. That is QUESTION-135's
persistence edge and CLAIM-554's reading of niche differentiation as
relief of frustration among persistence demands. CLAIM-tmp6kclm then
gives sufficient conditions under which adaptation of bounded niche
traits reaches a state in which all k demands are met at once and
robustly: each specialist recovers when rare against frozen residents. No
demand is deleted or weakened. The cost c and the supply b are fixed
throughout. In CASE-tmp5sk3q's Gaussian-niche runs, operators started
almost identical reach this state whenever the niche space has room for
them all. Near the identical start, the
demands of two persistence-seeking operators sharing a trait are not
robustly met, since such an equilibrium is never asymptotically stable.

Two limits. The demands here are persistence demands, not the
acceptance sets of CLAIM-347, so whether this counts as an answer in the
question's own terms is the owner's call. And joint feasibility is not
kept against re-adaptation. In CLAIM-tmpdsv4t's instance, losing one
specialist can tip the system into a lone generalist against which the
lost demand cannot be met again.

The question is unchanged and stays Open.
