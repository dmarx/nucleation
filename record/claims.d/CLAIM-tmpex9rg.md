# CLAIM-tmpex9rg: The sensitivity that formal chaos gives a will's dynamics is not control over them: in Kane's self-forming actions, chaotic amplification keeps the outcome open, but it magnifies the agent's efforts, uncontrolled fluctuations and outside inputs alike, so what would make the outcome the agent's is how its own control couples to the amplifying directions, relative to noise and to other parties' inputs and with what feedback, which sensitivity does not supply; and under weak noise what amplification touches lasts only where it reaches the slow modes

## The claim

Kane ([LIT-tmpnqv86](../literature.d/LIT-tmpnqv86.md), ch. 8, as the standard reading has it; not yet read here)
places indeterminacy in conflicted choice, amplified by chaotic neural
dynamics. Bishop's SEP entry ([LIT-tmp9hfqf](../literature.d/LIT-tmp9hfqf.md)) states the gap the owner points
at: incompatibilists "such Robert Kane (1996)" still have "to demonstrate
agents can effectively harness such indeterminism".

The owner's connection puts that gap in control terms:

- **Sensitivity is not control.** A positive Lyapunov exponent is a property
  of the dynamics. It amplifies whatever enters along the unstable
  directions: the agent's effort, noise, another party's input.
  [CLAIM-tmpptgk8](CLAIM-tmpptgk8.md) shows that it lowers the cost of reaching an outcome and,
  without feedback, lowers the agent's share of it against noise. What
  would make the outcome the agent's is a property of the agent's channel:
  its reach ([CLAIM-354](CLAIM-354.md), [CLAIM-345](CLAIM-345.md)), its allocation against other channels,
  and its feedback.
- **Openness is not control either.** Deterministic chaos has a unique
  future; it gives practical unpredictability, not Kane's metaphysical
  openness (A22 §2). And metaphysical openness, if present, gives no
  control by itself. That is the luck objection ([THEORY-328](../theory.d/THEORY-328.md), [THEORY-040](../theory.d/THEORY-040.md)),
  stated as the absence of a channel.
- **Slow modes decide what lasts.** Under weak noise, chaos makes generic
  influences short-lived: operator growth spreads them and they decay,
  while only what is near-conserved stays slow ([LIT-tmp8mp1c](../literature.d/LIT-tmp8mp1c.md),
  [THEORY-tmpggrkf](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpggrkf.md)). By analogy, an amplified effort shapes a will
  durably only where it reaches the slow variables, which is the owner's
  "slow modes" and [CLAIM-478](CLAIM-478.md)'s scale separation.
- **Reflexive control is the test case** (U19). The leverage that makes
  self-formation cheap makes outside steering cheap. Comparing the agent's
  channel with an outsider's for the same transition, by their Gramians, is
  one measurable part of whose the outcome is (A20 §4; [QUESTION-071](../questions.d/QUESTION-071.md)'s note
  of 2026-11-02).

## What it does not say

- **That Kane is wrong.** Kane's reply, that the indeterminacy is a
  hindrance internal to the effort that overcomes it, would make the
  separation of inputs ill-posed. It is the claim's defeater, unread here.
- **That responsibility follows from a good channel.** Guidance control
  without regulative control ([THEORY-121](../theory.d/THEORY-121.md)) is the nearest held account of
  what a channel could ground. Ultimate responsibility is set aside
  ([CLAIM-022](CLAIM-022.md)).
- **That the slow-mode point is shown for brains or wills.** It is shown
  for weakly dissipative quantum many-body systems and carried over by
  analogy.
