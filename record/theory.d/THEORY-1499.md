# THEORY-1499: Davis and Weinstein find that the Allied bombing of Japanese cities in World War II, which destroyed almost half the structures in the 66 targeted cities, changed relative city size only temporarily: across 303 cities, 1947–60 population growth undid 1940–47 growth instrumented on deaths and buildings destroyed per capita one for one, or by about three-quarters once 1925–40 trend growth is controlled, and wholly by 1965, with Hiroshima and Nagasaki back on their prewar trends; the authors read this as rejecting random growth and as showing that spatial catastrophes from temporary shocks are not a central tendency, favouring locational fundamentals

## Source

Davis & Weinstein (2002), [LIT-2205](../literature.d/LIT-2205.md); read in [NOTE-1696](../notes.d/NOTE-1696.md) from NBER
w8517 (October 2001). The AER version of record was not read, and by the
authors' companion paper it adds at least one analysis. Page numbers are
the working paper's.

## What was actually shown

- **The shock (pp. 17–20).** The bombing "destroyed almost half of all
  structures in these cities – a total of 2.2 million buildings"; about
  80% of the 303 cities "were virtually untouched".
- **First stage (Table 2).** Deaths and buildings destroyed per capita
  "explain about 41 percent of the variance" in 1940–47 growth.
- **Reversion (Table 3).** 2SLS coefficient of 1947–60 growth on
  instrumented 1940–47 growth −1.048 (s.e. 0.097); with 1925–40 growth
  controlled −0.759 (0.094) by 1960 and −1.027 (0.163) by 1965: "the
  effect of the temporary shocks vanishes completely in less than twenty
  years" (p. 25).
- **Hiroshima and Nagasaki (Figure 2).** "there is a clear indication that
  they returned to their prewar growth trends", Hiroshima a little more
  slowly.
- **The authors' reading (p. 29).** "The theoretical possibility of
  spatial catastrophes due to temporary shocks is not a central tendency
  borne out in the data."

What could have come out otherwise: a coefficient near 0 (a random walk),
or between 0 and −1 by 1965, would have meant the shock left a lasting
mark on relative size.

## What this does not say

- **That multiple spatial equilibria do not exist.** Thresholds beyond the
  shocks observed are not excluded, and the authors say their exercises are
  not a test of the increasing-returns theories (p. 27).
- **That fundamentals were measured.** They are inferred from the
  reversion.
- **That temporary subsidies cannot move a city.** That is the authors'
  argument from bombs to subsidies; a subsidy that builds fundamentals, as
  in the TVA ([THEORY-1461](THEORY-1461.md)), is a different shock.
- **Anything below the city aggregate.** Footnote 20 leaves sub-regions and
  industry mix open; the companion paper takes up industry mix.
- **That the city's upkeep has no threshold** ([CLAIM-604](../claims.d/CLAIM-604.md)). None was found
  within shocks of this size.
