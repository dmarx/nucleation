# THEORY-528: The cases offered for extended cognition are explained at least as well by embedded cognition, on which the organism leans heavily on external props, and memory research finds that external stores lack the interference, generation effect and power-law learning and forgetting of internal memory, so a kind spanning both does no explanatory work and conservatism favours the embedded account

## Source

Rupert (2004), "Challenges to the Hypothesis of Extended Cognition",
[LIT-422](../literature.d/LIT-422.md), read in [NOTE-343](../notes.d/NOTE-343.md) from the author's version.

## What was actually shown

**Two hypotheses (§II).** HEMC, embedded cognition: cognitive processes
"depend very heavily, in hitherto unexpected ways, on organismically
external props". HEC, extended cognition: the unit of analysis is organism
plus environment, and cognitive states "literally comprise—as wholes do
their proper parts—elements in that environment". Inferring HEC from HEMC
by dependence is invalid. Understanding Nazi Germany's invasion of Poland
needs 1920s German economics, which is not part of the invasion ([NOTE-343](../notes.d/NOTE-343.md),
C2, strong).

**The past-endorsement dilemma (§IV).** Directory assistance satisfies the
first three of [LIT-097](../literature.d/LIT-097.md)'s criteria, so without the fourth Otto believes
every listed number: "It is absurd". With it, it is arbitrary to require
endorsement for extended but not internal belief, and it makes the
organism's consciousness "the ultimate source of cognitive authority", so
the notebook is "best accommodated by HEMC" (C4 strong, C5 moderate).

**Memory differs (§§V–VI).** Holding a model of a long conversation needs
internal working memory, and keeping it on paper would "destroy the
dynamics of normal conversation". In A-B/A-C paired-associate learning,
internal memory shows interference; a written list shows none, and no
learning curve. The generation effect has no external analogue. These are
differences in learning time and access time, "at the very heart" of
memory research.

**No useful generic kind (§VII).** A kind like "has some sort of access to
the information that P" would cover notebooks. But internal systems share
power laws of learning and forgetting and Rescorla-Wagner learning that
external stores lack, and psychologists who split memory into systems tend
to drop the superordinate kind, not widen it.

**Functionalism does not help (§VIII).** Commonsense functionalism forbids
seeing one's own memory. Psychofunctionalism takes its roles from memory
science, whose roles external stores do not fill.

**What could have come out otherwise.** An external store could show
interference or a generation effect; the paper argues that one does not,
by thought experiment.

## What this does not say

- **That no coarse kind could be useful.** The case against a generic kind
  is a challenge plus conservatism, not a demonstration ([NOTE-343](../notes.d/NOTE-343.md),
  Limitations; C7 weak to moderate).
- **That external stores were tested.** No experiment on external memory
  is cited or run. The external side of each comparison is argued ("there
  is no reason to think...").
- **That HEMC wins without conservatism.** The second horn of the dilemma
  needs the conservatism principle to get from "the organism is
  privileged" to "prefer HEMC".
- **Anything about consciousness or groups.** Socially distributed
  cognition is set aside (§I). Note 38's remark that corporate bodies have
  no plausible theory of consciousness is a point about endorsement, not
  about phenomenal group consciousness.
- **That it is a claim about AI practice.** §IX's appeal to AI's aims is a
  2004 claim about what the field wants, offered as weak support (C9).

  different premise.

  different cognitively relevant properties ([THEORY-591](THEORY-591.md)).
