# NOTE-tmpnpbzl: Fineman (2010) — The Vulnerable Subject and the Responsive State

## Contribution

An account of state responsibility grounded in universal human vulnerability and in the state's role in constituting the institutions that confer resilience, offered as a "post-identity" alternative to anti-discrimination equality.

## Key results

1. Introduction, p. 255: "Vulnerability is posited as the characteristic that positions us in relation to each other as human beings and also suggests a relationship of responsibility between state and individual."
2. Introduction, p. 256: institutions "are themselves vulnerable to a variety of internal and external corruptions and disruptions", grounds for monitoring "in processes that are both transparent and inclusive".
3. Part I, p. 260: "Autonomy is not an inherent human characteristic, but must be cultivated by a society that pays attention to the needs of its members".
4. Part II, p. 264, on derivative dependency: "This form of dependency is neither inevitable, nor is it universally experienced."
5. Part II, p. 265: reallocating dependency's burdens to state and market was appropriate "since both state and market institutions reaped the benefits that care work produced in the form of the reproduction and regeneration of society."
6. Part IV, p. 269: "the counterpoint to vulnerability is not invulnerability, for that is impossible to achieve, but rather the resilience that comes from having some means with which to address and confront misfortune."
7. Part IV, p. 272: "To the extent that these legally constituted institutions distribute significant social goods, they should be monitored by the state."
8. Part IV, p. 273: "Riddled with their own vulnerabilities, society’s institutions cannot eradicate, and often operate to exacerbate, our individual vulnerability."
9. Conclusion, p. 274: "the state has an obligation not to tolerate a system that unduly privileges any group of citizens over others."
10. Conclusion, p. 275: "there would be an affirmative obligation on the state and its institutions to offer explanations justifying the disparate circumstances."

## Assumptions

- Equality of opportunity and access is the foundational commitment to be realized (Part I), asserted rather than argued.
- The state brings into legal existence, and dissolves, the institutions that distribute social goods (p. 272).
- A US constitutional and policy setting throughout: a political-legal argument about the state, not a general theory of wholes.

## Concepts

Vulnerable subject and liberal subject; universal, constant, complex and particular vulnerability; inevitable and derivative dependency; resilience; five kinds of assets (physical, human, social, ecological or environmental, existential); institutional vulnerability; the responsive state; a duty to justify privilege.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-tmp9bbpk](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmp9bbpk.md)**: about one whole in [TERM-074](../terms.d/TERM-074.md)'s sense 3, the state, and its persons. For it, the essay supports three of A12's grounds. Vulnerability (p. 255); constitution and governance, since the state constitutes the institutions that confer resilience and must monitor them (p. 272); and contribution, since state and market benefit from care work (p. 265). It is not an argument about wholes and parts in general, and does not transfer to sense 1, where the whole need not be an agent and the parts need not be persons. Filed as `bears_on`, with a dated note.
- **[CLAIM-tmpwn3wr](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpwn3wr.md)** (as retitled at the owner's review of [#488](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/488) on 2026-11-13: a transformation that increases a constituent's vulnerability to a responsible agent's discretion can strengthen that agent's responsibilities; until then, responsibilities growing with integration): qualified, for the sense-3 wholes the claim reaches (an agent, parts with standing). Under either title the reading does not support it. Fineman's vulnerability is universal and constant, not produced by a whole's taking over its parts' functions, and the state's responsibility does not wait on integration; it follows the state's role in constituting the institutions that confer resilience. That supports the claim's governance clause (A12 §10) more than its integration clause. Her derivative dependency, socially imposed, is the nearest thing to integration-made vulnerability. The support judgement is [ARG-tmpmc9mt](../arguments.d/ARG-tmpmc9mt.md), Rejected for the claim as titled.
- **[CLAIM-624](../claims.d/CLAIM-624.md)** (a second-order duty of answerability): corroborated, which the first search missed. The duty "to offer explanations justifying the disparate circumstances" (p. 275) and monitoring "in processes that are both transparent and inclusive" (p. 256) are a duty of justification and contestability on the state and its institutions. Filed as `corroborates`, with a dated note.
- **[CLAIM-511](../claims.d/CLAIM-511.md)** (an organization ought to preserve its corrigibility): bears on it; institutions call for "monitoring, evaluating, updating, and reforming" (p. 273). No relation written, since the reading argues it for the state's institutions, not the organization's own.
- **[CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md)**: Fineman too replaces self-sufficiency with reciprocity between state and individual (p. 257), without anything on capability loss. Not written as a relation.
- **A12's description** (universal vulnerability; institutions confer resilience; equality, not persistence, as the ground) is accurate. The bearer of the responsibility is the state.

## Limitations

A US legal-policy essay. Its premise, equality of opportunity, is asserted. The five assets are listed with little analysis. Carrying the state's responsibility over to other wholes needs an argument the essay does not give.
