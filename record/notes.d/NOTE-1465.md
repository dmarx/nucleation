# NOTE-1465: Santolin et al. — Freidlin-Wentzell and stochastic thermodynamics

## Contribution

The diffusion-process counterpart of the bounds in Falasco & Esposito's
review ([LIT-1892](../literature.d/LIT-1892.md), §IV.H), which the authors cite as previously derived
for jump processes with a macroscopic limit ([17, 18]). Two things are
added: a recursive expansion of the quasi-potential I_ss in powers of
the nonconservative force, each order an integral along
detailed-balanced relaxation trajectories (eq. 36); and a proof of the
escape-rate bracket (eq. 76) from the orthogonal decomposition of the
drift, with a numerical illustration in a sheared double well (§VIII).

## Key insight

Split the weak-noise drift as u = −d·∇I_ss + v with v·∇I_ss = 0
(eq. 45). Then relaxation and escape share the circulating part v and
differ only in the sign of the gradient part (eq. 61). The entropy flow
along either path is ∓ΔI_ss plus the same non-negative adiabatic term
v·d⁻¹·v (eqs. 68–70). So relaxation always produces at least ΔI_ss, and
escape at least −ΔI_ss; the rate e^{−ΩΔI_ss} sits between, and the gap
is twice the circulation's dissipation.

## Assumptions

- **Overdamped diffusion** with drift μ = −D·∇Φ + f and diffusion
  D ∝ 1/Ω (eqs. 3–6, §IV); autonomous; a unique steady state.
- **Weak noise**, Ω → ∞, with the large-deviation ansatz P ≍ e^{−ΩI}
  (eq. 23).
- **Isolated stable fixed points separated by a saddle**; limit cycles
  excluded (§VII).
- **Differentiable quasi-potential** along the paths used; caustics are not
  discussed.

## Key results

- **Emergent second law (eq. 32).** dₜI_ss(xₜ) + σ̇(xₜ) ≥ 0 along the
  deterministic dynamics: the entropy produced while relaxing bounds the
  drop in quasi-potential. An earlier derivation by Gaveau, Moreau and
  Toth (1998) "apparently went unnoticed".
- **Nonequilibrium expansion (eqs. 34–38).** I_ss = I⁽⁰⁾ + I⁽¹⁾ + …,
  I⁽⁰⁾ = ϕ, and u⁽⁰⁾·∇I⁽ᵏ⁾ is given by lower orders, so each order is a
  line integral along zeroth-order (detailed-balanced) trajectories. To
  first order the emergent second law is saturated (eq. 38). The linear
  response approximation works far from equilibrium wherever v·∇ϕ is
  small (eq. 48).
- **Instanton (eqs. 54–61).** ẋ = v + d·∇I_ss, against relaxation
  ẋ = v − d·∇I_ss; at f = 0 one is the time-reverse of the other.
- **Escape rate and bounds (eqs. 63, 72–76).**
  r ≍ e^{−Ω[I_ss(x_ν) − I_ss(x_i)]}, and
  −σ_{ν→i} ≤ lim Ω⁻¹ ln r ≤ σ_{i→ν}, with σ_{i→ν} = σ_a − ΔI_ss and
  σ_{ν→i} = σ_a + ΔI_ss.
- **Example (§VIII).** Φ = Ω(αx₁⁴ − βx₁² + x₂²) with shear f = γ(−x₂, x₁),
  α = 1, β = 1.5, Ω = 10–30. Relaxation entropy bounds the sampled rate
  function, tightly at γ = 0 (Fig. 4); first-order expansion departs from
  the sampled one as γ grows (Fig. 6); minimum-action instantons give the
  bracket of Fig. 8, which narrows as γ → 0. No numbers are given in the
  text for the bracket's width.

## My check

On dx = −Ax dt + √2 dW with A = [[1, g], [−g, 2]], I_ss = ½xᵀC⁻¹x from the
Lyapunov equation, and the endpoint x = (1, 0.5) with ΔI = I(x):

- g = 0: σ_relax = 0.75 = ΔI, σ_inst = −0.75 = −ΔI; the bracket is closed.
- g = 0.5: ΔI = 0.694, σ_relax = 0.771, σ_inst = −0.617.
- g = 1.5: ΔI = 0.719, σ_relax = 1.438, σ_inst = 0.000.
- g = 3: ΔI = 0.800, σ_relax = 4.000, σ_inst = +2.400.

Both inequalities hold, and in each case σ_relax − ΔI = σ_inst + ΔI, the
common adiabatic part, as eqs. (72)–(73) require. At g = 3 the escape
path *produces* entropy, so the upper bound on ln r is positive and says
nothing; the bracket's width grows quickly with the rotational drive.

## Concepts

- **Quasi-potential** — I_ss = lim −Ω⁻¹ ln P_ss, a Lyapunov function of
  the noiseless dynamics (§I).
- **Orthogonal decomposition** — u = −d∇I_ss + v, v ⊥ ∇I_ss (eq. 45).
- **Adiabatic entropy production** — σ̇_a = v·d⁻¹·v, the part that
  sustains the circulation, non-negative along relaxation and instanton
  alike (eq. 70).

## Connections

- Freidlin & Wentzell; Bouchet & Reygner 2016 for the nonreversible
  Eyring–Kramers prefactor (cited, appendix A); Freitas & Esposito 2022
  for the emergent second law; [LIT-1892](../literature.d/LIT-1892.md) for the jump-process version.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CASE-189](../cases.d/CASE-189.md).** For a diffusion model of a maintained regime, this
  is the theorem that converts "finite lifetime" into thermodynamic
  terms: the lifetime is e^{ΩΔI_ss}, and ΔI_ss lies between the
  dissipation of relaxing into the regime and minus that of escaping it.
  It does not say the more dissipative regime lives longer; that is true
  only where the bracket is tight ([THEORY-1264](../theory.d/THEORY-1264.md)).
- **[THEORY-671](../theory.d/THEORY-671.md).** The paper's bound is a constraint on rates, not a
  selection principle among steady states.
- **The A14 SLT analogy.** The quasi-potential plays the role a free
  energy plays at detailed balance; the disanalogy is the circulation v,
  which a Bayesian posterior (a Gibbs measure) does not have.
- Produces [THEORY-1264](../theory.d/THEORY-1264.md) with [LIT-1892](../literature.d/LIT-1892.md).

## Limitations

- Diffusions only; overdamped; fixed-point attractors.
- The example is two-dimensional, and computing instantons in higher
  dimension needs minimum-action methods whose cost the paper notes but
  does not assess.
- Prefactors are left out; at Ω = 30 they may matter.
