# ARG-tmpar7tl: Gardner's objection that Sen gives no argument for preferring capability to achievement qualifies the framing of the claim that support must be assessed by the capability trajectory it produces, and leaves its derivation standing, which holds for achieved functionings as well

## The inference

1. THEORY-tmpo67z2, the reading in NOTE-tmpsll57: Sen holds that "The central issue ... is the freedom to choose how to live" (IoJ p. 238, quoted by Gardner), so that public policy should secure capabilities, not achievements; Gardner: "But where is the argument?"
2. CLAIM-tmp6wuql states its conclusion in Sen's capability terms: support "cannot be ranked by present capability sets and has to be assessed by the capability trajectory it produces".
3. So if Sen's preference is unargued, the claim inherits an unargued choice of evaluative space.

## Critical questions

- **Does the evidence fit what the claim says?** Only for its framing. The derivation in CLAIM-tmp6wuql compares two supports in CLAIM-605's model. At t = 0 both deliver the demand (the same achieved functioning) and leave the same unaided capacity; by t = 5 the party under substitution achieves 0.23 unaided and the other 1.0. The divergence shows in achieved functionings as much as in capabilities. So the claim's point, that a snapshot cannot rank the two supports, holds in either space.
- **Is the source reliable?** For Sen's text, second-hand: the book (LIT-tmpl0haz) was not reached, and IoJ p. 238 is Gardner's quotation. For the objection, it is Gardner's own and reads as stated.
- **Verdict.** Proposed, as a qualification. The claim keeps its conclusion; its wording "capability trajectory" should be read as "the trajectory of what the party can reach and achieve", and its tie to Sen (through CLAIM-352) carries Gardner's open question with it.
