# THEORY-1459: The source of a state's revenue shapes its capacity and accountability: revenue earned by taxing citizens obliges the state to build a bureaucracy, stay present on the ground and bargain with taxpayers, while unearned income from natural-resource rents or aid does not, and lets those capacities decay

## Source

Moore (2004), [LIT-2182](../literature.d/LIT-2182.md); read in [NOTE-1663](../notes.d/NOTE-1663.md).

## What was actually shown

Nothing new was measured; the paper is a review and agenda essay.

- **The proposition**, stated (p. 299): "relative to other types of states,
  tax states will tend toward accountable, representative government."
  It is reconstructed from European state formation (Tilly, Brewer,
  Daunton) as an exchange of revenue for representation.
- **The rentier converse**, from the rentier-state literature: "Rentier
  states live largely off unearned income: the state is resourced with
  little organizational or political effort on the part of the state
  apparatus" (p. 304), with seven pathologies, among them neglect of the
  revenue machinery ("If the revenue-raising function is permitted to
  decay, weak states leave themselves vulnerable", p. 307) and no
  incentive to build a bureaucracy (pp. 307–308).
- **Oil**: others' cross-national work, which Moore says "convincingly
  demonstrates" that oil states are less democratic with weaker rule of
  law, on "partial or indirect measures" (p. 308).
- **Aid**: weak; "the causal connection may be partly or wholly reversed"
  (p. 306).
- **Concessional finance**: argued, not measured, to make governments
  "reject the negotiation route" (p. 311).

## What this does not say

- That reducing aid or rents would rebuild a state: Moore gives that "no
  serious chance of success" (p. 310), and he declines to say whether more
  tax reliance would pay a governance dividend.
- Anything about how much unearned income does harm: no thresholds, and
  the model "does not take into account the absolute size of oil rents"
  (p. 308).
- That the evidence for aid matches the evidence for oil.
- What happens when aid tapers or stops.
