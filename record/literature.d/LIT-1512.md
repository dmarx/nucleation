# LIT-1512: Mean Field Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

Yang, Y., Luo, R., Li, M., Zhou, M., Zhang, W. & Wang, J. (2018). Mean field multi-agent reinforcement learning. *Proceedings of the 35th International Conference on Machine Learning*, PMLR 80: 5571–5580. arXiv:1802.05438.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1148](../notes.d/NOTE-1148.md). Section and equation numbers are v5's.*

- **The approximation.** Q^j(s, a) ≈ (1/N^j) Σ_k Q^j(s, a^j, a^k) ≈
  Q^j(s, a^j, ā^j), with ā^j the empirical distribution of the
  neighbours' actions. The first-order term vanishes because the
  deviations sum to zero; the second-order remainder is bounded for an
  M-smooth Q and argued to cancel under homogeneity and locality.
- **The algorithm.** Each agent learns a best response to its neighbours'
  mean action with a Boltzmann policy, and the mean actions are
  recomputed from the new policies, alternately (Eqs. 9–12).
- **The theorem.** MF-Q converges to the Nash Q-value w.p.1 if every
  state-action pair is visited infinitely often, the policy is greedy in
  the limit, and every stage game met in training has a global optimum or
  a saddle point as its Nash equilibrium, an assumption the authors call
  "a strong constraint" (Theorem 1).
- **Experiments.** Gaussian Squeeze with 100, 500 and 1000 agents; a
  20 × 20 Ising model, whose order parameter and Curie temperature
  (τ ≈ 1.2) MF-Q matches against MCMC; a 64-versus-64 battle in which
  MF-Q outperforms independent Q-learning and actor-critic.
