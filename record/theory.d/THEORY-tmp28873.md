# THEORY-tmp28873: Generalized contextuality is strictly broader than Kochen–Specker contextuality: a qubit already has no preparation-noncontextual model, though no Kochen–Specker argument exists in dimension two

## Source

Spekkens (2005), [LIT-tmpevs68](../literature.d/LIT-tmpevs68.md), §III, §IV (Eqs. 13–36). Budroni et al. (2022), [LIT-tmp0el4c](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0el4c.md), §IV.E.1–4 (restatement; the experimental report of Mazurek et al. 2016 is cited there, not read).

## What was actually shown

Six pure qubit states give five decompositions of the maximally mixed state; distinguishable preparations cannot overlap in their ontic supports, and preparation noncontextuality then forces every distribution to vanish (§IV). Traditional Kochen–Specker proofs need dimension at least three, because a two-dimensional projective measurement has no non-trivial fine-grainings (§III). Budroni et al. restate the proof and note that Bell's and Kochen & Specker's own qubit models are preparation contextual (§IV.E.1, §IV.E.4), and cite an experiment reporting a violation of the noncontextual bound (§IV.E.2–3).

## What this does not say

- That a qubit is Kochen–Specker contextual; it is not.
- That preparation contextuality has been shown loophole-free; tomographic completeness is assumed (Budroni et al. §IV.E.3).
- Which notion is the right criterion of classicality. That rests on Spekkens's Leibnizian methodological principle, which a reader may reject.
