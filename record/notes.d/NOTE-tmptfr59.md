# NOTE-tmptfr59: Brenninger, Zug & Kokko (2025) — Infection dynamics of endosymbionts that manipulate arthropod reproduction

## Contribution

One framework (relative fecundity F, vertical transmission T, manipulation efficiency β) for all known reproductive manipulations, closing gaps where dynamics had not been modelled, and contrasting ease of spread with observed incidence.

## Key results

- §I: the interests of symbiont and host are "somewhat, but typically not completely, aligned".
- §I: "a male is a dead-end for the endosymbiont, and selection favours manipulations that increase the proportion of infected females in the host population".
- Abstract: "endosymbionts that a priori have the best invasion prospects are not necessarily the most commonly found ones in nature; priority effects play a role too, and cytoplasmic incompatibility excels in this regard."
- §VIII.2: "any mechanism that kills a male will suffice for a MK parasite, while transforming males into functional females is a far more specific task."
- §IX: in Armadillidium vulgare "a 3 MB insert from a feminizing Wolbachia genome functions as a novel female-determining sex chromosomal region" (the hyphen falls at a line end in the PDF; checked by the filer with pdftotext -raw, which shows "female-" before the break).
- §IX: Eurema mandarina "relies on the symbiont for female development, as removal of the infection results in all-male offspring".
- §X(4): CI's "invasion from rare is independent of its efficiency".

## Assumptions

Panmictic host population, non-overlapping generations, unbiased sex ratio in uninfected females, exclusively vertical transmission, no co-infection of individuals, at most two infections (§III.1).

## Concepts

Reproductive parasites; maternal vs paternal-origin manipulation; positive frequency dependence; priority effect; basic rate of increase; Jekyll-and-Hyde symbioses; host switching.

## Bearing on the record

**On the LIT's and the exchange's characterization.** Accurate. It could add the priority-effect explanation for CI's prevalence and that the review is about population dynamics, not mechanism.

- **CLAIM-tmpu69tn** (provenance, control and alignment separate): **supports A21's use** of Wolbachia: the manipulation acts on which offspring are produced or survive, not on the host's choices. It also supplies two cases the claim's table lacks, from the review's §IX: foreign-origin material that has become part of the host's own sex determination (Armadillidium), and a host that now depends on the symbiont for female development (Eurema). In these, origin outside, integration as host machinery and mixed alignment come apart, which fits the claim.
- **CLAIM-354** (autonomy in degrees, incl. viable reachability): **leaves open**. One could read reproductive manipulation as narrowing the host's reachable reproductive outcomes, but the review uses no such framing, and the claim concerns agents' will.

- Not held under another LIT (no DOI or "Kokko"/"cytoplasmic incompat" hits; "Wolbachia" appears only in NOTE-159, SEP philosophy of microbiology).
- Further held entries: **CASE-174** (Paulinella) and **CASE-177** (Hodgkinia) — endosymbiosis cases on the record; Brenninger's Eurema and Armadillidium examples are reproductive-manipulator parallels on host dependence and integration of symbiont material.

## Limitations

Only the framing, conclusions and evolutionary sections read; derivations unchecked. Incidence data are patchy, as the authors say (§VIII.3).
