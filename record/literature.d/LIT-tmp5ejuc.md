# LIT-tmp5ejuc: Induction and Profiling of Strong Multi-Componential Emotions in Virtual Reality

Ben Meuleman & David Rudrauf (2021), *IEEE Transactions on Affective
Computing* 12(1):189–202 — DOI-10.1109/TAFFC.2018.2864730

## Key takeaways

- **Why passive induction fails** (§1.1). Pictures and films make
  motivation and behaviour "ambiguous or inappropriate". Hypotheses "that
  involve all components of emotion simultaneously, such as componential
  synchronization" cannot be tested, because "Testing these hypotheses is
  prohibited in emotion induction paradigms that do not engage adequately
  all mental and bodily subsystems simultaneously" (ms p. 2).
- **Design** (§2). Seven commercial games for the HTC Vive, chosen for one
  or two dominant emotions. 53 participants, each playing 1–6 games, with
  a questionnaire after each: CoreGRID-derived physiology, motivation and
  feeling items, added appraisal and regulation items, the Geneva Emotion
  Wheel, and a body manikin.
- **Clusters** (§3.2). Item clustering gave a fear cluster (danger,
  unpleasantness, escape and stop tendencies, arousal, regulation by
  looking away or reminding oneself it is virtual) and a joy cluster
  (pleasantness, tendencies to stay absorbed, explore, repeat). A gaming
  cluster lacked physiology and feeling items and was not counted as an
  emotion.
- **Appraisal predicts best** (§3.3). The appraisal-only model fits fear
  (marginal R² 61.3%) and joy (25.7%) better than any other single
  component by AIC. Danger and pleasantness are the strongest predictors.
- **Multi-componential means across observations.** An emotion counted
  as "fully multi-componential" if its intensity had "At least one
  significant association with each emotion component" across the
  observations (ms p. 7, note 2). Only fear, joy and pleasure qualified.
- **Self-report throughout** (§4). "our study relied entirely on
  self-report, even for objectively measurable emotion components such as
  physiology" (ms p. 13).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 for the record's question of how to measure which
ordering is the potential in a negotiation ([QUESTION-tmpsqwu5](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpsqwu5.md)), as set out
in [NOTE-tmp4itog](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp4itog.md). It gives no per-episode measure of component
synchronization. It argues that active induction is a precondition for
one, and its componentiality criterion is a pattern of associations
across a sample. Its mild-fear cluster, fear appraisal together with a
strong tendency to explore, is a case of competing tendencies in one
episode, but the data cannot say which prevailed. Mohammadi and
Vuilleumier ([LIT-tmp7rygg](LIT-tmp7rygg.md)) cite it as the one prior study
with the same aim.

It is affective-computing methodology for emotion induction, not an
instruction for machine-learning practice.
