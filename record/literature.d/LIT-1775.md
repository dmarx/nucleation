summary: >-
  Brewer argues that close relationships and group memberships are two distinct forms of social identity, each a way the individual self is extended to include others: the relational self is personalized (particular, non-interchangeable others), the collective self depersonalized (a symbolic group). They are not two routes to one need for belonging. Measures of group and interpersonal attachment (r = .46 and .33) and of loneliness separate them, and men and women weight them differently. She models each level as a regulatory system balancing opposed needs (uniqueness and similarity; autonomy and intimacy; separation and inclusion), with transfer between them. The model predicts two collectivisms: relational collectivists draw tight in-group boundaries, while individualists identify more readily with large inclusive groups.
