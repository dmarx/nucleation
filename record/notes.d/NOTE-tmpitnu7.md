# NOTE-tmpitnu7: Personal Identity

## Contribution

The paper turns the division case from a puzzle about which answer is right
into an argument that the question does not matter. Before it, fission was
an objection to the psychological criterion (Williams's reduplication
argument). After it, fission is the case that separates identity from what
matters, and the psychological relations become the object of concern in
their own right. It also introduces q-memory into the identity debate, the
connectedness/continuity distinction, and a vocabulary of successive selves.

## Key insight

Ask what would make division worse than ordinary survival. The relation I
bear to each resulting person is the one I would bear to the only resulting
person if the other half-brain had been destroyed, and that would be
survival. So the only thing division lacks is uniqueness, and uniqueness
cannot be what I care about: "To regard these as the same is to confuse
nought with two" (p. 10). Identity is what we use to imply the relations
that matter, because in fact they come one-to-one.

## Assumptions

- **Brain-transplant survival.** "Most of us would agree, after thought,
  that the resulting person is me. I shall here assume such agreement"
  (p. 5). Footnote 5 says those who disagree need a different, multiple
  case, which the paper does not give.
- **Half-brain survival is possible.** "People have in fact survived with
  half their brains destroyed" (p. 5), and each half is "exactly similar"
  (p. 5).
- **Attitudes are criticisable.** An emotion "can be criticized for resting
  on a false belief, or for being inconsistent" (p. 9). The division
  argument works by showing regarding division as death to be one or the
  other.
- **Psychological continuity includes causal continuity** (p. 11). Footnote
  19 relies on it to answer Williams's objection about exact similarity.
- **The cases are imagined.** The paper says the problem cases "could never
  occur" may be false, "some, for instance, might become scientifically
  possible", but its claims do not need them to occur (p. 3).

## Key results

- **§I. Division (pp. 4–10).**
  - Three descriptions of Wiggins's case: I do not survive; I survive as
    one; I survive as both. The first and second are "highly implausible"
    (p. 5).
  - "I survive with two bodies and a divided mind" is not absurd. The exam
    case: with voluntary control over the bridge between the hemispheres,
    one could divide one's mind, work two calculations, and reunite. "A
    person's mental history need not be like a canal, with only one
    channel. It could be like a river, with islands" (p. 7).
  - But with two bodies, permanently, "it would become intolerable to deny
    that they were different people" (p. 7). Keeping identity by letting
    two people compose a third ("the Pope's three crowns") changes the
    concept of a person; the duel in footnote 8 shows the cost.
  - The paper's first conclusion: the relation to each resulting person
    "contains all that interests us—all that matters—in any ordinary case of
    survival" (p. 10).
- **§II. A criterion that survives branching (pp. 10–14).**
  - "Identity is a one-one relation. Wiggins' case serves to show that what
    matters in survival need not be one-one" (p. 10). "Identity is
    all-or-nothing. Most of the relations which matter in survival are, in
    fact, relations of degree" (p. 11).
  - Williams objected that a criterion must appeal to a logically one-one
    relation. Parfit answers that psychological continuity is "a ground for
    speaking of identity when it is one-one" (p. 12). He turns Williams's
    own principle, that an important judgment be asserted and denied only on
    importantly different grounds, against the Guy Fawkes argument: if
    continuity is the important ground, the branching case should attach
    the importance to each branch (p. 13).
  - The criterion: "X and Y are the same person if they are psychologically
    continuous and there is no person who is contemporary with either and
    psychologically continuous with the other" (p. 13). Footnote 17: a
    sufficient, not a necessary, condition.
- **§III. Relations without identity (pp. 14–18).**
  - Q-memory: "I am q-remembering an experience if (1) I have a belief about
    a past experience which seems in itself like a memory belief, (2) someone
    did have such an experience, and my belief is dependent upon this
    experience in the same way (whatever that is) in which a memory of an
    experience is dependent upon it" (p. 15). Following Shoemaker's
    quasi-memory.
  - That the remembered experience was mine "is something that I
    automatically assume", justified only because we do not in fact
    q-remember others' experiences (p. 15). "Memories are, simply,
    q-memories of one's own experiences" (p. 16); this "vindicates the
    'memory criterion' of personal identity against the charge of
    circularity".
  - Q-intention: the original person in division could "q-intend, as one of
    them, to continue his present career, and, as the other, to try something
    new" (p. 17), with limits set out in footnote 28.
  - A "bolder claim", that experiences might be thought of "in a wholly
    'impersonal' way", is named and not developed (p. 17).
- **§IV. Degree (pp. 18–21).**
  - Fusion: two people's bodies grow into one; compatible tastes and
    intentions combine, incompatible ones cancel or weaken (Palladio and
    Giotto; red hair and votes). Fusion is "not clearly survival", "not
    clearly failure to survive", "and hence … what matters in survival can
    have degrees" (p. 19).
  - Beings who reproduce by natural division form a "tree". Connectedness
    "requires the holding of these direct psychological relations" and "is
    not transitive"; continuity "only requires overlapping chains" and "is
    transitive" (p. 20).
  - "I suggest that connectedness is a more important element in survival.
    As a claim about our own survival, this would need more arguments than I
    have space to give" (p. 21).
- **§V. Successive selves (pp. 21–25).**
  - "Descendant self" and "ancestral self" imply continuity; "past self" and
    "future self" imply connectedness and come in degrees, from "my most
    recent self" to "not in any way one of my past selves—just an ancestral
    self" (p. 22).
  - For beings that fuse every autumn and divide every spring, "I" would
    cover too little; for everlasting beings with fading connectedness, it
    would carry none of its usual implications (pp. 23–24).
  - The revision: draw the line between successive selves by degree of
    connectedness, "left to the choice of the speaker" (p. 25). Unlike
    Proust, "there is no underlying person who we both are". Whether to say
    "I" or "one of my future selves" "is entirely a matter of choice".
- **§VI. Consequences (pp. 26–27).**
  - "The principle of self-interest has no force." Its competitors are
    biased rationality ("do what will best achieve what you actually want")
    and impartiality; it borrows its force from one or the other.
  - Egoism, the fear of distant death, and regret that so much of "one's
    only life" has gone by "are all strengthened by the beliefs about
    personal identity which I have been attacking" (p. 27).
  - The closing question: can these be weakened without weakening "loyalty
    to, or love of, other particular selves"? It ends on Hume, hoping "more
    of what is bad depends upon false belief".

## Concepts

- **survival** — at first equivalent to "will there be some person alive who
  is the same person as me?"; then used in a sense that "does not imply
  identity", so that one can survive as two (pp. 9, 14).
- **q-memory / q-intention** — memory and intention minus the requirement
  that the experience or action be one's own (pp. 15, 17).
- **psychological connectedness** — the holding of direct psychological
  relations (q-memory, q-intention, a lasting q-characteristic); not
  transitive, a matter of degree (p. 20).
- **psychological continuity** — overlapping chains of direct relations;
  transitive; runs in one direction of time (p. 20 and fn. 29).
- **ancestral / descendant self** — linked by continuity (p. 21).
- **past / future self** — linked by connectedness, by degree (p. 22).

## Connections

- **Williams, "The Self and the Future"** (filed beside this reading).
  Footnote 11 credits it with showing how natural it is to believe that
  the question "Shall I survive?" must have an answer, all-or-nothing, with a
  "risk" of getting it wrong. Parfit says those beliefs must be undermined by
  their causes, and locates them in misunderstanding remembering and
  anticipating. §II answers Williams's earlier reduplication argument
  (1956–57, 1960–61), not the 1970 paper.
- **Locke** (filed beside this reading). Footnote 2 says division is
  "implicit" in the Essay II.xxvii.18. §III's q-memory answers the charge of
  circularity pressed against memory criteria "from Butler onward".
- **Lewis, "Survival and Identity"** (filed beside this reading) is a reply
  to this paper, accepting C1 and C5–C6 and denying C2.
- **Survey** ([LIT-130](../literature.d/LIT-130.md), [NOTE-166](NOTE-166.md)). The survey's C1 is this paper's C1–C2. The

## Limitations

What is shown and what is asserted:

- **Shown, given its assumptions:** C1 and so C2. The assumptions are stated,
  and footnote 5 concedes the argument needs a different case for those who
  deny transplant survival, without supplying it.
- **Shown as coherent, not as true of us:** q-memory and the impersonal
  description (C5). The bolder "impersonal" claim is named and set aside.
- **Asserted:** C7, explicitly; and C8, as a proposal about how to speak.
- **Sketched:** §VI's consequences for rationality and the emotions. The
  argument against the self-interest principle is two pages with an analogy
  to nationalism.
- **Not specified:** what psychological continuity is, how much the
  criterion requires (p. 13), and the "same way" of dependence in q-memory.

## Open questions

- What is the dependence condition in q-memory, and does a non-standard
  cause (a transcript, a copy) satisfy it? The paper's parenthesis
  "(whatever that is)" leaves the question that later decides teletransporter
  and AI cases.
- If connectedness matters more than continuity, how much does it matter,
  and at what rate does prudential concern for a future self fall? The paper
  gives degrees without a measure.
