The owner asked for Posani, Wang, Muscinelli, Paninski & Fusi (2026),
"Rarely categorical, highly separable representations along the cortical
hierarchy", to be read and filed here. The anthology already holds it as
[ANTH-LIT-696](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-696.md), so this is an [ADR-013](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-013.md) second reading ([LIT-708](../../record/literature.d/LIT-708.md), with a
read NOTE).

The anthology reads the paper for what cortex implies about concept
geometry in machine learning. This record re-reads it for a different
question: do neurons within a cortical area form discrete functional types
or a mixed-selectivity continuum, and what does that do to the
separability of what the area represents? The paper is not read as
evidence about abstraction, because it measures separability, not
generalisation. No `anthology-candidate` flag is added, because the work
is already held there.

One Proposed theory, [THEORY-124](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-124.md): neurons within a single mouse
cortical area rarely form discrete functional types, yet the task
conditions the area distinguishes are linearly separable in nearly every
way. Its scope is the IBL task and the paper's categoricality test. The
contrast with neural collapse ([THEORY-083](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-083.md)) is explained in prose. The two
senses of "categorical" differ: here it means neuron types, and there it
means the arrangement of conditions.
