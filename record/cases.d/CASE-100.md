# CASE-100: A fan redub of an X-Men cartoon, circulated online in 2006, put "I'm the Juggernaut, bitch!" into X-Men: The Last Stand three months later

## The case

The owner, at U7: "a potentially informative example of a fandom 'making
something happen' is how fandoms and content can interact. as a concrete
example, consider the addition of the 'I'm the juggernaut, bitch!' meme to
one of the X-Men films."

Checked on 2026-10-16. Xavier Nazario and Randy Hayes redubbed "The
Phoenix Saga, Part 3: Cry of the Banshee", an episode of the 1990s
*X-Men* animated series, as "Juggernaut, Bitch!!". They had the idea in
November 2005 and released it on 14 February 2006. In *X-Men: The Last
Stand*, released in the US on 26 May 2006, Vinnie Jones's Juggernaut says
the line while fighting Kitty Pryde. Alan Siegel's oral history in The
Ringer (2024-03-20, theringer.com/2024/03/20/oral-history/x-men-juggernaut-viral-video-oral-history),
[LIT-1142](../literature.d/LIT-1142.md), interviews the creators and quotes the screenwriter
Simon Kinberg, via Polygon: "That was a reshoot Ratner line." A7 dated the
parody to November 2005; that is when it was conceived, not released.

## What it can show

A7: "a publicly observable change in an external artifact that can be
causally traced to activity within a fan community", with no one needing
to intend the result. Different agents did different stages: two fans
made it, a distributed audience selected and amplified it, production
staff took it up. "Those locally intelligible actions collectively
changed the informational environment in which the film's creators
operated." On A7's ladder it reaches collective causal efficacy and not
collective intention, which makes it a cleaner case than the record's
others of "having effects as a collective and acting as a collective
agent" ([QUESTION-119](../questions.d/QUESTION-119.md)).

A7 §4 drew the second lesson: the system that produced the outcome may
not be the fandom but a passing coalition of creators, sharers,
commentators and production staff, so "we shouldn't automatically credit
'the fandom' with every outcome resulting from activity somewhere within
it" ([CLAIM-152](../claims.d/CLAIM-152.md)).

It cannot show that the X-Men fandom acted as an agent, that the
spreaders were its members rather than a general internet audience, or
that circulation was the cause of the reshoot decision. A7's
strengtheners: production testimony on the decision, and evidence
tracing the parody's circulation into the production.
