# CASE-167: The nitroplast: UCYN-A, a streamlined nitrogen-fixing cyanobacterium in a marine alga, whose division is coupled to the host's and which imports host-encoded proteins, reclassified as an early-stage organelle in 2024

## The case

UCYN-A is a nitrogen-fixing cyanobacterium with a streamlined
metabolism, previously reported as an endosymbiont of a unicellular marine
alga. Coale et al. ([LIT-1562](../literature.d/LIT-1562.md)) show that it is tightly integrated into the
alga's cell architecture and organellar division, and that it imports
proteins encoded by the algal genome. They call these characteristics of
organelles, and conclude that UCYN-A "has evolved beyond endosymbiosis" as an
early-stage nitrogen-fixing organelle, the "nitroplast". Nitrogen fixation
had been known only in prokaryotes. [LIT-1560](../literature.d/LIT-1560.md) places it among the recently
evolved organelles with protein import.

## What it can show

- **A boundary drawn by criteria.** Coale et al. name two criteria for
  calling it an organelle: division coupled to the host's, which is
  cohesion (R), and imported host-encoded proteins, which is control (C).
  That makes the case a test of whether those dimensions mark the
  transition [QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md) asks about.
- **Recency.** Like *Paulinella* ([CASE-174](CASE-174.md)), it lets organellogenesis
  be studied without relying only on the ancient mitochondrion and plastid.

Documented in a work the record has not read. What it shows rests on its
abstract until it is read.

## Note of 2026-10-27 (batch 2): the review read

Sørensen et al. ([LIT-1560](../literature.d/LIT-1560.md), read in [NOTE-1241](../notes.d/NOTE-1241.md)) confirm the case from
Coale et al.'s results: one UCYN-A per host cell, coordinated division,
vertical transmission, and more than 350 imported nucleus-encoded
proteins. They add three points:

- **Import without transfer.** No gene transfer from UCYN-A to the nucleus
  has been found. Import alone gives genetic integration ([THEORY-1100](../theory.d/THEORY-1100.md)).
- **A new signal.** 62% of imported proteins carry a C-terminal "UCYN-A
  transit peptide", presumably cleaved, unlike any known organelle signal.
- **The host's dependence is conditional.** "When nitrogen compounds are
  externally provided in culture, B. bigelowii can lose UCYN-A". So the
  host's dependence (D) depends on its environment, while the symbiont's
  does not. R and C are high, and D is asymmetric.

UCYN-A's genome is about 1.4 Mbp, and the partnership is about 91 million
years old.
