# ARG-tmp8gimi: A population of operators that maximize fixed objectives and comes to hold durable authority would show self-constitution

## The inference

[CLAIM-284](../claims.d/CLAIM-284.md) is meant to be tested by building populations. A10 §7: "**Sufficiency
tests.** Construct systems satisfying the proposed organizational
conditions without adding predefined self-models or executive
controllers." A7's ideal first result is a population that develops
"specialized niches, persistent but revisable trust, and higher-level
governance structures solely through local transformation, temporally
discounted policy evaluation, and constrained endogenous endorsement." In
the model the exchange built, each operator is rewarded for something the
modeller wrote down: J_i = E[Q_i(a*, c)] − λ_i Cost(E_i) in A2 §6,
discounted reward r_i in A6 §1.

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the hypothesis were false?** Yes. A
  designer's reward can produce a system that maintains itself without
  that system setting its own norms. A10 §6.1 named the boundary case and
  left it open: "a system that learns to maintain itself only because an
  external designer rewards that behavior" ([CASE-081](../cases.d/CASE-081.md)).
- **Does it tell the hypothesis apart from its rivals?** Not from the rival
  in which governance arises from fixed local objectives, set from
  outside.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** No. This is the question
  that fails. [CLAIM-288](../claims.d/CLAIM-288.md): a population of reward-maximizing operators that
  came to hold durable, differentiated authority "would show that
  endogenous governance can arise from fixed local objectives. It would
  not show that the organization sets the conditions it maintains, and
  that is the constitutive part of the thesis." The ground is [CLAIM-288](../claims.d/CLAIM-288.md)'s
  subject-matter claim, from Barandiaran, Di Paolo and Rohde ([THEORY-071](../theory.d/THEORY-071.md):
  "Systems optimizing an externally fixed function are not models of
  agency") and Di Paolo ([LIT-1017](../literature.d/LIT-1017.md), [THEORY-628](../theory.d/THEORY-628.md)): norms must be norms *for*
  the system.

Proposed. [CLAIM-288](../claims.d/CLAIM-288.md) undercuts this inference. It is answered by operators
whose objectives are themselves produced, weighted and revised by the
organization, such as the viability of the coalition they belong to
(A10 §3.2), or by an argument that the modeller's objectives are only
initial conditions the organization then revises (A10 §6.1, the historical
origins of agency against its current constitutive organization).
[CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md) warns that viability alone does not settle what ought to
persist. The simulations still test [CLAIM-277](../claims.d/CLAIM-277.md) and [CLAIM-279](../claims.d/CLAIM-279.md), which make no
constitutive claim.

## Applied to training

The exchange of 2026-10-17 applied the point to training. A30 §8: "the
task loss is generally chosen by an experimenter. Lower loss is desirable
to the trainer, but that doesn't mean it has the same status as an
endogenously maintained condition of an organism." [CLAIM-356](../claims.d/CLAIM-356.md) objects to
[CLAIM-288](../claims.d/CLAIM-288.md)'s subject-matter clause, not to this verdict: an objective that
originates outside a learning system can become a goal of the training
organization. It grants that stable structure "does not alone show that
the learned network governs or endorses the criterion that selected it".
A38 §6 and A40 §2.3 use the distinction as a design control, a comparison
"against an external optimizer that implements the same protective
policy".
