# QUESTION-070: When an organized whole manipulates, as a learning system exploiting documented biases or a network of state channels does, is it the same wrong when no member intends to manipulate, and whose agency does the wrong run through?

## The question

Susser, Roessler and Nissenbaum ([LIT-1002](../literature.d/LIT-1002.md)) define manipulation as
*intentionally* and covertly influencing someone's decisions by exploiting
their vulnerabilities. Most of the accounts Noggle surveys ([LIT-1007](../literature.d/LIT-1007.md))
also locate it in a manipulator's aim. The record holds two read cases
where that aim is hard to place:

- **[LIT-179](../literature.d/LIT-179.md)** ([NOTE-171](../notes.d/NOTE-171.md)): an RL agent learned to move a target's lottery
  choices from 50% to 70% by exploiting documented biases. The authors add
  that such a system may exploit biases that are not yet known. No one
  chose the exploit.
- **[LIT-084](../literature.d/LIT-084.md)** ([NOTE-060](../notes.d/NOTE-060.md)): the EEAS sorts information-manipulation channels
  into tiers by their tie to a state, from official to non-attributed and
  state-aligned. Many channels in the network may not know they are part
  of a campaign.

The question has two parts:

1. **Is it manipulation without an intending member?** If an organized
   whole's operative course can come apart from what its members intend
   ([CLAIM-030](../claims.d/CLAIM-030.md)), can the whole have the aim the definition requires?
2. **If it is, whose agency does the wrong run through?** [THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md) holds
   that a conversable group agent is fit to be held responsible. Does that
   reach a learning system, or a network with no authorised avowal?

## Why the record needs it

- **[CLAIM-030](../claims.d/CLAIM-030.md)** gets a test case in which its thesis has normative
  consequences, and not only explanatory ones.
- **[QUESTION-009](QUESTION-009.md)** (Deferred) asks when coordination constitutes an
  additional agent. This question asks a narrower thing that does not wait
  on that answer: whether manipulation needs a manipulator who is an agent.

## What the record holds

- **[THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md):** a group that forms judgments by authorised avowal, and
  corrects its members' inconsistent views, is fit to be held responsible.
  Its limit: neither case above has authorised avowal.

## What would answer it

An account of manipulation stated in terms of a function or a selection
history, not an intention, that classifies the two cases. Or a reason why
manipulation requires intention, so that these cases are something else:
exploitation, or harm without a wrongdoer.
