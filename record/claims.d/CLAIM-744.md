# CLAIM-744: Where one party gives up something now for a benefit another controls later, whether waiting is good judgment depends on the controller's reliability, so the controller's discretion over delivery is discretion over whether the other's restraint is rational, and a controller not answerable for delivery can make that party's patience a means of its control

## The claim

The owner's U23 linked A49 §4's "Self-restraint as an achievement of
agency" to the marshmallow test. A51 §2 brought in Kidd, Palmeri and
Aslin's version, in which children who had seen the experimenter break a
promise waited far less ([CASE-281](../cases.d/CASE-281.md)), and drew the first half:

> **A refusal to delay gratification need not indicate a failure of self-government. It may reflect an appropriate judgment about the trustworthiness of the surrounding organization.**

A51 §3 split who controls what: the child controls eating and attention;
the experimenter controls "the terms of the choice", when the reward comes
and whether the promise is kept. "the child controls the immediate act of
restraint but not the conditions determining whether restraint will pay
off." A51 §4 drew the second half, for employers, governments and
institutions that ask for sacrifice now against benefits later:

> **The virtue of patience can be exploited by actors who control the conditions under which patience is rewarded.**

and stated it as a proposition:

> **Where one actor controls the future benefits offered in exchange for another's present restraint, the legitimacy of the arrangement depends partly on the controlling actor's obligations to make those benefits credible and on the restrained actor's capacity to challenge the terms of the exchange.**

**The decision side** (A53 §2). One reward now or two later; p the
probability the promise is kept, δ the value of a delayed reward relative
to an immediate one, nothing if the promise fails. Waiting is better when
2pδ > 1: the expected value of waiting is 2pδ and of eating 1. Checked:
at δ = 0.9 waiting pays only if p > 0.556, and at δ = 0.5 only if p = 1. So
whoever sets p sets whether restraint is the rational choice, without
touching the restrained party's preferences or self-control. A: "This is
not a psychological measurement of the child's ability. It's a model of
the decision under uncertainty."

**Why it is control.** In the contract reading (A53 §2) "The child
performs first", the adult later, and "The interesting question is not
merely whether the promise is sincere. It's whether the relationship
gives the promisor the ability to change the terms after the other party
has incurred the costs." A controller unanswerable for delivery holds, in
[TERM-124](../terms.d/TERM-124.md)'s terms, a capacity to set the terms on which the other party
acts, and the other's virtue is the channel: "An institution can praise
people's willingness to wait while refusing to make itself reliably
accountable for what they are waiting for."

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-601](CLAIM-601.md)** (Active, demonstrated): a household that must survive
  shocks declines profitable delayed investments below a buffer that
  rises with the delay, a choice "that reads as impatience and is the right
  policy". That is the same misreading from the other side: there the
  risk is in the household's buffer, here in the controller's reliability.
- **[TERM-110](../terms.d/TERM-110.md)** (Proposed): a principal unable to commit to future terms
  revises them on what the agent reveals. A53 §2 relays it faithfully,
  with Gerardi and Maestri ([LIT-2046](../literature.d/LIT-2046.md)): "Excellence today can reduce
  bargaining power tomorrow." That is a mechanism by which a controller
  changes delivery after restraint is shown; this claim is the general
  condition it exploits.
- **[CLAIM-344](CLAIM-344.md)** (Active): resolve keeps a commitment effective while
  keeping the capacity to revise it. A51 §7: "**The capacity to wait and
  the judgment that waiting is warranted must not be conflated.**" Resolve
  is the first; this claim is about what the second depends on (the note
  of 2026-11-17 on [CLAIM-344](CLAIM-344.md)).
- **[CLAIM-728](CLAIM-728.md)**: who holds residual control decides what the
  contract left open. Delivery of a deferred reward is often such a
  contingency.

## What it does not say

- Not that the ordinary marshmallow task is a wrong. A51 §3: "That doesn't
  make the experiment coercive. The ordinary task is not a moral wrong."
- Not that patience is misguided. A51 §4: the exploitation "is not
  evidence that patience itself is misguided."
- Not that every deferred-reward arrangement dominates. One that is
  "credible, mutually authorized" with "reasonably secured" benefits is
  A51 §4's contrast; the claim needs a controller not answerable for
  delivery.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Kidd, Palmeri and Aslin, read
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-1963 — new theory of 2026-11-18 and the case, Proposed -->

Kidd, Palmeri and Aslin 2013, read in full ([NOTE-2036](../notes.d/NOTE-2036.md), [THEORY-1963](../theory.d/THEORY-1963.md)): it supports the claim's first half, and the claim needs it, since [CASE-281](../cases.d/CASE-281.md), which it grounds on, reports this study; hence `add_grounds` rather than `corroborates`. Children who had just seen the experimenter break promises waited a mean 181.57 s against 722.43 s (§3), and the authors' hypothesis is the claim's: "Waiting is only the rational choice if you believe that a second marshmallow is likely to actually appear after a reasonably short delay" (§1.2). The reliability evidence came from the same adult who controlled delivery, which fits the claim's framing. The paper says nothing about control, exploitation or answerability; the second half stays A's interpretation. The authors also say their data "do not demonstrate that self-control is irrelevant" (§4), which the claim does not deny. Watts et al. ([LIT-2752](../literature.d/LIT-2752.md)) and Sperber et al. ([LIT-2723](../literature.d/LIT-2723.md)) find the task's later predictions largely explained by background, consistent with but not a test of a reliability reading. No status changes.
