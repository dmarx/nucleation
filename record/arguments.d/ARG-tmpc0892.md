# ARG-tmpc0892: Zidda and colleagues' finding that people with PTSD first look at the neutral background of their own trauma cue later than trauma-exposed controls, taken as evidence of reduced context sampling, gives weak and indirect support to the direction in which the sampling account says people with persistent PTSD differ from trauma-exposed controls after trauma reminders

## The inference

The sampling account (THEORY-285) predicts that, once a harmful-context
belief is primed, people with persistent PTSD take in the focal element
and attenuate the periphery more than resilient people do. Zidda and
colleagues (THEORY-tmp1ov9x) primed with a cue specific to each
participant's trauma, compared PTSD with trauma-exposed controls, and found
the focal element reached equally fast and the surround reached later in
PTSD. That is the predicted ordering. The scoping review
(THEORY-tmp8n4s7) shows no closer study to set against it. So the result
is evidence in the predicted direction, weak because the study is small
and indirect because its design differs from the one the question names.

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the hypothesis were false?**
  Partly. A general slowing in PTSD (which the study also found, p = .046)
  would push every latency up, and the later region more in absolute
  terms. The interaction test (p = .030) and the null for the cue argue
  against slowing alone, but with 19 and 18 participants and outliers
  replaced by the mean, the separation is not secure.
- **Does it tell the hypothesis apart from its rivals?** Not well. A
  cue-dominance or disengagement account (attention held by the cue, the
  reading the scoping review gives sustained gaze) predicts the same
  ordering with no claim about withheld evidence. The study cannot
  separate attenuating the periphery because it seems uninformative from
  being held by the cue.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** This is the weakest
  step, and the one the alternative (CLAIM-tmpc7tmg) denies. The question
  asks for sampling of disambiguating periphery; the study measured the
  latency of the first look at a neutral one. Latency is not amount, and
  a neutral background disambiguates nothing. The inference holds only if
  later first looks at the surround are taken as a marker of the same
  policy that would, with an informative surround, withhold evidence. That
  is an assumption, not a finding.
- **Is the source reliable?** A peer-reviewed study with a structured
  diagnosis (CAPS), unmedicated participants and trauma-type-matched
  stimuli; small, with effects smaller than its power analysis assumed;
  not yet replicated.

The step is kept as weak and indirect support because the first question
is answered in part and the second leaves the direction intact; the third
is the owner's call between this and CLAIM-tmpc7tmg.
