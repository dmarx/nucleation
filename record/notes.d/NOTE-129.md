# NOTE-129: Effective ontic structural realism

## Contribution

Three versions of effective realism (ER) have been proposed in the BJPS for three problems facing scientific realism. Fraser and Vickers apply ER to non-relativistic quantum mechanics to avoid underdetermination among its interpretations. Williams and Fraser apply it to quantum field theory. Robertson and Wilson use it for the ontological status of entities in superseded theories. Ladyman and Lorenzetti argue that ontic structural realism (OSR), with its realism about modal structure and its scale-relative ontology, unifies these proposals and makes clear in what sense they are realist. They also answer a recent critique of the OSR treatment of quantum mechanics that they rely on.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §2 (pp. 4–13) covers the effective ontology of quantum mechanics. §2.1 treats Fraser and Vickers' effective realism. §2.2 (p. 10) rebuts Egg's challenge that structural realism cannot dissolve the measurement problem.
- §3 (p. 13) argues that OSR supplies the realist metaphysics that effective-realist accounts of quantum field theory lack.
- §4 (p. 16) takes up the ontology of old theories. Robertson and Wilson's emergent entities and levels of abstraction are replaced by scale-relative real patterns, so Newtonian gravitational forces and general-relativistic gravitation both exist at different scales (p. 19).
- §5 (p. 19) concludes that the three debates are unified under OSR and that structural realism should "be brought back into the debate".
