# NOTE-tmpvfh43: Bardsley et al. — Explaining focal points: cognitive hierarchy theory versus team reasoning

## Contribution

An experimental test of two explanations of focal points.

## Key results

1. **Team reasoning.** "Roughly, the individual asks ‘What should we do?’,
   and acts upon the answer in the expectation that other members of the
   group think and behave analogously." (§1.3, p. 11).
2. **Cognitive hierarchy.** "It is a fundamental feature of cognitive
   hierarchy theory that, at each level, a player believes that his
   opponent’s level is lower than his own" (§1.2, p. 6).
3. **Amsterdam.** "In every case, more than 60 per cent of coordinators
   chose the team-optimal response" (§4.2, p. 27).
4. **Nottingham.** "It seems that the Nottingham coordinators are
   responding as if they were guessers." (§3.3, p. 26).
5. **Two modes.** "Our experiments seem to have identified two modes of
   reasoning, each of which is sometimes used by players of coordination
   and Hi-Lo games." (§7, p. 33).

## Assumptions

Adult students; guessers' responses reveal pre-reflective salience.

## Concepts

Pickers, guessers, coordinators; primary and secondary salience; team
reasoning; cognitive hierarchy; Hi-Lo games.

## Bearing on the record

- **THEORY-1630** (Sugden, team reasoning), a parallel: an empirical test
  by the same author, showing team reasoning operates in some contexts
  only.
- **The owner's view**, support and qualification: an odd-one-out or
  symmetric focal point can be reached by asking what is best for us
  rather than by stacked expectations, but team reasoning still assumes
  the partner reasons likewise, and some people coordinate by guessing
  others' picks.

## Limitations

Adults only; nothing on developmental prerequisites; working-paper
pagination.
