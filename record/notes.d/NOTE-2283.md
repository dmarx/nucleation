# NOTE-2283: Kosterlitz's Nobel lecture, read for the defect argument, topological sectors and what the 1973 paper got wrong, as a stand-in for Kosterlitz and Thouless

## Contribution

How Kosterlitz and Thouless came to defect-driven transitions in two
dimensions, and the physics, told by the first author 43 years on, with
the 1973 paper's errors named. The reading is [THEORY-2206](../theory.d/THEORY-2206.md).

## Key results

1. **No long-range order** (p. 87): low-energy phonons give "a mean
   square deviation of atoms … increasing logarithmically with the size
   of the system".
2. **The way out** (p. 89): "there can be a more subtle type of order
   called topological order in some two dimensional systems."
3. **Sectors and barriers** (pp. 90–91). In the planar rotor the winding
   numbers "are numbers defining a particular metastable state.
   Transitions can take place from one metastable state to another only
   if a vortex-antivortex pair is formed", and "there is an energy barrier
   proportional to the logarithm of the system size to prevent such a
   transition". The 2D Heisenberg model has a topological invariant N
   too, but "the energy barrier separating configurations with different
   values of N is of order unity", "which implies that there is no
   ordered state for the 2D n = 3 Heisenberg magnet".
4. **A free vortex** (p. 92): "∆F = ∆E − T∆S = kBT(πK0(T) − 2) ln(L/a)":
   energy and entropy both ∝ ln L; below the temperature where they
   balance a free vortex has vanishing probability, above it "a finite
   concentration of free vortices". The lecture places this argument in
   the 1972 letter, not the 1973 paper.
5. **Screening, and the 1973 error** (pp. 92–93): "Kosterlitz and
   Thouless [15] derived this self consistent integral equation for the
   effective interaction energy [16]" (ref. 16 is the 1973 paper); "KT
   made an unnecessary approximation by replacing U(r) by K(r) … but this
   led to incorrect results. A proper treatment has been given by Young
   [21] who showed that this is equivalent to the renormalization group
   equations of Kosterlitz [17]" (Kosterlitz 1974).
6. **Criterion** (p. 93): "a transition between a phase of bound dipoles
   and a phase of free charges will happen when πK(∞) = 2".
7. **Universal jump** (p. 94): ρs(Tc−)/Tc = 2m²k_B/πħ², "an exact,
   inescapable prediction"; the helium data were plotted "before the
   authors were aware of our theoretical prediction".
8. **Melting** (p. 95): neglecting a term "was an unfortunate error … We
   assumed that dislocation unbinding led directly to an isotropic fluid
   which is now known to be wrong"; the hexatic phase has "algebraic
   orientational order".
9. **Superconducting films** (p. 98): "In our original paper, we stated
   that true superconductivity in a 2D superconducting film could not
   exist because of the finite penetration depth λ(T) which limits the
   range of the logarithmic interaction between vortices", beyond which
   "the vortices are always free at any T > 0"; in practice λ can be
   about a centimetre, so films behave as λ = ∞.

## Assumptions

Short-range interactions with U(1) or translational symmetry in exactly
two dimensions; a logarithmic interaction between defects across the
system; dilute vortices of unit charge; equilibrium.

## Concepts

BKT transition; topological defect; winding number and topological
sector (the 1970s sense, not Wen's quantum sense); 2D Coulomb gas;
fugacity; dielectric screening; spin stiffness; universal jump; hexatic
phase; penetration depth.

- **[LIT-2969](../literature.d/LIT-2969.md)**: stands in for the argument and its history, not the 1973
  equations or wording ([ADR-114](../decisions.d/ADR-114.md)). "Evades the Mermin–Wagner theorem" is
  loose: there is still no long-range order (item 1).
- **[CLAIM-841](../claims.d/CLAIM-841.md)** (XY row): **supports, with the attribution moved.** A
  phase of bound vortices with no long-range order and power-law
  correlations over a range of T, reached without tuning; the RG and
  the fixed line are Kosterlitz (1974), [LIT-3059](../literature.d/LIT-3059.md), and the 1973
  screening "led to incorrect results" near Tc ([ARG-198](../arguments.d/ARG-198.md)).
- **[CLAIM-694](../claims.d/CLAIM-694.md)** and **[CLAIM-606](../claims.d/CLAIM-606.md)**: **support, with a condition the claims
  lack.** Item 3 is a case of a topological invariant that protects only
  when the barrier between its sectors grows with system size: the
  planar rotor's winding sectors are protected (barrier ∝ ln L), the 2D
  Heisenberg model's N protects nothing (barrier O(1)).
- **[QUESTION-508](../questions.d/QUESTION-508.md)**: **narrows it for the BKT phase.** The free vortex's
  energy and entropy both scale as ln L (item 4) only because the
  symmetry is U(1) and the dimension is two; a finite interaction range,
  the penetration depth λ, frees vortices and ends the untuned phase
  (item 9).
- **[LEAD-209](../leads.d/LEAD-209.md)**: item 9 is a second kind of scale-introducing
  perturbation, a finite interaction range, beside the
  symmetry-breaking field.
- **[CLAIM-862](../claims.d/CLAIM-862.md)**: its BKT case and its "finite interaction range"
  perturbation.
- **[NOTE-124](NOTE-124.md)** (McKenzie): **background.** It cites BKT and the hexatic
  phase as predicted before observed; item 7 bears it out.

## Limitations

A retrospective by an author, partly autobiographical; its equations were
checked against Drouin-Touchette. It does not say which results are in
the 1973 paper and which in the 1972 letter, beyond items 4–5 and 9.
