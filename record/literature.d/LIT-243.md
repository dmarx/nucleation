# LIT-243: Hilbert Spaces and the Riesz Representation Theorem

Ben Adler (2021), *University of Chicago Mathematics REU 2021 (student expository paper)* — <https://math.uchicago.edu/~may/REU2021/REUPapers/Adler.pdf>

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmp4c5up](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp4c5up.md).*

- **Riesz, by projection (Thm 3.5).** With the inner product
  conjugate-linear in its first slot, every continuous linear φ is
  v ↦ ⟨u, v⟩ for a unique u, ‖u‖ = ‖φ‖; u = conj φ(w) w for a unit w
  normal to ker φ. Completeness is used only in the projection theorem.
- **Riesz, by basis (Thm 4.9).** u = Σ_k conj φ(e_k) e_k for any
  orthonormal basis, and ‖φ‖² = Σ_k |φ(e_k)|². The groundwork is an
  infinite Pythagorean theorem, Bessel's inequality and the description
  of a closed span as the square-summable combinations (Thms 4.5–4.8).
- **Radon–Nikodym (Thm 5.1).** For finite positive μ, ν with ν ≪ μ,
  Riesz on L²(ν + μ) gives g with ∫ f(1 − g) dν = ∫ f g dμ, then
  0 ≤ g < 1 a.e. and dν/dμ = g/(1 − g), by truncation and monotone
  convergence. The σ-finite, signed and complex cases are asserted, not
  proved.
- **No kernels.** The paper never mentions kernels or RKHS.
