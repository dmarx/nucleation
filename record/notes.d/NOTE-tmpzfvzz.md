# NOTE-tmpzfvzz: Campbell et al. — Idiosyncratic genome degradation in a bacterial endosymbiont of periodical cicadas

## Contribution

LIT-1612 found *Hodgkinia* broken into dozens of circles in one
periodical cicada, *Magicicada tredecim*. This paper sequences the
other six *Magicicada* species and two relatives and finds the same
condition in all of them, reached by different paths.

## Key results

- **Complex in every species** (Highlights; Results). "The Hodgkinia
  genome in all Magicicada exist as complexes of ≥20 circular
  molecules"; each species "harbors Hodgkinia populations of at least
  20 genomic circles". The smallest complex: 152 contigs, 1.20 Mb (*M.
  tredecula*); the largest: 212 contigs, 1.68 Mb (*M. neotredecim*).
  Completed circles range from 0.69 to 70.5 kb, with 1 to 27 genes.
- **Gene set kept** (Results). Each complex holds "between 135 and 145
  of the 186 unique protein- and RNA-coding genes annotated as
  functional in Hodgkinia genomes from other cicada species"; some of
  the rest may be present and unassembled.
- **Little synteny** (Summary). "little synteny among the 256 Hodgkinia
  circles analyzed except between the most closely related cicada
  species".
- **Origins** (Summary). "multiple Hodgkinia lineages in the common
  ancestor of Magicicada and its closest known relatives but ... most
  splitting has occurred within Magicicada".
- **Dosage** (Discussion, "Lineage Splitting Leads to Different Gene
  Dosages"). Relative gene abundances are similar within species
  groups and not between them. That "suggests that there is not a
  convergent pattern of gene dosage outcomes as might be expected if
  the host were dictating the Hodgkinia splitting process or if the
  process were beneficial to the Hodgkinia community in some way."
- **Size** (Discussion). The process "results in a combined Hodgkinia
  'genome' size that is an order of magnitude larger than the
  ancestral single genome".
- **The host's part** (Discussion). How products are shared between
  cells "is unknown, but it is likely that the host is heavily
  involved". The gene loss "is most likely only tolerable because from
  the host's perspective, the combined HGCs seem to have retained
  Hodgkinia's overall nutritional contribution".

## Assumptions

- Circularity is from mate-pair data; only some contigs were closed,
  and circles cover 51.4% to 72.5% of each complex's DNA.
- Absence of a gene is uncertain at low coverage and high divergence.
- "Nonadaptive" is inferred from the lack of convergence across
  species, not from fitness measures.

## Concepts

- **Circle** — a circular-mapping *Hodgkinia* contig, whether a
  chromosome in a shared cell or a genome of its own cell type: "we
  currently do not know whether most of these molecules are chromosomes
  that share the same cell or genomes representing different cell
  types".
- **HGC** — *Hodgkinia* genome complex, the total complement assembled
  from one cicada species.

## Bearing on the record

- **CASE-177** takes from this work, jointly with LIT-1612, that "every
  *Magicicada* species holds at least twenty circles, with little
  synteny between species and very different gene dosages". All three
  hold. The case's figure of "almost ten times the ancestral size" is
  cited to LIT-1612 for *M. tredecim*; this paper's "an order of
  magnitude larger" supports it for the genus. The case's earlier
  correction, that circles are not shown to be cells, is the authors'
  own caution here.
- **QUESTION-277** (evidence, through CASE-177): the dosage result bears
  on control (C). Its pattern is what one would not expect "if the host
  were dictating the Hodgkinia splitting process", so the splitting is
  not host control. The host's likely part is in sharing products
  between cells, which is the authors' supposition.
- **NOTE-1235 / THEORY-1106** (background): the circles-as-cells
  assumption of the transmission simulation is the open point named
  here.

## Limitations

- STAR Methods and the supplements were not read; the dosage analysis
  is taken from the text and Figure 3's caption.
- No cytology here, so cell identity of circles is not addressed.
