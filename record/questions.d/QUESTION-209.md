# QUESTION-209: Do listeners in reference and signalling games invert an informative speaker when alternatives and costs are manipulated with the uttered form held fixed, the model's parameters are set in advance, and its predictions are tested on trial-level data against a literal listener and a likelihood-only model?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-172](../theory.d/THEORY-172.md) holds that listeners' mean bets match the inverted speaker
and not salience alone, in two sets of artificial games. Whether listeners
compute the inversion, as against a likelihood-only or literal listener
that could also follow the alternatives, a fit of condition means with
one model cannot say, and several of the 2012 predictions sit at 0 or
100. Until 2026-10-20 this was [THEORY-172](../theory.d/THEORY-172.md)'s promote_when. [QUESTION-167](QUESTION-167.md)
asks the same of the word-learning precursor.

## What would answer it

- Listener judgements in games whose alternative utterances and costs are
  manipulated while the uttered form is held fixed, predicted in advance
  by the inverted-informative-speaker model with its parameters set before
  the data are seen.
- The same predictions compared, on unaggregated trial-level data, with a
  literal listener with the same prior and a likelihood-only model.

What would not answer it: another high correlation of condition means
with a parameter-free model. That is what [THEORY-172](../theory.d/THEORY-172.md) already holds.
