# NOTE-tmp6yhyu: Survival and Identity

## Contribution

The paper shows that Parfit's premises do not force his conclusion. If
persons are aggregates of stages, the formal mismatch between identity and
the psychological relation is a mismatch between relations on different
things, and the relation that matters among stages can be identified with
the relation of belonging to one person. The cost is paid in counting:
persons may overlap, and we must count them by a relation weaker than
identity. It also gives the first worked treatment of degrees of personal
identity by supervaluation.

## Key insight

Do not compare identity with the R-relation; compare the I-relation with it.
"Stage S is I-related to stage S′" means "some one continuant person has
both as stages", and that relation can be intransitive and graded if
persons can overlap and personhood can come in degrees. Define persons as
the maximal R-interrelated aggregates, and the two relations cannot come
apart.

## Assumptions

- **Persons have stages.** Continuant persons are aggregates of person-stages
  (n. 4 prefers mereological sums). Postscript B (1983) defends this.
- **R is fixed in advance.** "Pretend that the open questions have been
  settled" about which version of continuity and connectedness matters
  (p. 20). The argument works for any version.
- **R is made symmetrical and reflexive** by stipulation, merging its
  forward and backward forms (pp. 23–24).
- **Counting need not be by identity.** It must be by an equivalence and at
  least partial indiscernibility relation (p. 28).
- **Bizarre cases are set aside.** Stages with no "personal time" ordering
  are "so indeterminate" that they "cannot serve as counterexamples" (n. 5).

## Key results

- **The two answers (pp. 17–19).** "What matters in survival is mental
  continuity and connectedness" (p. 17), versus "what matters in survival is
  survival", identity "between the I who exists now and the surviving I"
  (p. 18). "If the two answers disagreed … we would have to prefer the
  platitude of common sense" (p. 18).
- **Parfit's argument (p. 19).** (a) identity is one-one and not a matter of
  degree; (b) mental continuity and connectedness need not be; so (c) what
  matters is not identity. Lewis grants (a) and (b) (p. 19) and refuses a
  verbal redefinition of "identity" (p. 20).
- **Different relata (p. 20).** "Identity among stages has nothing to do
  with it, since stages are momentary. Even if you survive, your present
  stage is not identical to any future stage."
- **The I-relation is the R-relation (pp. 21–22).** A person is a maximal
  I-interrelated aggregate; if I = R, "something is a continuant person if
  and only if it is a maximal R-interrelated aggregate of person-stages"
  (p. 22).
- **Why I need not inherit identity's form (p. 23).** The argument that it
  must "wrongly takes it for granted that every person-stage is a stage of
  one and only one continuant person". The definition must use the
  indefinite article: some continuant person has both stages.
- **Fission and fusion (pp. 24–25, Figure 1.1).** The I-relation "will fail
  to be transitive if and only if there is partial overlap among continuant
  persons". Fission is two continuants C1 and C2 sharing pre-fission stages;
  fusion, sharing post-fusion stages.
- **Overpopulation and tensed identity (pp. 26–29).**
  - With overlap there are more continuants than stages at a time, and we
    count stages (p. 26).
  - "Continuants C1 and C2 are identical-at-time-t if and only if they both
    exist at t and their stages at t are identical" (p. 26). "Tensed
    identity is not a kind of identity" (p. 27).
  - The roads: the Chester A. Arthur Parkway and Route 137 merge, and one
    crosses both by crossing one (p. 27).
  - Counting fusion products as two but fission candidates as one uses
    identity-at-all-times-up-to-t (p. 28).
  - "Ned", the name of a person who will fission, is ambiguous, harmlessly
    until the fission (pp. 28–29).
- **Longevity (pp. 29–31).** If any two stages of Methuselah within 137
  years are R-related and no others are, the persons are exactly the
  137-year segments; continuum many share his 300th birthday, and tensed
  identity counts them as one.
- **Degree (pp. 32–36).**
  - Personal identity can come in degrees because personhood can (p. 32),
    as in fusion with someone very different.
  - Naive degrees fail: an aggregate R-interrelated to degree 0.9 but inside
    one R-interrelated to 0.88 "passes … to degree 0.9" but "flunks the
    maximality test to degree 0.88. Therefore it is a person only to degree
    0.02!" (p. 34). On Methuselah, no segment is a person to any
    significant degree.
  - Supervaluation: each cut-off for R is a delineation; at each, R and I
    coincide; so the measure of delineations on which two stages are
    I-related equals that on which they are R-related (pp. 35–36), after van
    Fraassen (n. 9).
- **Perry (pp. 36–39).** Perry's continuant persons are "lifetimes", the
  aggregates of stages R-related to a given stage at a given time; Lewis's
  are "branches". Perry identifies only time-indexed subrelations of R and
  I, and must exclude persons "not determinable" at a time. Counting from
  no time, "How many persons were involved in an episode of fission long
  ago? I say: two. Perry says: three. Or else he says: none now
  determinable" (p. 39).
- **Postscript A (1983, pp. 73–75), read but not the paper's text.** A
  shared pre-fission stage cannot desire survival for one continuant
  without desiring it for the other, so the most commonsensical desire
  available is plural, "let at least one of us survive" (weak) or "let all
  of us survive" (strong). The weak desire is equivalent to wanting
  I-relatedness to future stages. Leiber's survival insurance, a mind
  recorded and played back into a new body, satisfies the weak desire.

## Claims

| id | claim | strength | support |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | The formal mismatch between identity and R does not show that what matters is not identity, since they relate different things | strong | p. 20; the stage/continuant distinction |
| C2 | The I-relation and the R-relation are the same relation in all possible cases | conditional: holds by definition once persons are maximal R-interrelated aggregates | pp. 22–23; the definition is "at least, I cannot think of any that clearly is not" (p. 22) |
| C3 | Fission and fusion are cases of partial overlap of persons | moderate, given C2 | pp. 24–25 |
| C4 | Counting persons by tensed identity is acceptable | moderate | the roads analogy (p. 27); indiscernibility at a time (p. 28); the author calls it "somewhat counterintuitive" (p. 38) |
| C5 | Degrees of I-relatedness equal degrees of R-relatedness under supervaluation | strong as a construction | pp. 35–36 |
| C6 | Lewis's branches are better persons than Perry's lifetimes | moderate | the overpopulation objection when counting from no time (p. 39) |

## Concepts

- **person-stage** — a "more or less momentary" time-slice of a continuant
  person (p. 20); a physical object that does many things a person does, but
  briefly (Postscript B).
- **R-relation** — the relation of mental continuity and connectedness among
  stages that matters in survival, made symmetrical and reflexive
  (pp. 20, 24).
- **I-relation** — holding between two stages iff some one continuant person
  has both as stages (pp. 21, 23).
- **tensed identity** — identity-at-t: having identical stages at t; a
  relation among continuants that is weaker than identity when they share
  stages (pp. 26–27).
- **delineation** — a cut-off on the degree of R-relatedness (p. 35).
- **branch / lifetime** — Lewis's and Perry's continuant persons
  respectively (p. 37).

## Bearing on the record

No THEORY document cites it. If the record files a THEORY on whether copies
or parallel instances of an AI system are one subject or many, this paper
is the strongest case that the answer can be "many, counted as one until
they diverge" without giving up identity. It carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

What is shown and what is asserted:

- **Shown:** that Parfit's (a) and (b) do not entail (c) once stages and
  continuants are distinguished (C1); and that supervaluation gives equal
  degrees (C5).
- **Stipulated:** that persons are the maximal R-interrelated aggregates
  (C2). Lewis offers it as the claim that makes the reconciliation work, and
  its only support is that he cannot think of a counterexample.
- **Conceded as costly:** counting by tensed identity (C4).
- **Not addressed:** whether common sense really wants identity of a
  continuant, or only something a stage can want. Parfit pressed this in
  the same volume, and Lewis's answer, the plural desire of Postscript A,
  came in 1983.
- **No first-person argument.** The paper does not consider Williams's case
  that expectation follows the body; it takes the psychological answer as
  given.

## Open questions

- What does the shared pre-fission stage rationally fear or hope? Postscript
  A's answer, a weak plural desire, says what it can want; the paper does not
  say how it should feel about the branch that will soon die.
- Is the I = R identification stable under a different choice of R? The
  paper says any version will do (p. 20), but its maximality condition makes
  which aggregates are persons sensitive to that choice.
