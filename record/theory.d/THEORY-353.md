# THEORY-353: For a logically closed theory, the contractions satisfying the six basic AGM postulates are exactly the partial meet contractions, and those satisfying the two supplementary postulates as well are exactly the ones whose selection of remainders is generated by a transitive relation; the theory alone does not fix the contraction, and the results concern one change of one fixed theory

## Source

Alchourrón, Gärdenfors and Makinson (1985), [LIT-1050](../literature.d/LIT-1050.md), Journal of Symbolic Logic
50(2), read in full in [NOTE-874](../notes.d/NOTE-874.md) (Lemma 2.1, Observations 2.3–2.5, 3.1–3.4,
4.2–4.10, Corollary 4.5). Hansson, Logic of Belief Revision, [LIT-1072](../literature.d/LIT-1072.md), read in
full in [NOTE-866](../notes.d/NOTE-866.md), for the standing of these results and of Recovery in the field
since.

## What was actually shown

**The construction.** For a theory A (a set closed under Cn) and a proposition
x, A ⊥ x is the set of maximal subsets of A that do not imply x. A selection
function γ picks a nonempty subset of them, and partial meet contraction keeps
their intersection: A ∸ x = ⋂γ(A ⊥ x). Maxichoice (one remainder) is too large;
its revision "will be complete whenever x is a proposition inconsistent with A".
Full meet (all remainders) is too small; its revision is just Cn(x).

**The representation theorems.** Over any theory A, ∸ is a partial meet
contraction iff it satisfies closure, inclusion, vacuity, success, preservation
and recovery (∸1)–(∸6) (Observation 2.5). A partial meet contraction satisfies
the supplementary (∸7) and (∸8) iff γ is generated by a transitive relation over
remainders (Corollary 4.5): "This result is the promised representation theorem
for the collection of "basic" plus "supplementary" postulates". Revision is
defined by the Levi identity, A + x = Cn((A ∸ ¬x) ∪ {x}); that the revision
postulates follow from the contraction postulates is cited to Gärdenfors, not
proved here.

**What the theory leaves open.** Different selection functions give different
contractions of the same A. In the finite case none is redundant: distinct
selections always give distinct contractions (Observation 4.6). So what fixes a
contraction is A together with γ, or under Corollary 4.5 a transitive relation
over A's remainders.

**Recovery.** Even full meet contraction satisfies recovery over a theory
(Lemma 2.1): every ¬x ∨ a survives the contraction, so adding x back restores A.

## What this does not say

- **That the selection is explained.** The paper defers what makes remainders
  "most important" to other work. The theorems say some γ exists for any
  operation obeying the postulates, not which γ is right.
- **That it covers iterated change.** γ is a selection function for one A.
  Nothing fixes the selection for the theory that results, and Hansson reports
  for iterated revision that "opinions differ widely on the adequacy of these
  proposals."
- **That the theorems hold for belief bases.** The definitions are given for any
  set of propositions, but every theorem is for closed theories. Partial meet
  contraction of a base does not satisfy Recovery ([THEORY-347](THEORY-347.md)).
- **That the added state records reasons.** γ is a preference over which
  remainders to keep. It records nothing about what a belief rested on, and the
  input to change is a sentence accepted outright, not evidence.
- **That Recovery is right.** It is assumed as a postulate and not defended.
  Hansson calls it "the most debated postulate of belief change".
- **That relational selection is characterized.** Relational (not transitively
  relational) partial meet contraction has no representation by a condition on
  the contraction; the paper leaves it open.
