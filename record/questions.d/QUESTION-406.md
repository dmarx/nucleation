# QUESTION-406: Does an independent or better-powered estimate of manufacturing agglomeration on comparable US county data also find no density range with a higher elasticity, and was the Tennessee Valley Authority region's 1960–2000 manufacturing gain caused by the TVA?

## Why it is a question

Kline and Moretti cannot reject a constant log agglomeration elasticity across density ranges (p-values above 0.66, estimates 0.40–0.47), and conclude that the TVA region has a unique steady state ([THEORY-1461](../theory.d/THEORY-1461.md), [NOTE-1646](../notes.d/NOTE-1646.md)). Their test is on all US counties' manufacturing, 1960–2000, under 20-year independence of productivity shocks, with standard errors of about 0.10–0.15 and no reported power against modest nonlinearity in logs. Part of the persistence is credited to agglomeration by elimination, beside infrastructure kept up by local ratepayers.

## What would answer it

Either an independent estimate of the curvature, or a better-powered one, on comparable county data; or a showing of what caused the post-1960 manufacturing gain. The condition as it stood on [THEORY-1461](../theory.d/THEORY-1461.md): The version of record (QJE 129(1), 2014) read and found to report the same sub-period estimates (Table IV) and the same failure to reject a constant log elasticity across density ranges (Table VI); or an independent estimate of manufacturing agglomeration's curvature, on comparable US county data, that likewise finds no density range with a higher elasticity. Refuted if a corrected or better-powered estimate finds a significantly higher elasticity at low density, or if the 1960–2000 manufacturing gain is shown to come from a cause other than the TVA. Whether the region will in fact revert is a forecast this THEORY reports, not one it asserts, and is not asked for.
