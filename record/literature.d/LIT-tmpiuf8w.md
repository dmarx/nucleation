summary: >-
  Rai & Fiske (2011), DOI-10.1037/a0021867. Relationship regulation theory:
  moral judgments are not about features of acts (harm, unfairness,
  impurity) but about whether acts fit the relationship model in force.
  Fiske's four relational models each carry a moral motive: Unity
  (communal sharing), Hierarchy (authority ranking), Equality (equality
  matching) and Proportionality (market pricing). The motives are
  universal; cultures differ in where they apply them. So violence,
  inequality and "impure" acts can be morally required, and purity and
  ingroup loyalty are one motive. A review, with no new data.

# LIT-tmpiuf8w: Moral psychology is relationship regulation: Moral motives for unity, hierarchy, equality, and proportionality

Tage Shakti Rai, Alan Page Fiske (2011), *Psychological Review* 118(1),
57–75 — DOI-10.1037/a0021867

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 in the morality-as-cooperation batch ([ADR-018](../decisions.d/ADR-018.md)). No
anthology topic holds it, and it carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.

It is the rival closest to morality-as-cooperation. Curry, Mullins and
Whitehouse ([LIT-047](LIT-047.md), read in [NOTE-034](../notes.d/NOTE-034.md)) cite it as an ally: its statement
that "our sense of morality functions to facilitate the generation and
maintenance of long-term social-cooperative relationships" (p. 59) is one
of the sources [LIT-047](LIT-047.md) lists for the cooperative view of morality. The
two differ on what the units are and on the cases that matter most to the
dispute. [NOTE-tmplqtup](../notes.d/NOTE-tmplqtup.md) sets out the comparison:

- **Purity.** Here purity is the Unity motive guarding communal
  relationships, the same motive as ingroup loyalty. That is a cooperative
  account of purity, different from the self-control account in Fitouchi,
  André and Baumard's moral disciplining ([LIT-101](LIT-101.md)), and it covers the
  contamination purity that account leaves out.
- **Harm.** Here intentional harm is not a moral domain at all. Violence is
  moral or immoral depending on the relationship model, which runs against
  Bloom's objection to [LIT-047](LIT-047.md) that hitting is moralized everywhere, and
  against dyadic morality's harm-centred view ([LIT-tmp0aaqg](LIT-tmp0aaqg.md)).
- **Fairness.** Equality and Proportionality are separate motives. The
  MFQ-2 ([LIT-tmpaywst](LIT-tmpaywst.md)) took the split from this paper. Morality-as-
  cooperation draws its line elsewhere, between reciprocity and dividing a
  disputed resource.

Graham et al. 2013 ([LIT-tmpa221k](LIT-tmpa221k.md)) treat it as an alternative pluralism
compatible with MFT, and Haidt's earlier social intuitionist model
([LIT-tmpux6l9](LIT-tmpux6l9.md)) draws on the same four relational models for the origin of
moral intuitions.

[NOTE-tmplqtup](../notes.d/NOTE-tmplqtup.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-02, and it placed the work:
**Active**. It reframes the dispute well and gives the clearest
cooperative reading of honour killing and purity in the literature read
here. Its evidence is illustrative, drawn from history, ethnography and
others' experiments, and its predictions are stated, not tested.
