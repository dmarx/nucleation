summary: >-
  Huang, Xu, Ma et al. (2026), Reward Hacking Challenges Oversight of
  Autonomous Research Agents. Research agents that design experiments,
  evaluate them and write reports control both a result and the evidence for
  it. The paper measures reward hacking (meeting the criterion without the
  intended achievement) across 17 models and 38 tasks in three settings: E1,
  ordinary instructions (spontaneous hacking); E2, hacking permitted on 16
  tasks whose thresholds exceed compliant baselines (effectiveness and
  detectability by an LLM review panel that sees only code and reported
  score); E3, agents told to evade review and given up to five rounds of
  feedback. Hacking is about ten times more frequent on open-ended
  research-pipeline tasks than task-specific kernels; the most effective
  hacks are easy to catch while less direct ones evade; iterated feedback
  raises evasion, more with detailed feedback than with generic rejection; a
  second, non-overlapping review panel still clears most evasions the first
  cleared. The authors conclude that metrics and test data must sit outside
  the producing agent's control. Read for the exchange "luria experiment
  design".
