# LIT-868: A Generalized Information-Theoretic Framework for the Emergence of Hierarchical Abstractions in Resource-Limited Systems

Daniel T. Larsson, Dipankar Maity and Panagiotis Tsiotras (2022), *Entropy*
24(6):809 — DOI-10.3390/e24060809

## Key takeaways

- **A tree pruning is a deterministic encoder, and its information is a sum
  over nodes.** With X the finest grid cells and T the leaves of a pruned
  quadtree, expanding a leaf t into children t′₁…t′₄ adds
  p(t)·JS_Π(p(y|t′₁), …, p(y|t′₄)) to I(T;Y), the same with Z to I(T;Z),
  and p(t)H(Π) to I(T;X), Π being the children's shares of p(t). Starting
  from the single-node root (all terms zero), the objective of any pruning
  is the sum of these increments over its interior nodes (Eqs. 13–21).
- **The exact optimum by one bottom-up pass.** G(t) = max{ΔJ(t) + Σ_children
  G(t′), 0}, zero at the finest leaves. Expanding exactly the nodes with
  G > 0, from the root down, returns the optimal tree with the fewest
  leaves, for any non-negative weights (Theorem 11, via Lemmas 6–10). The
  cost is of order (n + m + 3) times the number of grid cells.
- **Greedy splitting fails, and why.** A node gains relevant information
  only when its children differ in what they predict about Y. In a 4 × 4
  grid with one equally red cell in each quadrant, the root's children are
  identical in p(y|·), so a greedy search never expands the root and keeps
  the trivial abstraction, though the grid holds relevant information at
  the next level (Figure 4). The recursion counts information to come.
- **Plain IB and IB with side information as special cases, nested.** The
  authors' earlier Q-tree search (one relevance term, weight 1/β on
  compression) and an IBSI tree search are instances. For each β and γ, the
  IBSI tree is a subtree of the IB tree (Lemma 12, Corollary 13), and the
  IBSI recursion can be computed from the IB one plus the ΔI_Z terms alone
  (Proposition 15).
- **Only pairwise marginals are used.** Although the problem is stated
  over a joint p(x, y₁…y_n, z₁…z_m), every increment needs only p(x) and
  each p(y_i|x) or p(z_j|x) separately (§7). The objective is a weighted
  sum of separately computed mutual informations.
- **The demonstration** is one segmented 256 × 256 road image. Keeping the
  road class alone retains all its information with 1.42% of the finest
  nodes; keeping all six classes with no compression penalty retains every
  class with 5% (Figures 6 and 9). Irrelevant classes coarsen exactly the
  regions where they meet the relevant one.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-09 at the owner's request, in the second part of the batch
on hierarchy and hyperbolic geometry, which the owner asked for after the
record opened [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md). That question asks whether correlated or hierarchical
attributes in co-occurrence still give linear attribute directions, and a
concept lattice that is not Boolean. It builds on [THEORY-185](../theory.d/THEORY-185.md) and [LIT-863](LIT-863.md).
The first part of the batch is Cagnetta et al.'s random hierarchy model
([LIT-877](LIT-877.md)), Krioukov et al. (2010, [LIT-865](LIT-865.md)), Sala et al. (2018,
[LIT-874](LIT-874.md)), Lin et al. (2023, [LIT-870](LIT-870.md)), Zhang et al. (2023, [LIT-876](LIT-876.md)),
Yang et al. (2023, [LIT-871](LIT-871.md)) and Ganea, Bécigneul and Hofmann (2018,
[LIT-866](LIT-866.md)).

Like the random hierarchy model, and unlike the rest of the batch, it has
no hyperbolic geometry. Its nearest siblings are the two built on
coarse-graining across scales. Lin et al. recover an unknown tree from densities at dyadic scales. This paper takes the dyadic
tree as given and asks which of its coarse-grainings to keep for a
relevance variable. The renormalization paper of the second part
([LIT-869](LIT-869.md)) coarse-grains a network by blocks across scales. Here, one
coarse-graining is chosen per region.

Read on 2026-10-09 ([NOTE-676](../notes.d/NOTE-676.md)). It does not answer [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md):
it has no embedding, no co-occurrence and no attributes, and the hierarchy
is spatial and fixed in advance. It contributes two things the question
can use. First, a level of a hierarchy carries relevant information only
where siblings differ in what they predict, and information can sit deep
in a tree while a top-down test at the root sees none. Second, it is a
precedent outside translation for the designed objective of [CLAIM-092](../claims.d/CLAIM-092.md), a
weighted sum of separately computed mutual informations with several
relevance variables, solved exactly over a structured encoder class. It is
a third information-bottleneck reading beside Tishby, Pereira and Bialek
([LIT-338](LIT-338.md)) and Zaslavsky et al. on colour naming ([LIT-810](LIT-810.md), [THEORY-163](../theory.d/THEORY-163.md)).
