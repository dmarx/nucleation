# TERM-144: Gibrat's law, or the Gibrat principle: growth in proportion to current size, the rule, kept apart from the lognormal it gives without entry or a floor and from the Yule–Simon power law it gives with entry

This is **alternative (a)**, the lean. Alternative (b) is the note of
2026-11-22 on [CLAIM-830](../claims.d/CLAIM-830.md), which records the clash without a term.

## Definition

**Gibrat's law** (the law of proportionate effect) is a rule of growth: a
unit's expected increment, or the distribution of its growth factor, is
proportional to its current size, so its growth rate does not depend on
its size. It is a rule, not a distribution. Said of a model, it is
satisfied or not; said of an outcome, the outcome has to be named, because
the same rule gives three:

1. **No entry and no floor: a lognormal.** Independent growth factors of
   finite variance make log size a sum, so size tends to a lognormal that
   only widens. This is Gibrat's own use (Gibrat 1930, as Mitzenmacher
   reports it, [LIT-3003](../literature.d/LIT-3003.md) §5, [THEORY-2150](../theory.d/THEORY-2150.md)) and the record's in [CLAIM-824](../claims.d/CLAIM-824.md)
   ("A pure Gibrat process with neither gives a lognormal that only
   widens") and in [CLAIM-830](../claims.d/CLAIM-830.md)'s title ("Gibrat's lognormal growth").
2. **A floor or reinjection: a power law.** A lower reflecting barrier
   (Champernowne, [THEORY-2150](../theory.d/THEORY-2150.md)) or an additive term (Kesten, [CLAIM-824](../claims.d/CLAIM-824.md))
   turns the lognormal into a Pareto tail; Newman's "random
   multiplication processes can also generate perfect power-law
   distributions with only a slight modification" ([LIT-2976](../literature.d/LIT-2976.md), p. 24).
3. **Entry: the Yule–Simon power law.** Proportional gains plus a steady
   stream of new units give p_k with α = 2 + (k0 + c)/m (Newman §IV.D).
   This is the setting in which Simon, by Newman's report, called the
   rule "the Gibrat principle" (Simon 1955, [LIT-3033](../literature.d/LIT-3033.md), unread; Newman,
   [LIT-2976](../literature.d/LIT-2976.md), p. 18), beside "Matthew effect", "cumulative advantage" and
   "preferential attachment".

## What it is not

- **Not a name for the lognormal.** "Gibrat growth gives a lognormal" is
  true only of sense 1's setting. Where the record means the outcome, it
  should say "Gibrat growth without entry or a floor".
- **Not opposed to preferential attachment.** Simon's usage and
  [CLAIM-830](../claims.d/CLAIM-830.md)'s thesis agree: proportionality is common to linear
  attachment and Gibrat growth, and entry is what separates the power law
  from the lognormal. The clash is in the name, not in the mathematics.
- **Not the empirical "Gibrat's law" of firm or city growth** as a finding
  that growth rates are independent of size; that is a claim about data,
  which this term does not adjudicate and the record holds no reading of.

## Note of 2026-11-22: Merton, read, and the Matthew effect

Newman's list that this term starts from ([LIT-2976](../literature.d/LIT-2976.md), p. 18) also gives
"Matthew effect" as a name of the Yule process. Read, Merton ([LIT-3002](../literature.d/LIT-3002.md),
[NOTE-2268](../notes.d/NOTE-2268.md)) uses it for something else: credit for a given contribution
allotted by the contributor's standing, with cumulative advantage "one
institutional version" of it. [TERM-145](TERM-145.md) separates the two senses. It
is the same kind of split as this term's: a name for a rule of
proportional gains that the sources also use for something narrower or
different, so that a claim using the name has to say which is meant.
