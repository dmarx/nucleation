# THEORY-459: Exact duality between two models is incompatible with ontological emergence of one from the other, reductive or not, because a duality is an isomorphism and ontological emergence needs a non-injective linkage whose interpretation does not commute; the two combine only when emergence runs beside the duality, as along the radial direction in AdS/CFT, through an approximate duality, or as merely epistemic emergence

## Source

De Haro & Butterfield (2025), [LIT-199](../literature.d/LIT-199.md), chapter 14, read in full in [NOTE-178](../notes.d/NOTE-178.md)
from the arXiv manuscript. Page numbers are printed ones.

## What was actually shown

**Definitions (§14.3).** A linkage map link: T_b → T_t from a bottom to a
top theory is non-injective and a partial homomorphism; it is reductive
(Nagelian) iff surjective. Ontological emergence is a non-commuting
diagram, i_b ≠ i_t∘link: the top theory's interpretation refers to
something the bottom's, carried across, does not. Epistemic emergence is
novelty only in the bare theories, as irreducibility or computational
intractability.

**The incompatibility.** A duality is an isomorphism of model roots, so the
duals are equinumerous and the map between them is a bijection. That rules
out the non-injective linkage that emergence needs, reductive or
irreducible. The reading grades this moderate (C7): strictly proven for
finite sets, with a physical-grounds argument for the infinite case
(fn. 57).

**Three ways to combine them (§14.3.4).**
1. Emergence beside duality: in AdS/CFT the emergence map runs along the
   renormalization-group (radial) direction, not along the duality map
   (Fig. 14.8).
2. Effective duality: an approximate or limiting duality with no exact
   isomorphism.
3. Epistemic emergence.

**A related result.** Reduction (formal) and ontological emergence
(interpretative) are compatible with each other (C8), shown on Ising
spontaneous magnetisation, massless against massive particles, and
Seiberg–Witten. Duality, being symmetric, in general gives no priority;
reductive emergence gives priority as definitional dependence (§14.4).

**What could have come out otherwise.** The definitions could have let a
bijection carry novel reference. Given them, the finite case is a proof,
and the infinite case is where the argument could still fail.

## What this does not say

- **That dualities indicate emergence or fundamentality.** They are "by
  themselves not indicative of emergence or fundamentality" (§16.2,
  p. 529).
- **That AdS/CFT is an exception to that.** It is the exception only to
  incompatibility with non-epistemic emergence, because an extra dimension
  appears beside the duality. On a theoretically equivalent reading,
  Horowitz and Polchinski's "emergent gravity" "cannot be understood as
  ontological emergence" (§14.3.3, p. 489).
- **That emergence in other senses is excluded.** The notion here is
  Butterfield-style novelty with the robustness condition dropped, a
  "semantic or interpretative" condition (p. 479). Information-theoretic
  measures of emergence answer a different question, and the two do not
  conflict ([NOTE-178](../notes.d/NOTE-178.md)).
- **That the infinite case is proven.** It is argued.
- **That gauge–gravity duality is exact.** It is a conjecture, and the
  common core there is partial.
