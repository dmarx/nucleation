# CLAIM-039: Affective states can be properties of organized systems, characterized functionally as Frijda characterizes emotions, without implying subjective experience

## The claim

U19: affective states as "properties of agents/systems rather than subjective
experience". U20 asked for Frijda's characterization to be mapped onto a
distributed corporate agent rather than relying on loose examples.

## Why it is set aside

The paper moved from affect to will by the evening of 2026-10-04. The
assistant also found prior art (A64): it "can no longer present genuinely
collective, non-phenomenal affect as the paper's principal novel conjecture".
Deferred, not rejected: it is a different paper.

## Note of 2026-10-12: grounds and a difficulty

[THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md) and [THEORY-125](../theory.d/THEORY-125.md) now ground this claim. [THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md) supplies the Frijda characterization the claim depends on (a mode of action readiness), not evidence that organizations meet it. [THEORY-125](../theory.d/THEORY-125.md) holds that a group emotion, being a functional kind, "need not be felt", though "no actual case has yet been shown".

The difficulty: [THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md) (Veit) identifies hedonic valence with a coordinated regulatory state. On that view a functional valence state is a felt one, which is the uncoupling this claim denies. It is not a ground, so no edge is written. It is limited to hedonic valence in animals, while this claim uses Frijda's action readiness. If the line is taken up again, it is the objection to answer first.

## Note of 2026-10-18: the owner reopened the line, with distress and then frustration

In the exchange of 2026-10-17 the owner brought this line back. At U27 the
owner asked how the framework "might account for illusory binding-type
phenomena in distributed agents", adding "Our material on emotion might be a
good place to start." A27 cited this claim as a ground of its binding thesis,
now [CLAIM-355](CLAIM-355.md), which names it in its body and does not rest on it. At U28
the owner corrected the category: "'fear' is too specific. 'distress' is
probably a lot easier to reason about within our framework." A28 granted it:
"**Distress is a much better starting point than fear**". At U33 the owner
moved again, "maybe we should focus less on distress and more on frustration",
and A33 granted that too ([CLAIM-368](CLAIM-368.md)).

The move is from a category, anger or fear, to a minimal regulation-defined
affect. Its natural grounds are already in the record: Di Paolo's adaptivity,
a graded norm the system itself has ([THEORY-628](../theory.d/THEORY-628.md)); Ashby's second feedback
through essential variables ([THEORY-324](../theory.d/THEORY-324.md)); and the viability kernel
([THEORY-134](../theory.d/THEORY-134.md)). A27 named [THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md) as the objection, which this claim's note
of 2026-10-12 already names as the one to answer first.

The status stays Deferred. Whether the line leaves Deferred is the owner's
decision, and it is not made here.

## Note of 2026-10-23: the line reopened

The owner, answering a reading of corporate psychopathy that granted a
corporation psychopathy's conduct but not its affect: "one of the
specific things our framework equips us to do is to assign affect to
corporation-like things." That is the decision the note of 2026-10-18
left to the owner, so the claim is Proposed again. Its first use is
[CLAIM-432](CLAIM-432.md), which assigns functional remorse, or its absence, to a
corporation by asking whether harm to others is among the concerns its
own appraisals track. The difficulty the note of 2026-10-12 named,
[THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md)'s identification of valence with a felt state, is still the
objection to answer first; [CLAIM-432](CLAIM-432.md) names it in its defeater.

## Note of 2026-10-23: contagion as a comparison condition, and Trivers

From the same audit (workbench entry of 2026-10-24, U5). [THEORY-123](../theory.d/THEORY-123.md)'s
group-mood contagion is one route to collective affective convergence,
members synchronized without the group having an organized response of
its own. It is a comparison condition for this claim: a group-level
functional affect has to show what the organization's own appraisal,
authority and coordinated readiness add. [THEORY-663](../theory.d/THEORY-663.md), Trivers on emotions
regulating reciprocal altruism, including strategically simulated ones,
sharpens [CLAIM-431](CLAIM-431.md)'s objection to [CLAIM-432](CLAIM-432.md)'s corporate remorse: repair and
expressed concern can be instrumentally successful without meeting
whatever criterion separates remorse from reputation management. The
discriminating design holds reputation, sanctions and commercial benefit
roughly fixed and asks whether others' harms keep independent authority
in the organization's decisions.


## Note of 2026-10-27: the concept, not the account

The status review of this date ([ADR-057](../decisions.d/ADR-057.md)) moved this claim from grounding
in [THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md) to using [TERM-094](../terms.d/TERM-094.md), Frijda's action readiness with
control precedence. The claim uses the concept. It does not need the
account's thesis about what elicits an emotion, which [THEORY-057](../theory.d/THEORY-057.md) contests
and which stays Proposed.

## Note of 2026-10-29: what holds it back

[THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md)'s identification of hedonic valence with a felt regulatory state
is still unanswered, as the notes of 2026-10-12 and 2026-10-23 say. The
record holds no claim showing either that action readiness with control
precedence ([TERM-094](../terms.d/TERM-094.md)) can be had without hedonic valence, or that Veit's
identity, argued for animal valence and rated a sketch in [THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md), does
not extend to it. A claim that settles either would answer it.
