# LIT-tmpijst5: Gradual regime shifts in fairy circles

Yuval R. Zelnik, Ehud Meron and Golan Bel, "Gradual regime shifts in fairy
circles", *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 112(40),
12327–12331 (2015), doi:10.1073/pnas.1504289112; arXiv:1703.00732.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmppk4i4.*

- **The foil.** The paper sets itself against catastrophic shifts as
  "abrupt transitions between two alternative stable states that occur
  uniformly across the ecosystem" (p. 1, citing Scheffer et al. 2001).
- **Hybrid states.** Within the bistability range lies "a subrange with
  many stable hybrid states" on a snaking branch (Fig. 1).
- **Fairy-circle birth and death** "correspond to transitions between
  hybrid states" (p. 5). They are reproduced from the 2004 images by
  one-year droughts (84 mm/yr) or wet spells (143 mm/yr) against a
  baseline of 102 mm/yr.
- **Two time scales.** Fairy-circle size changes much faster than their
  number. Size relaxes to a steady state, while number follows "the
  dynamics of fronts that separate domains" (p. 9).
- **A gradual shift**: "by successive shifts to hybrid states of larger
  gap numbers" (p. 6). A uniform or periodic start subjected to the same
  forcing: "No regime shift takes place" (Fig. 10).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-11-14 as next reading after Sun et al. (2026, filed in
dmarx/nucleation#494). That paper reads hollow shrub rings in grass as
metastable states near a first-order shrub–grass transition, and calls
the shift abrupt. This paper's fairy circles are a different object: bare
gaps in single-species grass. Its point bears directly on Sun et al.'s
evidence all the same. Plots in different states at the same rainfall are
what a stable mosaic looks like, and without time series an abrupt
regional jump cannot be told from a gradual cascade of local ones.

In the record it is a worked case for QUESTION-281's route of localized
structures, and it partly stands in for Knobloch on spatial localization
(LIT-1642, Unfetched), which it cites. Its field evidence falls short of
THEORY-136's bar for alternative stable states, though its model meets it.
