# LIT-tmpz19z8: Rights

Leif Wenar and Rowan Cruft (2005; substantive revision 2025). "Rights". *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, first published 19 December 2005, substantive revision 7 June 2025.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading ([NOTE-tmp2b9lt](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp2b9lt.md)).*

- Rights are "entitlements (not) to perform certain actions, or (not) to be
  in certain states; or entitlements that others (not) perform certain
  actions or (not) be in certain states" (lead).
- Hohfeldian incidents "fit together in characteristic ways to create
  complex "molecular" rights" (§2.1); "Power-rights to alter the authority
  of others are ... definitive of all developed legal and political
  systems" (§2.1.3).
- Will theory: a right gives "its holder control over another's duty";
  interest theory: it furthers "the right-holder's interests" (§2.2.2).
- "The inalienable right to life, the forfeitable right to liberty, and the
  waivable right that a promise be kept" (§1).
