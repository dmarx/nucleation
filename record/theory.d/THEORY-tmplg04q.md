# THEORY-tmplg04q: A Markov blanket, in any formulation including sparse coupling at nonequilibrium steady state and time-varying blanket states, does not individuate a system: where it falls is fixed by modelling choices made before it is found, so it presupposes the boundary it is used to find, and it cannot represent a boundary the system produces

## Source

The same three works as [THEORY-073](THEORY-073.md): Bruineberg, Dołęga, Dewhurst and
Baltieri ([LIT-603](../literature.d/LIT-603.md), read in [NOTE-447](../notes.d/NOTE-447.md)), Raja, Valluri, Baggs, Chemero and
Anderson ([LIT-598](../literature.d/LIT-598.md), read in [NOTE-450](../notes.d/NOTE-450.md)), and Friston (2013, [LIT-526](../literature.d/LIT-526.md), read in
[NOTE-421](../notes.d/NOTE-421.md)).

## The claim

[THEORY-073](THEORY-073.md), as narrowed, holds the account for Friston's 2013 formulation
and the applications the record holds. Both critiques state it without
that restriction: a Markov blanket, however defined, is placed by choices
the modeller makes first. The later formulations are the free-energy
literature's reply. They are the sparse-coupling definition at
nonequilibrium steady state (Friston 2019 and later) and time-varying
blanket states (Ramstead et al. 2020). The record holds neither, so the
general form is offered and not held.

## What it does not say

That the later formulations fail. It says that, if the critiques are right
about the source of the partition, they fail in the same way. Whether they
do is the promote_when's question.
