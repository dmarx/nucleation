The target design (THEORY-159's promote_when, CLAIM-082's defeat
condition): a term added to, lost from or merged in a language's inventory,
then a measure of whether the neighbouring terms' use and interpretation
shift, against terms outside the field as a control.

## Queries and what each turned up

1. `Nafaanra color naming evolution new terms existing categories shift
   Zaslavsky 2022` — Zaslavsky, Garvin, Kemp, Tishby and Regier (2022),
   "The evolution of color naming reflects pressure for efficiency:
   Evidence from the recent past", J. Language Evolution; bioRxiv
   10.1101/2021.11.03.467047; the CogSci 2019 pilot
   (lclab.berkeley.edu/papers/nafaanra-cogsci-2019.pdf). A term-addition
   case: Nafaanra 1978 (3 terms) against 2018 (new terms for yellow,
   green, blue, purple; older categories smaller). **Decisive candidate.**
2. `communicative need modulates competition in language change semantic
   neighbours frequency Karjus` — Karjus, Blythe, Kirby and Smith (2020),
   arXiv:2006.09277. A rising word's near-synonyms lose frequency, or not,
   depending on topical "advection". Thousands of words; the measure is
   frequency, not range of use. **Decisive candidate (large-scale,
   with a topic-level baseline).**
3. `lexical field new word added neighbouring words meaning narrowed
   diachronic corpus study control words` — Ryskina et al. (SCiL 2020),
   "Where new words are born" (neologisms emerge in sparse neighbourhoods
   whose neighbours grow in frequency; matched control words); a 2026
   Twitter follow-up (arXiv:2602.13123, not opened); Trier/Coseriu
   word-field background. Ryskina registered.
4. `Ameel Storms Malt bilinguals naming bottles jars convergence category
   boundaries Dutch French` — Ameel, Storms, Malt and Sloman (2005), JML:
   bilinguals' Dutch and French container categories converge on one
   pattern (a merge-like reorganisation within the speaker), with
   monolinguals as controls; White, Malt and Storms (2017) replication.
   Ameel 2005 registered.
5. `learning a new color term shifts boundaries of existing color
   categories experiment adult learners` — Özgen and Davies (2002)
   training study (perceptual, not use; not registered); Malik-Moraleda
   et al. (2023). 
6. `Xu Kemp 2015 computational evaluation two laws of semantic change
   differentiation parallel change synonyms control pairs` — Xu and Kemp
   (CogSci 2015): near-synonym pairs change in parallel more than they
   differentiate, against control pairs. Registered (no add/loss event,
   but a control design over a field's neighbours).
7. `when a word becomes obsolete do its semantic neighbours expand to fill

8. `Greek English bilinguals ghalazio ble colour category boundary shift

9. `Malik-Moraleda 2023 Tsimane Spanish bilinguals color concepts

10. `loanword introduction native near-synonym meaning narrows corpus study

## Verdict of the search

No study found adds, loses or merges a term and measures neighbours' use
against terms outside the field as an explicit control. The nearest kinds:
(a) natural term additions in colour with the old terms' extensions
re-measured (Nafaanra; Tsimane' bilinguals), with no outside-field control
term; (b) corpus competition studies over many words, measuring frequency
rather than range of use, with a topic-level baseline (Karjus et al.);
(c) bilingual convergence, with monolingual groups as controls (Ameel et
al.; Athanasopoulos).
