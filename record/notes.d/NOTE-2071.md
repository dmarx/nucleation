# NOTE-2071: Hohfeld — Some fundamental legal conceptions

## Contribution

An analytic scheme of eight fundamental legal relations in four correlative
and four opposite pairs. It distinguishes a claim from a privilege and a
power from an immunity, separates legal power from physical power and from
the privilege of exercising it, and separates operative from evidential
facts.

## Key results

- **The problem** (p. 30): "the term 'rights' tends to be used
  indiscriminately to cover what in a given case may be a privilege, a
  power, or an immunity, rather than a right in the strictest sense".
- **The method** (p. 30): "exhibiting all of the various relations in a
  scheme of 'opposites' and 'correlatives'". Correlatives: right–duty,
  privilege–no-right, power–liability, immunity–disability. Opposites:
  right–no-right, privilege–duty, power–disability, immunity–liability.
- **Claim and privilege** (p. 32): for "right" in the strict sense "perhaps
  the word 'claim' would prove the best"; "a privilege is the opposite of a
  duty, and the correlative of a 'no-right.'" "The closest synonym of legal
  'privilege' seems to be legal 'liberty'" (p. 41).
- **Independence** (pp. 36–37): "X's right that Y should not eat the food
  persists even though X's own privilege of doing so has been
  extinguished." Whether a liberty carries protective claims "is
  ultimately a question of justice and policy".
- **Power** (p. 44): "a legal power (as distinguished, of course, from a
  mental or physical power) is the opposite of legal disability, and the
  correlative of legal liability"; its holder is the one "whose volitional
  control is paramount". One must "distinguish carefully between the legal
  power, the physical power to do the things necessary for the 'exercise'
  of the legal power, and, finally, the privilege of doing these things"
  (p. 52). "the power of alienation is frequently one of the fundamental
  elements of a complex legal interest" (n. 67).
- **Liability and immunity** (pp. 54–55): "Perhaps the nearest synonym of
  'liability' is 'subjection'"; "a power is one's affirmative 'control'
  over a given legal relation as against another; whereas an immunity is
  one's freedom from the legal power or 'control' of another". Immunities
  vary by object: "X might be immune or exempt in relation to certain
  parcels of property, and be liable as to others".
- **Operative facts** (pp. 20, 25): legal relations are to be kept apart
  from "the physical and mental facts that call such relations into being".
- **Conclusion** (p. 58): the eight conceptions are "the lowest common
  denominators of the law."

## Assumptions

Legal relations hold between persons, and each has a correlative held by
another. Positive law only; what rights there ought to be is "justice and
policy". The relations are sui generis, exhibited rather than defined.
Anglo-American case law of the time is the test material.

## Concepts

Claim (right in the strict sense); duty; privilege (liberty); no-right;
power; liability (subjection); immunity; disability; jural opposites and
correlatives; operative and evidential facts; legal against physical power.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-722](../claims.d/CLAIM-722.md).** The source of A87 §2's separation of CanPhysically
  from Power_N: legal power "as distinguished ... from a mental or physical
  power", and "the physical power to do the things necessary" for its
  exercise, kept apart (pp. 44, 52). A wrongdoer's physical power over the
  world is not a power over the legal relation.
- **[CLAIM-720](../claims.d/CLAIM-720.md).** Bears out A87 §4's correction, that an immunity is a
  limit on another's power: "an immunity is one's freedom from the legal
  power or 'control' of another" (p. 55). A holder's inability to alienate
  would be a disability of the holder, a relation Hohfeld names but does not
  apply to inalienable rights.
- **[CLAIM-738](../claims.d/CLAIM-738.md).** Hohfeld's positions are independent in the same way
  the claim's rights, demands and commitments are: a claim implies no
  privilege, and a privilege no protective claim. He says nothing of
  demands or commitments, and the claim's own body warns that a Hohfeldian
  claim-right is not the record's CLAIM.
- **[CLAIM-743](../claims.d/CLAIM-743.md), [CLAIM-735](../claims.d/CLAIM-735.md), [CLAIM-727](../claims.d/CLAIM-727.md).** The vocabulary they
  use: changes in legal relations come from facts outside anyone's control
  or from a power exercised by the one "whose volitional control is
  paramount"; powers and immunities are separate relations, so holding one
  power implies no other. Hohfeld makes no claim about justification,
  release or challenge.
- The exchange's A85 counts "four basic legal positions"; Hohfeld speaks of
  "eight conceptions" (four advantage positions with their correlatives is
  a later reading the text supports). A87's "A right is not one kind of
  thing" is faithful.

## Limitations

Legal relations only, from Anglo-American cases of 1913; moral rights,
demands and commitments are outside it. Rights in rem and in personam are
left to the 1917 sequel, not registered. Case illustrations were read
quickly.
