# QUESTION-128: Does the record's organizational account give a path to normativity from first principles: can norms of function and failure be defined from an organization's own dynamics, and what more do practical and moral authority need?

## Why it is a question

The owner, at U16: "does our account provide or perhaps at least allude to
a path towards an account of normativity from first principles?"

A16's verdict: "**the account offers a plausible route from physical
dynamics to system-relative norms of functioning and failure. It does not
yet derive moral or rational authority from physical first principles.**"
And on viability theory: "**It does not explain why \(K\) is the right
set.**"

A16's ladder of normativities, its proposal:

1. **Organizational:** "Conditions under which an organization continues or
   fails."
2. **Functional:** "Conditions under which a component fulfills or fails to
   fulfill its role in sustaining that organization."
3. **Representational:** "Conditions under which internal indications or
   expectations can be correct or mistaken."
4. **Practical:** "Conditions under which an agent has reasons to act,
   endorse, or revise its commitments."
5. **Moral:** "Conditions under which actions are right or wrong, or agents
   owe things to one another."

A16: "The first two look comparatively tractable through organizational and
causal models. Bickhard's account suggests a possible bridge to the third.
The fourth and fifth require considerably more."

For the second rung A16 proposed a measure of a process's contribution to
the organization's persistence, Δ_p(T) = Pr(τ_O > T | do(p intact)) −
Pr(τ_O > T | do(p disabled)), and called it "a **functional contribution
measure**, not yet a moral value or a proof of normativity". It also kept
the limit: "**self-generated normativity and normative legitimacy must
remain separate**."

## What the record holds

- **The limit.** [CLAIM-075](../claims.d/CLAIM-075.md) (Active, disclaimed): viability does not
  establish what ought to persist; "An unjust institution may be highly
  viable." A16's virus, cancer and destructive institution are its cases,
  and [CLAIM-060](../claims.d/CLAIM-060.md)'s.
- **Self-generated normativity as a condition of agency.** [THEORY-071](../theory.d/THEORY-071.md)
  (Barandiaran, Di Paolo and Rohde): minimal agency requires individuality,
  interactional asymmetry and self-generated normativity.
- **Function from present organization.** [THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md) (Bickhard). Its
  promote_when asks for the model A16 sketched, "contribution to
  self-maintenance ... defined from the dynamics alone", and names the
  test Δ_p has not met: "The account is in trouble if functions so defined
  cannot be told from mere causal contributions to persistence that no one
  counts as functional".
- **The objection to the first step.** [THEORY-628](../theory.d/THEORY-628.md) (Di Paolo): self-production
  alone gives only "an all-or-nothing norm, to go on or to die, and that
  distinction is available to an observer, not to the system"; a graded
  norm needs adaptivity. A16's step from self-maintenance to "endogenous
  conditions of success and failure" passes over this.
- **A rival operationalization.** [THEORY-217](../theory.d/THEORY-217.md): malfunction as failure to
  correlate with a "normative field" computed from viability.
- **Norms that are the modeller's.** [CLAIM-288](../claims.d/CLAIM-288.md) (Proposed, counter): norms an
  operator maximizes because the modeller fixed them are "the modeller's,
  not set by the organization itself". That is A16's "endogenous rather
  than simply assigned by an observer", already in use as an objection.
- **A stipulated K.** [CLAIM-195](../claims.d/CLAIM-195.md): for an argument, the constraint set that
  persistence is relative to "is fixed by an amendable decision, not
  discovered". The record's own case of a K it chose.
- **A narrower question.** [QUESTION-087](QUESTION-087.md) asks whether a self-reproducing
  authority structure can be pathological by the organization's own
  standard. That is this question's second rung applied to authority. It
  is narrower, so this question does not refine it.
- **The cross-agent half, set aside.** [QUESTION-007](QUESTION-007.md) (Deferred, authenticity
  line) asks what grounds normativity between agents at one level. A16's
  fifth rung includes it.

## What would count as an answer

For the first two rungs: a model in which function is defined from the
dynamics, applied without tuning to cases [THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md) names, that separates
functions from mere causal contributions to persistence, and meets
[THEORY-628](../theory.d/THEORY-628.md)'s demand for a norm the system itself has. For the rest: an
argument that a standard so grounded has authority for anyone, or a showing
that it cannot have it without a premise from outside the dynamics. A16:
"If it means **deriving every normative truth from purely descriptive
physical facts without any additional assumptions**, we do not have that
derivation." The relational form of the judgments at every rung is
[CLAIM-363](../claims.d/CLAIM-363.md). Whether the standards themselves can be adjudicated is
[QUESTION-129](QUESTION-129.md).

The owner's smoke detector ([CASE-243](../cases.d/CASE-243.md)) and debugging test
([CASE-240](../cases.d/CASE-240.md)), from the exchange "Meaning Transmission Explained" (https://chatgpt.com/share/6af517f7-cb48-83ea-8395-df7d52333dc7, 2026-11-07), are the
plainest cases on the record of a norm of function and failure that is
not the system's own. A4 §3: the false alarm raises "whether that norm
belongs intrinsically to the system or is supplied by the humans who
designed and evaluate it". A4 §4, A5 §4, A8 §7 and A10 §6 each grant that
the standard is "stipulated" or imposed by designers. A5 §5 asks the
question in this question's terms: "whether the expected-response profile
itself must originate in an intentional designer, or whether it can
emerge from the system's own history of adaptation, self-maintenance, or
learning." None of the four cites this question. Of the readings it
names, Bickhard's ([THEORY-220](../theory.d/THEORY-220.md)) is the one that answers A5 §5 directly: a
self-maintaining system that selects its next interaction anticipates
its success, and the anticipation can be false by the system's own
lights, so the expected profile is the system's. The detector has no
viability of its own, so on Bickhard's, Di Paolo's ([THEORY-628](../theory.d/THEORY-628.md)) and
Barandiaran's ([THEORY-217](../theory.d/THEORY-217.md)) accounts its norm is borrowed. The owner's
claim that the debugger's silence is evidence of a broken mechanism
([CLAIM-649](../claims.d/CLAIM-649.md), narrowed at the review of [#483](https://github.com/dmarx/nucleation/issues/483) the owner relayed on
2026-11-12) holds against the designer's response model and says
nothing on this question. The question stays Open.

## Note of 2026-11-12: working from relations among organizations, from "Medical Ethics and Media Enshitification"

A37 §§2 and 11 of the exchange (2026-11-11) propose a route this question
allows. Instead of deriving norms from one organization's self-maintenance,
"use organizational theory to explain how independently warranted norms
become effective, fail, or are systematically excluded", and start from
"conflicts among overlapping organizations and their standards of success":
parasite and host, platform and user, newsroom and public. The parasite case
shows the limit sharply: "A standard for successful biological functioning
is not yet a standard for what is owed to another being." A also flags
Leffler on constitutivism ([LIT-tmpu0vef](../literature.d/LIT-tmpu0vef.md), unread) as the adversarial test.
The question stays Open.
