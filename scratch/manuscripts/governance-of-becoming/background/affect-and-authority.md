## 3. Claims, in A51's order

### 3a. Authority against control (A51's lead)

- **CLAIM-889** (Proposed; demonstrated; thesis; not a member). The assistant's, from CASE-325. "a will-organization … beats control models that see the arrangement of authority only as an input by more than its Occam cost, and its gain is prediction of combined changes of authority from changes made one at a time … priced on the training arrangements alone, the same test also passes a will-organization whose relations compose otherwise than it says".
- **CLAIM-890** (Proposed; interpretive; rests on CLAIM-889; not a member). The assistant's. "against them the will-organization variables are implemented and not replaced; planning theory … gives no difference at all; institutional theory already writes the relations as rules-in-use, so for an organization the difference is one an existing theory gives, and what is left of the explanatory-gain claim is that one description serves across realizers, which a model of one realizer cannot test". QUESTION-056's note of 2026-11-22 says the same: "not idle, yet not new either. Still open for a person."

### 3b. Differentiated authority and recursive grants

- **CLAIM-283** (Active; interpretive; thesis; member; promoted "at the owner's ruling of 2026-10-27"). A's (A4 §2, reading Frijda). "The precedence a motive claims over action and the authority the rest of the organization grants it are different quantities: a motive can claim control without receiving it, and be granted control it does not claim". It "does not say which wins". Version 1's identification of internal conflict with the gap is split off as **CLAIM-484** (Proposed).
- **CLAIM-552** (Active since 2026-11-06, "at the status review's ruling (the owner delegated these rulings)"; interpretive; rests on CLAIM-534 and CLAIM-347; member, A49's formal centre). **The owner's U12** ("i was thinking more along the linesof tolerances expressing endorsement criteria … maybe I'm confusing necessary vs sufficient conditions"). "tolerances are directed … meeting the tolerance is necessary for that conferral where the criterion binds, and sufficient only for a grant, under a rule that grants influence on it". "The reading … is the reader's"; the wording is A's.
- **CLAIM-550** (Active; demonstrated; not a member). The first link of **the owner's U19 chain** ("motivational threshold -> motivational tolerances"): the tolerated discrepancy is "(θ + κ⁻¹ logit ε − b(c))/α(c)". **CLAIM-549** (Active; demonstrated): "The quiescent complex … is not the compatibility complex of their requirements … a perspective can be quiet while its requirement is unmet". **CLAIM-548** (Active; interpretive; **the owner's U18**): activation, grant and efficacy "are separate quantities". **CLAIM-553** (Proposed; the owner's U15 as corrected at U17): "a perspective that does not intervene has not thereby endorsed".
- **CLAIM-282** (Active; demonstrated; role granted; A2's analysis of **the owner's U2 design**, conceded). "Forbidding self-granting does not stop an operator from amplifying its own authority: under damped recursive delegation, reciprocal and cyclic grants recycle authority". **CLAIM-375** and **CLAIM-502** (Active; demonstrated) cover one-hop allocation and leave-one-out delegation, which "can keep an operator's authority exactly independent of its own grants".
- **CLAIM-443** (Active, "at the status review's ruling (the owner delegated these rulings)"; interpretive; not a member). **The owner's** ("what if we just treat this as a kind of momentum term? or like a hebbian prior?"). It is "a candidate mechanism for durable authority and for amortized governance; but persistence is not amortization, which is shown only when earlier work replaces later computation, communication or recruitment".

### 3c. Frustration, appraisal and action readiness

- **CLAIM-368** (Active; interpretive; rests on CLAIM-283 and CLAIM-027; member). **The owner's U33** ("maybe we should focus less on distress and more on frustration"). "The primitive for an organization's regulatory difficulty is frustration, not distress … independent of strain … and distinct from distress". "it says what to start from, not that a frustrated organization is distressed or feels anything".
- **CLAIM-347** (Active; interpretive; rests on CLAIM-368; not a member, though A51 lists it). **The owner's U37 correction** ("We can do quite a lot with access to an ordering"). "the jointly satisfiable sets of demands form a downward-closed complex … dropping or suppressing a demand is not relief".
- **CLAIM-923** (Proposed; demonstrated) answers QUESTION-131 in a model: "structural frustration and thwarting are independent in both directions".
- **CLAIM-tmpe6yfm** (Proposed; interpretive; member). **The owner's U14** ("Memes, Contracts …"). "Processes need not owe one another moral duties for a system of them to enter a pathological regime of incompatible behaviour, one that takes intervention and a reorganization of their commitments to resolve its systemic frustration". Its minimal instance is CASE-tmptpna1. "It does not say that every pathological regime is resolved by reorganizing commitments".
- **CLAIM-523** (Active; interpretive; member, A49 Art. 8). **The owner's synthesis** (2026-10-31). Legibility, volitional efficacy and correctability "are needed and any can fail while the others hold; none establishes that the demand or the revision is justified". These are necessary conditions.

### 3d. Functional and corporate affect

- **CLAIM-039** (Proposed; interpretive; member). **The owner's U19–U20**, reopened by the owner on 2026-10-23. "Affective states can be properties of organized systems, characterized functionally as Frijda characterizes emotions, without implying subjective experience". "The objection to answer first is THEORY-101's identity of valence with a felt state." CLAIM-432, 440 and 355 rest on it.
- **CLAIM-440** (Proposed; interpretive; rests on CLAIM-347, 283 and 039; member). **The owner's** ("what if the 'appraisal' is the process by which competing perspectives negotiate amongst themselves?"). Appraisal is the negotiation, and valence is "a regime in which an ordering of the organization's own states by their bearing on its maintenance prevails". Ambivalence "differs from neutrality".
- **CLAIM-437** (Proposed; the owner's wanting/liking, "I'm not sure if this resolves anything"); **CLAIM-442** (Proposed; **the assistant's**): "an emotion … is a claim, a perspective's valence is read on its claims, and the system's valence is read on what the negotiation grants"; **CLAIM-527** (Proposed; from readings of Massumi): "an organization can run ahead along its prevailing ordering while its viable reach contracts".
- **CLAIM-432** (Proposed; interpretive; rests on CLAIM-039 and CLAIM-271; not a member). The record's reading of Bakan, "filed at the owner's request … and revised the same day when the owner pointed out that the record's framework assigns affect". "it lacks remorse, functionally, when harm it does to others is not among the concerns its own appraisals track and gains precedence … only when a regulator or a market turns it into a threat". Its case is CASE-151.
- **CLAIM-431** (Proposed; counter; objects to CLAIM-432). "A corporation's functional remorse cannot be told from prudence … the clinical word adds nothing to a description of its incentives". It has no `defeated_if` and is unanswered.
- **CLAIM-1178** and **CLAIM-1113** (both Proposed; filed as alternatives on 2026-11-23): the board-change result is either evidence for part 5's redesign half or "no evidence". The note leans to CLAIM-1113.

### 3e. Learned accommodation, flow, pathology and nonintegrability

- **CLAIM-358** (Proposed; interpretive; rests on CLAIM-261; member). **The owner's U45** ("processes which can navigate disagreement amongst themselves require fewer resources …"), put in A45's conditional form at the owner's review. "Learned local accommodation **can** reduce the amortized cost of recurrent arbitration … though local negotiation and the relations it needs are not free". "it does not say local settlement is always cheaper".
- **CLAIM-364** (Proposed; counter; member). A45's. "Processes conditioned never to object coordinate without override at the same behaviour and supervisory cost as processes that negotiate, so the two part only under intervention, when a constituent dissents, or in their history".
- **CLAIM-451** (Proposed; interpretive; rests on CLAIM-434; not a member). **The owner's** ("could we characterize cycles as a kind of internal tension or cognitive dissonance?"). Cycling wins are "organizational nonintegrability"; it "becomes organizational tension only when realized as costly reversal … and cognitive dissonance only when that tension is detected and appraised as aversive"; and relief by "accommodation, by entrenched precedence or by cheap periodic coordination, which observed coherence does not tell apart". Its formal base, **CLAIM-434** and **CLAIM-436**, is Active and demonstrated: "some function rises with every move that wins … exactly when no chain of winning moves returns to where it began"; "the lexicographic order on the unit square has no real-valued representation". **CLAIM-448** (Active; counter) adds that "observed coherence is not evidence" where legibility selects contests.
- **CLAIM-446** (Proposed; interpretive; rests on CLAIM-443, 283 and 349; member, A49 §6). **The owner's** ("I think you may have inadvertently landed on a formalization of anxiety"). Some anxiety "may be governance-maintained rather than belief-maintained … the regime can outlast a corrected belief". "Its evidence is model-level and adjacent".
- **CLAIM-tmp0gs0t** (Proposed; member). A's (A34, A36). The capacities to recruit, maintain and dissolve an emergency regime are separable, and "the regime can outlast its justification".
- **CLAIM-162** (Proposed): in flow "no interest has to be overridden"; "the flow literature is registered unread". **CLAIM-441** (Proposed; the assistant's counter) holds that flow tells against "valence is undefined where no pair is contested". **CLAIM-1075** (Active; A's): "flow-regulation as an organizational capability".

### 3f. Biases and governance heuristics (the exchange's U10 to U12)

## 4. Cases

- **CASE-325** (Active; executed-study; the assistant's, LEAD-089; scratch/studies/lead-089/; not a member). **What was run.** Northstar's three authority bits, three regimes, and five control-only models (C0 to C4: 16, 128, 14, 38 and 64 parameters, against WO's 4). **Traps from REVIEW §1, as they recur here.**
- *Standing told too strongly.*
  - A51 calls CASE-325 "considerably better evidence" for will-organization.
