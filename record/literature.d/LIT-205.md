# LIT-205: One equation to rule them all: a philosophical analysis of the Price equation

Victor J. Luque (2016), *Biology & Philosophy 32(1):97–125 (January 2017; online 2016-09-16); preprint on PhilSci-Archive (#12404)* — DOI-10.1007/s10539-016-9538-y

## Key takeaways

- The Price equation is not a model on its own, as critics say, but a Kuhnian "generalization-sketch" — an abstract, unifying, invariant schema specialised into concrete models — which defuses disputes such as the one over Hamilton's rule and vindicates the analogy with Newton's second law.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Luque analyses the Price equation's role in evolutionary theory. Traditional population-genetic models build in simplifying assumptions to stay tractable, whereas the Price equation starts from assumptions taken to be true and derives the system's mathematical rules. He argues it is a generalization-sketch whose main job is to give researchers a unifying framework for building specific models, a role it can play because, like other scientific principles, it is abstract, unifying and invariant. Seeing it this way defuses recent disputes about it.
