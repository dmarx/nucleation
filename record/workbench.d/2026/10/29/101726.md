**Checks run before anything was applied.** Three of the formal readings
were promoted on re-derivations, and their scripts were re-run here.

- **[THEORY-019](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-019.md) (the real Schur corollary).** One group of each real type
  matches the predicted commutant and symmetric-commutant dimensions: S₃ for
  ℝ (9 and 6), ℤ/5 for ℂ (18 and 9), and Q8 for ℍ (16 and 6). The generic
  multiplicities match too: (2,2,2), (2,2,2) and (4,4).
  - Two controls have simple spectra: a random symmetric matrix, and a
    commuting operator given a 10⁻³ non-commuting perturbation.
  - The 3-cube Laplacian commutes with both B₃ and S₃, and its two middle
    levels are irreducible under the first and reducible under the second.
    So one spectrum fits two groups, which is [CLAIM-385](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-385.md)'s point.
- **[THEORY-156](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-156.md) (Blackwell's converse).** The garbling identities hold to
  3×10⁻¹⁵. The convex-order splitting residual falls from 8.3×10⁻³ to
  1.1×10⁻³ as the iteration count grows. A control pair that is not in convex
  order stays at 5.5×10⁻².
- **[THEORY-168](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-168.md) (Theorem 4.6 and CbD 1.0).** A brute-force check by max flow
  agrees with nominal dominance on every random pair at k = 3, 4 and 5. The
  worked non-heredity case reproduces:
  - Pr[V₂≠V₃] is forced to 0.52, where a maximal coupling needs 0.20.
  - The case is noncontextual under 1.0, contextual under 2.0, and its
    subsystem is contextual under 1.0.

  The reviewer's side finding was checked by hand. For a binary content in
  three contexts, maximizing Pr[all equal] fixes Pr[all 1] = p₁ and
  Pr[all 0] = 1 − p₃, and then p₂ fixes the last two patterns. So the maximal
  coupling is unique, and binary connections first separate 1.0 from 2.0 at
  four contexts. The summary now says so.
