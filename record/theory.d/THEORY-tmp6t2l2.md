# THEORY-tmp6t2l2: In mice, offspring's tolerance of non-inherited maternal antigens is sustained only by the small fraction of maternal microchimeric cells that co-express LysM and CD11c, and depleting that fraction removes the tolerance while most maternal microchimeric cells persist

## The account

Mothers leave cells in their offspring; about one cell in a million is of
maternal origin. According to the post, Peng, Way and colleagues
developed tools to deplete defined types of these cells in mice. Removing
the cells that co-express LysM and CD11c removed the offspring's tolerance
of the maternal antigens it did not inherit. Most maternal cells remained.
So in this system the presence of foreign-origin cells, and the function
some of them serve in the host's immune regulation, are separable by
intervention.

## Evidence

The post's own statements ([NOTE-tmph9lnd](../notes.d/NOTE-tmph9lnd.md)), and, as a check only, the
paper's abstract on the Europe PMC API, which says that conditional
depletion "does not diminish overall MMc levels, dissociating NIMA-specific
tolerance from MMc persistence". The paper ([LIT-tmpxdjn3](../literature.d/LIT-tmpxdjn3.md)) is Unfetched.

## What it does not say

That the result holds in humans; what the persisting cells do; or anything
about organismal individuality. It is a result about which cells serve one
regulatory function.
