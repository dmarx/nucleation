# LIT-tmp8qzkt: Inverted Earth

Ned Block (1990), *Philosophical Perspectives* 4 (*Action Theory and
Philosophy of Mind*), pp. 53–79 — DOI-10.2307/2214187.

The brief's citation (Phil Perspectives 4, 1990) is correct. The paper began
as a reply to Gilbert Harman's "The Intrinsic Quality of Experience" in the
same volume (n. 1: delivered at the Chapel Hill Colloquium, 17 October
1987). Harman's paper is not in the record.

## Key takeaways

- **The fallacy of intentionalizing qualia (pp. 55–57).** If spectrum
  inversion is possible, contents expressible in public language, such as
  *looking red*, are intentional, not qualitative: "'Red' is a public
  language word, not a word that means one thing in your mouth and another
  thing in mine." The intentional content of colour experience is
  referential, fixed by the colours it is caused by and used about.
- **Quasi-functional qualia realism (p. 58).** Intentional content of
  experience is functional, in "long-arm" roles that reach to coloured
  things; qualitative content is not functionally characterisable.
- **The case (pp. 62–67).** Inverted Earth (Harman's example) has
  complementary colours and inverted colour vocabulary. A kidnapped subject
  with inverting lenses notices no change. On the first day his intentional
  contents are still Earth's, and wrong; after fifty years they have shifted
  to the natives', and his functional states have shifted with them. In the
  interpersonal version, twins with molecularly identical brains look at
  their skies: same qualitative content, inverted intentional content,
  inverted long-arm functional role. If Q = F₁ and Q = F₂ with F₁ ≠ F₂,
  transitivity fails, so qualitative content is no functional state.
- **Why it beats the inverted spectrum (pp. 67–68).** No internal switch,
  no adaptation period to cast doubt on testimony, and supervenience is
  asymmetric: identical brains establish identical qualia, while physically
  different brains never establish different qualia.
- **The escape Block concedes (pp. 69–71).** Brain-internal functional
  descriptions make the twins functionally identical, and could serve a
  "two factor" functionalism (long-arm roles for intentional content,
  short-arm roles for qualia). Block's answer is a burden of proof: say what
  the short-arm roles are, the qualia in "the 'program' of the mind". "Good
  luck to him!" The same escape would sink the inverted spectrum argument.
- **Method (p. 73).** "Elicit simple intuitions about complex cases rather
  than complex intuitions about simple cases": Harman's introspective
  diaphanousness claim is the wrong way round.

  the other.

**Where it bears.**

- *Chalmers* ([LIT-392](LIT-392.md), [NOTE-347](../notes.d/NOTE-347.md); [LIT-406](LIT-406.md), [NOTE-336](../notes.d/NOTE-336.md)). Chalmers's principle of
  organisational invariance is about "fine-grained functional
  organization", which is internal. Block's twins are internally identical,
  so Chalmers's principle agrees they share qualia; Inverted Earth refutes
  only *long-arm* functionalism about qualia. What Block leaves as a burden
  for the functionalist, a short-arm functional theory of qualia, is the
  position Chalmers's fading- and dancing-qualia arguments defend. That
  pairing is mine; neither cites the other on this point.
- *Representationalism.* The paper is the basis of Block's later case against
  representationism about phenomenal character in "Mental Paint"
  ([LIT-tmpep9zj](LIT-tmpep9zj.md)), and [NOTE-089](../notes.d/NOTE-089.md) notes that Nagel's paper predates the
  functionalist and representationalist accounts this argues against.
- *Meaning.* Note 26 cites "Functional Role and Truth Conditions"
  ([LIT-tmp1az05](LIT-tmp1az05.md)) for the equivalence of long-arm roles to short-arm roles
  plus reference; Block's own two-factor theory of meaning is what licenses
  the "two factor" functionalism he here challenges the functionalist to fill
  in for qualia.
- *IIT* ([LIT-445](LIT-445.md)). The premise that qualitative content supervenes on
  internal physical constitution is one IIT shares; nothing more is claimed.
