# CLAIM-989: Alternative reading (b) of the constitutivism test: where an organization's own constitution fixes the standard a judgment about it is indexed to, fixing the relata leaves the verdict's truth non-relative but not the standard's force, which Leffler's argument makes relative to that constitution, so the claim that indexing does not by itself make a judgment relative holds for truth and not for force

## The claim

[CLAIM-363](CLAIM-363.md) says a normative judgment is well-defined only once its
relata are fixed, "and that dependence does not by itself make it
relative: once they are fixed, the judgment can be false". Its example is
A17's: "an action may objectively undermine the viability of a particular
organization under specified physical conditions."

Leffler's plight inescapability ([THEORY-2268](../theory.d/THEORY-2268.md)) gives the same
structure for agents, and shows what it leaves out. Once an agent's
constitution fixes "the thing to do", whether the agent did it is a fact,
and the agent can fail: that is his OICF and criticizability. Yet the
force of the standard is relative: "agents with different constitutions
have different norms applying to them as forceful". So a judgment can be
non-relative in its truth and relative in its force at once.

Where the standard relatum of a judgment about an organization is the
organization's own (its viability, its upkeep, [CLAIM-648](CLAIM-648.md)'s profile),
the same holds by [ARG-217](../arguments.d/ARG-217.md). [CLAIM-363](CLAIM-363.md)'s second part is true of
the verdict's truth. Read as saying the judgment is not relative at all,
it overstates: its standard binds only what it constitutes. The claim's
own "What it does not say" already separates the choice of relata from
the verdict and declines A17's fourth row; this reading asks that
"relative" in the title be read as truth-relativity, and that the
relativity of force be named as what the claim leaves open.

## What it does not say

- It does not deny either part of [CLAIM-363](CLAIM-363.md); it narrows the sense of
  the second.
- It does not cover standards that are not the organization's own (a
  standard of freedom from enslavement, [CASE-257](../cases.d/CASE-257.md)); the narrowing
  applies only where the organization's constitution fixes the standard.
- It does not say force is relative for every standard, only on the
  constitutive route Leffler examines.
