# NOTE-tmpzok4t: Ivern and Carbonell (2026) — Categorization of Loot Box Design

## Contribution

The most granular literature-derived checklist of loot-box design features
so far, against Ballou et al.'s 32 and Denoo et al.'s 51 (p. 12), with a
coding scheme meant to standardize vocabulary and let screening tools and
regulation address design variants rather than "the loot box" as one thing.

## Key results

- **Scope (abstract).** "72 empirically based studies published between
  2017 and 2026 met the inclusion criteria. These studies revealed 136
  distinct loot box design features organized into 22 subcategories across
  six broader dimensions."
- **The gap (abstract).** "Significant research gaps were identified,
  especially regarding the impact and interaction of these features."
- **Categories (pp. 4, 12).** Obfuscation 35, Value/Data/Engagement
  Manipulation 28, Psychological Manipulation 23, Social Influence and
  Promotion 23, Self-Regulation Obstacles 17, Gambling Resemblance 10.
- **Codes rank prevalence (p. 4).** "A lower third number indicated a higher
  prevalence in the literature." The commonest: displayable cosmetic
  rewards, openings that need in-game currency, pay-to-win rewards,
  virtual goods exchangeable for real money, and a high odds disparity
  between rare and non-rare rewards.
- **Odds and prices that vary (Table 2, pp. 13–15).** "Reward pool,
  availability, or odds change with progression or system conditions";
  "Odds vary across player accounts"; "Dynamic LB odds, pricing, or offers
  based on player behavior"; "Higher odds for new or returning accounts";
  "Odds increase when purchasing in bulk"; and "Personalized LB ads or
  offers based on profile and gameplay data". Disclosed odds "may not
  consistently reflect the actual chances players face" (p. 13).
- **Self-Regulation Obstacles (Table 2).** Among them "LBs purchasing
  process lacks confirmation steps", "LB spending or opening limits cannot
  be set by the player or parents" and "LB-related notifications or offers
  cannot be turned off".
- **Discussion (pp. 17–18).** The update added mostly obfuscation features,
  "particularly odds changeability and information opacity", which "likely
  reflects the regulatory prominence of transparency-related measures";
  fixed currency bundles leave "leftover balances that encourage additional
  spending"; pity timers are what "players often view as offering better
  value", "yet they may also encourage additional spending".
- **Limits and conclusion (p. 18).** "assessing the relative severity or
  harm of individual features remains difficult"; the review is "a catalog
  of features rather than a detailed evaluation of individual mechanics";
  "the risks associated with LBs stem from the interaction of multiple
  features within broader monetization systems, rather than a single design
  element."

## Assumptions

That the literature's attention marks the features that matter (the count is
of studies, not of games or of harm); that each feature belongs in one
subcategory by its most relevant problematic aspect; that what researchers
call problematic is the frame, though players may see the same features
favourably (p. 17).

## Concepts

Loot box; design-feature checklist; obfuscation; odds changeability;
information opacity; pity timer; kompu gacha; pay-to-win versus cosmetic;
cash-out; forced engagement; bulk purchasing; self-regulation obstacles.

  identifying 136 loot-box design features across 72 studies, with little
  known of their separate and joint effects") is accurate, with two
  refinements: the 136 are features studied as problematic, ranked by how
  often the literature names them, not a census of games; and "little
  known" is the authors' own finding. The citation is Current Addiction
  Reports 13, article 64, not "13(1)".

## Limitations

- It counts mentions, not effects; prevalence in the literature is not
  prevalence in games or harm, as the authors say.
- Internal inconsistencies: "55 published in peer-reviewed journals and 12
  in conference proceedings" (p. 4) sums to 67, not 72; the update is dated
  March 2026 in the methods and February 2026 in the discussion.
- The update was narrower than the main search and admitted only studies
  with new features, so later features are undercounted.
- Ordering features by the problem they cause presupposes harms the review
  says are unmeasured.
- Supplementary File 1 was not seen.
