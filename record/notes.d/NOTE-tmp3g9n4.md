# NOTE-tmp3g9n4: Identity fusion: The interplay of personal and social identities in extreme group behavior

## Contribution

It introduces identity fusion as a form of allegiance to a group distinct
from identification. In identification, as self-categorization theory
describes it, the personal self recedes and the member becomes
interchangeable with others. In fusion, the personal self stays "potent"
and joins the group self. The paper gives a one-item measure, shows it
picks out a distinct class, and shows that in that class the personal
self drives pro-group extremity rather than competing with the group.

## Key insight

For a fused person, a threat to who they are as an individual is answered
by acting for the group. Told that an evaluator saw their faults as
virtues, fused participants were readier to fight and die for Spain;
nonfused participants were unmoved. The group is treated as part of what
the person must keep true about themselves. The authors put it this way:
the fused "feel that they care as much about the outcomes of the group as
their own outcomes" (p. 996).

## Assumptions

- **Self-verification theory** (Swann 1983). People are motivated to keep
  their self-views, negative ones included, and a challenge prompts
  compensatory effort to restore them. Experiments 1 and 2 rest on it:
  positive feedback on negative traits is a *challenge*.
- **Willingness to fight and die is self-reported.** Five fight items
  ("I would fight someone insulting or making fun of Spain as a whole")
  and two die items ("I would sacrifice my life if it saved another group
  member's life"), on −3 to 3 scales.
- **The country as a collective group.** Chosen because affinity with it
  is "largely uncontaminated by affection toward individual members"
  (p. 995).

## Key results

- **The measure** (pp. 997–998). Five pairs of circles, self and Spain, at
  0%, 25%, 50%, 75% and 100% overlap. The last, option E, is "fused",
  adapted from Schubert and Otten's version of Aron et al.'s
  inclusion-of-other-in-self scale.
- **Preliminary studies 1–5** (UNED distance students, N = 200, 120, 177,
  151, 251).
  - Study 1: fusion is bimodal (b = .653, cutoff .555); 40.5% chose E;
    r with Mael and Ashforth's identification scale .42 (dichotomous) and
    .56 (continuous). US rates were about half.
  - Study 2: choosing D or E took longer (about 9 s) and felt harder than
    A to C, F(4, 115) = 55.51 for difficulty.
  - Study 3: only fusion predicted saying one would fight (β = .43) or die
    (β = .23) for the group *more than other members would*. Nonfused
    participants said others would do more than they would.
  - Study 4: an added option between D and E, to rule out reading E as
    "central" or "special", gave the same result; the two fused options
    did not differ.
  - Study 5: fusion with Spain was uncorrelated with fusion with friends,
    family, religion, sport team or party (|r| ≤ .10). It is not a trait.
- **Experiment 1** (N = 602 adults, waves 4–5 months apart; 237 fused).
  Fusion × challenge on fighting, β = .17, p < .001; on dying, β = .14,
  p < .006. The challenge raised fused participants' scores and left
  nonfused participants' unchanged. Fusion and prototypicality were
  uncorrelated (r = .05).
- **Experiment 2** (N = 326). The same, with the feedback from someone in
  another EU country, to rule out a self-categorization reading in which
  an in-group member's disagreement threatens interchangeability.
  Fusion × challenge: fight β = .27, p < .002; die β = .20, p < .02.
- **Experiment 3** (N = 421 high-school students, mean age 15.8, waves 10
  days apart). Instead of feedback, participants rated what they would
  fight for, *for themselves* (personal activation) or *for Spain* (social
  activation), or did nothing.
  - Willingness to die: activation × fusion, F(2, 411) = 4.50, p < .01.
    In the fused, both activations raised it equally. In the nonfused,
    only social activation did.
  - Certainty of one's personal self-views: in the fused, both
    activations raised it equally. In the nonfused, only personal
    activation did.
- **Levels.** Adults' means for dying stayed below the scale midpoint in
  every condition (fused, Experiment 1: M = −0.67).

## Concepts

- **identity fusion**: a "visceral" oneness with a group in which the
  self–other barrier is blurred, the group is functionally equivalent to
  the personal self, and the personal self stays salient.
- **functional equivalence**: activating either identity has the same
  downstream effects, so a challenge to one is repaired through the other.
- **identification** (as the authors read SCT): agreement of the personal
  self-concept with the group prototype, reached by depersonalization.
- **self-verification**: the motive to keep one's self-views, positive or
  negative, confirmed by others.

## Connections

- **Self-categorization theory ([THEORY-tmpo85ke](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpo85ke.md)).** The paper is aimed at
  functional antagonism (Turner et al. 1987: raising the salience of one
  level lowers the other) and at depersonalization. The fused claim they
  would do more than other members, which is not interchangeability. The
  authors cite Simon, Spears, Deaux, Postmes and Jetten as earlier doubts.
  They also note (p. 1008) that the theory holds social identity can drive
  behaviour "not in the individual's self-interest".
- **León's personalized "we" ([THEORY-tmpsm03n](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpsm03n.md)).** A second route to the
  same conclusion: a we-identity that does not dissolve the individual.
- **Aron et al. ([LIT-tmp9rujh](../literature.d/LIT-tmp9rujh.md)) and Cialdini et al. ([LIT-tmpfxss5](../literature.d/LIT-tmpfxss5.md)).** The
  measure descends from Aron's overlap circles. Cialdini's "oneness" is
  measured with them too.
- **Hogg's uncertainty account** is the rival the authors set themselves
  against: extremity from firm identities, not shaky ones.

## Bearing on the record

- **The owner's thesis ([CLAIM-tmpjylq8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjylq8.md)).** This is the closest empirical
  work the record holds to it, and it bears on it in three ways. These
  are inferences; the paper does not discuss selflessness.
  - It supports "larger self" against "changed self". The claim's "What it
    does not say" notes that depersonalization is a change of self, not
    plainly a larger one. Fusion is the case where the personal self is
    kept and the group added to it.
  - Its key result is a test the thesis would predict. If the group is part
    of the self, defending the self should go through the group. A
    challenge to one's personal self-views raised willingness to die for
    Spain only in those whose measured self includes Spain.
  - It does not say the sacrifice is self-interested. The authors call
    dying for the group "not in the individual's self-interest" and
    explain it by self-verification, the motive to keep one's self-views
    true. A thesis that recruits this paper says that the self being
    served is a self-concept to be kept true, not a welfare to be
    maximized.
- **The triviality objection ([CLAIM-tmprudek](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmprudek.md)).** Fusion is a criterion of
  the self's extent measured by perceived overlap, not by what one cares
  about. It is group-specific and not reducible to identification. But
  the paper never measures caring separately, and its own gloss defines
  the fused by how much they care. So it offers the kind of criterion the
  objection asks for without showing that the criterion and caring come
  apart. A dated note is added to the claim.
- **Batson's objection ([CLAIM-tmpybc4k](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpybc4k.md)).** Not addressed: fusion is with a
  group, and nothing here concerns empathic helping of individuals.
- **No THEORY filed.** Fusion is recorded in the thesis and its
  objections; the later fusion literature is not held.
- **No ML instruction.**

## Limitations

- **Intentions, not behaviour.** Every outcome is agreement with a
  statement. Nobody fought or died.
- **One country, one population.** UNED distance students (older, mostly
  women in the experiments) and one high-school sample, all Spanish, in a
  period the authors describe as one of heightened national tension.
- **A single item** carries the construct. The authors grant a verbal
  battery might capture the same oneness.
- **Experiment 3's design.** All participants first got the challenging
  feedback, so the activation and the feedback may have acted together;
  the authors say so (p. 1007).
- **Prototypicality was one item** that did not correlate with
  identification, so "fusion is unrelated to prototypicality" rests on a
  measure the authors doubt.
- **Typos.**
  - In Study 2, option C's mean time is printed as "50.10" s and option
    A's SD as 6.36.
  - In Experiment 2 the dying result is described as "willingness to
    fight", and the nonfused SD as ".095".
  - Experiment 3's η² is printed as "10".
  - "ensures" stands for "ensues" (p. 1008).

## Open questions

- Does fusion predict costly action, not only endorsing it? The later
  fusion literature claims so; the record holds none of it.
- Can fusion and caring be pulled apart? A study that measured concern for
  the group's welfare alongside overlap, and asked which predicts sacrifice
  when the other is held fixed, would settle what this paper contributes
  to [CLAIM-tmprudek](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmprudek.md).
