# CLAIM-483: An argument developed with collaborators who keep no memory fails mainly at identity and not at structure, so that coding what recurs, not argument mapping, is the remedy, and this holds beyond one owner and one assistant family

## The claim

[CLAIM-177](CLAIM-177.md) version 1 said that such an argument "fails at identity, not at
structure". Two things in that are more than the record observed:

- **The comparison.** Only the will exchange was read move by move for
  both kinds of defect ([ADR-032](../decisions.d/ADR-032.md)). There, 7 of about 30 claims in the final
  manuscript had more than one independent line of support. Structure was
  thin, and identity failures were what went wrong.
- **The population.** Every exchange since has been one owner with one
  assistant family.

If both hold generally, the remedy follows: code a thing the second time
it turns up ([CLAIM-176](CLAIM-176.md)), and do not expect argument mapping to help. That
is the design argument of the nucleating-argument line.

## Why it is Proposed

What would settle it is the move-by-move reading the defeater names, done
on a second exchange and on an exchange with another owner or another
assistant family.

## Note of 2026-10-29: what holds it back

The comparison was counted in one exchange and the population is one owner
and one assistant family. It waits on the move-by-move reading its defeat
condition names, done on a second exchange and on one with another owner or
assistant family; none is in the record.
