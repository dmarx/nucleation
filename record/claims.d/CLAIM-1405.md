# CLAIM-1405: Who pays the cognitive load of making something legible is an allocation, and an institution steers inquiry by it

## The claim

The record's legibility program says an institution steers inquiry by who
must pay to make ideas legible to it. [CLAIM-1117](CLAIM-1117.md): "with no rule against any
idea". [CLAIM-1132](CLAIM-1132.md): possibility is shaped by what members can afford to make
intelligible to those empowered to let them act. On [CLAIM-1424](CLAIM-1424.md), that cost
is cognitive load, and it has to be paid by someone:
- **by the writer,** who converts the work into a form of low load for the
  reader: a grant proposal, a summary, a metric;
- **by the reader,** who carries the load of the work as it was developed.

[CLAIM-1258](CLAIM-1258.md) is the writer's side. The form in which knowledge is
institutionally legible is split from the form in which it is productively
developed, and the conversion is the writer's bill.

## The reader's bound selects

An authority's attention is bounded. It reads what is cheap to read and
defers or rejects the rest. Merit held fixed, it admits forms of low reader
load:
- **work that fits its readers' schemas,** since that lowers intrinsic load
  for them;
- **work in its preferred formats,** since that lowers extraneous load;
- **numbers over arguments.**

That is how legibility becomes a filter. [CLAIM-448](CLAIM-448.md) warns that a record whose
legibility regime selects which contests are admitted can look coherent
because of what it left out. On this claim, a common cause of the selection
is the reviewers' load. Contests whose distinctions are costly to make
legible to them are the ones that drop out.

## Ties

- **Metrics.** A metric is the extreme of low reader load: one number. That
  is part of why institutions govern by them, and why [TERM-173](../terms.d/TERM-173.md)'s measurement
  naivety tempts.
- **Rankings.** [CLAIM-1392](CLAIM-1392.md)'s legible ranking is the same trade with
  publishers as writers and a ranker as reader.

## What it does not say

That every low-load form loses something. Some conversions only remove
extraneous load. The loss comes when the conversion drops the distinction
itself.
