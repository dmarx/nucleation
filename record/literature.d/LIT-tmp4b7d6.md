# LIT-tmp4b7d6: The current status of research on the structure of evaluative space

Catherine J. Norris, Jackie Gollan, Gary G. Berntson & John T. Cacioppo
(2010), *Biological Psychology* 84(3):422–436 —
DOI-10.1016/j.biopsycho.2010.03.011

## Key takeaways

- **Separable channels, bipolar output.** Behaviour "may be constrained to
  a single bipolar dimension" of approach and withdrawal. The mechanisms
  feeding it are two systems, positivity and negativity, that are "not
  equivalent in their constitution, operations, or consequences"
  (functional separability). Their combined outputs "produce a
  behavioral predisposition to either approach or avoid the stimulus".
- **Three modes.** "Positivity and negativity can be reciprocally
  activated, independently activated or co-activated" (Table 1).
  Reciprocal activation is the usual mode and is the more stable one
  (energetic efficiency).
- **Ambivalence is coactivation.** Coactivation is "objective
  ambivalence", "resulting in a conflict between approach and avoidant
  behaviors". It is "generally rare and highly arousing, as they provide
  indeterminant guidance for behavior". Fast oscillation between
  opposite stimuli produces the same state, acting as a low-pass filter.
- **Neutral and ambivalent give the same output.** On the response
  surface (Figure 2), the coactivity diagonal "which represents different
  evaluative processes (e.g., neutral to ambivalent) – yields the same
  middling expression". The mapping from evaluative space to output is
  many-to-one.
- **Asymmetric activation functions.** The gain for negativity is higher
  (negativity bias), and the intercept for positivity is higher
  (positivity offset). Both recalibrate with context, like
  light-adaptation in vision.
- **Heterarchy.** Evaluation is represented from the spinal cord to the
  frontal lobes. At low levels responses may be constrained to
  reciprocal activation. "higher levels of the neuraxis have the ability
  to override these dominant responses".
- **Clinical.** Depression and anxiety differ in which channel is
  dysregulated: heightened negativity in both, and diminished positivity
  in depression.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 for the record's proposal that hedonic valence is
dominance by one ordering (CLAIM-tmpz8lli, TERM-tmp8gbrt). That proposal
separates ambivalence from neutrality, and the evaluative space model did
so first. It is the only first-hand statement of the model the record
could reach lawfully: the 1994 and 1997 papers are Unavailable. The
bearing is set out in NOTE-tmpb0ouu. In short:

- the model anticipates the distinction;
- it locates ambivalence in the activation of two channels, not in a
  failed or split ordering;
- its output is always an ordering, a net approach–avoid predisposition;
- its asymmetries are something the proposal's symmetric sign clause
  does not predict.

The record's other dimensional account, Barrett's (LIT-1353, LIT-542),
takes valence as one bipolar dimension of core affect. This model denies
that.
