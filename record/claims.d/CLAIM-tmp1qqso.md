# CLAIM-tmp1qqso: Read by its definition, the closing-nucleus loop is organizational closure of two constraints: the teacher constrains, without being consumed, the formation that produces nuclei, and the nucleus constrains, without being consumed, the production of teachers, so each depends directly on the other and is depended on

## The claim

[TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md), after Montévil and Mossio, sets out three things.

- **A constraint.** It acts on a process at a time scale at which its
  relevant aspects are conserved.
- **Dependence.** C₁ depends on C₂ when C₂ constrains a process that
  produces aspects of C₁.
- **Closure.** A set is closed when every member depends directly on
  another member and is depended on.

In [CASE-tmpy4orj](../cases.d/CASE-tmpy4orj.md) the loop meets each condition:

- **The teacher T is a constraint.** It acts on formation (material into
  nuclei) without being used up. It turns over at k_T, slowly against any
  single formation event.
- **The nucleus N is a constraint.** It acts on teacher production
  (material into teachers) without being used up. It turns over at d.
- **Each depends on the other.** N depends on T, since T constrains the
  process that produces N. T depends on N in the same way.
- **The set is closed.** {T, N} has two members, the least
  [CLAIM-395](CLAIM-395.md) allows.

The material flux is a process the constraints act on, not a member. That
is why the material supply can never be withdrawn while the teacher
supply can.

## What it does not say

- **The time-scale corollary.** At the base point k_T = d, so each
  constraint is produced at the scale at which it is conserved. That is
  the case [CLAIM-395](CLAIM-395.md)'s reading (B) sets aside. The definition is still
  met, but the slow-and-fast corollary does not apply there. At the
  e1 grid points where k_T ≠ d, it applies.
- **Nothing more.** Closure here is not life, agency or a self
  ([TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md), "What it is not").
