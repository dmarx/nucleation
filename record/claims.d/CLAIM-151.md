# CLAIM-151: Which communicative act an utterance performs is partly constituted by its relations to participant roles, norms and expected uptake, so a transformation that changes those relations can change the act and not only its realization

## The claim

A187 §3, after the owner asked at U52 whether the manuscript could be
connected to ontic structural realism: "**The identity of a communicative act
is partly constituted by its relations to participants, norms, interpretive
conditions, and possible conversational consequences.** That is stronger than
claiming that observers merely infer those relationships. It says that
changing certain relations can change the act itself."

The example is [CASE-018](../cases.d/CASE-018.md)'s sentence, "You blow it all on sex and booze":
"being a reprimand is not simply an intrinsic property of a sequence of words.
It depends upon speaker position, relevant norms, audience expectations". A187
then separates a representational transformation (same organization, new
realization) from a constitutive one: "converting shared joking into a
reprimand by changing speaker authority and expected uptake". That is
[CLAIM-004](CLAIM-004.md)'s passive/active distinction from A18, re-derived without citation.
The claim rests on it, and on [CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md)'s finding that the proposition does not
fix the communicative event.

A191 §1 put it in two sentences: "Changing u while preserving the appropriate
relationships may conserve the act's identity. Changing the appropriate
relationships while holding u fixed may transform the act." A214 §13 gives the
conceptual argument: acts such as promising, authorizing, teasing and
reprimanding "cannot be adequately specified without reference to constitutive
social and normative relationships". A203 §3 and A218 §2 restate it.

What is preserved across realizations is a type, not an event. A191 §2: "Our
transport theory often concerns preservation of **act type** across
realizations, rather than numerical identity of a single historical event."
The type is a communicative kind ([TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md)). Which of an act's relations
are constitutive, rather than descriptive or causal, is the distinction
[TERM-038](../terms.d/TERM-038.md) draws. The claim says only that some are.

It answers [QUESTION-021](../questions.d/QUESTION-021.md) for its second position. [QUESTION-021](../questions.d/QUESTION-021.md) noted that the
manuscript already takes that position "without saying so".

## What it does not say

- It does not say every communicative property is relational. It says
  "partly".
- It does not say that teasing is constituted by relations. That is
  [QUESTION-tmpcs4qg](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpcs4qg.md), and until it is answered the claim's "can change the
  act" is carried by acts with standing conditions.
- It does not say utterances, speakers or audiences do not exist. It is a
  thesis about social acts, not about fundamental ontology. A191 §2: "We do
  not need to deny that speakers, utterance tokens, audiences, and physical
  interactions exist."
- It is not a structural realism. "Partly constituted" leaves intrinsic
  identity-fixing features a role, and that is the intrinsic horn of
  Chakravartty's dilemma ([THEORY-034](../theory.d/THEORY-034.md)). On the record's reading a view on that
  horn is not ontic structural realism. So "domain-specific moderate OSR"
  (A187 §7) and "moderate communicative structural realism" (A191 §2) are the
  wrong names for it. Its subject matter is that of social ontology. Searle's
  status functions and deontic powers ([LIT-482](../literature.d/LIT-482.md), read in [NOTE-388](../notes.d/NOTE-388.md)) are the
  held, read account of facts constituted by collective acceptance and norms,
  and they are the nearer prior art. Austin and Goffman, whom A187 §8 names,
  are held but unread.
- It does not say that an effect of telling an interpreter about the
  situation shows the act changed. That is interpretation under instruction
  ([CLAIM-130](CLAIM-130.md)).
- It does not revise [CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md). A187 proposed a new central thesis
  ("Communicative identity is relationally constituted, observationally
  accessed, and transformation-relative in its persistence"), and the owner
  did not take it up.
- "Partly" must stay explicit. A214 wavers: §14's second step says "partly
  constituted", while its closing thesis says "relationally constituted"
  without the qualifier. Only the qualified form is filed here.

The wording is not new. "Partly constituted" was the superseded [CLAIM-055](CLAIM-055.md)'s,
about the transformations an utterance induces. Here it returns as a claim
about the act's relations.
