# LIT-435: The emergence of understanding in a computer model of concepts and analogy-making

Melanie Mitchell, Douglas R. Hofstadter (1990), *Physica D: Nonlinear
Phenomena 42(1–3):322–334* — DOI-10.1016/0167-2789(90)90086-5

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-01 at the owner's request for Douglas Hofstadter's work in
this record: the Fluid Analogies Research Group's programme that
analogy-making is the core of cognition, of which Copycat is the founding
program. It is the journal paper the group itself cites for Copycat's
mechanics.

`Deferred` because no lawful full text could be reached (see the history
note), so no NOTE is filed. What the record can say without it comes from
Chalmers, French & Hofstadter ([LIT-414](LIT-414.md)), read in full, which describes
Copycat and sends readers here "for further details". On that account:

- Copycat solves letter-string analogies ("if abc changes to abd, what does
  xyz change to?") by building its own representations of the strings, not
  by being handed them.
- Many small agents (codelets) build and break bonds, groups and
  correspondences in parallel and nondeterministically. A network of
  concepts (the Slipnet) is activated by what they find and biases which
  codelets run next. A computational temperature, falling as coherent
  structure accumulates, sets how random their choices are.
- The xyz problem is the showcase. The successor of z does not exist in
  Copycat's non-circular alphabet, so the program hits a snag, raises its
  temperature, breaks structure, and can re-perceive xyz as a predecessor
  group to answer wyz. [LIT-414](LIT-414.md) is careful that it gives xyd more often
  than wyz, and attributes "further details" of this run to this paper.

The title's claim, that *understanding* emerges, is what a reading would
test. It places the paper in the record's emergence cluster: McKenzie's
survey ([LIT-141](LIT-141.md)) counts self-organisation and downward causation among the
marks of emergence, and Copycat's Slipnet is explicitly a top-down
influence on bottom-up agents. Whether "emergence" here means more than
"no single codelet does it" is the question.

It also sits beside Minsky's account of a mind as many small, mindless
agents ([LIT-429](LIT-429.md)), filed in the same session: Copycat is a working
program in that spirit, though this record has not read whether the paper
cites Minsky. Hofstadter's book-length account of the group's programme, *Fluid
Concepts and Creative Analogies* (1995), is being filed beside this work.

What a reading should check: the run statistics it reports for its
problems, the exact definition of temperature, whether it compares
Copycat's answer distributions with people's (its companion paper says the
distributions are "qualitatively similar", citing Mitchell's 1990
dissertation), and what it means by "understanding".
