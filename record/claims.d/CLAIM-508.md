# CLAIM-508: Gene ancestries alone cannot tell a symbiotic partner from a source of transferred genes: a partner whose genes were mostly lost, replaced or spread thin would leave the diffuse trace that Tobiasson and colleagues read as piecemeal transfer, and Bernabeu and colleagues concede that their donor signals say nothing of the kind of interaction

## The claim

Tobiasson and colleagues ([THEORY-1104](../theory.d/THEORY-1104.md)) read the absence of functional coherence among non-alphaproteobacterial genes as the mark of isolated transfers rather than further endosymbioses. But coherence is what a partner leaves only if its genes survive together. A partner whose genes were mostly lost, replaced by host or other genes, or retained thinly across pathways would leave the same diffuse trace. Bernabeu and colleagues ([THEORY-1105](../theory.d/THEORY-1105.md)) find major donors and say themselves that their data "do not provide information on potential types of interaction". On either reading, whether a donor was a partner is a question gene ancestries do not settle.

Known partnerships bear the premise out. In the nitroplast no gene transfer from the endosymbiont has been found, though more than 350 nucleus-encoded proteins are imported into it; in the pea aphid no functional transfer from *Buchnera* occurred, and the one host protein shown inside it is encoded by a gene from another bacterium; in *Paulinella* about 60 nuclear genes come from the chromatophore and about 170 from other bacteria (Sørensen and colleagues, read in [NOTE-1241](../notes.d/NOTE-1241.md); [THEORY-1100](../theory.d/THEORY-1100.md)). A partner's trace can be small or absent, and genes from bacteria that were not partners can outnumber it.

## What it does not say

Not that there were further partners. Tobiasson and colleagues' reading may be right; the claim is only that the ancestries cannot show it, and that the same holds for Bernabeu and colleagues' donors.
