# LIT-tmpf496v: Pain experience in hermit crabs?

Robert W. Elwood & Mirjam Appel (2009), *Animal Behaviour*
77(5):1243–1246 — DOI-10.1016/j.anbehav.2009.01.028

## Key takeaways

*Registered, not read beyond the abstract.*

- **The criterion.** "Pain may be inferred when the responses to a
  noxious stimulus are not reflexive but are traded off against other
  motivational requirements, the experience is remembered and the
  situation is avoided in the future."
- **The trade-off.** "Only crabs given shocks evacuated their shells
  indicating the aversive nature of the stimulus, but fewer crabs
  evacuated from a preferred species of shell indicating a motivational
  trade-off." The abstract speaks of a single "stimulus level", and of
  shell quality as two values, a preferred species and a less preferred
  one.
- **A lasting shift.** "Most crabs, however, did not evacuate at the
  stimulus level we used". When later offered a new shell, shocked crabs
  were more likely to take it, and did so more quickly.
- **The inference.** "The results are consistent with the idea of pain in
  these animals."

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 for the test CLAIM-tmp83ge2 proposes. As far as the
abstract shows, the design cannot separate a continuous trade-off from a
threshold. It uses one level of cost and two of resource, and its
measure is the proportion of crabs evacuating. A shift in that
proportion is what either regime predicts, if crabs differ in where
their thresholds sit. It does show that the response to the cost
depends on the resource, which is what THEORY-101's promotion asks for,
but it shows nothing about proportion. The graded-cost companion study
is LIT-tmpwwll5.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing here belongs in the
anthology.
