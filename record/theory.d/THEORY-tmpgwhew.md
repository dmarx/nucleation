# THEORY-tmpgwhew: Abel and colleagues prove that a single-vertex crease pattern with a mountain–valley assignment folds rigidly from flat exactly when it contains a bird's foot, a same-parity tripod or cross together with one crease of the opposite parity

## Source

Abel et al. (2016), [LIT-tmpry5n6](../literature.d/LIT-tmpry5n6.md); read in [NOTE-tmpj2th4](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpj2th4.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Theorem 1**: an assigned single-vertex pattern "can be continuously
  parameterized in a family of rigid origami folds if and only if" it
  contains a bird's foot. Sufficiency by closed spherical linkages (Lemma
  1), closure under adding creases (Lemma 2) and a direct construction for
  degrees 4 and 5 (Lemma 3); necessity by a distance argument on the
  sphere.
- **Theorem 2**, the unassigned case: at least four creases, every sector
  angle strictly below π, and not an unspecified cross.
- **Popping**: a same-parity tripod or cross of mountains lets the vertex
  pop up; one of each lets it snap between the two.

## What this does not say

- Anything about vertices joined in a multi-vertex pattern, where fold
  angles must also agree between adjacent vertices.
- Anything about folds far from flat.
- Anything about motions that let the paper pass through itself.
