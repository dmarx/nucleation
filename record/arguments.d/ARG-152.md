# ARG-152: Bialek, Nemenman and Tishby support the separation of entropy from predictive information on which the claim about criticality and complexity relies, and not its doubt that predictive information diverges at critical points, which they assert without proof

## The inference

[CLAIM-805](../claims.d/CLAIM-805.md): entropy, correlation and scaling are distinct, and a
critical point is not the maximum of complexity; predictive information,
among others, "may or may not diverge" there (A21 §8). Bialek, Nemenman and
Tishby: "predictability is a deviation from extensivity"; a complexity
measure "should be zero both for purely regular and for purely random
systems"; "most of what we observe is irrelevant to the problem of
predicting the future". That is the split the claim's table draws between
how uncertain a system is and how much structure it shares.

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the claim were false?** The split of
  extensive from subextensive entropy holds whatever criticality does; it
  supports the first half of the claim and is neutral on the second.
- **Does it tell the claim apart from its rivals?** The rival is
  "complexity peaks at criticality". The paper sides with it for its own
  measure: "Under these conditions the predictive information can grow
  without bound" (§3), "At critical points the predictive information
  will diverge with the size of the system" (§6), with the details "to be
  worked out" (fn. 9). It also says logarithmic divergence arises with no
  transition, in learning. It does not claim complexity in general peaks
  at criticality. So the paper is evidence against "need not diverge" for
  predictive information, but the evidence is an assertion: no
  calculation at a critical point is given, and §5.3 notes that
  one-dimensional local models have none.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** The claim's "need not
  peak" is about maxima across a parameter; the paper's "diverge" is about
  growth with system size or time at the critical point. A divergence with
  size at T_c is compatible with a finite maximum elsewhere at fixed size,
  so the two are not flatly opposed.

## Verdict

Supports the separation the claim relies on; does not support, and mildly
cuts against, its doubt about predictive information at criticality,
with an unproved assertion. The work moves from the claim's grounds to
corroborating it. The claim's "What it does not say" already excludes "no
measure peaks there". Proposed.
