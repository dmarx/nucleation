  the Fisher information is not positive definite: neural networks,

# LIT-616: A Widely Applicable Bayesian Information Criterion

Sumio Watanabe (2012), arXiv preprint; *Journal of Machine Learning
Research* 14(27):867–897 (2013) — [ARXIV-1208.6338](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.6338)

## Key takeaways

- **Singular is the common case.** "If a statistical model contains
  hierarchical layers, hidden variables, or grammatical rules, then it is
  singular." In a singular model the likelihood cannot be approximated by
  any normal distribution, and AIC, BIC and MDL do not apply.
- **The free energy of a singular model** (Theorem 2, from the book and
  earlier papers): F = −log ∫ Π p(X_i|w) φ(w) dw = nL_n(w0) + λ log n −
  (m − 1) log log n + R_n. Here λ, the RLCT, is a positive rational number,
  a birational invariant, equal to d/2 for regular models (Lemma 3) and
  smaller than d/2 when the prior is positive at the singularities (§7.1).
- **The RLCT, defined.** Resolution of singularities (Hironaka) gives a map
  w = g(u) in which K(g(u)) = u^{2k} and the prior measure is b(u)|u^h| du,
  locally (Theorem 1). Then λ = min over charts of min_j (h_j + 1)/(2k_j),
  and m counts the coordinates attaining it (Eqs. 18–19).
- **WBIC.** WBIC = E_w^β[nL_n(w)] with β = 1/log n (Eq. 4). Theorem 4:
  WBIC = nL_n(w0) + λ log n + U_n √(λ log n / 2) + O_p(1), so its first two
  terms match F. Theorem 5: for regular models WBIC = BIC + o_p(1).
  Corollary 3: the difference of the posterior means at two inverse
  temperatures, divided by the difference of 1/β, converges to λ. One MCMC
  run estimates the free energy without knowing λ, which depends on the
  unknown truth.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the lawful substitute for
Watanabe's *Algebraic Geometry and Statistical Learning Theory* ([LIT-354](LIT-354.md)),
which is in copyright and stays Deferred. The record's rule for books asks
for a shorter work by the same author that states the same results. This
one does, for the results the record needs: the definitions of regular and
singular models, the standard representation theorem (Theorem 1, the
book's Theorem 6.1), the RLCT and its multiplicity, and the free-energy
asymptotics (Theorem 2). It cites the book for each. It extends the book in
the plain sense: its new results (Theorems 3–5, Corollaries 1–3) are built
on those. What it does not carry is the book's proofs of Theorems 1 and 2,
its treatment of the generalisation error, and WAIC. For WAIC this paper
gives only the statement E[WAIC] = E[G] + O(1/n²) (Eq. 39), with a
citation.

**Anthology.** The anthology holds the singular-learning-theory follow-ons
in deep learning: [ANTH-LIT-541](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-541.md) (*Deep Learning is Singular, and That's
Good*) and [ANTH-LIT-542](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-542.md) (the local learning coefficient). [ANTH-LIT-541](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-541.md) already
cites this paper by name, as the criterion that replaces BIC for singular
models, without holding it. This paper is the statistical source those
build on, and an anthology topic
(`analysis-and-evaluation`) could hold it, so it carries the
`anthology-candidate` flag last, as [LIT-354](LIT-354.md) does. Nucleation reads it for
its own question: model comparison and Fisher geometry as one line. WBIC is
model comparison. The definition of "singular" that it rests on is a
statement about the Fisher information matrix.

Where it meets the rest of the batch:

- **MacKay ([LIT-623](LIT-623.md)).** MacKay's Occam factor, (2π)^{k/2} det^{-1/2}A
  under a Gaussian approximation, gives BIC's (d/2) log n. This paper says
  exactly when that fails: a degenerate Hessian at the optimum. It also says
  what replaces it, λ log n with λ ≤ d/2 under a positive prior. The
  remark in §7.1 that singular models therefore "can employ a larger
  model" with smaller generalisation error, at the price of weaker
  model-selection consistency, is the singular counterpart of MacKay's
  Occam hill.
- **Natural gradient ([LIT-607](LIT-607.md)).** Amari's Fisher efficiency needs the
  Fisher matrix at the optimum to be invertible. This paper's "regular"
  is that same condition, and its "singular" is the case where it fails.
- **Bayesian model reduction ([LIT-614](LIT-614.md), [LIT-620](LIT-620.md)).** BMR scores
  reduced models with variational free energies under a Laplace
  (Gaussian) posterior. That is the approximation that, by this paper's
  account, is wrong for the hierarchical and latent-variable models BMR is
  used on. The papers do not cite each other, and neither tests the
  other's claim.
