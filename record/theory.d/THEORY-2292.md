# THEORY-2292: Schmietendorf argues that the slaving principle of synergetics, on which near a critical point a separation of time scales lets a few slow order parameters describe the macro-dynamics while the stable modes follow them almost at once, supports by itself only a descriptive reading, that few variables suffice, and that a causal reading, the order parameter determining its constituents, needs further argument; weak emergence makes enslavement reducible and diachronic, downward causation through constraints makes it irreducible, and Synergetics alone does not settle between them

## Source

Schmietendorf (2026), "How causal is “circular causality”?", *Frontiers in
Network Physiology* 6: 1858616, [LIT-2231](../literature.d/LIT-2231.md), read in [NOTE-2394](../notes.d/NOTE-2394.md).

## The account

Synergetics explains self-organization by order parameters that emerge
when a critical point separates time scales, and by the stable modes'
enslavement to them, with circular causality between the two. Stephan's
distinction separates a descriptive reading of slaving from a causal one;
moving from the first to the second without argument is post hoc ergo
propter hoc. Against downward causation stand circularity and causal
exclusion. Bedau's weak emergence absorbs enslavement as diachronic,
reducible downward causation; Bishop's downward causation through
constraints treats formed structures as irreducible internal constraints.
Synergetics fits both and settles neither.

## Bearing

On [CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md), a caution about wording: the claim's "constraint" is
Montévil and Mossio's, time-scale relative, closer to the descriptive
reading than to the causal one. On [CLAIM-526](../claims.d/CLAIM-526.md), agreement that a collective
variable's closure does not by itself give it causal powers of its own.
