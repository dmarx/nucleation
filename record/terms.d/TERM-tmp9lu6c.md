# TERM-tmp9lu6c: richness, of an ecosystem: the maintained plurality of differentiated, viable ways in which it can recognize possibilities, formulate demands and realize responses, in three dimensions that come apart, the representational, the functional and the developmental

## Definition

An ecosystem's **richness** is the plurality of ways, held by its
constituents, of recognizing possibilities, formulating demands and
realizing responses, counted only as far as those ways are:

- **differentiated**: they preserve different regions of what the
  ecosystem can ask and answer, not copies of one another;
- **viable**: they can persist under the ecosystem's resources and
  organization;
- **maintained**: the practices and competencies behind them are kept up,
  so that a dormant way can be reactivated. "an archived description is not
  the same thing as a maintained capacity" (A60 §5).

A60 §7's three dimensions, which come apart:

| Dimension | A60: what it measures |
|---|---|
| Representational | "Diversity of distinctions and questions that can be made legible" |
| Functional | "Diversity of affordable and reliable responses" |
| Developmental | "Diversity of viable pathways to acquiring new capabilities" |

A60's examples of their coming apart: a university that sustains "many
competing theories but lack[s] the material capacity to test them"; an
industrial organization with "extensive production flexibility" that
evaluates "every possible change through one narrow managerial framework";
diverse specialists without "the coordination needed to combine their
capabilities".

A60 §3's two sources map onto the last two. **Extensive** richness is what
the maintained repertoire covers now (functional). **Generative** richness
is what it can come to cover through "affordable learning and
reorganization within a further horizon" (developmental): two perspectives
with the same present repertoire can differ in it. That is [CLAIM-754](../claims.d/CLAIM-754.md)'s
operational and developmental frontiers, and [CLAIM-675](../claims.d/CLAIM-675.md)'s point that equal
present capability sets can leave different capability later.

A60 §6 calls the representational dimension **perspectival richness**: "a
plurality of distinct, consequential, and potentially corrigible ways of
making its circumstances intelligible and organizing responses to them",
so that diversity widens the questions an ecosystem can ask as well as the
answers it can give. The ways are legibility regimes in [CLAIM-455](../claims.d/CLAIM-455.md)'s sense,
ordered only relative to a task.

## What it is not

- **Not diversity.** Diversity is what is observed of the constituents;
  richness is what diversity is offered as evidence of ([CLAIM-tmp6thmv](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp6thmv.md)).
  A60: "the same number of constituents can correspond to different degrees
  of functional differentiation and possibility coverage".
- **Not species richness**, ecology's count of species, which is one
  diversity index. The collision of names is unfortunate; the sense here is
  closer to what ecologists call functional diversity together with its
  capacity to develop.
- **Not welfare or justice.** "Diversity can coexist with exclusion,
  exploitation, fragility, and conflict" (A60 §6; [CLAIM-546](../claims.d/CLAIM-546.md)).
- **Relational.** "The value of differentiated constituents depends on
  what their differences make possible within the ecosystem's actual
  organization" (A60 §7): which constituents can coexist and coordinate is
  their compatibility complex ([CLAIM-554](../claims.d/CLAIM-554.md)), not their number.
