# LIT-tmpu4yh1: Fluctuation of Flow and Affect in Everyday Life: A Second Look at the Paradox of Work

Stefan Engeser and Nicola Baumann (2014). *Journal of Happiness Studies*
17(1): 105-124 (2016). DOI-10.1007/s10902-014-9586-4

## Key takeaways

- Flow, positive activation, negative activation and valence were all
  reported at each random signal (4,476 usable reports).
- Within persons, flow correlates .59 with positive activation, .31 with
  valence and -.19 with negative activation at the same signal (Table 2).
- Flow at one signal does not predict valence at the next, with concurrent
  flow and prior valence controlled; concurrent flow does, in three of the
  four transitions from work (B = .39 to .57; .33, not significant, into
  active leisure; Table 5). The hypothesis that flow at work pays off in later leisure
  happiness was not supported.
- Perceived outcome importance raises flow (B = .09) and lowers valence
  (B = -.16) (Table 6), and accounts for the work-leisure difference in
  flow against active leisure.

## Standing in the record

The second of two in-episode studies of flow and affect the record has
read (LEAD-054), with Rheinberg et al. (LIT-tmpu5vec), whose Potsdam
sample it appears to share. Its lagged analysis is the most direct test
the record holds of CLAIM-441's third exit. It reports Fullagar and
Kelloway (LIT-tmpvw1m3), with the opposite lagged finding.
