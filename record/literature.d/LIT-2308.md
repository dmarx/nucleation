summary: >-
  Huang et al. (2026), DISCERN: Can AI Agents Work Like Scientists and Guide
  Discovery?. DISCERN is a benchmark of 203 tasks on real public
  life-science datasets across eight tracks (GWAS, clinical variants, bulk
  and single-cell RNA, CRISPR screens, enhancers, chromatin, protein
  stability), at three independently scored levels: L1 data integrity (79
  tasks, controlled defects and matched clean controls), L2 analysis
  verification (102 tasks, confounds and "tool traps" such as a narrow-peak
  setting on a broad histone mark), and L3 hypothesis generation and
  revision (22 dossiers) under three rounds of adversarial review by other
  LLMs, including counterfactual dossiers retained only when they conflict
  with the agent's elicited prior. Eight agents are evaluated. Aggregate
  scores hide level-specific weaknesses; recognising a problem is often not
  carried into the conclusion; caution is frequently misplaced (rejecting
  sound data); hypothesis production and novelty vary widely; computational
  expenditure ranks models more consistently across tracks than evidence
  judgement does. Read for the exchange "luria experiment design".
