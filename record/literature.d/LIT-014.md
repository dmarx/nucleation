# LIT-014: Reckless disregard for the truth coming from cops, doctors, and scientists: A rant.

Andrew Gelman (2025), *Statistical Modeling, Causal Inference, and Social Science (blog, Columbia University); also cross-posted to Gelman's Substack* — <https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2025/06/24/reckless-disregard-for-the-truth-coming-from-cops-doctors-and-scientists-a-rant/>

## Key takeaways

- Gelman argues that scientists, expert witnesses and law-enforcement spokespeople routinely make statements they must know are false, such as calling a 3-day study "long-term". These statements work as socially accepted "terms of art" with essentially no consequences, and he proposes the label "reckless disregard for the truth" for the pattern.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

The post extends an earlier discussion of scientists writing false things without regarding them as lies. It sets side by side three professional settings: research papers whose titles or abstracts overstate their findings, a medical professor's paid testimony for the tobacco industry, and DHS statements accusing officials of "assault" or "lunging" that press accounts say video contradicts. Gelman adopts a commenter's term, "reckless disregard for the truth". He argues the motive is to strengthen a weak case, that the mechanism is a channel that propagates the claim (journals, courts, media), and that the pattern persists because it is accepted and goes unsanctioned.
