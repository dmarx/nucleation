# CLAIM-195: For an argument, the constraint set that persistence is relative to is fixed by an amendable decision, not discovered: the record stipulates what counts as the same claim persisting, which makes the choice explicit without making it non-arbitrary

## The claim

Viability theory ([THEORY-134](../theory.d/THEORY-134.md)) measures persistence relative to a
constraint set K fixed independently of the dynamics, and it does not
supply K. The will-organization line runs into this at its weakest step.
[ARG-007](../arguments.d/ARG-007.md)'s mapping premise gives "no non-arbitrary way to choose the
maintenance constraints", and "'precarious' is relative to that choice".
[CLAIM-079](CLAIM-079.md) ends: "Who fixes the constraint set is the open mapping
question."

For an argument the record answers who fixes it: a decision does. What
counts as the same claim persisting is given by codes, by versions that
keep a code, and by supersession that names a successor. These were set by
[ADR-032](../decisions.d/ADR-032.md) and are amended by later decisions. [CLAIM-185](CLAIM-185.md)'s persistence, and so
its anchoring, is persistence relative to that stipulated K.

This bears on [ARG-007](../arguments.d/ARG-007.md) in a limited way. It is an existing case where K is
fixed explicitly, in a public, amendable decision, rather than left
implicit. It does not show that the choice is non-arbitrary. It shows that
K is chosen, and where the choice is recorded. For an agent, the analogous
move would be to treat the constraint set as something an organization
decides and revises, not something an analyst discovers. Whether that
transfers is open.

Holling's distinction ([THEORY-132](../theory.d/THEORY-132.md)) gives the cost side. Managing a system
for constancy can erode its resilience. A record that enforces identity
strictly maximizes constancy, and may thereby reduce the argument's
capacity to reframe. That is the risk [CLAIM-181](CLAIM-181.md) and [CLAIM-184](CLAIM-184.md) name from
the grammar and from entrenchment.

## What it does not say

- It does not say the record's K is the right one. Viability does not
  establish what ought to persist ([CLAIM-075](CLAIM-075.md)), and a stipulated K does not
  either. A claim can persist, perfectly anchored, and be wrong.
- It does not say an argument is an agent, or that its record is a
  self-maintaining organization. The record's grammar is maintained from
  outside, in luria's repository and by the owner. Aubin's K constrains a
  state space given in advance; the record's K is partly constitutive,
  since it defines what the objects are. The mapping is partial.
- It does not answer [ARG-007](../arguments.d/ARG-007.md)'s mapping premise for agents.
