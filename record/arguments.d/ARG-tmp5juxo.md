# ARG-tmp5juxo: Winner's essay supports the premise and the occasion of the irreversibility burden, that the latitude of choice about a technology is greatest at its introduction and vanishes once commitments are made, so that innovations deserve the scrutiny given to laws, but not the rule that the burden of justification scales with the size and irreversibility of the narrowing

## The inference

1. **The premise.** CLAIM-tmpsmg1c needs a choice that "could substantially and durably narrow" later possibilities. Winner: "By far the greatest latitude of choice exists the very first time a particular instrument, system, or technique is introduced", and "the original flexibility vanishes for all practical purposes once the initial commitments are made." Moses's overpasses show the durability: an arrangement that "after a time, becomes just another part of the landscape".
2. **The occasion.** The claim is a rule for deciding before lock-in. Winner draws the same occasion: innovations "are similar to legislative acts or political foundings that establish a framework for public order that will endure over many generations", so "the same careful attention one would give to the rules, roles, and relationships of politics must also be given to" them.
3. **The mechanism of closure.** After commitment, "the kinds of reasoning that justify the adaptation of social life to technical requirements pop up as spontaneously as flowers in the spring": irreversibility is partly practical reasoning that forms after the fact, which is why a burden placed later comes too late.

So Winner supports the claim's premise and occasion, independently of the exchange.

## Critical questions

- **Does the work say this, read in context?** Yes. The passages close the essay's first part and state its thesis about choice; they are not asides.
- **Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** For technologies, yes; the claim also covers institutions, and Winner's comparison runs the other way (technologies are like legislation), which is consistent with it. The scaling rule is not in Winner: he asks for scrutiny at introduction, not for a burden that rises with the magnitude and irreversibility of the change. Nor does he give the claim's reason for irreversibility, uncertainty with later learning (CLAIM-559); his reason is that flexibility vanishes.
- **Does the claim lean on the reading for more than it holds?** The claim does not rest on Winner; the relation is `corroborates`, beside Postman (THEORY-tmp8mi75) and Moen (THEORY-tmpzuniv). Winner is the strongest of the three: he gives a mechanism and the legislative analogy.
- **Would the evidence be expected if the claim were false?** Winner's observation (choice closes) would hold even if the burden should not scale with irreversibility; it supports the premise, not the rule, and the claim's defeaters (no later learning; a worse durable status quo preserved) are untouched.

## Verdict

Proposed. Winner corroborates CLAIM-tmpsmg1c's premise and occasion; the scaling rule remains the claim's own, interpretive. Status unchanged.
