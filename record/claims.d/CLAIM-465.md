# CLAIM-465: An organization's flexibility among regimes depends on the conditions under which a regime gives up authority as well as those under which it takes it, so escalation without a release condition leaves authority with a regime after the demand that justified it has passed

## The claim

[CASE-135](../cases.d/CASE-135.md), the Brookhollow apartment fire in Wichita: "the mayday regime
acquired authority without a clear way of giving it back. A24: flexibility
depends 'also on **the conditions under which they relinquish it**'.
[TERM-017](../terms.d/TERM-017.md) names suspension; it does not name release." The case's note of
2026-10-22, from the independent review ([LIT-1374](../literature.d/LIT-1374.md)): the department's policy
said "During a mayday, no fire ground assignment should be abandoned"
(p. 54), yet "search and rescue of Building 300 was not reprioritized and
reinitiated for over eleven (11) minutes after the Mayday was called,
despite the Mayday being under control within a few minutes" (p. 55), "nor
did Command announce when the Mayday was complete per WFD policy" (p. 63).

[CLAIM-027](CLAIM-027.md) lists the maintained relations that decide which regime is
effective: accessibility, precedence, persistence, jurisdiction,
suspension and transformation. Suspension is the incumbent regime giving
way to an escalated one. The incident shows the reverse step as its own
fact: the escalated regime handing authority back once its demand is
met. A procedure can provide the first and not the second, and then
authority stays where the emergency put it.

## What it does not say

- **That the delay caused harm.** The review records it as a deviation
  from policy; the claim is about the structure, not the outcome.
- **That release must be explicit.** A condition can be built into the
  escalation (it lapses when the trigger is cleared); the claim is that
  some condition must exist, explicit or not.
- **More than one case.** One documented incident, so Proposed. A second,
  in another domain (an emergency power, a crisis team), is what would
  move it.

## Note of 2026-10-30: what holds it back

[CASE-135](../cases.d/CASE-135.md), the one case, shows a release that policy provided for and that
was not carried out (Command was to announce when the Mayday was complete,
[LIT-1374](../literature.d/LIT-1374.md) p. 63), not escalation without a release condition; the second
case the body asks for would need one where no release condition existed.
The defeat condition's second clause is unanswered: [TERM-017](../terms.d/TERM-017.md)'s suspension,
when an applicable constraint may be disabled, applies to the escalated
regime as well as the incumbent, and the claim does not say why release is
not that. Its first clause also contradicts "What it does not say", which
counts a lapse built into the escalation as a release condition, so the
condition needs restating.

## Note of 2026-11-04: rumination as failed release, and hysteresis, from "Organizational Nucleation and SLT"

A19 and A26 cited this claim. A29 §3 applied it to rumination without
citing it: "It could instead be a failure of **authority release**. The
perspective activates appropriately but cannot stop governing attention."
The rival is reactivation by "self-referential or threatening material",
which is CLAIM-286's recurrent coalition. The proposed test is to
"separately measure changes in negative material's motivational salience
and changes in its control over attention." The analogy, a regime "that
remains in power after the original emergency has passed", is CASE-036.

A20 §5 gave a mechanism, hysteresis with θᵢ^off < θᵢ^on: "Hysteresis can
be useful. It prevents rapid switching in response to noisy input. But it
can also produce lock-in." CASE-112, CLAIM-459 and CLAIM-463 already hold it
in structural form. Neither is the second case this claim waits for, and
the note of 2026-10-30's question, why release is not TERM-017's
suspension, stays unanswered.

**Jurisdiction.** (a) A29's §3 heading, "a perspective that cannot
relinquish jurisdiction", means the persistence of authority, not TERM-017's
or TERM-047's jurisdiction. (b) Read as intended: the absence of a release
condition in this claim's sense. (c) The owner made no correction.
No status changes.
