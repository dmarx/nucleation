# LIT-tmpog11n: Basic Rights: Subsistence, Affluence, and U.S. Foreign Policy

Henry Shue (1980). *Basic Rights: Subsistence, Affluence, and U.S. Foreign Policy*. Princeton: Princeton University Press. 2nd ed. 1996; 40th-anniversary ed. 2020, DOI-10.23943/princeton/9780691202280.001.0001.

## Key takeaways

*From the Introduction and index ([NOTE-tmpzchma](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpzchma.md)); chapter 2 unread.*

- "Part I attempts to show that rights to three particular
  substances—subsistence, security, and liberty—are basic rights" (p. 9).
- "once some presumption has been established in favor of a right, the main
  task is to answer the objection that the duties involved would ask too
  much of others" (p. 9).
- The index: "correlative duties (of avoidance, protection and aid)", with
  a "tripartite analysis" at pp. 54–55.
