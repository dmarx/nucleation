# NOTE-tmp8x2ha: Soros — Fallibility, reflexivity, and the human uncertainty principle

## Contribution

It is the fullest short statement of Soros's two principles, written for
a special issue of the *Journal of Economic Methodology* that answered
it. He claims no discovery ("Of course I did not discover reflexivity")
and names Knight, Keynes's beauty contest, Merton's self-fulfilling
prophecy and Popper's Oedipus effect as forerunners. What it adds is the
framing of the loop as two functions of one mind interfering with each
other, a demarcation of social from natural science built on it, which
the postscript retracts, and its application to boom–bust processes and
the euro crisis.

## Key insight

A thinking participant's view of a situation is both an attempt to know
it and a lever on it. When both run at once, "The independent variable
of one function is the dependent variable of the other, thus neither
function has a genuinely independent variable". The facts then stop
being an independent test of the view: "if there is interference from
the manipulative function, the facts no longer serve as an independent
criterion because the statement may be the product of the manipulative
function." His example: "It is raining" is checked against the weather;
"I love you" changes what it reports.

## Assumptions

- **Fallibility.** "the participants' views of the world never perfectly
  correspond to the actual state of affairs"; theories and overall views
  are "bound to be either biased or inconsistent or both". Facts of
  detail are excepted.
- **Reflexivity.** These views "can influence the situation to which
  they relate through the actions of the participants". It "applies
  exclusively to situations that have thinking participants".
- **Influence, not determination.** "The participants' views influence
  but do not determine the course of events, and the course of events
  influences but does not determine the participants' views."
- **Two kinds, and a third.** Reflexive relations connect subjective
  aspects (marriage, politics); reflexive events connect subjective and
  objective (the fiscal cliff, the euro crisis); "self-reflexivity" is a
  solitary person reflecting on their own identity.

## Key results

The essay argues and illustrates; it proves and tests nothing.

- **Human uncertainty principle.** Fallibility and reflexivity "lumped
  together". He then says "it is fallibility that is the key source of
  Knightian uncertainty in human affairs"; reflexivity "is only one
  source of uncertainty, albeit a powerful one". Not every fallibility
  is Knightian: road accidents and the biases behavioural economists
  catalogue are statistical.
- **Against Popper's unity of method.** Reflexivity breaks the symmetry
  of prediction and explanation and endangers testing, since initial and
  final conditions either include participants' thinking, which is not
  observable, or exclude it, and then do not fix the outcome. He
  proposes, "not as an empirical truth but as a methodological
  convention", that social science not be held to physics's standards.
- **Postscript.** After Beinhocker's paper in the same issue and a CEU
  workshop on 8 October 2013, he says the convention "could do more harm
  than good in the long term" and that "we should study the spectrum
  rather than attributing reflexivity exclusively to the domain of the
  social sciences".
- **Feedback signs.** Negative feedback brings views and situation
  together and is self-correcting; equilibrium is "an extreme case of
  negative feedback, a limiting case". Positive feedback drives them
  apart, is self-reinforcing, and cannot go on forever.
- **Boom–bust.** A prevailing trend and a misconception about it
  reinforce each other, are tested, survive or fail, reach a "twilight",
  and reverse; booms are slow and busts abrupt. Figure 4's caption
  stages it A–I. Worked cases: real estate (the misconception "that the
  value of the collateral is independent of the availability of
  credit"), the 1982 sovereign-debt crisis (debt ratios "considered
  objective criteria, but in fact they were reflexive"), the 2007–2008
  "super-bubble", the euro crisis.
- **Fat tails.** Offered as a partial explanation: dichotomies simplify a
  reality too complex to grasp, and a market's switch from one side of a
  dichotomy to the other is violent.

## Concepts

- **Cognitive function**: the participant as observer; causation runs
  "from the world to the mind".
- **Manipulative function**: the participant as actor, "to make an impact
  on the world and to advance the participants' interests"; "manipulative"
  is chosen "to emphasize intentionality".
- **Reflexivity**: the interference of the two, when both operate; a
  "two-way feedback loop connecting thinking and reality".
- **Human uncertainty principle**: fallibility and reflexivity together;
  "much more specific and stringent than the subjective skepticism that
  pervades Cartesian philosophy".
- **Near- and far-from-equilibrium**: the regimes of balanced feedback
  and of preponderant positive feedback.

## Connections

The loop is Merton's self-fulfilling prophecy ([LIT-1398](../literature.d/LIT-1398.md)), which Soros
cites through *Social Theory and Social Structure* (1949), generalized to
run in both directions and to be mostly unintended. Land's hyperstition
([LIT-1396](../literature.d/LIT-1396.md), [NOTE-tmphc9i5](NOTE-tmphc9i5.md)) is the same loop made a programme, where Soros
makes it a limit. MacKenzie's performativity ([LIT-1395](../literature.d/LIT-1395.md)) is the empirical
study of one channel he names in passing: a theory of markets ("if
investors believe that markets are efficient then that belief will
change the way they invest") changes the markets. Espeland and Sauder
([LIT-1330](../literature.d/LIT-1330.md)) is the same loop for rankings. His remark that behavioural
economists study only "cognitive fallibility leading to the mispricing of
assets" and not the mispricing's effect on fundamentals is the point
[CLAIM-426](../claims.d/CLAIM-426.md) turns on.

## Limitations

- Argued from the author's experience, not tested. He says so: "my
  approach is still very rudimentary", and "Knightian uncertainty cannot
  be quantified".
- The demarcation of social from natural science, which Section 3 is
  built on, is withdrawn in the postscript, and the body was not revised.
- The argument that reflexive statements escape the correspondence test
  is made by example ("I love you"), and the link to the Liar paradox and
  Russell is suggestive only.
- **Errors and slips.**
  - "the chess contest between Big Blue and Gary Kasparov": the machine
    was Deep Blue.
  - "If agents act on the basis of their perfect understanding,
    equilibrium is far from a universally and timelessly prevailing
    condition" reads backwards; the argument needs "imperfect". It may
    be the web copy's or the article's; the journal's text was not seen.
  - Hayek and Popper "recognized in their famous exchange in the pages of
    Economica (Popper, 1944)": the reference is Popper's "The poverty of
    historicism, II", not an exchange with Hayek; Hayek's "Scientism"
    essays ran in *Economica* in the same years, so the loose wording
    may be meant.
  - "a steam of objective facts" for "stream"; "set a part" for "apart".

## Open questions

- When is a reflexive loop positive and when negative? Soros says
  "myriad feedback loops" of both signs run at once and gives no way of
  telling which will win before it does.
