# THEORY-tmpnan16: No principle of mereology is merely a fixing of what 'part' means: even reflexivity, transitivity and antisymmetry face counterexamples that do not rest on equivocation, and every principle beyond them is contested, so classical mereology is one substantive theory among several and not a neutral default

## Source

Achille Varzi, "Mereology", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, [LIT-124](../literature.d/LIT-124.md)
(revision of 27 June 2026), read in full in [NOTE-163](../notes.d/NOTE-163.md), §§2.1–5.

## What was actually shown

**The core is contested (§2.1).** Each axiom of Core Mereology has dissenters.
Against Reflexivity: Kearns's time-travel and fractal cases. Against
Transitivity: functional or "φ-part" readings, multilocation and Frege's Etna
argument. Against Antisymmetry: Thomson's statue and clay, the Net of Indra,
perichoresis, time-travel walls and structured propositions. Antisymmetry "can
hardly be regarded as constitutive of the basic meaning of 'part'". The section
ends with Donnelly's (2011: 246) doubt that "any useful core mereology" is
common to all plausible theories. M is "the common starting point of all
standard theories" (§2.2), a claim about what is standard.

**Equivocation removes some objections, not all.** Most ordinary-language
objections to Reflexivity and Transitivity equivocate between "part" and a
restricted "φ-part" (Simons 1987: 107f). The genuine threats are metaphysical:
time travel, multilocation and structured propositions.

**Beyond the core (§§3–4).** Supplementation is "a minimal but substantive
addition", with Brentano's accidents, Fine's singletons, Whitehead's
boundary-free regions and coincidence cases against it. Extensionality needs
Antisymmetry as well as Strong Supplementation, so a non-antisymmetric theory
can keep Strong Supplementation without extensionality (Cotnoir and Bacon
2012). Composition principles: restricted ones give only sufficient conditions
and, made biconditional, face the sorites; universalism is defended by Lewis's
argument from vagueness and charged with ontological exuberance. The Special
Composition Question has no agreed answer.

**Interaction effects.** The additions interact in ways easy to miss.
Supplementation with Reflexivity and Transitivity entails Antisymmetry.
Supplementation with products for overlappers entails Strong Supplementation
and so extensionality. How a sum is defined changes which theory an
unrestricted-sum axiom produces ([THEORY-tmpbrxt2](THEORY-tmpbrxt2.md)).

**What could have come out otherwise.** Some principle could have had no serious
dissent. The survey finds none.

## What this does not say

- **That the core is false.** It says no axiom is safe as a meaning postulate.
  The entry still builds on M.
- **That every principle is equally contested.** The entry calls bounds for
  overlapping things, (P.11ξ) with ξ = O, "rather uncontroversial" (§4.1), so its
  own headline in §4.5 overstates its treatment.
- **That the survey is neutral.** Varzi is a known extensionalist, and §3.2 and
  §4.5 give the extensionalist replies the last word, though the objections are
  reported fairly.
- **That the biological or systems senses of "part" are covered.** Note 1
  excludes the systems-theory and ecological senses of "mereology", and note
  18 quantum part–whole formalisms.
- **That a restricted part relation is a rival to parthood.** A φ-part (a
  functional part, a component, a body part for moral purposes) need not be
  transitive even when P is. The entry treats such relations as restrictions.
