summary: >-
  Putnam (1988). Models international negotiation as two games played at
  once: a chief negotiator bargains with foreign counterparts while any
  agreement must be ratified at home, so a deal is possible only where
  the domestic win-sets overlap. A win-set's size depends on domestic
  preferences and coalitions, ratification institutions and the
  negotiators' strategies; a small one is both a bargaining advantage and
  a risk of involuntary defection, and domestic division can make
  agreement easier.
