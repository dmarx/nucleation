  A safe adaptive controller must stay safe under every model it has not
  ruled out. Its first action that leaves no safe continuation under an
  alternative is a commitment, and the evidence for it must be in hand
  before the action: what that action reveals comes too late. With the
  information gathered before commitment measured as a KL divergence
  between stopped records, a data-processing argument bounds any uniformly
  safe policy's regret below by the cost of not committing with
  probability ½e^{−I_pre} − α. So bounded pre-commitment information
  leaves a fixed fraction of the oracle gap, and a linear gap gives linear
  regret. Safe recovery at O(√(T log T)) is proved when a safe experiment
  is repeatable, informative and returns to a common state.

# NOTE-1466: Saligrama — information before commitment in safe adaptive control

## Contribution

It asks whether safety constraints permit the informative experiment that
adaptive control needs, rather than how fast uncertainty shrinks once
they do. Most finite-time analyses assume persistent excitation of the
uniformly safe closed loop, under which "feasibility is already settled;
only the rate remains" (abstract). The paper identifies the object that
decides feasibility, the commitment rule, and reduces safe attainability
of the oracle to three quantities it induces (Theorem 1). It then proves
linear regret in two constrained linear–quadratic examples, gives a
list-size converse for many alternatives (Proposition 1), proves recovery
under a repeatable-experiment condition (Theorem 2), and gives SDP upper
certificates for the information quantity (§6).

## Key insight

Evidence must precede commitment, and the margin that commitment spends
can also be what generates the evidence. "Because the commitment decision
is made from (H_{t−1}, U_t), the observation produced by U_τ arrives too
late to justify that action" (p. 2). So the full trajectory is "the wrong
statistical experiment"; the right one is the record stopped at
commitment. If the actions that tell two models apart consume a safety
margin that must be preserved under the alternative, the information
available before commitment is capped by the margin, however long the
horizon.

## Assumptions

- **Uniform chance safety (Def. 1, eq. 7):** the same causal policy keeps
  P(violation) ≤ η_T under every θ in the initial class Θ₀; typically
  η_T = T⁻². Performance is measured against the oracle that knows θ and
  is safe for θ alone (eq. 9).
- **Commitment (Def. 3):** for an ordered pair (θ, θ′), a predictable set
  of history–action pairs; in deterministic systems, the first action
  after which no hard-safe continuation remains under θ′. τ is a stopping
  time for F_t = σ(H_{t−1}, U_t).
- **Lemma 1** needs absolute continuity of the observation kernels; the
  theorem itself does not.
- The lower bound needs no Markov property, linearity or Gaussian noise
  (Remark 5). The examples are deterministic or linear–Gaussian; the
  recovery theorem treats a scalar parameter with a known conservative
  direction.

## Key results

- **Stopped KL identity (Lemma 1).** D(P^pre_θ ∥ P^pre_θ′) =
  E_θ[Σ_t 1{t < τ} kl_t], where kl_t is the one-step KL of the
  observation kernels. The policy kernels cancel, including the one that
  generates the committing action.
- **Theorem 1 (binary limit, p. 10).** With α_T = sup P_θ′(commit) over
  uniformly safe policies, G_T(θ, θ′; q) the least target regret among
  safe policies that fail to commit with probability ≥ q, and
  I_pre = sup stopped KL:
  R_T(π; θ) ≥ G_T(θ, θ′; ψ_{α_T}(I_pre)) ≥ G_T(θ, θ′; [½e^{−I_pre} − α_T]₊).
  With a linear profile G_T(q) ≥ [G_T q − ζ_T]₊ this is
  R_T ≥ G_T(½e^{−I_pre} − α_T − ζ_T/G_T). If β_pre = sup_T I_pre < ∞,
  α_T → 0 and ζ_T = o(G_T), every uniformly safe policy has worst-case
  regret at least ½e^{−β_pre} G_T asymptotically (eq. 37). Proof: data
  processing on the event "no commitment" and Bretagnolle–Huber.
- **The evidence threshold.** The explicit bound stays positive until
  I_pre ≥ log(1/(2α_T)), which is 2 log T − log 2 at α_T = T⁻² (p. 11).
- **Corollary 2.** Regret r_T = o(G_T) requires unbounded pre-commitment
  information whenever α_T → 0.
- **Finite headroom (pp. 3, 7).** Margin d_{t+1} = d_t − θu_t², sensor
  y_t = θu_t + ξ_t. Before leaving the worn device's budget Σu² ≤ d₀/θ_d,
  so I_pre ≤ (θ_d − θ_b)²d₀/(2σ²θ_d), independent of T. For a healthy
  target θ_b = 0 the gap is Θ(T) and regret is linear; for 0 < θ_b < θ_d
  it is Θ(√T). On a continuum Θ₀ = [0, θ̄] the worst alternative
  approaches the boundary target at rate T^{−1/3} and
  R_T ≥ (UT/2)(1 − o(1)) (eqs. 44–46).
- **Corollary 4 (linear regulation, p. 18).** Two variants, one-sided
  linear margin and signed quadratic margin, both give R_T ≥ c_i T. A
  cross-loading vanishing as T^{−1/2} still blocks the whole linear
  advantage (eq. 68).
- **Proposition 1 (safe lists).** To return a list of size ≤ L containing
  the true one of M alternatives with error ε,
  I(J; S) ≥ (1 − ε)log(M/L) − h₂(ε). With finite sensing, partial
  adaptation through a certified list is still possible: at N = 36 in
  §7.3 the list policy recovers 27.6% of the robust-to-oracle gap.
- **Theorem 2 (recovery, p. 22).** If a conservative experiment is safe,
  repeatable from a common state, informative (V_t grows) and its value
  loss is bounded by a modulus ω of the parameter error, a lower-confidence
  rule is chance-δ safe with regret ≤ r_max + Σ E ω(2b(V_{t−1}, δ; ν)) +
  r_max Tδ. Braking example: O(σ√(D/θ)·√(T log T)) (Corollary 7).
- **Certainty equivalence fails at an active boundary (§5.6.1).** Plugging
  in the sample mean makes each braking episode unsafe with probability
  exactly ½, for every n; more data shrink the overshoot, not its
  probability.

## Method

The lower bound is a recipe (§3.6): define a predictable commitment event;
bound α_T under the alternative; bound the noncommitment regret profile
G_T; bound the stopped KL. For deterministic linear–Gaussian classes the
last step is a QCQP whose SDP dual gives upper certificates (§6).

## Concepts

- **Commitment** — "the first action that forecloses" a safe continuation
  under an alternative (abstract).
- **Precommitment information** — "the KL divergence between
  learner-visible laws stopped before commitment" (abstract).
- **Guarantee or exclude** — the two options of a controller with
  uncertainty unresolved: stay where every plausible model is safe, or act
  as if an alternative were no longer in play; "The exclusion is
  statistical, not literal" (p. 2).
- **Oracle regret** — against the safe oracle that knows the realized
  model; the max–min criterion does not distinguish guaranteeing from
  excluding (p. 2).

## Connections

- Persistent-excitation analyses of constrained LQR (Dean et al., Li et
  al., Hutchinson et al.) are placed "in a persistent
  precommitment-information regime" (p. 35).
- Dual control and controlled sensing (Chernoff-style sequential design)
  are the nearest frameworks; the paper adds the decision-time constraint
  and endogenous admissibility.
- Recursive feasibility, safety filters and returnability are the
  constrained-control tools; noncommitment is "exactly preservation of
  hard-safe recursive feasibility under that alternative" (p. 35). In
  the record that is viability ([THEORY-134](../theory.d/THEORY-134.md)): a commitment is the first
  action that leaves the alternative's viability kernel.
- Budgeted cumulative-violation constraints fall outside the theorem
  (Remark 4).

## Bearing on the record

- **The registration's description** is accurate in substance. Two
  precisions: the "fixed fraction" is ½e^{−β_pre} of the *noncommitment*
  gap G_T, net of α_T and ζ_T, against the safe oracle; and the paper
  also proves the positive side (Theorem 2) and partial adaptation by
  lists, which the summary omits.
- **[CLAIM-312](../claims.d/CLAIM-312.md) and [TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md).** A formal companion to both. [CLAIM-312](../claims.d/CLAIM-312.md) says
  failure by the deadline establishes unavailability. Saligrama adds an
  information-theoretic floor on the evidence needed *before* the
  deadline: about log(1/(2α)) nats to act on an exclusion at risk α.
  The evidence a corrective act itself generates does not count towards
  that floor. For [TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md)'s correctability, a corrective objective that
  requires committing before enough evidence can be gathered has
  correctability bounded away from the oracle, whatever the update rule.
  That is A20's use, and it holds.
- **[CLAIM-511](../claims.d/CLAIM-511.md) and [CASE-128](../cases.d/CASE-128.md).** The "guarantee or exclude" choice is a
  clean case of the disclaimer's point. Staying correctable (preserving a
  safe continuation under every live alternative) has a price, ½e^{−β} of
  the gap, that is fixed by the information geometry, not by preference.
- **[QUESTION-285](../questions.d/QUESTION-285.md) (inquiry) and [CASE-186](../cases.d/CASE-186.md).** The sensor ablation (§7.2)
  is a crisp instance: the same safe prefix gives bounded evidence with a
  rate sensor and linearly growing evidence with a level sensor that
  records a persistent imprint. Whether a record of the past persists is
  what decides whether inquiry can continue.
- **No instruction for ML practice** beyond the recipe for control
  analysis; no anthology flag.

## Limitations

- The lower bound is a sufficient certificate; failure of the certificate
  does not prove recovery (p. 36).
- Pairwise: a large pairwise profile can hide that no single safe policy
  resolves all alternatives (Remark 6, p. 36).
- Recovery is proved only for a scalar ordered parameter with a common
  reset; vector parameters and non-resetting dynamics are open.
- The SDP certificate omits process noise; the case of unbounded but
  sublinear pre-commitment information is unresolved (p. 36).
- Numerical sections are illustrations; safety is analytic throughout.

## Open questions

- Is there a converse matching Theorem 2 when information grows
  sublinearly?
- For many alternatives, what replaces the pairwise profile when one
  policy must inform about all of them at once?
