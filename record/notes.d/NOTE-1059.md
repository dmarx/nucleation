# NOTE-1059: Lee-Youngzie et al. — Towards a mathematical structure of global phenomenal consciousness

## Contribution

The paper joins the two kinds of "qualia structure" in the structural
approach to consciousness. Intra-phenomenal structures model the parts of
one experience. Inter-phenomenal structures place an experience among
others by similarity judgements. The proposal is a presheaf from the first
to the second. Each part of an experience is assigned the dissimilarity
matrix measured for it, and each inclusion of parts a restriction map. Then
"Conditions for phenomenal binding are formalized as the compatibility
condition for the gluing of local sections" (§5.2). Nothing is measured. The
article type is "Hypothesis", and its application is a two-region visual
field, worked symbolically.

## Key insight

Treat the measured similarity structure of each part of an experience as
local data over that part, and ask whether the local data glue. If the
narrow qualia of the left and right hemifields agree at the vertical
meridian and glue into the measured broad quale of the whole field, then
the whole is "wholly determined by, and decomposable into" its parts
(§4.1.5). If they do not, something else is going on, and the paper offers
two readings of that something: disunity, as in binocular rivalry, or a
broad quale that depends on context, as in the Ebbinghaus illusion.

## Assumptions

- **Phenomenal unity is a structural relation between experiential parts**,
  not an added property (§1, after Wiese). The paper takes as given that
  unity is always present: "their conjoint phenomenology always possesses
  the phenomenal quality of unity" (§1).
- **Subsumption is a preorder.** "experience A subsumes experience B" when
  what it is like to have B is an aspect of what it is like to have A, and
  this relation is assumed transitive (§4.1.1).
- **Selective attention is restriction.** Restricting a broad quale to a
  part "corresponds to selectively attending to a narrower experiential
  aspect" (§4.1.3). The narrow quale of a hemifield is measured by
  similarity judgements made while attending to it.
- **Attention never shrinks dissimilarity.** Definition 1's morphisms
  require d_X(x_i, x_j) ≤ d_Y(m(x_i), m(x_j)), so narrower qualia are at
  least as dissimilar as the broad ones they restrict. The authors call
  this a speculation from studies of attention and appearance: "we
  speculate a general relationship between overall and specific
  dissimilarity of experiences" (§4.1.3).
- **Dissimilarity is symmetric**, "for simplicity", although the paper's
  own case for categories over metric spaces is that measured similarity
  violates symmetry (§3.2, Definition 1).
- **Similarity judgements measure qualia.** Inter-phenomenal structures are
  the empirical measure of "what it feels like", and the paper notes that
  the method of eliciting them changes the structure obtained (§5.3.2).
- **Structural equivalence licenses transfer.** "if phenomenal states and
  their underlying physical states are assumed to be structurally
  equivalent ... conclusions drawn in the phenomenal domain can be expected
  to hold in the physical domain" (Mathematizing section). Structural
  equivalence is left undefined: "we use the term in a broad sense without
  committing to a specific formal notion of equivalence" (Definition A12).

## Key results

The results are definitions and readings. They are given here with what a
check by script found (scratch script, 2026-10-22: enumeration of up- and
down-sets, topology axioms, and the sheaf condition for the paper's
restriction maps).

- **The intra-phenomenal topology (§4.1.1, Definition A7).** A finite
  preorder of experiential aspects induces an Alexandroff topology, whose
  open sets are said to be "upwards-closed sets". The worked example
  gives the chain E_Location ≤ E_Color ≤ E_Shape ≤ E_ColorShape and lists the
  open sets {∅, {Location}, {Color, Location}, {Shape, Location},
  {Shape, Color, Location}}. *Checked:* that list is not a topology on the
  four aspects, since it lacks the whole set. It is not the up-sets of the
  chain, which are ∅, {CS}, {S, CS}, {C, S, CS} and the whole set. It is
  exactly the down-sets of a different order, a diamond with Location below
  Color and Shape and both below ColorShape, less the whole set. The
  diamond is what the prose describes. Definition A8 also calls an
  Alexandroff space one "whose underlying set is a finite set", which is a
  special case of the property its next sentence states correctly. The
  repairs are routine (take the diamond and include the whole set), and
  nothing later depends on the error.
- **The visual field (§4.1.1, Figure 5).** τ is written as
  {∅, {VM}, {LeftHF}, {RightHF}, {TotalVF}}. *Checked:* read as a topology
  on the four aspect labels it fails, since {LeftHF} ∪ {RightHF} is not
  listed. Read as regions of a field of points, with LeftHF ∩ RightHF = VM
  and LeftHF ∪ RightHF = TotalVF, it is a topology, and this is the reading
  the text uses.
- **The presheaf (§4.1.2–4.1.3, Definition 1).** Q sends each region U to a
  dissimilarity matrix Q(U), an object of a category DIS whose morphisms
  are functions between items that never decrease dissimilarity. The
  "local sections" are rows of the matrix, one per trial: e(i,j) is the
  relational embedding of the trial with left grating at angle θ_i and
  right at θ_j. *Checked:* DIS is a category (non-decreasing maps compose
  and identities qualify).
- **Binding as gluing (§4.1.4, Figure 7).** For a unified visual field the
  restrictions of the left and right sections to the meridian are
  "expected to result in identical measures of narrower quale", and
  matching sections glue into a section over the whole field. If gluing
  and restriction recover the measured sections everywhere, Q is a sheaf.
  The paper: "The condition for compatibility in the overlap can be
  understood as a formalization of the condition for phenomenal binding of
  narrow qualia into a broader quale." *Checked:* a morphism of DIS is a
  function on items with an inequality on their dissimilarities. It does
  not say what row a restricted row becomes. If restriction is the
  trial-to-trial function the text describes, the left and right
  restrictions of e(0,30) are the same item, every pair matches, and the
  sheaf condition holds trivially. The text says the restriction
  "provides the predicted measure of the narrower quale in the overlap",
  but Definition 1 supplies no predicted measure. The content of the
  test is therefore the empirical comparison §5.1 describes, of measured
  matrices by a consistency radius (Robinson's), not a consequence of the
  stated presheaf.
- **Sheaf failure, first reading: disunity (§4.1.5, Figure 8).** The
  Penrose tribar is pairwise compatible at each junction with no global
  configuration, "a form of contextuality". The paper places it "at the
  level of cognitive interpretation". Binocular rivalry is then "a
  phenomenal 'impossible figure'": "no global section ... exists that
  coherently integrates all local sections", and "violation of the sheaf
  condition may be diagnostic of phenomenal disunity". Split-brain
  disunity is a predicted case, left "for future research" (§5.1).
  *Checked against its own definitions:* Definition A15's gluing axiom
  applies only to sections that "agree throughout their intersection". In
  rivalry the monocular sections disagree on the binocular overlap, so the
  axiom has nothing to glue and is not violated. Rivalry as described is a
  non-matching family, not a failure of the sheaf condition. The tribar,
  a cycle of three junctions with pairwise agreement and no whole, is a
  genuine gluing obstruction of the kind [CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md) describes.
- **Sheaf failure, second reading: context dependence (§4.1.6, Figure 9).**
  Narrow qualia measured with each grating shown alone may fail to glue
  into the measured broad quale. That failure means "interactions between
  parts that are irreducible to a predictable combination of narrower
  qualia considered in isolation", as in the Ebbinghaus illusion. Here a
  non-commuting diagram marks an ordinary, unified experience whose whole
  is not fixed by its separately measured parts.
- **A spectrum, not a verdict (§5.2).** "At one end of the spectrum,
  measures of broad qualia may be completely decomposable", which is the
  sheaf case. "In many other cases, however, measures of broad quale may
  not be predictable from those of its narrow qualia." The framework is
  offered "without presupposing that phenomenal experience is either fully
  decomposable or entirely unanalyzable".
- **Compression (§4.1.5).** At the editor's suggestion, a sheaf that glues
  admits a "compressive" description of the local measures, and a presheaf
  that does not glue admits "no unique compressive description". Stated,
  not derived.
- **Experimental programme (§5.3).** The paper lists what it lacks: richer
  partitions than two regions, other modalities, time, a method for
  measuring broad qualia, and a test of whether attention is the right
  probe of restriction. "few empirical studies construct inter-relational
  structures of broad qualia" (§5.3.2).

## Concepts

- **Narrow and broad qualia**: the phenomenal qualities of an aspect of
  experience and of an experience that includes it as a part; "qualia in
  the broadest sense correspond to the phenomenal qualities of the total
  field of consciousness" (Figure 1).
- **Intra-phenomenal structure**: the mereological organization of parts
  within one experience.
- **Inter-phenomenal structure**: the relations of an experience to other
  experiences, measured as perceived similarity.
- **Subsumption**: B → A when what it is like to have B is an aspect of
  what it is like to have A.
- **Local section**: here, one row of a measured dissimilarity matrix, the
  relational embedding of one trial.
- **Global section**: reserved for "the coherent global data that results
  from a sheaf" (Definition A14). It is a measurement-level object, not an
  experience or a subject.
- **Phenomenal versus functional binding**: phenomenal binding integrates
  phenomenal qualities into one experience; functional binding integrates
  perceptual information into percepts, and "can occur without conscious
  experience" (§1).

## Connections

The paper builds on the structural approach of Tsuchiya, Kleiner and
others, on category-theoretic work by Phillips and Saigo (thanked for
guidance), on IIT's phenomenal structure for the intra-phenomenal side, on
colour qualia-structure studies with Gromov–Wasserstein alignment for the
inter-phenomenal side, and on Robinson's applied sheaf theory and
consistency radius (Robinson is a co-author). Its tribar example cites
Penrose's cohomology of impossible figures (ref. 132), Abramsky's
"Contextuality: At the Borders" (ref. 133) and Abramsky & Brandenburger
(ref. 134). Of these, the record holds Abramsky & Brandenburger, the
sheaf-theoretic account of contextuality ([LIT-016](../literature.d/LIT-016.md), [NOTE-016](NOTE-016.md)), and
the IIT 3.0 statement ([LIT-445](../literature.d/LIT-445.md)). It registers unread Binding and the
Phenomenal Unity of Consciousness ([LIT-1208](../literature.d/LIT-1208.md)), neurophenomenal structuralism
([LIT-1344](../literature.d/LIT-1344.md)), and two arguments against structuralism about consciousness

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-255](../questions.d/QUESTION-255.md) (is unity a global section).** The record filed this
  question after the exchange cited the paper as a sheaf-theoretic account
  of global unity. What the paper establishes, exactly:
  - It does **not** identify the unity of an experience with the
    existence of a global section. It assumes unity is always present
    (§1), and it calls a broad quale that is not fixed by its parts, which
    is a sheaf failure, an ordinary unified experience with context
    dependence (§4.1.6, §5.2).
  - It **does** propose that phenomenal *binding* of narrow into broad
    qualia is compatibility on overlaps (§4.1.4, §5.2), and it reads one
    kind of sheaf failure, rivalry, as disunity (§4.1.5).
  - Its two readings of a sheaf failure, disunity and context dependence,
    are told apart by the example, not by the formalism. By its own
    Definition A15, the rivalry case is not a sheaf failure at all.
  - Its global section is a section of measured similarity data
    (Definition A14). Whether a gluing of such data corresponds to anything
    in the experience is the structure-preserving premise the paper
    assumes and does not test.

  So it gives the question no answer either way. It does supply a worked
  case for A7's alternative. The tribar is a presheaf of interpretations
  with pairwise agreement and no global section, and the paper places
  that "at the level of cognitive interpretation". Yet the figure is seen
  as one figure. Read with the record's cover of interpretive contexts,
  that is an interpretive gluing failure inside one experience. The paper
  does not draw this conclusion; the inference is the record's.
- **[CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md), [CLAIM-197](../claims.d/CLAIM-197.md), [CLAIM-198](../claims.d/CLAIM-198.md).** The paper's two-region examples
  (hemifields, monocular fields) are two-set covers. Under [CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md), two
  contexts that agree on their overlap always glue, so on a two-set cover
  only two things can go wrong. The sections can disagree on the overlap,
  which is the rivalry case and not a gluing failure. Or the measured
  whole can differ from the glued one, which is the Figure 9 case and a
  failure of the parts to determine the whole, a different property from
  [CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md)'s monodromy. Only the tribar, a three-junction cycle, has
  [CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md)'s shape. This supports [CLAIM-153](../claims.d/CLAIM-153.md)'s point that a genuine
  obstruction needs a cycle. [CLAIM-143](../claims.d/CLAIM-143.md)'s requirement, that the
  correspondence between contexts be fixed independently, is met here by
  design: the overlap is the meridian, fixed by fixation, before any
  judgement is taken.
- **[CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md) and [TERM-072](../terms.d/TERM-072.md).** The paper begins from phenomenal
  structure, sense 3 of [TERM-072](../terms.d/TERM-072.md), and measures it by report. It
  does not derive phenomenality from organization, and it bears on the
  constitutive claim only by keeping phenomenal binding apart from
  functional binding, which "can occur without conscious experience"
  (§1). That is [CLAIM-411](../claims.d/CLAIM-411.md)'s point made about binding: functional
  integration does not suffice for phenomenal unity.
- **[QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md).** Every measure in the framework is a similarity
  judgement, a report. The paper says so and lists the ways task demands
  change the structure obtained (§5.3.2). It adds a kind of report
  evidence, but no route around report.
- **[THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md) (Metzinger's unity).** Metzinger's unity is a single
  functional cluster, a dynamic criterion. This paper's is a condition on
  measured qualia data. They are different proposals for the same unity
  ([ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)'s phenomenal unity), and neither is tested.
- **THEORY warranted.** No THEORY in the record states the proposal. One
  is filed, [THEORY-875](../theory.d/THEORY-875.md), at the strength above: a framework that
  proposes binding as gluing of measured qualia data, with its two
  readings of failure and their unresolved difference.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- No data. Every result is a definition, a reading or a proposed design.
- The worked Alexandroff example is wrong as printed: it calls the
  opens up-sets, lists down-sets of a different order, and omits the
  whole set (checked by script). The visual-field τ is a topology only on
  the region reading.
- As stated, the gluing condition is either trivial (items matched by trial
  label) or undefined (rows of measured values), because Definition 1
  constrains dissimilarities and does not map data. The operational test is
  a consistency radius on measured matrices.
- Rivalry is offered as a sheaf violation, against the paper's own
  definition of gluing.
- The restriction-as-attention assumption and the non-decreasing
  dissimilarity rule are speculative, and symmetry is assumed against the
  paper's own motivation for categories.
- Two readings of a sheaf failure, disunity and context dependence, are
  given without a criterion that separates them.
- The formalism stops at two regions, one modality and one moment (§5.3.1).

## Open questions

- What, in the data, separates a sheaf failure that is disunity from one
  that is context dependence? Without that, the formalism cannot diagnose
  disunity.
- What is the restriction of a measured row? A definition that maps data,
  not only items, would give the gluing condition content.
- Is there a phenomenal case of the tribar's shape, three or more parts
  pairwise compatible with no whole, and is it experienced as one? That
  would bear directly on [QUESTION-255](../questions.d/QUESTION-255.md).
- Does attention to a part in fact never decrease dissimilarity? It is a
  measurable claim, and the framework's restriction maps depend on it.
