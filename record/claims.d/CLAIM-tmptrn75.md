# CLAIM-tmptrn75: An institution can make others' circumstances highly legible to itself, recognizing and predicting their needs and vulnerabilities, and use that legibility for its own objective while their justified claims gain no authority over what it does, so the functional profile the word sociopathy points at is legibility without volitional efficacy for others' claims, not a failure to understand them

## The claim

A46 §5's definition: "A persistent regime of organizational agency in which
the institution maintains substantial competence in pursuing its own
objectives while systematically failing to give independently justified
claims of affected parties appropriate practical authority, particularly
where its organization reproduces that failure." "This is not a clinical
diagnosis." "It also does not require the institution's individual
participants to be personally callous."

A46 §6 separates six capacities: epistemic sensitivity ("Recognizes another
party's circumstances"), predictive sensitivity, instrumental sensitivity,
motivational responsiveness ("Allows their circumstances to recruit
corrective activity"), normative responsiveness ("Gives their justified
claims appropriate authority") and correctability. "An institution might
excel at the first three while failing at the latter three. A platform
could become better at predicting a user's frustration while optimizing
its ability to retain that user under increasingly frustrating
conditions." Hence: "**Institutional sociopathy need not be characterized
by an inability to understand others. It may involve the ability to
understand others without allowing their interests to govern activity in
the ways their standing warrants.**"

A48 §10: certain realizations of shareholder primacy "can **institutionalize
a separation between understanding another party and being answerable to
that party**" ([CLAIM-tmpa61rf](CLAIM-tmpa61rf.md)).

## Against the held answers to [QUESTION-265](../questions.d/QUESTION-265.md)

- [CLAIM-432](CLAIM-432.md): the corporation "lacks remorse, functionally, when harm it
  does to others is not among the concerns its own appraisals track and
  gains precedence over what it does only when a regulator or a market
  turns it into a threat". This claim moves the deficit later: the harm can
  be among what its appraisals track, as a prediction it uses, and still
  get no authority. It is [CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md) with the first achievement held and the
  second failing, and [CLAIM-283](CLAIM-283.md)'s claimed precedence without granted
  authority.
- [CLAIM-431](CLAIM-431.md): "any procedure or role duty that makes others' harms its
  concern was put there for its own ends", so remorse cannot be told from
  prudence. A46 does not answer this. If it holds, A46's motivational and
  normative responsiveness cannot be told from instrumental sensitivity by
  conduct, and this claim's profile is a description of incentives, as
  [CLAIM-431](CLAIM-431.md) says the clinical word is. [CLAIM-1169](CLAIM-1169.md) says when a test could
  tell them apart; [ARG-tmp4er8c](../arguments.d/ARG-tmp4er8c.md) applies it to A48's test.
- Members and institution: A46 §11, "The institution can contain moral
  agents without itself exhibiting adequate moral responsiveness", is held
  in [CLAIM-432](CLAIM-432.md) ("changed by redesigning the corporation's concerns, not its
  members' characters") and [CLAIM-271](CLAIM-271.md).
- A46 §8's ladder (error, negligence, instrumental indifference,
  exploitation, reflexive counter-normativity, sociopathy) relabels
  [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md), [CLAIM-714](CLAIM-714.md) and [CLAIM-670](CLAIM-670.md); "These are not necessarily stages on
  a universal continuum."

Bakan ([LIT-1430](../literature.d/LIT-1430.md)) is held and Unavailable; A46 notes that the clinical
application "has itself been criticized".
