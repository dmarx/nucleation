# LIT-946: The Geometry of Prompting: Unveiling Distinct Mechanisms of Task Adaptation in Language Models

Artem Kirsanov, Chi-Ning Chou, Kyunghyun Cho and SueYeon Chung (2025), *Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: NAACL 2025*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-789](../notes.d/NOTE-789.md).*

- **Same accuracy, different mechanisms.** In base Llama-3.1-8B (and
  Gemma-2-2B), an instruction beats up to five demonstrations and more
  examples overtake it; a trained soft prompt beats both.
- **Demonstrations reshape the input.** They raise the separability of the
  input sentence's representation at early-to-middle layers; an
  instruction does not. Gold, letter and shuffled labels make little
  difference: the examples' sentences do the work.
- **An instruction acts on the last token**, raising separability from
  about layer 8 on, less than demonstrations do.
- **Failures sit at the readout.** At five demonstrations accuracy varies
  from below 0.1 to about 0.6 across draws of examples while separability
  barely moves; consistently swapped labels fail with separability intact.
- **Cross-task effects.** Demonstrations for one labelling of the same
  sentences help other labellings early and hurt them late; soft prompts
  hurt them more.
- **The limit.** Base models only, one instruction wording, mostly
  synthetic data, and a readout explanation inferred, not measured.
