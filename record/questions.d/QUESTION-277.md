# QUESTION-277: When formerly autonomous partners come to depend on one another, which of mutual dependence, reproductive cohesion, the distribution of essential functions across genomes, the host's regulatory control and the alignment of selection does each route to dependence bring about, and which of them, alone or together, make the partnership a new evolutionary individual?

## Why it is a question

The record has a theory of when a collective becomes an individual, and it
has almost no cases of how partners come to depend on each other. Its
readings set the conditions on the collective. Selection between collectives
must come to trump selection within them ([THEORY-141](../theory.d/THEORY-141.md)). Collaboration and
generatively entrenched interactions must give organizational closure
([THEORY-418](../theory.d/THEORY-418.md)). Darwinian and physiological individuality come apart
([THEORY-443](../theory.d/THEORY-443.md)), and the reading reports Godfrey-Smith placing aphid–*Buchnera* as both. [CLAIM-060](../claims.d/CLAIM-060.md) holds that
constitutive dependence does not entail alignment of interests. What the
record lacks is the biology of the routes by which partners lose autonomy.
The exchange of 2026-10-27 named five, which "are not mutually exclusive":

- **Adaptive gene loss.** A costly, leaky function is lost because others
  supply it, and the loser becomes dependent (Black Queen, [LIT-1568](../literature.d/LIT-1568.md)).
  Selection acts on the individual, not on the collective.
- **Drift-driven decay.** Small, asexual, vertically transmitted populations
  accumulate mildly deleterious mutations (Muller's ratchet, [LIT-1573](../literature.d/LIT-1573.md);
  reviews [LIT-1559](../literature.d/LIT-1559.md), [LIT-1571](../literature.d/LIT-1571.md)). The resulting dependence need not
  benefit either partner.
- **Complementary loss across lineages.** Pathways are split among nested
  symbionts and the host ([LIT-1565](../literature.d/LIT-1565.md)), and a symbiont splits into two
  lineages with the functionality of one ([LIT-1563](../literature.d/LIT-1563.md)). The number of
  dependent parts grows without an obvious new collective function.
- **Gene transfer and import.** Genes move to the host nucleus and their
  products are imported back (*Paulinella*, [LIT-1557](../literature.d/LIT-1557.md); the nitroplast,
  [LIT-1562](../literature.d/LIT-1562.md); review [LIT-1560](../literature.d/LIT-1560.md)). This route gives the host control.
- **Host compensation.** The host maintains the collective's function by
  transport, regulation or rescue, including with genes taken from third
  lineages ([LIT-1569](../literature.d/LIT-1569.md)).

The exchange's thesis, in its words: "A system can become more
interdependent without becoming more cooperative, more unified, more
autonomous, or even more adaptive." Two of its cases show the routes do not
run one way. In mealybugs the inner symbiont has been replaced repeatedly
while host-encoded transferred genes stayed ([LIT-1572](../literature.d/LIT-1572.md)). In
*Monocercomonoides* a whole mitochondrion was lost once a bacterial Fe–S
system replaced its one essential job ([LIT-1567](../literature.d/LIT-1567.md), [LIT-1574](../literature.d/LIT-1574.md)). So
losing genes is not the same as losing function, losing autonomy or gaining
integration.

The exchange proposed scoring a partnership on five dimensions, as "a
conceptual schema, not an established metric":

- **D**, physiological dependence. It is directional: the host's dependence
  on the symbiont need not equal the symbiont's on the host.
- **G**, how essential functions are distributed across the genomes.
- **R**, reproductive cohesion: co-transmission, synchrony, bottlenecks.
- **C**, regulatory integration: the host's control of transport,
  expression, division and allocation.
- **S**, selection alignment: conflict within the collective relative to
  selection between collectives.

No single one is to stand in for individuality. Athreya, Czuppon and
Gokhale's model ([LIT-1570](../literature.d/LIT-1570.md)) gives the first formal separation, of D from
R. In it, mutual dependence evolves faster than reproductive cohesion even
when investments in the two are uncorrelated. With a carrying capacity for
collectives, complete dependence or cohesion may not evolve at all.

**What would count as an answer.** For each route, which of D, G, R, C and S
it moves, shown on worked systems, not assumed. Then a stated criterion,
tested on the same systems, for which combination makes a new evolutionary
individual. The criterion must say whether high D with low R is a candidate
individual or only a dependency. It must also say whether a partnership
whose constituents keep being replaced is one individual or a succession.
[THEORY-418](../theory.d/THEORY-418.md) predicts that metabolic complementation alone is not closure, and
the mealybug patchwork is a place to test that. Eukaryogenesis is a harder
test. The three phylogenomic programmes the exchange set together disagree
on whether it was one two-partner merger or a sequence of associations: late
mitochondrion after a complex host ([LIT-1566](../literature.d/LIT-1566.md)), mostly Asgard
inheritance ([LIT-1558](../literature.d/LIT-1558.md)), and several waves of bacterial transfer
([LIT-1561](../literature.d/LIT-1561.md)). [LIT-1564](../literature.d/LIT-1564.md) adds the chimeric metabolism. If the
programmes disagree, the event may not have a single route at all. [NOTE-159](../notes.d/NOTE-159.md)
already doubts that eukaryogenesis fits the transitions narrative.

Selection for extreme specialization within a group (Cooper & West,
[LIT-1225](../literature.d/LIT-1225.md), unread) is a sixth route. It works within a species, where the
exchange's routes cross lineages.

The question is the exchange's. The record takes no position on its answer
until the works are read. A2 put it as follows: "The biological literature
would not automatically settle these philosophical questions. It would,
however, constrain what a successful theory of individuality must explain."

## Note of 2026-10-27 (later): cases, and works for what the exchange named without citing

Six documented cases now hold the systems the question names. All are
Proposed, because their sources are unread.

- Aphid–*Buchnera* ([CASE-173](../cases.d/CASE-173.md)): dependence produced largely by drift.
- The mealybug's nested symbionts ([CASE-171](../cases.d/CASE-171.md)): genes from third
  parties, and constituents replaced.
- *Hodgkinia*'s splitting in cicadas ([CASE-177](../cases.d/CASE-177.md)): more parts and more
  dependence with no new function, and a host that compensates at
  transmission.
- *Paulinella* ([CASE-174](../cases.d/CASE-174.md)) and the nitroplast ([CASE-167](../cases.d/CASE-167.md)): control
  through gene transfer and import.
- *Monocercomonoides* ([CASE-168](../cases.d/CASE-168.md)): an organelle lost.

The exchange named several things without citing a work. Works now
registered for each:

- **Gene transfer to the nucleus:** [LIT-1588](../literature.d/LIT-1588.md).
- **Host compensation:** [LIT-1590](../literature.d/LIT-1590.md).
- **Population-genetic models of gene loss:** [LIT-1611](../literature.d/LIT-1611.md) and
  [LIT-1616](../literature.d/LIT-1616.md).
- **Metabolic-network constraints:** [LIT-1585](../literature.d/LIT-1585.md).
- **Multitype branching processes:** [LIT-1608](../literature.d/LIT-1608.md). This is a general text;
  no endosymbiosis work using the method was found.
- **Mutualism theory:** [LIT-1603](../literature.d/LIT-1603.md).
- **Host control and within-collective conflict, the S and C dimensions:**
  Frank's two models, [LIT-1617](../literature.d/LIT-1617.md) and [LIT-1605](../literature.d/LIT-1605.md).
- **The eukaryogenesis scenarios A1 listed:** syntrophy ([LIT-1597](../literature.d/LIT-1597.md),
  [LIT-1591](../literature.d/LIT-1591.md)), and complexity before the mitochondrion ([LIT-1595](../literature.d/LIT-1595.md)).

[LIT-1605](../literature.d/LIT-1605.md) makes a point that bears on the answer. Reproductive cohesion
(R) can be imposed by the host's control (C), by sequestering a germ line
of symbionts. If so, the two dimensions are not independent, and an answer
must say which drives which.

## Note of 2026-10-27 (readings): what the first three readings say

Three of the four works the exchange called "Critical" have been read in
full. The fourth, Moran 1996 ([LIT-1573](../literature.d/LIT-1573.md)), is Unfetched: its free copy is
behind a challenge this client could not pass.

- **The Black Queen route moves D one way and G, and nothing else**
  ([LIT-1568](../literature.d/LIT-1568.md), [NOTE-1212](../notes.d/NOTE-1212.md), [THEORY-1048](../theory.d/THEORY-1048.md)). Loss of a costly, leaky
  function is favoured until helpers just supply the community. No
  collective forms. There is no cohesion and no control, and the
  dependence runs from beneficiaries to helpers only. It is the record's
  clearest case of interdependence that is not individuality. The paper
  states it for free-living microbes.
- **Past a genome-size threshold, G forces C** ([LIT-1571](../literature.d/LIT-1571.md), [NOTE-1213](../notes.d/NOTE-1213.md),
  [THEORY-1049](../theory.d/THEORY-1049.md)). Below about 400 kb a symbiont cannot build its own
  envelope, and below about 200 kb translation erodes. The host then
  presumably supplies membranes, transport and translation parts, with or
  without gene transfer. Host control can therefore be a consequence of
  the symbiont's losses, not only of transfer and import. This couples
  two dimensions the question lists as separate.
- **D and R are separate, but their order is not settled** ([LIT-1570](../literature.d/LIT-1570.md),
  [NOTE-1211](../notes.d/NOTE-1211.md), [THEORY-1050](../theory.d/THEORY-1050.md), [THEORY-1051](../theory.d/THEORY-1051.md)). This corrects the
  question's summary and its "Why it is a question" section. Athreya et
  al.'s "mutual dependence evolves faster than reproductive cohesion even
  when investments in the two are uncorrelated" holds under their chosen
  mapping: free growth depends on a partner's own obligacy, and
  dissociation on both partners' adhesions. Their own appendix shows the
  bias disappearing under matched mappings and reversing under the
  opposite one. So which comes first is an empirical question about how
  the traits act.

  What the model does show robustly is the asymmetry and its sequel. The
  faster-evolving partner becomes obligate first. The other then stays,
  becomes fully dependent, or returns to independence according to
  births and deaths inside the collective. That gives the question one
  candidate variable for when dependence becomes individuality.

One open lead from the readings: [LIT-1605](../literature.d/LIT-1605.md) (Frank, unread) models
host control of transmission. If a host can impose cohesion on its own,
dissociation depends on one partner's trait, and on Athreya et al.'s
Table S1 the dependence-first bias largely goes. Reading Frank is the
next step on the R and C dimensions.

## Note of 2026-10-27 (batch 2): ten more readings

Ten more works have been read in full. The other 21 unread works in the
cluster could not be fetched (11 Unfetched, 10 Unavailable; see their
LITs).

**Routes.**

- **Transfer and import is mostly import** ([LIT-1560](../literature.d/LIT-1560.md), [NOTE-tmpl32gd](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpl32gd.md),
  [THEORY-tmp74xnn](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp74xnn.md)). In the two systems with import at scale:
  - the *Paulinella* chromatophore imports hundreds of proteins, "mostly
    from the host itself", and transfer from the symbiont played "only a
    circumstantial role";
  - the nitroplast imports more than 350, with no transfer from the
    symbiont found.

  So the route above ("Genes move to the host nucleus and their products
  are imported back") is wrong as stated. Import moves C and G without the
  symbiont's genes moving, and it shares third-party genes with the
  host-compensation route.
- **An order to test** ([THEORY-tmpkvjih](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpkvjih.md)). Sørensen et al. propose:
  - host effectors first (C);
  - then dependence (D), when one redundant imported protein lets the
    symbiont's gene decay;
  - then general import, which shifts G.

  Vertical transmission (R) is a condition: rhizobia are targeted by host
  peptides, are not transmitted vertically, and are not integrated. This
  reverses the insect order of [THEORY-1049](../theory.d/THEORY-1049.md), where the symbiont's losses
  come first. Neither order has been watched in one lineage.
- **Complementary loss moves G and the symbiont's D, not C** ([LIT-1596](../literature.d/LIT-1596.md),
  [NOTE-tmp82xw1](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp82xw1.md), [THEORY-tmpehubi](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpehubi.md)). *Buchnera* keeps the pathways for the
  amino acids its host cannot make, and builds them on host-made
  precursors. It has lost its own regulators, which is not the same as
  being regulated. The genome paper names no mechanism of reduction; drift
  is [LIT-1573](../literature.d/LIT-1573.md)'s account, which is Unfetched.
- **Host compensation maintains R, perhaps at a cost to C and S**
  ([LIT-1590](../literature.d/LIT-1590.md), [NOTE-tmp6a57o](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp6a57o.md), [THEORY-tmpjel7l](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpjel7l.md)). Cicadas send more *Hodgkinia*
  cells per egg as the symbiont splits, so every lineage still reaches
  every egg. The authors speculate that larger transmitted populations
  weaken bottlenecks and host control. If so, keeping R costs S, and an
  answer must say how the dimensions trade off. The rise is significant in
  one species of four.
- **Integration undone in steps** ([LIT-1574](../literature.d/LIT-1574.md), [NOTE-tmpuq8at](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpuq8at.md),
  [THEORY-tmp247k7](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp247k7.md)). *Monocercomonoides* lost its mitochondrion only after
  the organelle's essential jobs went one at a time. First, Fe–S assembly
  moved to a SUF system that relatives with organelles share. Then the
  glycine cleavage system was lost. This is host compensation with a third
  lineage's genes, run to dissolution.
- **No import below the tipping point.** The host proteins seen inside
  insect symbionts are in *Buchnera* and *Moranella*, both above
  [THEORY-1049](../theory.d/THEORY-1049.md)'s thresholds ([CASE-171](../cases.d/CASE-171.md), [CASE-173](../cases.d/CASE-173.md)). Host supply of
  tipping-point endosymbionts remains inferred.

**Dependence stays directional even in an organelle.** The nitroplast's
host can lose UCYN-A when given fixed nitrogen in culture ([CASE-167](../cases.d/CASE-167.md)).
The symbiont cannot live without the host. D must be scored in each
direction, as the question says.

**Eukaryogenesis: the disagreement is narrower than stated above.** The
"Why it is a question" section set three programmes against each other on
whether eukaryogenesis was "one two-partner merger or a sequence of
associations". The readings change that.

- **All three accept one endosymbiosis**, an Asgard-related host with an
  alphaproteobacterium. All three also accept bacterial genes from other
  donors, some before the mitochondrion ([LIT-1566](../literature.d/LIT-1566.md), [LIT-1558](../literature.d/LIT-1558.md), [LIT-1561](../literature.d/LIT-1561.md),
  read in [NOTE-tmp2el8z](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp2el8z.md), [NOTE-tmppkn6v](../notes.d/NOTE-tmppkn6v.md), [NOTE-tmpe5t7q](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpe5t7q.md)).
- **Tobiasson et al. are themselves mitochondrion-late**, so they agree
  with Kay et al. on order ([THEORY-tmpfoo2c](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpfoo2c.md)). Kay et al. argue against
  serial endosymbiosis ([THEORY-tmp5wptr](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp5wptr.md)).
- **The live dispute is between Tobiasson and Bernabeu.** Are the other
  bacterial genes diffuse transfers or a few major donors in waves? And
  can stem lengths time acquisitions at all? Tobiasson et al.'s own test
  says mostly not ([THEORY-tmpbinho](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpbinho.md)), while Bernabeu et al.'s waves rest
  on them ([THEORY-tmphywi5](../theory.d/THEORY-tmphywi5.md)). Pittis and Gabaldón's mito-late order rests
  on the same measure ([LIT-1595](../literature.d/LIT-1595.md), [NOTE-tmpjgenc](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpjgenc.md), [THEORY-tmpckv2l](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpckv2l.md)).
- **No donor is shown to be a partner.** Bernabeu et al. say their data
  "do not provide information on the host or endosymbiotic nature of any
  partner". So "distributed" means distributed gene donors, not a series
  of collectives. For this question, the eukaryogenesis papers bear on G
  and not on R or S.
- **The metabolism is chimeric, but that does not single out syntrophy**
  ([LIT-1564](../literature.d/LIT-1564.md), [NOTE-tmphab0m](../notes.d/NOTE-tmphab0m.md), [THEORY-tmpyr6q4](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpyr6q4.md)). Any archaeal host taking up
  a bacterium with a TCA cycle predicts the split. The paper adds an
  instance of C: enzymes of non-alphaproteobacterial origin now work
  inside the mitochondrion. It also offers, as a possibility, a mechanism
  the routes above do not name: host genes duplicated and sent into the
  organelle "might have promoted the genome reduction of the
  endosymbiont".
- **A dependence before any endosymbiosis** ([LIT-1591](../literature.d/LIT-1591.md), [NOTE-tmpeb08c](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpeb08c.md),
  [THEORY-tmpyig9c](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpyig9c.md)). The cultured Asgard archaeon grows only with
  interchangeable methanogen partners. That is high D with no R and no C:
  interdependence without individuality at the start of the story. In the
  authors' E3 model the host makes the hydrogen, and they favour an
  interaction other than hydrogen transfer as the driver. So this case
  does not support the hydrogen hypothesis ([LIT-1597](../literature.d/LIT-1597.md), unread), and the
  previous note's grouping of the two under "syntrophy" needs that
  caveat.
