# LIT-tmp8nw3w: The Social Dimensions of Scientific Knowledge

Helen Longino (2002; substantive revision 2025), "The Social Dimensions of
Scientific Knowledge", *The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, ed. E. N.
Zalta and U. Nodelman.

## Key takeaways

- **Underdetermination makes knowledge social.** Data bear on hypotheses
  only through background assumptions, and the chain of justification ends
  in assumptions no evidence supports. "The only check against the
  arbitrary dominance of subjective (metaphysical, political, aesthetic)
  preference in such cases is critical interaction among the members of
  the scientific community or among members of different communities"
  (§5).
- **Institutions of criticism must meet norms to confer objectivity.**
  Communities do institutionalise criticism, for example peer review, but
  it counts only if (1) there are venues for critical interaction, (2)
  criticism has uptake, shown by a change in the distribution of belief
  over time that responds to it, (3) the standards regulating discourse are
  public, and (4) intellectual authority is shared in "tempered equality":
  any perspective has prima facie standing, lost by failing to engage or
  respond (§5).
- **The processes are social, not only the review** (her 2002 book,
  [LIT-tmphc6w3](LIT-tmphc6w3.md)), and in 2022 she criticises individual-centred social
  epistemology for taking evidence as given.
- **Against rivals.** Solomon locates normativity in the distribution of
  "decision vectors" and the community's acceptance of empirically
  successful theories, with no role for deliberation; Longino locates it in
  deliberative interaction (§5).
- **Network models** (§2). Zollman's models make sparse communication more
  reliable and slower; Rosenstock, O'Connor & Bruner find the effect very
  sensitive to parameters. The entry says Zollman "proves" the result;
  [LIT-tmp8lzwo](LIT-tmp8lzwo.md) is a simulation study, so "proves" overstates it.
