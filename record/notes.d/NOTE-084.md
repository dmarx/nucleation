# NOTE-084: Moynihan, "Mike Johnson's Institutional Betrayal"

## Contribution

Moynihan, writing as a public-administration commentator, recounts the state of a Department of Homeland Security shutdown. The Senate passed a bill funding DHS except ICE and CBP. The House, under Johnson, passed instead a Trump-backed bill funding all of DHS, and both chambers recessed. Meanwhile the President announced he would pay TSA employees without naming a legal appropriation source. Moynihan argues that letting the executive both impound appropriated money and spend unappropriated money guts Congress's central check. He quotes Matt Glassman and Josh Chafetz in support.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Timeline as the author gives it: the President's memo on paying TSA employees, a record 12% TSA sick-call rate, Senate passage at 2:20 a.m. on Friday, a House bill funding DHS to late May, and both chambers in recess.
- The author's point that ICE and CBP are already funded for multiple years under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, so the dispute is really about accountability conditions (masks, warrantless entry) and about funding the rest of DHS.
- The author's framing of Johnson's options: (A) reclaim the purse with the Senate bill, or (B) let the President dictate appropriations. "Johnson chose B."
- Quoted authorities: Glassman on purpose restrictions as "the most bedrock feature of appropriations"; Chafetz calling unilateral spending "anti-constitutional", with a Plutarch/Caesar analogy.
- A closing aside: shutdowns stem from a 1980 legal interpretation of the Antideficiency Act, and Moynihan prefers amending the Act to leaving the question to the executive.
