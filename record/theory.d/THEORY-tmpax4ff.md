# THEORY-tmpax4ff: X is a cause of Y exactly when some intervention on X with respect to Y would change Y, where an intervention is an exogenous causal process that alone sets X (M1), affects Y only through X (M2), is not caused by anything that affects Y by another route (M3), and leaves Y's causes off the path through X unchanged (M4); so defined, causation is not reduced to non-causal facts but is not viciously circular, and needs no human agent

## Source

Woodward, "Causation and Manipulability" ([LIT-tmp0ifeg](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0ifeg.md)), read in
[NOTE-tmpxh2wm](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpxh2wm.md). The book it summarizes, [LIT-tmp6yv4f](../literature.d/LIT-tmp6yv4f.md), is unread.

## What was actually shown

Woodward states and defends the account in the encyclopedia entry
([LIT-tmp0ifeg](../literature.d/LIT-tmp0ifeg.md)).

- **Why intervention and not action.** A correlation can persist when A is
  produced by a free act without A causing B: the experimenter's free act
  of giving a placebo is correlated with recovery. What is needed is a
  "surgical" change in A, such that any change in B comes only through A.
- **The conditions.** Relative to Y, an intervention I on X must (M1) be
  the only cause of X; (M2) not cause Y by a route avoiding X; (M3) not be
  caused by anything that affects Y by such a route; and (M4) leave the
  values of Y's other causes, off the path from I through X, unchanged.
- **The analysis.** Whether X causes Y is what Y would do under such an
  intervention. X is a total cause if some intervention on X changes Y; a
  direct cause if Y changes when the other variables, including mediators,
  are held fixed by interventions too.
- **The objections.** It is not a reduction, since intervention is a
  causal notion, and it is not viciously circular, since it does not
  presuppose causal facts about X and Y themselves. It is not
  anthropocentric: a natural process with the right causal history is an
  intervention.

The account is philosophical, so nothing in the entry could have come out
otherwise experimentally. What makes it more than a definition is that it
disagrees with rivals on cases: on omissions and double prevention it gives
judgments that causal-process theories reject, which Woodward takes to show
it is not vacuous.

## What this does not say

- **Not that a cause must be manipulable in practice.** Interventions need
  not be feasible, and observational evidence can confirm what an
  intervention would do.
- **Not a reduction of causation.** The entry says so.
- **Not a theory of levels.** This version of the entry does not discuss
  invariance, proportionality, supervenience or higher-level causation.
  Its only macro-level remark is that true macro-level interventionist
  counterfactuals plausibly have more fundamental explanations, without
  needing non-counterfactual truthmakers.
- **Not settled on unmanipulable causes.** Whether "being female" or "being
  a raven" can be causes depends on whether one takes setting or
  possibility-constrained interventions, and the entry presents both.

## Connections

- **List & Menzies ([THEORY-tmp3fc4z](THEORY-tmp3fc4z.md)).** Their difference-making account of
  higher-level causes applies this kind of counterfactual test across
  realizations. The interventionist account supplies the notion of a cause;
  realization-insensitivity is their criterion for when the higher-level
  property, rather than its realizer, is the cause. No relation is
  declared: their paper uses Lewis-style semantics, not M1–M4.
- **French ([THEORY-tmp2zcvf](THEORY-tmp2zcvf.md)).** French establishes corporate intentional
  acts by licensed redescription, not by intervention. This account says
  what a causal version of his claim would need: an intervention on the
  corporate variable, such as its policy, that changes the outcome by no
  route through members' independent reasons (M2, M4).
- **The owner's essay.** For §5.3 it fixes what an agentive explanation of
  Northstar must show to count as causal. Whether such interventions are
  ever clean in an organization, where any change of policy is made by
  members, is the essay's problem, not one the entry addresses.
