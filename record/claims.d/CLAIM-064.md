# CLAIM-064: A translation reconstructs not only what is said but the situation of its saying

## The claim

A12: "**A translation doesn't merely reconstruct what is said; it reconstructs
the situation of its saying.** I particularly like the second formulation
because it gets at the question your example raises: a translation must somehow
recreate not only a text, but an *act of communication*."

## Where it went

Last present at A12. At U8 the owner asked for the "native vernacular" of
speech-act theory, and A14 restated the point as footing, stance and force;
the situation as such was not mentioned again in owner turns U1–U13. Nothing
argued against it. Recovered, as the exchange's best one-line statement of
what [CLAIM-061](CLAIM-061.md) is for.

## What it does not say

Not that a translation must reproduce the source's situation: Henley moved
Villon's into another century ([CASE-010](../cases.d/CASE-010.md)). It must reconstruct one that plays the
same role. Weaker than the manuscript's central thesis ([CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)) in one respect
and stronger in another: it names what is reconstructed, but not who must be
able to recover it.
