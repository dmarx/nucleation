  <https://ophi.org.uk/sites/default/files/Sen-1979_Equality-of-What.pdf>.
  Sen argues that utilitarian equality (equal marginal utility),
  total-utility equality (completed as leximin) and Rawlsian equality
  (maximin/leximin on an index of primary goods) are each insufficient,
  and so is any combination of them, even for the needs-based part of
  equality: the test case is a disabled person who has no marginal-utility
  claim, no total-utility deprivation (he is contented) and no
  primary-goods deprivation, yet has an evident claim. He proposes "basic
  capability equality" — equality in "a person being able to do certain
  basic things" (p. 218) — as a non-fetishist, culture-dependent extension
  of Rawls, explicitly only "a partial guide" to the equality part of
  morality, and says his main (negative) thesis does not depend on this
  constructive proposal (p. 220).
