# NOTE-173: Griesemer & Shavit — Scaffolding individuality (3Cs)

## Contribution

Evolutionary transitions, in which entities join a new reproducing collective and lose the capacity to reproduce independently, are hard to describe without circularity, because the notions used (units of selection, individuality, multilevel inheritance) are themselves products of the transition. The authors describe transitions through the kinds of interaction among community members that such transitions require. Reproducing systems hybridize with environmental components through eco-devo scaffolding and form communities of diverse members with heterogeneous interactions, and these communities can become individuals with their own inheritance systems. They argue for treating individuality as a special case of collectivity. They propose an idealized sequence (coordination, then cooperation, then collaboration, the "3Cs") that scaffolds new collective individuals, and use hominid evolution and learning to stress the role of development in both new levels of individuality and new inheritance systems.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 (3Cs conceptual framework): the sequence is "idealized" the way embryology's normal tables are, and departures are "heterochronic". How many transitions actually follow it is left as an open empirical question. The sequence may fit "egalitarian" (get-together) transitions better than "fraternal" (stick-together) ones. The authors call it "not a fully-fledged model but only a first and rough sketch".
- §2 and Table 1 (3Cs conceptual model): three interaction types map onto three levels of "unithood": collection (pairwise coordination), conexus (connected system of cooperation) and community (collaboration with shared construction of interactions). This marks the transition in individuality and a potential new inheritance system.
- §2 on MLS1 and MLS2: the framework is about levels of individuality and reproduction, not levels of selection. It recasts the MLS1/MLS2 contrast as a developmental progression from unstable MLS1 to stable MLS2 situations. Trait-group versus demic structure decides candidacy as a higher-level "reproducer".
- §3 "Group-level reproduction": drawing on Caporael's "truly social cognition", a community can only reproduce by exporting an organized generative package larger than any single member. Griesemer's sampling account of inheritance (propagules as samples) is applied to migrant groups. A Neolithic thought experiment (hunters, gatherers, farmers and shamans; after Shavit & Sharon) shows that founding groups must sample heterogeneous roles and interactions.
- Close of §3: contrary to those who separate transitions in individuality from the evolution of inheritance systems, the two "go hand in hand". The 3Cs may refine Maynard Smith & Szathmáry's limited versus unlimited heredity distinction.

## Open questions

- A process-first account of biological individuality and of the part–whole relation among reproducers. Relevant to nucleation notes on identity and individuality, alongside [LIT-167](../literature.d/LIT-167.md) (Baedke) and the niche papers [LIT-169](../literature.d/LIT-169.md) and [LIT-127](../literature.d/LIT-127.md).
- The claim that it is non-circular deserves scrutiny: do "coordination / cooperation / collaboration" avoid presupposing the units whose emergence they are meant to explain?
- The move from biological transitions to human cultural and academic communities (the example of discipline formation in §1) is analogical. A deeper reading should check how much it carries.
