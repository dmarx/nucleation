# NOTE-2076: Shue — Basic rights, introduction and index

## Contribution

From the parts read: the book's thesis, that subsistence is a basic right
on a par with security and liberty, and its strategy, answering the
objection that the correlative duties ask too much.

## Key results

- **Thesis** (p. 9): "Part I attempts to show that rights to three
  particular substances—subsistence, security, and liberty—are basic
  rights."
- **Priority** (p. 8): "at least one small set of what are normally counted
  as economic rights belongs among the rights with the highest priority."
- **Strategy** (p. 9): "once some presumption has been established in favor
  of a right, the main task is to answer the objection that the duties
  involved would ask too much of others."
- **The duties, by the index** (p. 257): "correlative duties (of avoidance,
  protection and aid), and basic rights", with a "tripartite analysis" at
  pp. 54–55; entries "duty to aid", "to avoid deprivation", "to protect
  rights".
- **Contents** of the 2020 edition: Part I, "Three Basic Rights" (chs. 1–3,
  ch. 2 "Correlative Duties"); Part II, "Three Challenges to Subsistence
  Rights"; Part III, "New Challenges to Basic Rights", with ch. 7
  "Right-grounded Duties and the Institutional Turn (1996)" and ch. 8
  "Basic Rights and Climate Change (2020)".

## Assumptions

From the Introduction only: the case for a right rests on the importance
of the interest it protects ("Virtually any argument in favor of a right
will depend at bottom on emphasizing that the interest to which the right
is asserted is genuinely important", p. 8).

## Concepts

Basic rights; subsistence, security and liberty; correlative duties of
avoidance, protection and aid (by the index); economic and political
rights.

## Bearing on the record

- **[TERM-085](../terms.d/TERM-085.md).** It names "the duties to respect, protect and fulfil (Shue's
  Basic Rights, 1980, and the UN treaty bodies' usage)". Shue's own triad,
  by the index, is avoidance, protection and aid; "respect, protect and
  fulfil" is the later treaty bodies' wording, which the term runs
  together with Shue's.
- **[CLAIM-729](../claims.d/CLAIM-729.md).** Weakly. Shue's triad divides what a right demands of
  duty-bearers; the claim divides what a person owes toward rules and
  institutions (comply, support, establish). Chapter 7's title suggests he
  later treats duties to build institutions; not read.
- The exchange's A87 is confirmed in outline by the index: duties to avoid
  depriving, to protect, and to aid. Shue's wording of them was not read.

## Limitations

Only the Introduction, contents and index. The triad's text and argument
(chapter 2) and chapter 7 are unread.
