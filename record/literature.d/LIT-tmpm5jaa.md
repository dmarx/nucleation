# LIT-tmpm5jaa: Perceptual consciousness overflows cognitive access

Ned Block (2011), *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 15(12), 567–575, an
Opinion article.

## Key takeaways

- **The argument restated.** In Sperling's task subjects report 3–4 of 12
  letters but 3–4 from any row cued after offset, so about 10.5 letters'
  worth of specific information is held. The overflow argument says that
  information is conscious. Overflow means that "necessarily most are
  unaccessed", not that any item is inaccessible, as not everyone can win a
  lottery that each can win.
- **Generic and fragment illusions.** Critics (Kouider, de Gardelle,
  Dehaene; Cohen and Dennett; Phillips; Stazicker; Lau and Rosenthal)
  hold that only generic letter-likeness, gist or fragments are conscious
  before the cue, and that the specific information is unconscious until
  attention amplifies it. Block argues that "there is no independent
  evidence for highly detailed unconscious iconic memory", and that
  conscious generic letters with no specific shape are hard to make sense
  of.
- **Kouider et al.'s pseudo-letter experiment** used low contrast and a mask
  that cut recall to 1.47 letters per row (4.41 of 12), which by the usual
  criterion leaves no icon. Its illusion rate in uncued rows was 10–15%,
  which a rich view with feature fragments can accept.
- **Fragile visual short-term memory.** The Amsterdam group's third store:
  cortical (no rod effect after 1 s, unaffected by a light flash then,
  visible in V4 and not V1–V3), with capacity 7, 6 and 5 of 8 at 1, 2.5 and
  4 s against a working memory of 4. Withdrawing attention, and TMS to
  right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, reduce working memory and leave
  fragile VSTM almost intact.
- **Unconscious working memory is weak.** Soto et al.'s subjects who saw
  "nothing" compared a grating 5 s later at slightly above 55%, far short
  of what an unconscious store would need to do the Amsterdam tasks.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, with six other papers by Block
on consciousness. No anthology topic holds a theory of perceptual capacity
and consciousness, and the paper carries no instruction for
machine-learning practice.

**It extends Block (2007), [LIT-tmpqw586](LIT-tmpqw586.md).** It defends that paper's overflow
argument against the objections raised in its commentaries and after, and
it replaces the 2007 picture of iconic memory with three stores, pure
iconic memory, fragile VSTM and working memory, of which the second carries
the argument.

Its chief opponents, Cohen and Dennett's "Consciousness cannot be separated
from function" (2011) and Kouider et al.'s "partial awareness" (2010), are
not held. Dennett's position on the question is represented in the record
only by "Quining Qualia" ([LIT-433](LIT-433.md)) and "Time and the Observer" ([LIT-431](LIT-431.md)),
which the 2007 paper rivals.
