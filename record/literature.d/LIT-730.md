# LIT-730: Episteme Symposium on Group Agency: Replies to Gaus, Cariani, Sylvan, and Briggs

Christian List & Philip Pettit (2012), "Episteme Symposium on *Group
Agency*: Replies to Gaus, Cariani, Sylvan, and Briggs", *Episteme*
9(3):293–309 — DOI-10.1017/epi.2012.18

## Key takeaways

- **The premise-based procedure is an illustration** (p. 1). It "was never
  intended to be the only, or even the default, method"; real group agents
  use less mechanical methods, and the book rejects the procedure as an
  organising design because it fixes the premises (pp. 11–12, citing GA
  60–62).
- **Rationality is classical, its means ecological** (Gaus, pp. 2–3).
  Ecological rationality is "simply classical rationality, conditional on
  a particular environment"; organisational structures can suit some
  environments and not others.
- **Robustness can be adjusted** (pp. 3–4). Universal domain and
  completeness may be relaxed case by case, like building codes in
  California and London. Even so, they say, aggregation rules satisfying
  proposition-wise supervenience remain "extremely limited", so "our
  central findings stand". This is asserted from the literature, not
  proved here.
- **Group epistemics** (Cariani, pp. 5–10). Chapter 4's results are
  possibility results; reliability is a pair, positive and negative, not
  an average; incomplete group judgments can be epistemically optimal.
- **Non-redundant realism** (Sylvan, pp. 10–14). Role-based member
  acceptance is necessary for every group belief but does not reduce it:
  "it is the group belief that explains the role-based member acceptance".
  No "plausible, accessible recipe" renders group agents redundant (p. 14).
- **Responsibility and rights** (Briggs, pp. 14–17). Quoting the book (GA
  182): "there is every reason to hold [group agents] responsible in their
  own right", with restricted rights. They concede the book argues for the
  possibility of group agents and takes their existence "mostly ... for
  granted" (p. 14).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-05 at the owner's request. It is the second lawful text
by the authors beside "Group Agency and Supervenience" ([LIT-705](LIT-705.md)), which
states Part I's formal result. These replies reach the book's Parts II and
III, design and status, through quotation, which [LIT-705](LIT-705.md) does not. The
book ([LIT-391](LIT-391.md)) stays Deferred: a reply quoting it is not a reading of it.

It adds to two accounts without being the source of either. To [THEORY-127](../theory.d/THEORY-127.md),
the authors' own statement that relaxing robustness does not rescue
proposition-wise aggregation. To [THEORY-139](../theory.d/THEORY-139.md), the book's verdict, as
quoted here, that group agents are responsible in their own right, and
the concession that the range of their responsibilities is barely
addressed. Details are in [NOTE-582](../notes.d/NOTE-582.md).
