# THEORY-2116: Tomasello and colleagues propose that what is uniquely human in cognition is shared intentionality, an evolved motivation and capacity to take part in activities with shared goals and joint intentions, which emerges by about 14 months from a primate-general understanding of intentional action plus a uniquely human motivation to share, is absent in great apes, and from which language, belief–desire psychology and social norms derive

## The account

Intention-reading is primate-general; sharing is not. Humans add a
motivation to share psychological states, and with it collaborate under
shared goals in which each wants that we do X together, attend jointly and
reverse roles. From about 9 to 14 months infants pass from dyadic to
triadic to collaborative engagement. Internalizing these dialogic
representations yields collective intentionality, conventions, language
and institutions.

## Evidence

Review of experiments with infants, children with autism and great apes,
mainly from the authors' lab; no new data.
