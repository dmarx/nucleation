# LIT-tmpuur53: Critical Slowing Down in Momentary Affect as Early Warning Signal of Impending Transitions in Depression

Smit, Helmich, Bringmann, Oldehinkel, Wichers & Snippe (2025), Clinical
Psychological Science 13(4), 760–773. DOI 10.1177/21677026241305136.
Read from the authors' 2022 preprint (osf.io/u6vdg).

## Key takeaways

- A replicated single-subject design: 37 people in remission who were
  tapering antidepressants, a median of 545 momentary assessments each;
  19 had a reliable, persistent, clinically relevant rise in depressive
  symptoms.
- Early warning signals (a rising trend in 28-day-window autocorrelation
  or variance) preceded recurrence in about a third of those who relapsed
  and appeared in about a sixth of those who did not.
- Where they appeared they began weeks ahead: on average 56.7 days
  (autocorrelation) and 37.8 days (variance) before the transition.
- The authors: the low sensitivity "may indicate that in the majority of
  patients, the recurrence of depressive symptoms is not a critical
  transition", though a transition can also occur without detectable
  slowing.

## Standing in the record

Filed by LEAD-032 for QUESTION-045's "individual data". It is the nearest
the search found: within-person dynamics before a transition into
depression, in many people. It shows a tipping-point sign in a minority,
not hysteresis, and nothing about what keeps the depressed state up once
entered. It replicates across 37 people the single-patient study of
Wichers et al. 2016 (LIT-tmpdpj4x).
