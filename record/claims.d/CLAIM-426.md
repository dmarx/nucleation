# CLAIM-426: Where a judgement's circulation helps bring about what it says, its visibility, repetition and attributed plurality are evidence about what it will bring about, so the rule that a judgement's authority should come from its grounds and not its visibility holds for judgements of what already is, and not without qualification for judgements of what someone or something will become

## The claim

[CLAIM-425](CLAIM-425.md) holds that a perspective governs itself with respect to
social judgement by making a judgement's authority depend on its grounds,
"not on their visibility, repetition or attributed plurality". That is
right for a judgement about what a person already is. It needs
qualification for a judgement whose circulation is part of what makes it
true.

- **The case it misses.** Land, defining the CCRU's term ([LIT-1396](../literature.d/LIT-1396.md)):
  "Hyperstition is a positive feedback circuit including culture as a
  component. It can be defined as the experimental (techno-)science of
  self-fulfilling prophecies." His example is cyberspace: "The (fictional)
  idea of Cyberspace contributed to the influx of investment" "that
  rapidly converted it into a technosocial reality." Before the investment,
  the idea's visibility was not evidence that cyberspace existed. It was
  evidence that it would.
- **The person's version.** A characterization of who someone will become
  ("she'll be a leader", "he'll end up in prison") can act through others'
  treatment ([TERM-075](../terms.d/TERM-075.md), sense 1) and through its appropriation (sense
  4), and its breadth is part of its force. A person deciding how much
  weight to give it is not asking only whether it is well grounded; they
  are also judging what its circulation will do. The lyric's second line,
  "If I wasn't then why would I say I am" ([LIT-1394](../literature.d/LIT-1394.md)), is a person
  making their own attribution true by asserting it.
- **Circulation can also falsify.** MacKenzie's counterperformativity
  ([LIT-1395](../literature.d/LIT-1395.md)) is the opposite loop: acting on a model makes it less
  true. Soros's reflexivity ([LIT-1397](../literature.d/LIT-1397.md)) is the general condition: a
  participant's view is both an attempt to know a situation and an
  intervention in it. So visibility is evidence about the future in either
  direction, and it is evidence only of what the judgement's spread will
  do, not of whether it was right.

## What it does not say

- **Not that popularity is credibility.** For a judgement of what already
  is, [CLAIM-425](CLAIM-425.md) stands. A characterization of someone's past conduct
  does not become truer by being repeated.
- **Not that hyperstition is a mechanism the record lacked.** Merton's
  self-fulfilling prophecy ([LIT-1398](../literature.d/LIT-1398.md)) and the looping effects held
  through [CLAIM-402](CLAIM-402.md) already run on the same circuit. What the CCRU's term
  adds is the case where the idea is a fiction or a forecast and its spread
  is the point; Land allows that it can "transmute lies into truths"
  ([NOTE-1378](../notes.d/NOTE-1378.md)), so the idea need not be true of the present.
- **Not an endorsement of the CCRU's programme.** Land's theory-fiction was
  written to bring futures about. The claim takes the definition and the
  case, and makes no judgement on the programme.
- **Not a term.** "Hyperstition" turns up here for the first time, and the
  record gives a thing a code the second time.

## Note of 2026-10-29: Land and Soros read, and Franklin's arranged plurality

Two of this claim's grounds are now read, and both bear it out with one
correction to the body.

- **Land**, [LIT-1396](../literature.d/LIT-1396.md) ([NOTE-1378](../notes.d/NOTE-1378.md)). The definition is as quoted. But
  "What it does not say" gives the term's addition as the case where the
  idea is "never false of the present", and the interview does not say
  that. Its Jerusalem example and its last sentence say the reverse: under
  favourable circumstances hyperstition can "transmute lies into truths".
  That distinction is the record's, not Land's. Nor does the interview use
  "theory-fiction". It gives no condition under which the loop runs, so
  it supports the premise, that some judgements' circulation helps make
  them true, and gives nothing for telling such judgements apart from
  others, which is the part of this claim that bears weight.
- **Soros**, [LIT-1397](../literature.d/LIT-1397.md) ([NOTE-1362](../notes.d/NOTE-1362.md)). His views "influence but do not
  determine" outcomes, his loops run both ways, and his example of a
  theory acting on markets changes them "though not necessarily making
  them more efficient". So circulation is evidence about what a judgement
  will bring about, but not about which way. That supports the narrowing
  of [CLAIM-425](CLAIM-425.md) and the body's "in either direction"; the title's "what it
  will bring about" should be read to allow the loop to run against the
  judgement as well as for it.

Franklin's autobiography, [LIT-1402](../literature.d/LIT-1402.md) ([NOTE-1360](../notes.d/NOTE-1360.md)), adds first-hand cases.
The Junto's satellite clubs and the newspaper pieces that "prepare the
minds of the people" are circulation arranged so that a judgement would
bring about what it said. Franklin arranged the appearance of plurality:
the watch reform was "communicated to the other clubs, but as arising in
each of them". That is the attributed plurality [CLAIM-425](CLAIM-425.md) discounts and this claim keeps
as evidence about the future.

The title and status are unchanged.

## Note of 2026-10-30: what holds it back

The claim's own second defeat condition is unanswered: nothing in the
record says whether a judgement's circulation, when it helps bring
about what it says, is better described as a new ground for a
judgement about the future, in which case [CLAIM-425](CLAIM-425.md)'s rule survives
intact. And the read sources (Land, Soros) give no condition for
telling which judgements' circulation helps make them true.
