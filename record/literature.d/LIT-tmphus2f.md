## Reading of 2026-11-16

Read in full in the publisher's open-access PDF. It is a two-page poster. It defines designed friction as "deliberate inefﬁciencies designed to foster engagement, critical reason[ing], and user agency", introduced "at critical points" of a human–AI interaction against automation bias, lists the mechanisms proposed in HCI (prosocial friction and speed bumps after Frischmann and Vardi, seamful and slow design, programmed inefficiencies, cognitive forcing functions, AI provocations), and sets an agenda for calibrating friction against cognitive load. It reports no data. Its friction is the inefficiency (delay) sense that [TERM-tmp91lqy](../terms.d/TERM-tmp91lqy.md) sets aside, located in a single user's interaction, not in a process between parties, so it does not bear on [CLAIM-tmpuiaop](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpuiaop.md)'s institutional claim except as a neighbouring sense. The exchange (A21 §2, A23 §7) describes it accurately and rightly classes it as cognitive friction. The evidence it relies on, Buçinca et al. 2021, is held ([LIT-2283](LIT-2283.md), [THEORY-1765](../theory.d/THEORY-1765.md)). It cites Frischmann and Benesch, "Friction-in-design regulation" (Yale J.L. & Tech. 25, 2023), not held, as a likelier source for regulatory friction. See the NOTE and THEORY filed from this reading.
