# NOTE-tmpbzli4: Davis and Weinstein — A search for multiple equilibria in urban industrial structure, read in part from the NBER working paper

## Contribution

The 2002 paper ([LIT-tmpntaj3](../literature.d/LIT-tmpntaj3.md)) asked whether the bombing data reject a
unique stable equilibrium and whether they meet "a stated condition that
would be sufficient to establish multiple equilibria in city population
shares" (p. 2); both answers were no. This paper adds a test that "no
longer requires that we treat unique equilibrium as the null" (p. 3), so
that multiple equilibria get a fair chance, and extends the data from
population to aggregate city manufacturing and eight industries, the
level at which physical geography constrains least.

## Key insight

In two-period growth space a temporary shock and its recovery separate the
two models cleanly. With one equilibrium, "Whatever happens in the period
of the shock is precisely undone in the period of the recovery", so
observations lie on one line of slope −1 through the origin. With several,
shocks past a threshold land on parallel slope −1 lines whose intercepts
are the displacement to the new equilibrium (pp. 7–8). A test therefore
needs shocks large enough to cross a threshold and "clearly temporary so
that we can see that we fail to return to the status quo ante" (p. 2):
"Small shocks mimic the effects of a globally stable equilibrium" (p. 6).

## Assumptions

- **Own-size dynamics.** Each city's (or city-industry's) dynamics depend
  on its own share alone, independent of other cities; the authors call
  this "clearly an unrealistic assumption" (p. 42).
- **Common thresholds.** Thresholds are the same in log-share units across
  cities and across industries (p. 9).
- **ρ = 0 in the threshold regressions.** Full reversion within the window
  is imposed; the authors argue that a positive ρ would bias the test
  towards finding multiple equilibria, which strengthens their rejection
  (p. 35).
- **Marshallian expectations** (footnote 5): expectations are not
  modelled, so coordination by expectations is not tested.
- **Data.** City manufacturing for 114 cities (98 in the production
  regressions), 1932, 1938, 1948 and up to 1969; eight industries; shocks
  instrumented with deaths and building destruction, interacted with
  industry for the industry runs.

## Key results

- **The verdict (abstract).** "The data provide no support for the
  existence of multiple equilibria. In the aftermath even of immense
  shocks, a city typically recovers not only its population and its share
  of aggregate manufacturing, but even the specific industries it had
  before." And: "The data prefer a model with a unique stable
  equilibrium" (p. 3).
- **Intercept ordering criterion (p. 33).** A multiple-equilibria fit is
  admissible only if bigger negative shocks lead to lower equilibria and
  thresholds lie between equilibria.
- **Population (pp. 32–33).** The IV replicates 2002; the Schwarz
  criterion slightly favours two equilibria, but the low intercept has the
  wrong sign: "Following enormous shocks there is no evidence that cities
  unravel further or fail to return to their former size."
- **Aggregate manufacturing (pp. 34–35).** "the Schwarz criterion clearly
  favors the model of one equilibrium"; the IV coefficient on wartime
  growth is indistinguishable from −1 (ρ point estimate 0.26, not
  significant).
- **Industries (pp. 37–39).** "the Schwarz criterion prefers the model in
  which there is a unique equilibrium", so "even shocks to particular
  industries within those cities were reversed". The best two-equilibrium
  fit puts a threshold at −0.67 log units, about a 93% fall in output, but
  "the point estimate violates the intercept-ordering criterion".
  "Our parameter estimate for ρ -1 is 0.655 (i.e. ρ is 0.34)", perhaps
  because "places hit extremely hard take longer to recover"; dropping
  Hiroshima makes ρ indistinguishable from zero, so "we cannot reject the
  hypothesis that industries hit with non-nuclear ordnance returned to
  their prewar locations after 24 years".
- **Reconstruction spending.** Not significant for population twenty
  years on (p. 32); correct sign and significant at 10% for manufacturing
  (p. 34); no effect for industries (p. 37).
- **Policy (pp. 4, 41).** "Our results provide a strong caution against
  the idea that one may use limited and temporary interventions to select
  equilibria with large and permanent effects on city development."

## Concepts

- **Spatial catastrophe and hysteresis (pp. 6–7).** "Even if the initial
  shock is only temporary, once a threshold is passed, the change in
  equilibrium will be permanent."
- **Necessary against sufficient conditions.** Failing a necessary
  condition is the stronger rejection.
- **Intercept ordering.** The admissibility condition on a fitted
  multiple-equilibria model.
- **Prior pattern as focal point (p. 43).** The authors' own alternative
  account of the recovery: land, claims to land, a surviving labour force
  and infrastructure could make "the prior pattern of economic activity"
  act "as a focal point for reconstruction".

## Connections

It completes [LIT-tmpntaj3](../literature.d/LIT-tmpntaj3.md) and answers that paper's footnote 20 for
industry mix. It is the strongest contrast to Juhász's persistent
temporary protection ([LIT-1489](../literature.d/LIT-1489.md), [THEORY-919](../theory.d/THEORY-919.md)) and to Bleakley and Lin's
portage cities ([LIT-tmppaj4l](../literature.d/LIT-tmppaj4l.md)), who read Japanese reversion as a product of
its large natural heterogeneity. Kline and Moretti ([LIT-2141](../literature.d/LIT-2141.md), [THEORY-1461](../theory.d/THEORY-1461.md))
reach the same unique-equilibrium verdict by a different route, a constant
agglomeration elasticity, for a positive support. Balboni et al.
([THEORY-1465](../theory.d/THEORY-1465.md)) find the threshold at household level that this paper does
not find at the level of cities and industries.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-604](../claims.d/CLAIM-604.md) (ignition threshold, collapse, hysteresis): qualifies,
  strongly.** A direct field test of the threshold signature, with shocks
  as large as any on record, finds none for cities, manufacturing or
  industries; the one candidate threshold (−0.67 log units) fails the
  ordering criterion. The model is not contradicted, since thresholds may
  lie beyond these shocks or vary across cities, which the test assumes
  away; its empirical reach is narrowed.
- **[CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md) (when support is safe to withdraw): supports the caution,
  sharpens the unit clause.** Recovery holds at three levels of
  aggregation, and is slowest at the finest level and the hardest hit,
  so the unit whose persistence is measured changes the answer's speed if
  not its sign. The authors' caveat that greenfield investment is "a
  different question" marks where the result stops.
- **[CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md) (supplied upkeep never becomes optional): by analogy.** If
  the prior pattern is a focal point kept by surviving land, people and
  infrastructure, the place's state is held by stocks it was not
  stripped of.
- **[CLAIM-602](../claims.d/CLAIM-602.md): none.** **[QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md): bears, as for [LIT-tmpntaj3](../literature.d/LIT-tmpntaj3.md).** The
  long-run state is independent of the shock at every level tested.

## Limitations

- **Read in part.** §III, the tables and the concentrated-industry run
  were not read in full.
- **Restrictive structure.** Own-size dynamics and common thresholds are
  the authors' admitted simplifications (pp. 9, 42).
- **Power.** "we do not have enough observations to conduct tests of
  multiple equilibria for individual industries that have power to
  distinguish between hypotheses" (p. 39).
- **Not the version of record.** The 2008 journal text may differ.
- **Recovery, not founding.** The experiment asks whether established
  cities and industries survive shocks, not whether a new pattern can be
  made by investment (p. 43).

## Open questions

Whether thresholds that vary by city, or depend on neighbours, would
change the verdict; and whether greenfield support behaves as the bombing
shocks did.
