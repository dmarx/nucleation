# LIT-tmpntsdw: The evolution of color naming reflects pressure for efficiency: Evidence from the recent past

Noga Zaslavsky, Karee Garvin, Charles Kemp, Naftali Tishby and Terry Regier
(2022), *Journal of Language Evolution* 7(2), 184–199 —
doi:10.1093/jole/lzac001 (preprint bioRxiv 10.1101/2021.11.03.467047)

## Key takeaways

- Unread. From the abstract of its precursor (LIT-tmpom3sr): Nafaanra's
  three 1978 terms "are still used but they now name smaller categories",
  beside new terms for roughly yellow, green, blue and purple, and the
  later system, like the earlier, lies near the efficiency bound.

## Standing in the record

Registered by LEAD-056 for THEORY-159's promote_when and CLAIM-082's
defeat condition: a term added to a language's colour inventory, with the
older terms' extensions measured before and after. Of the works found, it
is the closest to the design, short of a control set of terms outside the
field. The 2019 abstract LIT-tmpom3sr stands in for it only as far as the
abstract goes: the inventory change and that the old categories shrank,
with no figures, method or speaker counts. Malik-Moraleda et al.
(LIT-tmpyky5z) caution that many speakers also spoke English by then, so
contact may explain the change as well as efficiency.
