# LIT-1291: The Immanent Rationality of Copyright Law

Balganesh, S. (2017). The Immanent Rationality of Copyright Law [Review of *What's Wrong with Copying?*, by A. Drassinower]. *Michigan Law Review*, 115(6), 1047–1069.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1017](../notes.d/NOTE-1017.md).*

- **Drassinower's thesis, as reported.** Copyright protects "the integrity
  of the work as a communicative act" (p. 1051, quoting the book at 8). The
  right inheres in persons as speakers. Infringement is the communicative
  reproduction of a work, wrong as "compelled speech": the defendant speaks
  for the author without consent (p. 1053).
- **The derivation.** Independent creation (speaking in one's own words)
  anchors originality and the defence alike, and implies the equal right of
  every other author. The idea/expression line, transformative use, *Baker
  v. Selden*, the public domain and the line between limitations and
  exceptions are derived from it (pp. 1051–1054).
- **A defence of rationalism.** Against Lemley, a theory that cannot be
  tested empirically is not therefore faith-based. Demonstrability is not
  empirical verifiability, and welfarism's own premise is untestable too
  (pp. 1056–1061).
- **Four objections.** No criterion of what is fundamental, so coherence
  may decide essentiality (fixation is omitted). Explanation and
  justification are run together without a defence of autonomy. The
  private-law structure of enforcement is assumed and not explained. The
  rationality is taken as static and pervasive, so courts' consequentialist
  reasoning goes unexplained (pp. 1055–1068).
