# CASE-388: A proposed study: project proposals of independently rated promise and matched requirements, run through centralized review, translation support, protected exploration and distributed evaluation, comparing the cost and time to a meaningful evaluation, approval, implementation, and whether unfamiliar but valuable classes of proposal survive long enough to produce evidence

## The design

A56 §8: "Take project proposals with independently evaluated technical
promise and matched implementation requirements." Present them under four
arrangements:

| Arrangement | Legibility resources | Authorization |
|---|---|---|
| Centralized review | Fixed managerial categories | Prior approval required |
| Translation support | Added resources for making proposals understandable | Prior approval required |
| Protected exploration | Limited resources for experiments before full justification | Staged approval |
| Distributed evaluation | Several qualified evaluative perspectives | Local exploration, broader review for consequential action |

Measure the cost and time to a meaningful evaluation, the approval rate, the
rate of successful implementation, and whether unfamiliar but valuable
classes of proposal survive long enough to produce evidence. The comparison
that matters is whether the arrangements differ in their capacity to
"discover and realize valuable possibilities not already well represented by
the dominant evaluative framework". A56's caution: "We would need
independently defined success criteria, otherwise the governance arrangement
could simply reward proposals that fit its own categories and declare itself
successful."

## What it would show

Translation support against centralized review isolates who pays for
legibility ([CLAIM-1117](../claims.d/CLAIM-1117.md)). Protected exploration against both isolates the
circle between evidence and permission ([CLAIM-1140](../claims.d/CLAIM-1140.md)). Distributed
evaluation tests the alternative regimes ([CLAIM-1119](../claims.d/CLAIM-1119.md)) and the thesis
([CLAIM-1132](../claims.d/CLAIM-1132.md)).

## Note of 2026-11-23: what the design leaves open

<!-- inactive-ok-block: CLAIM-1016 — Proposed; filed with this -->
Three things the design needs and A56 does not supply. (1) Where the
"independently evaluated technical promise" comes from: a panel rating
proposals before the arrangement acts is itself an evaluative framework, so
it should be drawn from outside the dominant one, or the promise should be
scored after the fact by realized outcome on proposals run under every
arrangement. (2) The unit: an organization cannot run four arrangements on
one proposal, so either proposals are randomized across units, or the study
is run in a simulation whose proposals carry a hidden true value, which makes
the success criterion independent by construction. (3) Protected
exploration and distributed evaluation change the unit of authorization from
a question to a domain ([CLAIM-1016](../claims.d/CLAIM-1016.md)), so their effect should be read as
that change, not as lower cost per proposal; a fifth arm with standing
budgets and centralized review would separate the two. The note is the
assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it is the owner's call.

## Note of 2026-11-24: the exchange 'Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity', A27, the manuscript's Study C

The manuscript ChatGPT drafted at U20 ("Becoming Through One Another",
§11, Study C, "Inquiry under contractual constraints") says it extends
this case, which it cites by its code on main. It adds four design conditions this
case does not state: match "the cost of maintaining standing grants" in
the compared budgets; measure "ability to recognize independently
specified novel discrepancies"; "Include tasks in which novel inquiries
are genuinely unhelpful, so that indiscriminate authorization is not
rewarded"; and "Independently evaluate the adequacy of answers and
consequential action". The third is a control the case needs: without it,
a regime that authorizes everything wins by construction. An extension of
the design, proposed and not run. The wording of this note is the assistant's (Claude, in this session), reporting A's (ChatGPT's) text; adopting what it reports is the owner's call.
