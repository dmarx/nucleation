# NOTE-1815: Shumailov et al. — AI models collapse when trained on recursively generated data

## Contribution

- A definition of model collapse and a taxonomy of its errors.
- Proofs of collapse in two minimal settings with full replacement.
- An LLM fine-tuning demonstration.
- A policy argument for preserving original data and tracking provenance.

## Key results

- **Abstract:** "We find that indiscriminate use of model-generated content in training causes irreversible defects in the resulting models, in which tails of the original content distribution disappear."
- **Definition 2.1:** collapse is a process "in which the data they generate end up polluting the training set of the next generation".
- **Theoretical intuition** (scope of the proofs): "For the mathematical models to come, we consider βi = γi = 0, that is, data only from a single step are used".
- **LM section, on the 10% retention setting:** "We find that preservation of the original data allows for better model fine-tuning and leads to only minor degradation of performance."
- **LM section, a caveat:** "complete collapse may or may not occur, even after a small number of steps".
- **Discussion:** "it is unclear how content generated by LLMs can be tracked at scale".

## Assumptions

- The theory covers the replacement regime only; data from earlier generations or originals are not retained in the proofs.
- Training on generated data is "indiscriminate", with no curation or filtering.
- The experiments use small models (OPT-125m) and fine-tuning rather than pre-training.

## Concepts

- model collapse, early vs late
- statistical, expressivity and approximation errors
- learning with generational data
- tail loss
- absorbing states
- data provenance
- "first mover advantage"

## Bearing on the record

The LIT says A cites this work for compounding errors when generations train on predecessors' outputs and treats it as a caution. A also says the paper does not show a record will collapse. All of that is accurate.

Three refinements:
1. The paper's proofs assume full replacement of data each generation.
2. Its own LM experiment shows that retaining 10% of the original data mostly prevents degradation.
3. The paper does not address curated, selected or evaluated inheritance. Those are exactly the conditions a governed record would add.

A's proposed record analogues are A's own extrapolations: loss of source diversity, derivative claims cited as independent evidence, narrowing categories. The paper discusses none of them. Its nearest point is tail loss, the disappearance of rare events.

- **[QUESTION-457](../questions.d/QUESTION-457.md)** (recursive improvement of a collective's inquiry without losing failure detection): **qualifies.**
  - It supplies a mechanism by which recursive self-training loses rare cases. An inheriting collective would need to guard against that.
  - The mechanism depends on replacement and on the absence of access to originals. A record that keeps sources and cites them limits the analogy.
  - The paper gives no evidence about records or collectives.
- **[LIT-971](../literature.d/LIT-971.md)** (Gerstgrasser et al., held): **qualified by it, and the two should be read together.**
  - Gerstgrasser et al. show that accumulation rather than replacement avoids collapse.
  - Shumailov et al.'s own 10%-retention arm points the same way.
  - Together they give the condition under which the caution applies.

## Limitations

- The proofs use toy settings with full replacement.
- The LLM evidence comes from one small model on one dataset, over a finite number of generations.
- The Supplementary Information was not read, so the VAE and GMM evidence is unverified here.
