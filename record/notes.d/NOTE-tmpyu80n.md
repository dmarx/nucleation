# NOTE-tmpyu80n: Moerman, Schraagen, Braaksma and van Dongen — Graceful extensibility in asset management: extending the capacity to adapt in managing cyber-physical railway systems

## Key results

1. Definition (abstract): graceful extensibility is "the ability of a system to extend its capacity to adapt when surprise events challenge its boundaries".
2. Structure (§1.1): "The theory of GE is presented as 10 proto-theorem (S1–S10) divided into three subsets"; subset C "captures how constraints, such as perspective bounds and mis-calibration of adaptive capacity, can be addressed".
3. Woods 2015 (§1): four senses of resilience, "rebound, robustness, graceful extensibility, and architectures for sustained adaptability".
4. Brittleness (§1): "Brittleness is defined as the rapidity of a system's performance decline when it nears or reaches one or more boundary."
5. Surprise (§1, quoting Woods 2018 p. 6): "surprise is model surprise where base adaptive capacity represents a partial model of fitness".
6. Maladaptation patterns (§1.1, Woods and Branlat 2011): "decompensation (lack of capacity to adapt when disturbances cascade), working at cross-purposes", and "getting stuck in outdated behaviors when relying on past successes".
7. Finding (abstract): "the majority of theoretical patterns postulated by the theory are corroborated by the data"; but for S1 "the concept of CfM was not observed".

## Assumptions

Single historical case; qualitative coding of documents; pattern matching aims at gross matches;
the theory's statements are taken as Moerman et al. operationalise them.

## Concepts

Graceful extensibility; brittleness; Capacity for Maneuver; saturation; units of adaptive
behaviour (UABs); base vs extended adaptive capacity; mis-calibration; sustained adaptability;
decompensation; working at cross-purposes; getting stuck in outdated behaviours.

## Bearing on the record

It **restates** Woods's definition, the ten proto-theorems' headline statements and grouping,
the Capacity-for-Maneuver concept, the 2015 four senses of resilience, and Woods's definition of
surprise as model surprise, with page citations to Woods (2018). It **does not restate** Woods's
derivations from his two assumptions (finite resources, continuing change), his arguments for
each proto-theorem, or his examples. It is a test of the theory on one case, not an exposition.
