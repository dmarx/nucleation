# NOTE-tmp9fq2y: Belot — Quantum states for primitive ontologists

## Contribution

On primitive-ontology approaches, such as some Bohmian and spontaneous-collapse theories, describing a quantum system's history attributes properties to regions of spacetime. This promises an easy link to ordinary macroscopic bodies. But these approaches are dualistic, positing a quantum state alongside the material degrees of freedom. Belot sets out and compares the primitive ontologist's options for understanding that quantum state.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 (pp. 1–3): separates the measurement problem from the "macro-object problem" (truth conditions for ordinary properties, illustrated by Chester the walrus). The primitive ontology's material degrees of freedom are not dynamically autonomous without ψ.
- §2.1–2.2 (pp. 3–5): the Bohmian formalism, and three puzzles: the quantum state, the particle degrees of freedom, and the role of |ψ|² as typicality.
- ψ as object (pp. 5–7): ψ is unlike a field. It does no conservation work, has no back-reaction and transforms oddly under boosts. A field on configuration space raises the question of why the world looks three-dimensional; the multi-field on 3-space (Forrest) is the alternative.
- ψ as law (pp. 7–9): the analogy with the Hamiltonian is weakened because ψ is time-dependent, and the view needs grades of necessity. He compares these with the Past Hypothesis in statistical mechanics.
- ψ as property / dispositional history (pp. 10–13): a particle in a box gives infinitely many inequivalent energy eigenstates φn with the same dispositional history (at rest everywhere). This is "dismaying", but the view can bite the bullet by denying determinate facts about energy and typicality in such cases.
- §3 "Inconclusive Conclusion" (pp. 13–14): of the four options, orthodox quasi-Quinean standards favour the multi-field view, but Belot's "heresy" (van Fraassen) values keeping other well-motivated interpretations alive.

## Open questions

- A careful comparative case study for the wave-function cluster, closely paralleling Chen's taxonomy ([LIT-tmpwqkvr](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwqkvr.md)). His objections to ψ-as-law are ones Allori ([LIT-tmpvtra0](../literature.d/LIT-tmpvtra0.md)) must answer.
- The particle-in-a-box counterexample to dispositional readings bears on Dorato's powers-of-events view ([LIT-tmpnyiy7](../literature.d/LIT-tmpnyiy7.md)) and on Esfeld's holistic dispositions.
- His closing remark on the aims of interpretation (truth versus understanding) is a methodological point worth recording.
