# QUESTION-309: Within one design, do contingent if-then plans outperform schedule plans for the same goal, sample and outcome, does recording the plan weaken it, and does rehearsal work through the accessibility of the cue or the strength of the cue–response link?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-908](../theory.d/THEORY-908.md) holds that, between tests, contingent if-then plans had larger
effects than schedule plans, rehearsed plans larger effects than plans not
rehearsed, and recorded plans smaller ones. Contingent and schedule plans
come from different studies, outcomes and settings, and the authors' reading
of the recording cost, deliberation undermining automaticity, was not
measured. No within-study comparison was pooled.

## What would answer it

An experiment that randomly assigns contingent against schedule plans for
the same goal, sample and outcome, and recording against not recording the
plan, finding the contingent advantage and the recording cost within one
design; and a test that rehearsal works through cue accessibility or
cue–response link strength. The differences are refuted as effects of the
plan if within-study comparisons show no format difference, or if the
recording cost disappears once outcome type is held fixed. More
between-test moderator analyses cannot answer it.
