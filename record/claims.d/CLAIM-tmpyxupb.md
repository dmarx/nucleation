# CLAIM-tmpyxupb: Removing a constraint on an organization's members does not move it along one scale of more or less governance: it changes a profile of who holds rule-making, monitoring, enforcement, appeal and revision, in which the authority the constraint held can be eliminated, distributed or transferred, and the members' first-order options and their share in revising the conditions of their action can move in opposite directions

## The claim

The owner, at U18 of the exchange of 2026-10-20: "what does this suggest
about e.g. "deregulation" as an ideological end?" A18's headline:
"**Deregulation can reduce the constraints imposed by one governing
authority without reducing the extent to which behavior is governed.**"
Removing a constraint changes what is possible, "But the resulting
behavior is still governed by something: contracts, property rights,
bargaining power, prices, organizational hierarchies, network effects,
technical standards, or private enforcement arrangements." So the
question to ask of a removal is A18 §3's: "*Does the proposed change
eliminate an exercise of authority, distribute that authority, or
transfer it to another locus?*"

Two things follow.

- **A profile, not a scale.** A18 §7 treats a regulatory change as a
  transformation of "a **governance profile**: G = (rule-making,
  monitoring, enforcement, appeal, revision). For each component, identify
  who holds authority, over whom, and under which conditions. A reform can
  then change the profile in several directions simultaneously. This is
  more informative than assuming a single scale from regulated to
  unregulated." The record already holds a smaller profile of the same
  kind. [TERM-017](../terms.d/TERM-017.md)'s precedence, jurisdiction and suspension "can vary
  independently" ([CLAIM-057](CLAIM-057.md), Active, demonstrated by the Northstar
  variants): "a formally absolute rule can be weak because another process
  controls entry into its domain."
- **Two freedoms that come apart.** A18 §6: "First-order freedom": "What
  am I permitted or able to do?"; "Second-order self-governance": "Who
  determines and revises the conditions under which I can act?" "Neither
  logically entails the other." Removing a public restriction "might
  expand everyone's legal choice set while leaving an affected group
  dependent on the decisions of a single private intermediary.
  Alternatively, removing an externally imposed restriction might allow a
  community to construct its own governing institution." This is
  [CLAIM-086](CLAIM-086.md) (Active): "A constraint may govern conduct without governing
  the conditions under which it remains authoritative." Removing the
  constraint can change either, and not necessarily the same way.

Why a removed constraint's authority need not vanish: [CLAIM-282](CLAIM-282.md) (Active,
demonstrated) shows that forbidding one route of self-amplification does
not stop authority circulating through others. A18 §3: "eliminating a
visible channel of concentrated authority does not guarantee that the
resulting governance arrangement has no concentrations of authority."

The readings the record holds give one instance of each direction. In
[THEORY-149](../theory.d/THEORY-149.md), Ostrom's "recognised rights to self-organise", removing an
outside authority's rule-making can add to the members' authority over
revision, which is A18's positive sense: "**Deregulation can mean enabling
an organization or community to govern its own activity rather than having
the governing rules specified elsewhere.**" In [THEORY-470](../theory.d/THEORY-470.md), Doctorow's
reading (Proposed, argued from examples), the weakening of regulation
among other constraints releases a platform's power over its users: the
authority moves rather than disappears. [THEORY-429](../theory.d/THEORY-429.md) (North) is the nearest
held reading on institutions as constraints.

The idea recurs. A19 §6 restated it as "first-order and second-order
regulation", the second changing "the feedback mechanisms through which
particular distributions of authority are sustained or destabilized". A20
§6, of capture: "The defining feature is not the number of rules." A18
§8's verdict: "**The absence of an external governor is not equivalent to
the presence of self-governance.**"

A18 §2 gave a strategic illustration, checked here: in a prisoner's
dilemma with payoffs (3,3), (0,5), (5,0) and (1,1), a rule holding both to
cooperation gives (3,3), and removing it makes defection dominant and
(1,1) the unique equilibrium. "Expanded individual action sets ⇏
Pareto-improved equilibrium outcomes." That is [CLAIM-354](CLAIM-354.md)'s
non-monotonicity of options and autonomy, in a two-agent form.

## What it does not say

- **Nothing about whether any deregulation is good.** A18: "This doesn't
  establish that deregulation is good or bad." Nor that private
  governance is the same as public regulation. A18: "A commitment
  specifically to limiting state coercion can be a coherent political
  value in its own right. It does not require assuming that all forms of
  private governance are equivalent to state regulation."
- **Nothing about any actual reform.** A18's caption on its two outcomes:
  "These are possible outcomes, not an exhaustive dichotomy or an
  empirical prediction about a particular reform." Which way a given
  removal moved the profile is a question of evidence about that case,
  which the record does not hold.
- **Not that second-order authority is a fair process.** Authority over
  revision is Sen's process aspect only in part, and not its fairness
  ([CLAIM-352](CLAIM-352.md)).
- **Not the governance-response profile.** [TERM-tmpf5k7x](../terms.d/TERM-tmpf5k7x.md) is about whether
  a description preserves how a system responds when its governance is
  changed. The profile here says who holds each governing function. They
  share a word and are different objects.
- **Not that the analogy to will-organization is an identity.** A18 applied
  [TERM-023](../terms.d/TERM-023.md) "analogically to institutions". The claim rests on the
  institutional results above ([CLAIM-086](CLAIM-086.md), [CLAIM-057](CLAIM-057.md), [CLAIM-282](CLAIM-282.md)) and not on
  the analogy.
