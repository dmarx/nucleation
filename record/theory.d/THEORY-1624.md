# THEORY-1624: Doris, Stich, Walmsley and Schulz report that Batson's experiments tell against the social-punishment and aversive-arousal versions of egoism, argue that self–other merging would make helping egoistic only as perceived numerical identity, which none of the four merging measures in the 1997 exchange tests, and hold that psychological altruism is not yet established

## The account

Altruism is an ultimate desire for another's well-being, and egoism
holds every ultimate desire self-interested; the biological sense is
neither entailed nor excluded. Batson's experiments make the
social-punishment and aversive-arousal versions of egoism much less
plausible. On self–other merging, in [NOTE-1274](../notes.d/NOTE-1274.md)'s gloss of §5.3, "The relevant
identity is numerical, not qualitative": helping would be egoistic
only if the helper took herself to be numerically identical with the
other. Perceived similarity and trait overlap measure likeness, and "It
is far from clear what the other two tests [IOS and use of 'we'] are
measuring" (§5.3). Principlism, norm compliance, divine punishment and
habitual helping remain open, so altruism is not yet established.

## Evidence

Secondary ([NOTE-1274](../notes.d/NOTE-1274.md)). The Janet and Elaine studies are given with
their table values; McAuliffe et al. (2018, N = 680, preregistered) were
"unambiguously supportive" of the empathy–helping link. The 1997 reply
is reported as "a pair of experiments", against "four" elsewhere in the
record, with neither problems nor results named.

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-300](../questions.d/QUESTION-300.md) keeps the question open on Batson et al.'s report "that
empathy-induced helping survives control for self–other merging", and
asks for a criterion of the self's extent apart from caring. The entry
is the record's only independent report of that outcome, and its
argument says the merging measures in the exchange do not fix the
self's extent at all. [CLAIM-494](../claims.d/CLAIM-494.md) grounds on the work itself.
