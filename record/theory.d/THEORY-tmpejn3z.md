# THEORY-tmpejn3z: An agent can be morally responsible for what she did although she could not have done otherwise, when what made the action unavoidable played no part in bringing it about, so the principle of alternate possibilities is false

## The evidence, and what is unread

The argument is read first-hand: Frankfurt's "Alternate Possibilities and
Moral Responsibility" ([LIT-tmpwts2i](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwts2i.md), [NOTE-tmpour9o](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpour9o.md)), and the willing addict
of "Freedom of the Will and the Concept of a Person" ([LIT-tmpuutyy](../literature.d/LIT-tmpuutyy.md),
[NOTE-tmpnpe9o](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpnpe9o.md)). **The objections and replies are not**: Widerker, Kane,
Ginet, Fischer, Pereboom, Mele and Robb, Hunt, and Frankfurt's own later
replies (1983, 2003) are held only through Robb's SEP entry ([LIT-294](../literature.d/LIT-294.md),
[NOTE-269](../notes.d/NOTE-269.md)), McKenna and Coates ([LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md), [NOTE-270](../notes.d/NOTE-270.md)) and Talbert ([LIT-tmppa1o1](../literature.d/LIT-tmppa1o1.md),
[NOTE-tmpzy6ay](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpzy6ay.md)). The owner authorised filing it on this evidence.

## What the sources show

**The argument.** [LIT-tmpwts2i](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwts2i.md) separates what makes an action unavoidable
from what brings it about. Black, who "prefers to avoid showing his hand",
will intervene only if Jones4 is about to decide otherwise, by any means a
reader thinks sufficient, up to manipulating "the minute processes of
Jones4's brain". Jones4 decides "for reasons of his own" and Black never
acts. Jones4 "will bear precisely the same moral responsibility … as he
would have borne if Black had not been ready", and it would be "quite
unreasonable to excuse Jones4 … or to withhold the praise to which it would
normally entitle him" (pp. 835–836). The general premise is that a fact
which "could have been subtracted from the situation without affecting what
happened or why it happened" has no weight in assessing responsibility (p.
837). Frankfurt's later recipe, quoted by Robb ([LIT-294](../literature.d/LIT-294.md), §3.4.3, from 1983:
322), states the same distinction: "circumstances which make it inevitable
that a person will perform some action but which do not bring it about that
he performs it". Talbert ([LIT-tmppa1o1](../literature.d/LIT-tmppa1o1.md), §1) quotes the 2006 gloss: "making
an action unavoidable is not the same thing as bringing it about that the
action is performed".

**The replacement.** [LIT-tmpwts2i](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwts2i.md) proposes that "a person is not morally
responsible for what he has done if he did it only because he could not
have done otherwise", and says it "does not appear to conflict" with
compatibilism (pp. 838–839). It rejects the intermediate revision
("because", without "only") because, on the incompatibilist reading of
"could have done otherwise", it would excuse every determined act (p. 838).

**A second route.** [LIT-tmpuutyy](../literature.d/LIT-tmpuutyy.md)'s willing addict cannot do otherwise,
since his desire "will be effective regardless", but takes the drug "of his
own free will" and "may be morally responsible" (pp. 19–20). Robb
([LIT-294](../literature.d/LIT-294.md), §3.2) reports it as a counterexample and limits it: it is really
overdetermination, with a responsibility-undermining factor present.

**The objections, as Robb reports them** ([LIT-294](../literature.d/LIT-294.md), [NOTE-269](../notes.d/NOTE-269.md)).

- **The dilemma defence** (§4.3; Kane, Widerker, Ginet, Wyma). Black needs a
  prior sign. Either the sign determines the decision, and the libertarian
  will not grant that Jones is responsible, or it does not, and Jones keeps a
  robust alternative. The replies are Fischer's on the first horn, that a
  causally idle factor cannot be what makes the agent not responsible
  (1999: 113, cf. Frankfurt 2003), and, on the second, Pereboom's buffer
  case, Hunt's blockage and Mele and Robb (1998), against which the
  critics say blockage collapses into determination.
- **Prior signs.** The sign is in the original case: a twitch that precedes
  deciding A and never B, which Frankfurt says "does not beg the question
  of determinism" and does not require that the earlier phases be
  "deterministically related to still earlier events" ([LIT-tmpwts2i](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwts2i.md), n. 3).
  That is the point the dilemma presses: the footnote leaves open whether
  the twitch determines the decision, and the dilemma says either answer
  defeats the case.
- **Flickers of freedom** (§4.2.2). Jones could still have shown the sign,
  or refrained on his own; Frankfurt concedes it is "in a way up to him
  whether he acts on his own or as a result of Black's intervention"
  ([LIT-tmpwts2i](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwts2i.md), p. 836). Fischer's reply is that such alternatives are not
  "robust" (1994: 140–147); the counter-replies are that a flicker may mark
  indeterminism, or that refraining is itself a doing.
- **General abilities** (§4.2.1; [LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md), §4.1.5). The new
  dispositionalists say a Frankfurt agent keeps the general ability to do
  otherwise, so PAP-general survives.
- **The case is too unusual** (§4.1). PAP-ordinary restricts the principle
  to ordinary circumstances. The reply is that some Frankfurt cases are
  ordinary, and that one coherent case shows the tie contingent.
  Frankfurt himself lets Black be "a machine" or "natural forces involving
  no will or design at all" ([LIT-tmpwts2i](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwts2i.md), n. 4).

Robb's assessment is that "not much consensus" has emerged (§5). McKenna and
Coates say "No clear victor has emerged" ([LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md), §3.2).

## What this does not say

- **It does not say determinism is compatible with responsibility.** It
  removes one premise of the classical incompatibilist argument. Robb (§5.1)
  and Talbert (§1) report that determinism may still threaten
  responsibility by the way it actually produces action.
- **It does not say what responsibility does require.** Frankfurt's own
  replacement is "only because"; Fischer and Ravizza's is guidance control
  ([THEORY-tmpmjijs](THEORY-tmpmjijs.md)); the hierarchical account is [THEORY-tmpev5mk](THEORY-tmpev5mk.md).
- **It does not cover volitional necessity on its own.** Robb (§3.3) cites
  Frankfurt's 1982 essay ([LIT-tmp3tj26](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3tj26.md)) for cases like Luther's, and
  reports that tracing restores PAP for them. This account rests on the
  counterfactual-intervener cases, not on those.
- **It does not settle the dilemma.** The record reads the defence and
  every reply only in Robb's summary.

## Relations

- **`rivals` [THEORY-tmp6tk5g](THEORY-tmp6tk5g.md) (Wolf's asymmetry).** Wolf, as McKenna and
  Coates report her ([LIT-291](../literature.d/LIT-291.md), §4.3), holds that praise needs no ability to
  do otherwise but blame does, the ability to have acted on the right
  reasons. This account says neither needs it. On praise they agree. On
  blame they cannot both be right: Frankfurt states his case for both
  excusing and withholding praise ([LIT-tmpwts2i](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwts2i.md), p. 836), and his action is
  neither good nor bad, so a Frankfurt case of wrongdoing is blameworthy
  here and excused on Wolf's view unless she has an answer the record has
  not read. Neither yet corrects the other.
- **Fischer and Ravizza ([THEORY-tmpmjijs](THEORY-tmpmjijs.md)) build on this.** Their account
  says responsibility requires guidance control "and not regulative control
  over alternative possibilities", and its Frankfurt-case basis is this
  argument (their sources include [LIT-294](../literature.d/LIT-294.md) for it). That is `extends` from
  their side. It is not declared here, because their file would hold it
  without a sentence explaining it; the coordinator should add it there.
- **[THEORY-tmpev5mk](THEORY-tmpev5mk.md): no relation.** The 1971 willing addict serves both, but
  the hierarchical account is about what makes a motive one's own, and
  this one is about alternatives.
