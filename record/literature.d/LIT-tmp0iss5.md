# LIT-tmp0iss5: Moving away from lexicalism in psycho- and neuro-linguistics

Alexandra Krauska, Ellen Lau (2023), *Frontiers in Language Sciences 2, 1125127 (Hypothesis and Theory article)* — DOI-10.3389/flang.2023.1125127

## Key takeaways

- Standard production and comprehension models built on "lemmas" (stored triads of meaning, syntax and form, with syntax stopping at the word) inherit a lexicalist assumption that typology has refuted. Replacing that assumption needs new algorithms, not just new representations: separate meaning↔syntax and syntax↔form mappings with one integrated stage for retrieving and assembling syntactic structure. This predicts different aphasia profiles.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Mainstream psycho- and neurolinguistic models retrieve "lemmas" or "lexical items" and combine them. This assumes lexical items store meaning, syntax and form together, that lexical and syntactic processes are separate, and that syntax does not go below the word. Decades of cross-linguistic work contradict this: syntax operates both above and below the word, and meaning and form depend partly on syntactic context. The authors review the arguments against lexicalism, show how lexicalist assumptions shape production models, and list phenomena those models struggle with. They sketch a non-lexicalist production model without lemmas, using separate meaning–syntax and syntax–form mappings.
