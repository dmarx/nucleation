# LIT-1075: Deictic codes for the embodiment of cognition

Ballard, D. H., Hayhoe, M. M., Pook, P. K. and Rao, R. P. N. (1997). Deictic codes for the embodiment of cognition. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences* 20(4), 723–742. DOI 10.1017/S0140525X97001611

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-883](../notes.d/NOTE-883.md)). At about a third of a second, the time scale of a saccade, the article models fixation as a pointer that binds a variable in a behavioural program to a place in the world, so that what is bound is only the task-relevant feature, fetched just before use. In a block-copying task subjects looked back at the model about 1.3 times per block on average, the commonest cycle being two looks per block (one apparently for colour, one for position), and chose this slowest, lowest-memory strategy (about 3 s a block, against about 1.5 s for copying from memory) though told to be fast; separating model and copy by 70° cut looks to 1.0 per block, and one-colour patterns that could be chunked cut them to 0.7. The authors read this as working memory having a "carrying cost" that exceeds the cost of re-acquiring from the world, but that cost is inferred from the choices and never measured or modelled, and the evidence is one task with small samples (seven subjects for the main data, three for the colour-change experiment) and no statistics reported in the article. What is left in the world is unprocessed input, not a computed result, and the article argues against precomputation where the needed products cannot be foreseen.

## Why this is here

The owner, after the cockpit reading ([LIT-1066](LIT-1066.md)): "amortized retrieval is actually a kind of amortized computation. there's probably a clearer relationship here that I imagine has likely been clarified/formalized at some point in the 30 years since the cockpit essay was published." Registered with six others for [QUESTION-095](../questions.d/QUESTION-095.md).

The case for the world as a memory consulted when needed, with its costs: the alternative to the cockpit's precomputation.
