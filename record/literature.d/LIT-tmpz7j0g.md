# LIT-tmpz7j0g: Maximizing exposure therapy: An inhibitory learning approach

Craske, M. G., Treanor, M., Conway, C. C., Zbozinek, T. & Vervliet, B. (2014). Maximizing exposure therapy: An inhibitory learning approach. *Behaviour Research and Therapy* 58: 10–23. doi:10.1016/j.brat.2014.04.006.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpqrvri](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpqrvri.md). The manuscript has no page numbers;
locations are sections.*

- **Not erased.** "the original CS-US association learned during fear
  conditioning is not erased during extinction, but rather is left intact
  as new, secondary inhibitory learning about the CS-US develops"
  (Inhibitory Learning Model).
- **Fear reduction is not the index.** Fear reduction during exposure
  "is not predictive of the fear level expressed at follow-up assessment
  in fearful human samples" (Inhibitory Learning vs Habituation).
- **Mismatch is the lever.** "The more the expectancy can be violated by
  experience, the greater the inhibitory learning" (Expectancy
  Violation). Restructuring before exposure is held to shrink the
  mismatch, so cognitive work is moved to after it.
- **Safety signals: mixed evidence, firm advice.** "However, recent data
  have presented contradictory findings", yet "If willing, immediate
  removal of safety signals is preferred" (Removal of Safety Signals).
- **No data of its own.** The cases are "exemplary", and several
  strategies are flagged as unstable or needing "further evidence".

## Why this is here

The exchange cited it at turn U15 for exposure as new inhibitory learning that competes with the old association rather than erasing it, with expectancy violation and the removal of safety signals as its levers. The abstract matches.

**Corrected 2026-10-24, from the reading.** The description is right. Two
additions: the paper reports the evidence on removing safety signals as
contradictory, and its levers number eight or nine, listed differently in
the abstract, the body and Table 6. For [CLAIM-tmp8z857](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp8z857.md) it supplies the
clinical form of the identifiability clause (avoidance prevents
expectancy violation) and a rival mechanism for persistence that the claim
does not name: the old association survives and competes for retrieval.
It also predicts that correcting the stated belief *before* exposure
weakens the behavioural learning. Filed from it: [THEORY-tmpfun40](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpfun40.md) and
[THEORY-tmpxdq9s](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpxdq9s.md).
