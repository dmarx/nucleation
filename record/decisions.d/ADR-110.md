# ADR-110: A postmortem is due when a schema change has been used, a batch of pull requests closes, a bulk filing lands, a decision is revised soon after landing, a process incident is fixed, or the owner asks; triggers count use and sessions, not calendar time, and a starting policy expected to be superseded

## Context

[ADR-112](ADR-112.md) adds the POSTMORTEM scheme but says nothing about when one is
written. The owner asked for a policy: every pull request probably does
not need one, but batches might. The owner also expected this policy to
change, and asked for a reasonable one to start with.

A first draft used calendar time ("two weeks"). The owner pointed out that
this misjudged the record's pace, and git and the record bear that out.
The record began on 2026-09-25, about eight weeks before this decision,
and has about 547 pull requests (some ten a day) and about 110 decisions
(some two a day). Entries filed per day typically run from 100 to 350,
with peaks of 516 (11-05), 675 (11-12) and 524 (11-19); this clone's git
history, which starts on 2026-11-13, shows 16 pull requests merged on
11-17 and 12 with 124 commits on 11-19. The LEAD scheme ([ADR-109](ADR-109.md)) went
from decision to 197 entries in under a day. Two weeks is about 150 pull
requests, 2,000 entries and 25 decisions: far too late to review a change
before it is built on.

## Decision

A postmortem is due on any of these triggers.

1. **A schema change has been used.** A change to luria.yaml (a scheme,
field, vocabulary, relation or form) is reviewed once about 20 entries use
it, or at the close of the first working session that used it after it
merged, whichever comes first, and before the next change to the same
scheme.
2. **A batch closes.** A session's work comes to five or more pull requests,
or includes any stack that had to be folded. Reviewed at the close of that
session.
3. **A bulk filing.** A pull request that adds more than about 50 entries
generated by script. Reviewed in the same session, before the next batch
builds on it.
4. **A decision revised soon after landing**, within the session it landed or
the next. Fast revision is a sign the decision outran its evidence.
5. **A process incident**: CI red more than once on the same cause; a revert;
an entry retracted because it was filed wrong; a near-miss on a standing
rule (lawful copies, no email address sent to a service). Reviewed once
fixed.
6. **The owner asks.**

Not triggers: a routine filing (one reading, one exchange) that goes green
first time; the bot's repair commits; a single pull request with no schema
change and no bulk filing.

- **No calendar intervals.** Use and sessions are the units. A session is a
working session of the owner with an assistant, closing when its batch is
merged or set aside.
- **Who raises it.** The assistant checks the triggers when a batch closes
and says which are met; the owner decides whether to write it. A
postmortem is not filed unasked.
- **Size.** The full form ([POSTMORTEM-002](../postmortems.d/POSTMORTEM-002.md)) for a schema change; a short
one for a batch or an incident: what happened, what it cost, what changes,
or a paragraph saying nothing changes.
- **What it reviews.** Every batch follows some decision's procedure
([ADR-068](ADR-068.md) for filings, [ADR-108](ADR-108.md) for reader notes, [ADR-109](ADR-109.md) for leads), so
`reviews` stays required.
- **Calibration.** The thresholds come from the pace measured above. When
the pace changes, or postmortems show the triggers fire too often or too
seldom, this decision is superseded.

## First application

Checked against the work of 2026-11-19 and 2026-11-20:

- The LEAD scheme ([ADR-109](ADR-109.md)) met trigger 1 within the day, and the
explication form ([ADR-107](ADR-107.md)) trigger 4; [POSTMORTEM-002](../postmortems.d/POSTMORTEM-002.md) reviewed both,
at about the right moment.
- The backlog audit's 184 generated leads (pull request 542) met trigger 3;
[POSTMORTEM-002](../postmortems.d/POSTMORTEM-002.md) covers it in part, as the scheme's use.
- Pull requests 535 to 540 met trigger 2: six pull requests, a stack folded
top-down, conflicts on shared notes, and a rebase onto concretized codes.
Written as [POSTMORTEM-001](../postmortems.d/POSTMORTEM-001.md).
- Pull requests 541 to 545 met trigger 2: five pull requests, one folded
into another, and two rounds of conflicts on dated notes appended to the
same entries. Written as [POSTMORTEM-003](../postmortems.d/POSTMORTEM-003.md).
- Pull requests 546 and 547 (this scheme, and the LEAD revision) will meet
trigger 1 once postmortems are written under them.

The owner asked for both due batch postmortems on 2026-11-20; they are filed with this decision, in the short form.

## Alternatives considered

- **A postmortem per pull request.** Most pull requests are routine filings
that go green; reviewing each would be noise, and would cost more than the
problems it finds.
- **Calendar intervals.** At this pace a week is 70 pull requests; a
calendar trigger reviews too late, and fires on quiet weeks for nothing.
- **Only on the owner's request.** It leaves the assistant, who sees the
conflicts and repairs first, without a duty to say when a review is due.
- **Folding the policy into the scheme's decision.** The owner asked for it
separately: the scheme is meant to last, and the policy to be revised.

## Consequences

Postmortems arrive at the close of a session or soon after a change is
used, and are short unless a schema changed. The assistant says at each
batch close which triggers are met. The policy is a starting point; its
own first postmortem will show whether the thresholds are right.
