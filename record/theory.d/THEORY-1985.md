# THEORY-1985: Integrate when one side's investment dominates

Grossman & Hart (1986), [LIT-2745](../literature.d/LIT-2745.md).

## The model

Two managers make non-contractible investments; then the owner of each
asset chooses its use, which can be renegotiated costlessly with the
gains split evenly. Renegotiation always reaches the efficient use;
ownership matters because "The distribution of ex post surplus ... will
be sensitive to ownership rights" (§I.C), and so investment is distorted
"in spite of the fact that the noncooperative outcome never occurs!"
(§III.A). Under one firm's control it over-invests and the other
under-invests.

## What it does not show

- **That the holder decides unilaterally.** In the model it bargains from
  a stronger position; the outcome ex post is efficient.
- **Power over people as such.** The object is assets; §V extends the
  idea to the employer's residual rights over the job only informally.
- **Robustness.** The results rest on strong assumptions about the
  bargaining game and on second-order conditions (Assumptions 5–7).
