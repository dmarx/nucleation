# THEORY-tmp9u7p2: Prest argues that white performers' imitations of slave dance in the public theatres of pre-revolutionary Saint-Domingue presented a desexualized, Europeanized and non-threatening version of it, by which colonials sought to control and defuse a power they reluctantly attributed to slave dance, and that their never imitating vodou dance shows that they feared and recognized its potency

## The account

Prest argues that white performers' imitations of slave dance in the public theatres of pre-revolutionary Saint-Domingue presented a desexualized, Europeanized and non-threatening version of it, by which colonials sought to control and defuse a power they reluctantly attributed to slave dance, and that their never imitating vodou dance shows that they feared and recognized its potency

## What it does not say

What enslaved dancers meant, or how the staged dances looked; the evidence is announcements. It documents no staged chica.
