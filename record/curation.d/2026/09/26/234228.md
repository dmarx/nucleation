The owner filed the Riesz representation theorem, bra–ket notation and the GNS
construction next to a reading list on representation learning, and asked "do
we even need Riesz?". A dossier agent answered with four connections:

- Riesz makes kernels exist;
- a linear probe is a functional, and so a vector;
- Radon–Nikodym underwrites KL, mutual information and the bottlenecks;
- GNS says a state determines its representation up to unitary equivalence,
  echoing the Platonic hypothesis.

The source pages made none of these connections themselves. The owner asked
for them to be expanded and pursued. Three research agents took one strand
each and read the relevant theorems in the papers' own texts:

- Riesz and reproducing kernels;
- Radon–Nikodym and density ratios;
- GNS and unitary equivalence.

## The one object the strands meet in

The three strands converge on a single object: the positive-pair density ratio
K⁺(a,b) = p⁺(a,b) / (p(a)p(b)). It is four things at once.

1. **A Radon–Nikodym derivative.** It is dP⁺/d(P⊗P), and InfoNCE, the
   logistic loss and the spectral contrastive loss all have it as their
   population optimum. InfoNCE and skip-gram reach it in the log domain, as PMI
   ([THEORY-tmp34v8v](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmp34v8v.md)).
2. **A Mercer kernel.** Its kernel principal components are the eigenfunctions
   of the positive-pair Markov chain. Their span is the minimax-optimal linear
   basis for view-invariant targets ([THEORY-tmpqoglm](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpqoglm.md)).
3. **The kernel of the conditional-expectation operator on L²(p).** The
   record's own derivation: (Tf)(a) = E[f(A′) | A = a] = ∫ K⁺(a,b) f(b) p(b)
   db. T is Hilbert–Schmidt exactly when 1 + χ²(P⁺‖P⊗P) < ∞. L²(p) is the
   space the commutative GNS construction builds from the state f ↦ E_p f
   ([THEORY-tmpfit2t](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpfit2t.md), Proposed: no source states this whole).
4. **The exponential of the PMI kernel.** Huh et al.'s Platonic hypothesis
   proves convergence to the PMI kernel under its assumptions ([ANTH-LIT-458](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-458.md),
   §4.2).

The owner's reading list therefore has one topic under four headings. The
spectral self-supervised learning papers, the density-ratio view of
contrastive learning, and the Platonic hypothesis all describe the same
operator.

## Answers, connection by connection

**Riesz makes kernels exist: true, and stated.** Muandet et al. ([LIT-tmpyrhlj](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpyrhlj.md))
state Riesz (Thm 2.4) and derive the reproducing property from it
(Prop. 2.1).

- **The kernel mean embedding is a Riesz representer.** It is the representer
  of taking an expectation (Lemma 3.1). This is the one place in the family
  where Riesz is invoked by name.
- **Self-supervised learning can be read as separating mean embeddings.** Li
  et al.'s SSL-HSIC ([LIT-tmp1d4lh](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp1d4lh.md)) turns this into an objective: it maximizes the
  average squared MMD between each image's view distribution ([THEORY-tmp8uwye](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmp8uwye.md)).

Two parts of the original connection did not survive:

- **The representer theorem does not use Riesz.** Schölkopf, Herbrich &
  Smola's proof ([LIT-tmpz72k8](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpz72k8.md)) uses the reproducing property and orthogonal
  projection instead.
- **No spectral SSL paper invokes Riesz.** Their self-adjointness comes from
  the symmetry of the positive-pair distribution ([THEORY-tmpwf2pt](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpwf2pt.md)).

**So, do we need Riesz?** Yes, for defining a reproducing-kernel space
abstractly, for kernel mean embeddings, and for the adjoint of a general
bounded operator. No, for the finite-space spectral theorems the
self-supervised learning papers actually prove.

**Asymmetric methods are where Riesz would earn its keep.** Predictor and
stop-gradient methods (BYOL, SimSiam) and cross-modal pairs (CLIP) are not
covered by the symmetric theory. There the natural object is the singular
value decomposition of a non-self-adjoint operator, which needs the adjoint in
earnest. No such account was found. That is the open question worth pursuing
next.

**A probe is a functional: true, finite-dimensional, and already held.** Park,
Choe & Veitch ([ANTH-LIT-606](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-606.md)) use a "Riesz isomorphism" to map a concept's
probe direction to its steering vector. Their substance is that training does
not fix the inner product.

Harvey, Lipshutz & Williams ([LIT-tmptlaw2](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmptlaw2.md)) prove the representation-side fact.
What a regularized linear readout decodes depends only on the representation's
normalized kernel. CKA, CCA and GULP are averages of readout agreement ([THEORY-tmpqq4es](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpqq4es.md)).

No paper treats probes on an infinite-dimensional feature space as bounded
functionals.

**Radon–Nikodym underwrites mutual information: true.** Belghazi et al.
([LIT-tmprn7bc](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmprn7bc.md)) write mutual information as the expected log Radon–Nikodym
derivative. The Donsker–Varadhan bound is tight exactly there.

Poole et al. ([LIT-tmp0jmv4](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp0jmv4.md)) prove that InfoNCE lower-bounds mutual information for
every critic and never exceeds log K ([THEORY-tmpl293u](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpl293u.md)). Tschannen et al.
([LIT-tmp0wgvt](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmp0wgvt.md)) show that tightening the bound does not explain why the
representations are good.

Two caveats from this strand:

- The conditional entropy bottleneck ([LIT-226](../../record/literature.d/LIT-226.md)) works with entropies, not the
  Radon–Nikodym form.
- Vera et al. ([LIT-240](../../record/literature.d/LIT-240.md)) could not be read, so whether it states the
  absolute-continuity condition is unverified.

**GNS: true as a parallel, stated by one source, and used by no
representation-learning paper.** Jorgensen & Tian ([LIT-tmpy9oi9](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpy9oi9.md)) name the
kernel-to-Hilbert-space construction "the GNS construction" (Remark 1.34).
They prove a kernel's minimal realizations are unique up to a unitary
(Cor. 1.35).

On the representation side:

- Harvey, Larsen & Williams ([LIT-tmpdc8x2](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpdc8x2.md)) prove that Procrustes distance equals
  the Bures distance between kernels ([THEORY-tmpcawyi](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmpcawyi.md)). So the kernel is the
  invariant, and a representation is one realization of it.
- Nielsen et al. ([LIT-tmpjctxp](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpjctxp.md)) show that a softmax model's distribution fixes its
  embeddings only up to an invertible linear map. Closeness in KL does not
  bound representational dissimilarity.
- The Platonic hypothesis's convergence, read carefully, is convergence of
  kernels, up to the symmetry group of whatever is observed ([THEORY-tmp4sf4k](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-tmp4sf4k.md),
  Proposed).
- Huh et al. measure with mutual nearest neighbours, which is much weaker than
  kernel equality.

The strand's own caveat stands: GNS of E_p gives L²(p), which contains no
learned representation. What connects them is the training objective, through
the density ratio.

## Status of what was filed

- **Works: sixteen, all `Deferred` with `Skimmed` notes.** Two works were
  found by two strands (HaoChen et al., Johnson et al.). Each is filed once,
  with both skims in its note.
- **THEORY documents: nine, all `Proposed`.** Six are theorems proved in
  their sources that this record has only skimmed; each promotes on a close
  reading. Three are inferences across papers, and each names the kind of
  result that would settle it.
- **Relations.** The operator claim and the self-adjointness claim extend the
  eigenbasis claim, which extends the shared-optimum claim. The readout and
  Platonic claims extend the kernel-invariance claim.

## Open questions the strands left

- Are Simon et al.'s NTK-based kernel ([LIT-242](../../record/literature.d/LIT-242.md)) and K⁺ the same operator in
  any limit? Simon et al. say their representations differ from the
  optimum-based accounts.
- Rank-d truncation of PMI versus of exp(PMI): do InfoNCE-style and
  spectral-style representations differ in the top-d subspace? No paper
  compares them.
- Is χ²(P⁺‖P⊗P) < ∞ stated anywhere as the condition for the spectral account
  on continuous inputs?
- Do models with high mutual-nearest-neighbour alignment also have high Bures
  alignment of their centred kernels?
- Worth filing next: NeuralEF (arXiv 2205.00165, read but not filed), its
  follow-up (arXiv 2210.12637), Aronszajn (1950), and Wang & Isola's
  alignment/uniformity paper. Whether its uniformity term is a squared
  mean-embedding norm is unchecked.
