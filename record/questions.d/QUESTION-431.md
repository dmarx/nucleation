# QUESTION-431: Does an independent pooled analysis or large cohort of adult corticosteroid users, tested at least 24 hours after the last dose, reproduce the route ordering, the rise of adrenal suppression with dose and duration and a persisting fraction of about 15–25% weeks to months after stopping, and does a comparative trial show any withdrawal scheme effective or safe?

## Why it is a question

Broersen and colleagues pool 74 observational articles at high risk of bias,
without individual-patient data, with dose and duration strata crude and
drawn from asthma patients of all routes, and some tests within 24 hours of
the last dose ([THEORY-1513](../theory.d/THEORY-1513.md), [NOTE-1703](../notes.d/NOTE-1703.md)). Their verdict on withdrawal schemes
is cited, not shown. Whether the pattern survives a better design is open.

## What would answer it

An independent test of the same result, as the condition asked. The
condition as it stood on [THEORY-1513](../theory.d/THEORY-1513.md): An independent pooled analysis or
large cohort, ideally with individual-patient data and testing at least 24
hours after the last dose, reproduces three things: the route ordering, with
oral and intra-articular use far above nasal, topical and inhaled use; a
monotone rise of suppression with dose and with duration; and a persisting
fraction of the same order, about 15–25%, at 4 weeks to 6 months after
stopping. The claim that no route, dose or duration excludes the risk needs
no more than nonzero shares in every stratum; that no withdrawal scheme has
been shown effective or safe stands until a comparative trial reports.
