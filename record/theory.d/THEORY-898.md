# THEORY-898: In Gangneung, South Korea, in 2022, people outwardly ignored routine retaliatory missile launches and beach-front military sites, while a missile malfunction at night under a news embargo was followed by tweets voicing fear of war with North Korea and blaming the government and military

## Source

Ku & Finch, [LIT-1478](../literature.d/LIT-1478.md), Review of International Studies 52(2): 306–331, read
in full in [NOTE-1133](../notes.d/NOTE-1133.md): pp. 315–329, Tables 1–7. The supplementary
materials were not read.

## What was actually shown

- **Routine (25 May 2022).** Launches every ten minutes for an hour, loud
  and visible from beach cafés; most patrons "continued conversations or
  took in the sights while outwardly ignoring" the launches
  (pp. 319–320). Of 19,630 unique tweets that day matching the keywords,
  "only 23 unique tweets mentioned war" (p. 325).
- **Disruption (4–5 October 2022).** A Hyunmoo-2 malfunctioned and exploded
  on the airbase; the news was embargoed until a 7:00 am press conference.
  Tweets filtered for "war" and "North Korea" asked whether war had broken
  out, then criticised the military's competence and the government's
  secrecy (Tables 1–6).

What could have come out otherwise: a securitised public living beside an
airbase could have reacted visibly to routine launches. It did not, while
the reaction to the malfunction shows the threat framing was available.

## What this does not say

- **That publics resist securitisation.** The authors argue the opposite:
  inaction "may allow for the reproduction of securitising practices and
  the structure of securitisation" (p. 314).
- **That the calm reflects inner calm.** "While we cannot access people’s
  internal reactions, they seemed unfazed based on their outward
  behaviour" (p. 317, fn. 57).
- **That latent anxiety was measured.** It is inferred from the reaction to
  disruption, in tweets selected on words that express it.
- **That this is ontological agency rather than habituation.** No
  alternative is tested.

## Connections

- **[CLAIM-445](../claims.d/CLAIM-445.md).** Corrects its "What it does not say" bullet: the
  routines are kept against anxiety, not against the security narrative,
  and the authors read routine inaction as letting securitisation persist.
  Their delegation argument (p. 312), that accepting securitisation hands
  the management of anxiety to elites, is the claim's epistemic feedback
  from the audience's side. Disruption by the protector, followed by blame
  of it, is one observed way such a regime is questioned.
- **[CLAIM-446](../claims.d/CLAIM-446.md).** The record's analogy: routine as avoidance of
  attention that keeps anxiety latent and its object unexamined.
