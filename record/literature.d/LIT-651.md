# LIT-651: Linear Mode Connectivity in Multitask and Continual Learning

Seyed Iman Mirzadeh, Mehrdad Farajtabar, Dilan Gorur, Razvan Pascanu and Hassan Ghasemzadeh (2020), arXiv preprint, listed at ICLR — [ARXIV-2010.04495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.04495)

## Key takeaways

- **Multitask and continual solutions from one start are linearly
  connected; consecutive continual solutions are not.** After learning task
  1, the model either learns task 2 alone (continual, ŵ₂) or tasks 1 and 2
  together (multitask, w*₂). The loss of task 1 stays low on the line from
  ŵ₁ to w*₂, and from ŵ₁ to later multitask solutions up to w*₅, but rises
  on the line from ŵ₁ to ŵ₂ (Figs. 3–4). Euclidean distance and CKA do not
  separate the two, since ŵ₅ is nearer to ŵ₁ than w*₅ is (Fig. 2).
- **The explanation is curvature.** The direction from ŵ₁ to ŵ₂ lies in the
  span of task 1's top Hessian eigenvectors; the direction to w*₂ is nearly
  orthogonal to all 50 top eigenvectors, so it moves through flat
  directions (§3, Fig. 5). Low curvature at the start is necessary but not
  sufficient: with more epochs both directions leave the top subspace, but
  only the continual one forgets (Fig. 5c).
- **MC-SGD.** Minimise the loss averaged over points on the lines from the
  candidate to the previous solution and to the current task's solution
  (Eqs. 3–5), with a replay buffer of one example per class per task. After
  20 tasks: Permuted MNIST 85.3% (multitask 89.5), Rotated MNIST 82.3%
  (89.8), Split CIFAR-100 63.3% (68.8), ahead of Stable SGD, ER-Reservoir,
  A-GEM, EWC and naive SGD (Table 1).

## Standing in the record

Filed with the owner's batch on mode connectivity and model merging, under
`loss-landscapes` ([ADR-027](../decisions.d/ADR-027.md)). `information-geometry` is the second topic: the
paper's explanation runs through the Hessian eigenspectrum of the earlier
task, its bulk-plus-outliers shape, and the alignment of update directions
with its top eigenvectors.

It extends Frankle et al. ([LIT-654](LIT-654.md)). The whole design rests on their
finding that solutions sharing an initialisation are linearly connected: the
multitask and continual runs are started from the same ŵ₁ for that reason
(§1, §2.1, App. A), and App. B.1 shows that starting the multitask run
elsewhere breaks the line on CIFAR-100. It cites Garipov et al.
([LIT-673](LIT-673.md)), Draxler et al. ([LIT-653](LIT-653.md)) and Kuditipudi et al.
([LIT-671](LIT-671.md)) for curved connectivity between independently started runs,
the case it sets aside.

Within the batch, Wen et al. ([LIT-681](LIT-681.md)) start from this paper and report
that in class-incremental learning the line between consecutive continual
solutions crosses a high-loss ridge, and that a non-linear path is needed.
Gueta et al. ([LIT-658](LIT-658.md)) cite it as the multitask precedent for their
regions.

It carries an instruction for practice (MC-SGD), hence the
`anthology-candidate` flag. The anthology's nearest holdings are on forgetting and
linear connectivity in LLM post-training ([ANTH-LIT-235](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-235.md), [ANTH-LIT-237](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-237.md)), the
context in which its curation log named this paper.
