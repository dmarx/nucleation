# LIT-2210: Dynamics of solitons in nearly integrable systems

Kivshar, Y. S. & Malomed, B. A. (1989). Dynamics of solitons in nearly integrable systems. *Reviews of Modern Physics* 61(4): 763–915. doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.61.763.

## Key takeaways

*Not read. What follows is from the neighbours, not from the review.*

- The review is cited by Zabusky & Porter (2010) for the point that solitary
  waves of nonintegrable equations are studied by "perturbative methods,
  asymptotic analysis, and/or variational approximations (Kivshar, 1989)".
- Grimshaw, Smyth & Stepanyants (2018), [LIT-2198](LIT-2198.md), carry the method this
  review is known for, in one case: a weakly damped soliton of the
  Benjamin–Ono equation shrinks along its family of shapes at a rate set by
  an energy balance, with KdV laws quoted, not derived ([THEORY-1509](../theory.d/THEORY-1509.md)).

## Standing in the record

Filed with the soliton batch of 2026-11-06 as the main source for perturbed
solitons. Unread. How far the neighbours stand in:

- **Grimshaw, Smyth & Stepanyants (2018)**, [LIT-2198](LIT-2198.md): the adiabatic
  (energy-balance) treatment of a weakly damped integrable soliton, for the
  Benjamin–Ono equation, with decay laws for six losses checked against
  numerics; KdV laws only by citation. It does not cite this review and
  carries nothing of its own content.
- **Zabusky & Porter (2010)**, [LIT-2208](LIT-2208.md): the definition of a soliton and
  its elastic collision, with Zabusky and Kruskal's 1965 origin
  ([THEORY-1501](../theory.d/THEORY-1501.md)). Malomed reviewed that article.
- **Not carried by anything read**: the review's NLS results under damping
  and gain, kink dynamics, perturbed collisions and radiation
  calculations. Dissipative solitons held by a supplied balance of gain and
  loss are the subject of [LIT-1642](LIT-1642.md) (Knobloch 2015, Unfetched) and of Herr et
  al. (2014) and Grelu & Akhmediev (2012), filed in the same batch.

A reading should check first what the review gives for the NLS soliton
under linear damping and under gain, whether a damped soliton ever breaks
up rather than shrinking, and whether any perturbation it treats gives a
threshold or hysteresis.
