**What moved.** Asked about stability theory, the assistant surveyed the
record (curation 2026/10/11/214307) and proposed four works and one
reading. The owner had them filed.

- **Persistence is relative to a constraint set.** Read against [THEORY-134](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-134.md),
  the persistence claim ([CLAIM-185](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-185.md)) has a second input it did not name.
  Persisting means staying within a constraint set fixed independently of
  the dynamics, and the theory does not supply that set. For an argument,
  the set is what counts as the same claim persisting, and the record's
  codes, versions and supersession rules fix it. That joins [CLAIM-185](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-185.md) to the
  counter [CLAIM-181](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-181.md): the grammar is the constraint set. [THEORY-134](../../record/theory.d/THEORY-134.md)'s split
  between viability and stability also gives a sharper statement of drift.
  A thesis that keeps coming back in some form is stable but not viable
  unless it stays the same claim.
- **Settling does not rule out bursts.** Generalized stability theory says a
  system stable in every mode can still amplify a disturbance for a while,
  when its operator is non-normal. Claims resting on claims are directed
  coupling, a standard source of non-normality. A note on [CLAIM-186](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-186.md) says
  that drift should be measured in the short run after a disturbance, not
  only on average.
- **A size limit.** May's threshold, s√(nC) < 1, gives flooding ([CLAIM-179](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-179.md))
  a second possible cost: past a size and connectance, a record might stop
  settling. Whether a structured record escapes the bound is the open part.
