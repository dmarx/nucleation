# QUESTION-433: Does an independent meta-analysis or large longitudinal study of procedural skill retention over months find a roughly linear decline of about 0.05 to 0.1 standard deviations a month, about half of training gains lost within six to fifteen months, and slower decline when intermittent practice other than an earlier retention test falls in the interval?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-1424](../theory.d/THEORY-1424.md) reports Tatel's (2023) meta-regression over 457 sources.
Decline is linear, at 0.08 SD a month for accuracy and 0.06 for speed and
mixed measures, and about half of training gains are lost within six to
fifteen months. Accuracy declines more slowly when a performance
opportunity falls in the interval ([NOTE-1560](../notes.d/NOTE-1560.md)). That opportunity is mostly
an earlier retention test on the same people. Whether a first test helps
the second "remains an open question" (p. 29). The 2025 article ([LIT-2088](../literature.d/LIT-2088.md))
is by the same author and is unread.

[QUESTION-403](QUESTION-403.md) is related but not the same. It asks whether an independent
review of emergency physicians' skill retention finds the same gaps in
that literature, not the same rates.

## What would answer it

[THEORY-1424](../theory.d/THEORY-1424.md)'s former promote_when, verbatim:

> An independent meta-analysis, or a large longitudinal study, of motor
> or procedural skill retention over intervals of months finds a
> monotonic decline of comparable size (on the order of 0.05 to 0.1
> standard deviations a month, or about half of training gains lost
> within six to fifteen months), together with slower decline when
> intermittent practice occurs during the interval.

For the practice part, Tatel's own design separates practice from an
earlier test: three between-subject groups, retested after the first
interval only, after the second only, and after both (p. 29). Only first
retention trials should be used, to exclude relearning (p. 142).
