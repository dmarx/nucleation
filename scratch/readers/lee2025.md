## Summary

The paper argues that training-data influence is not static. It models training, after developmental interpretability, by a Bayesian learning process in singular learning theory (SLT), and replaces the classical influence function (IF), which needs an invertible Hessian at a minimum, by the Bayesian influence function (BIF) of Kreer et al. 2025: the negative posterior covariance of two per-sample losses, estimated with RMSProp-SGLD localized at each checkpoint. Writing the posterior near a first-order transition as a two-phase mixture and applying the law of total covariance, it predicts that influence can change sign and peak at transitions. It checks this in a two-layer deep linear network on the Saxe et al. (2019) hierarchical semantic dataset (BIF, an analytic IF from the closed-form mode dynamics, and leave-one-out retraining agree), and in Pythia-14M and small transformers trained from scratch, where token-class influences shift with induction-circuit formation and delimiter learning, and a timed upweighting of induction tokens speeds or delays induction heads depending on when it is applied.

## Contribution

A framework ("stagewise data attribution") tying influence to SLT phase transitions; a decomposition giving between-phase influence πUπV(µi,U − µi,V)(µj,U − µj,V); an analytic influence for the deep linear network through the time-dependent mode strengths; empirical demonstrations in a toy model and small language models, including two intervention experiments (time-windowed ablation; time-windowed upweighting).

## Key results

1. **BIF as covariance and susceptibility** (§2.2 Eq. 2; §2.3; A.1 Eq. 8). "BIF(zi, ϕ) = ∂/∂βi E[ϕ(w)] = −Cov(ℓi(w), ϕ(w))"; "the BIF is an example of a generalized susceptibility"; "In physical systems, susceptibilities diverge at phase transitions" (§2.3). "when the regularity assumptions hold, the BIF asymptotically recovers the classical IF in the large-data limit" (§2.2).
2. **Two predictions from a bimodal posterior** (§2.3). Cov(ℓi, ℓj) = E[Cov | Z] + Cov(E[ℓi | Z], E[ℓj | Z]), with the second term "πU πV (µi,U − µi,V)(µj,U − µj,V)". "Influence Can Change Sign"; "Inter-Phase Influence Peaks at Transitions ... maximized when the posterior mass is evenly split (πU ≈ πV ≈ 0.5), which under some conditions may cause a peak"; "peaks in influence identify the specific samples that characterize a given transition." These are derived for the Bayesian posterior, not for SGD.
3. **Toy model: sign flips and peaks track hierarchy** (§3, Figs. 3–4). Three measures agree: "All three measures agree that the influence one sample has on another can vary non-monotonically over the course of training" (Fig. 3). "A data point dog is helpful (negative influence) to learning sparrow early on in the learning, while learning to distinguish animal vs. plant, but it is harmful (positive) later on when learning to distinguish mammal vs. bird" (§3). "the time point of the branching node matches the peaks in influence" (§3, MDS).
4. **Analytic influence in the deep linear net** (C.6, Eqs. 30, 55–57). Influence on W(t) from upweighting point p is IpW = UAG(S,t)V⊤ + U(diag(g′t(s)) ⊙ diag(S′ε))V⊤ + UG(S,t)BV⊤, with g_t the Saxe closed-form mode strength; "influence is a function of singular mode strength of data input-output covariance learned by the network, which is a time-dependent variable" (§3). The basis-rotation terms A, B have denominators s_j² − s_i², and degenerate singular values are handled by a block perturbation (Eqs. 48–54). Figures use a finite ε = −0.1.
5. **Timing matters causally, toy** (C.5, Fig. 11). Ablating dog for 100-epoch windows: "the loss difference is the biggest around t = 6000, when the highest influence occurs"; the critical window for sparrow (shared animal level) is earlier than for cat (shared mammal level).
6. **Language models** (§4, Fig. 5; D.3, Fig. 16). In Pythia-14M, "the formation of model structure corresponding to induction relationships starts as early as 128 steps ... continues to strengthen for the next 30k training steps before appearing to peak and fall", matched to Tigges et al. 2024. Left/right delimiter influence "quickly inverts". In a Pythia-14M retrained with dense checkpoints, BIF between repeated-segment tokens "undergoes a sudden, large increase, peaking and then stabilizing", and the induction score "begins to rise right before the BIF increases" (D.3).
7. **Timed upweighting** (D.4, Fig. 17). 3M-parameter transformer, induction tokens upweighted ×4 in 500-step windows: early windows, "while the BIF is more positive (indicating interference) leads to delayed learning or completely disrupts learning altogether"; windows at 4k–5.5k, "when the BIF is decreasing rapidly, leads to accelerated learning".
8. **Type-B transitions with falling LLC, named** (A.2). "if the two phases agree on the linear term (they have the same minimum loss), then the trade-off will be pushed down into lower-order terms, and there can be a 'Type-B' transition, in which complexity (as measured by the LLC) decreases, in exchange for an increase in lower-order terms." Cited to Carroll et al. 2025 and Hoogland et al. 2025. Stated, not used or tested in the paper.

## Assumptions

- SGD trajectories are modelled by a Bayesian learning process; "The primary theoretical gap remains the link between the Bayesian learning process of SLT and the non-equilibrium dynamics of SGD" (§5).
- The local BIF at checkpoint w*_t is a localized SGLD posterior (γ, nβ fixed per experiment), not the global posterior; transitions in training time are read as posterior transitions in n.
- The analytic toy influence assumes whitened inputs, aligned singular vectors, small balanced initialization (the Saxe closed form) and first-order SVD perturbation; plotted at finite ε = −0.1.
- LM token classes by pattern matching (Baker et al. 2025), "not exhaustive, nor is it exclusive" (§4); normalized BIF (Pearson correlation) in LM experiments; same-token pairs dropped.
- Toy BIF hyperparameters are chosen to maximize correlation with LOO (C.2), so BIF–LOO agreement is partly by selection.

## Concepts

Training data attribution (TDA); classical influence function (Cook 1977; Koh & Liang); Bayesian influence function (Giordano et al. 2017; Kreer et al. 2025); local/normalized/per-token BIF; generalized susceptibility; law of total covariance, within-phase vs between-phase influence; first-order Bayesian phase transition, Type-A (loss–complexity trade) and Type-B (equal loss, LLC decreases); local free energy and internal model selection; stagewise data attribution; critical window; implicit curriculum; unrolling attribution (TracIn, HyDRA, SOURCE) and possible "cancellation effects" (§5); Saxe singular-mode dynamics; induction score.

## Verdict on how the exchange characterized it

LIT-3001's summary ("an example's influence changes, even in sign, across developmental stages") and CLAIM-828's attribution ("training-data influence changes sign and peaks at stage transitions") are **accurate** as statements of the paper's predictions and observations. Three cautions the filer should carry: (i) the theory is for the Bayesian posterior (a mixture across phases), and the link to SGD is the paper's own stated gap; (ii) the peaks are predicted only "under some conditions" (§2.3); (iii) "stages" in the LMs are matched to known transitions (induction, delimiters) by eye and by prior literature, not divided by the LLC. Bibliography: authors confirmed Lee, Smith, Adam, Hoogland; the LIT should add the ICLR 2026 venue from the v2 header and the full first names.

## Bearing on each named code

- **CLAIM-828 — qualifies (and parallel on its influence step).** The claim's assistant-added step says that in the gated equilibrium model the influence on q* is (∂q*/∂s)(yiφ(xi) − ŝ)/n, "zero for every example below the gate's threshold" and a step at activation, and asks whether Lee et al.'s dynamics "have this source". Lee et al.'s own analytic model is different in kind: no gate and no weight decay, and the influence is taken at a fixed training time through the time-dependent mode strength, via the term diag(g′t(s)) ⊙ diag(S′ε) (C.6 Eq. 56), plus basis-rotation terms with denominators s_j² − s_i². Their Bayesian account puts the peak in a between-phase term "maximized when the posterior mass is evenly split" (§2.3), not in a step of an optimum's derivative. **Reader's check, not in the paper:** in the Saxe closed form Eq. 30 (s = 1, g0 = 10⁻³, τ = 1), ∂g_t/∂s rises from 0, peaks at ≈ 2.13 near t ≈ 3.8 (mid-transition), then settles to 1. So under gradient flow the same derivative the claim uses has a transient peak during the transition, overshooting the equilibrium step, because a larger s also makes the transition earlier. This is a peak with a dynamical source, closer to the paper's finding than the equilibrium step is. It does not touch the claim's thesis (gating, partial order, three times); it bears on the "From the optimum's derivative to influence" paragraph, which should say the equilibrium derivative gives a step and the gradient-flow derivative a peak. The paper's hierarchy result also bears on the partial-order thesis: influence peaks order by hierarchy level (animal vs plant, then mammal vs bird, then dog vs cat; C.5 sparrow window earlier than cat), but in Saxe's model that order is by singular-value strength, i.e. the order the claim says THEORY-740 and THEORY-283 already give, not an order by dependency. Leaves the dependency order untested.
- **QUESTION-tmphkb1w (LLC decreases) — leaves open, adds a named candidate.** A.2 names the "Type-B" transition, equal minimum loss, LLC decreases "in exchange for an increase in lower-order terms", citing Carroll et al. 2025 and Hoogland et al. 2025. This is the question's first candidate ("More degeneracy at equal loss") with a name and two sources the record may not hold; note the paper places the trade against lower-order terms (log log n and beyond), while the question's candidate phrases it via nL + λ log n. No LLC is measured over training in this paper (LLC is used only to calibrate SGLD in the toy, C.2), so no decrease is explained.
- **THEORY-tmpr9w2r / LIT-2966 (Hoogland et al.) — parallel and extends.** Same group and method family (SGLD at checkpoints, localization γ, fixed nβ); this paper swaps the LLC (the first moment, a degeneracy measure) for a second-moment, per-sample observable, and its LM stages are matched to the same structures (induction). It cites Hoogland et al. 2024 among the works "explaining" stagewise emergence (§5). It inherits the estimator cautions the record holds (CLAIM-812, LEAD-207): toy hyperparameters picked to match LOO, LM nβ ∈ {256, 1024}, γ ∈ {500, 1000}, "did not vary substantially" (D.1.2). Neither supports nor contradicts the THEORY's sentence.
- **CLAIM-823 (reading (b)) — supports the weak half by the same analogy; leaves the strong half open.** The paper adopts reading (b)'s picture, phases competing in a local free energy nL + λ log n with first-order transitions between them (A.2), and uses it to predict influence dynamics that are then observed. But it again uses the Bayesian process as a model of SGD and states the link as the main gap (§5); it never measures a change of dominant singular region. It adds that Type-B transitions, at equal loss, lower λ (A.2), which is the qualification CLAIM-823's note already records.
- **CLAIM-802 (reading (a)) — background; does not bear.** No resolution or blow-up appears; transitions are posterior mass moving between regions U and V.

## Limitations

- The theory (§2.3) is a qualitative decomposition, not a derivation that influence peaks in any given model; "under some conditions".
- The Bayes-to-SGD gap is acknowledged and untouched.
- Toy model: one dataset (8 items), one architecture, apparently one training seed; BIF hyperparameters chosen by agreement with LOO.
- LMs: Pythia-14M with 600 sequences × 55 tokens for BIF; matches to known transitions are qualitative ("Dashed lines indicate major inflection points"); the intervention (D.4) uses one initialization and order; CIs are posterior bootstrap, not over training seeds. Internal inconsistencies in D.4: "five experimental runs" with six windows listed; induction score "peaking ... at timestep 9k" in the text vs "forms induction heads at step ∼7k" in the Fig. 17 caption.
- No LLC trajectories reported, so the paper does not itself tie influence peaks to measured changes in degeneracy.

## Candidate THEORY sentence

Lee, Smith, Adam and Hoogland find that the Bayesian influence of one training sample on another's loss, estimated as a negative posterior covariance from SGLD localized at each checkpoint, changes over training in sign and magnitude: in a two-layer linear network on an eight-item hierarchical dataset it agrees with leave-one-out retraining and an analytic influence, flips sign and peaks as each level of the hierarchy is learned; in small language models token-class influences shift around induction-circuit formation; and upweighting induction tokens speeds induction heads when applied in the window where that influence falls, and delays them when applied earlier.

promote_when: An independent run, with the authors' code or a reimplementation, reproduces in the hierarchical deep-linear setting the sign flip and the peaks of the BIF aligned with each hierarchy level across several training seeds and over a range of SGLD settings not chosen by agreement with leave-one-out; and reproduces in a small transformer the timing effect of upweighting induction tokens across seeds.
