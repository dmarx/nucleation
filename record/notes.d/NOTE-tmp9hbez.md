# NOTE-tmp9hbez: Solms — The Hard Problem of Consciousness and the Free Energy Principle

## Contribution

Joins a localisation, upper brainstem rather than cortex, to a formal
function, precision optimisation in free-energy minimisation, as one
dual-aspect mechanism for affect-first consciousness.

## Key results

1. **Dual aspect.** "the brain does not “produce” or “cause”
   consciousness" (p. 2).
2. **Affect.** "who ever heard of an unconscious feeling" (p. 3);
   "Consciousness persists in the absence of cerebral cortex, as does
   volitional behavior." (p. 4); "we must conclude that consciousness is
   generated in the upper brainstem" (p. 6).
3. **Free energy.** "the so-called level of consciousness is a function
   of variational free energy" (p. 5); "I do not believe that thermostats
   are conscious." (p. 7); "In short, consciousness is felt uncertainty."
   (p. 7).
4. **The gap closed.** Self-organisation alone gives proto-selfhood:
   "there is nothing about such proto-mental dynamics which explains the
   emergence of feeling" (p. 10); "Expected free energy just is
   uncertainty about the consequences of any putative action." (p. 11).
5. **Function.** "precision (consciousness) controls the influence of
   prediction errors on action (motivation) and perception (attention)"
   (p. 12); "Consciousness itself is affective." (p. 12).
6. **Automatism.** "it is easy to envision an organism (or machine) in
   which precision values are set in such a way that the system’s
   responses to prediction error are automatized" (p. 13).

## Assumptions

Dual-aspect monism; decorticate consciousness inferred from behaviour;
homeostatic needs are categorically distinct and must be prioritised.

## Concepts

Dual-aspect monism; upper brainstem (ERTAS, PAG); affect; Markov blanket;
free-energy principle; precision-weighting; felt uncertainty;
reconsolidation.

consciousness inferred from behaviour; not the book.
