# LIT-924: Principles of Crystal Nucleation and Growth

James J. De Yoreo and Peter G. Vekilov (2003), *Reviews in Mineralogy and Geochemistry* 54(1), 57–93 — DOI-10.2113/0540057

## Key takeaways

*Filed, not read.* Known here by what it is standardly cited for.

- In classical nucleation theory, a cluster of a new phase grows only once
  it passes a critical size. Below that, the cost of its surface outweighs
  the gain from its bulk, and it dissolves.
- The barrier and the critical size both fall as supersaturation rises, so
  the nucleation rate depends very steeply on supersaturation.
- A heterogeneous site, such as a surface, an impurity or a seed, lowers
  the barrier. That is why seeding works.
- Growth after nucleation proceeds at steps and kinks on the crystal
  surface, and impurities can block it.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-11 to ground [CLAIM-176](../claims.d/CLAIM-176.md), which holds that recurrence
is the nucleation threshold. This review is the physics behind the word
"threshold": below a critical size a cluster dissolves, and a site lowers
the barrier. A reading should check whether the analogy holds where it
matters most. In the record the threshold is a count of occurrences. In
the physics it is a size that depends on supersaturation.

Two readings of 2026-10-11 bear on this chapter. Vekilov 2010
([LIT-919](LIT-919.md), [NOTE-708](../notes.d/NOTE-708.md)) confirms that the critical size moves with
supersaturation, as Δμ⁻³, and reaches one molecule at a solution–crystal
spinodal. It disputes one takeaway above: near the spinodal, it argues,
sites act "not by lowering ΔG2*, but by assisting the growth of the
ordered clusters". That is an inference without substrate data, and a
reading of this chapter should note the disagreement. Ostwald 1897
([LIT-920](LIT-920.md), [NOTE-709](../notes.d/NOTE-709.md)) gives the qualitative ingredients of a
critical nucleus (small fragments are more soluble; a fragment can dissolve
at one supersaturation and grow at another) and thresholds that are a
degree of supersaturation and a seed size. Neither reading supports a
threshold that is a count, which bears on this work's link to
[CLAIM-176](../claims.d/CLAIM-176.md).
