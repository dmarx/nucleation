# ADR-068: A Read note on a work marked Unavailable, Unfetched or Deferred fails lint

## Context

A NOTE's status says how deeply a work was read, and a LIT's status says
whether the work was reached. The two are recorded separately, and nothing
checked that they agree. A survey of authors on 2026-11-01 found
[NOTE-1437](../notes.d/NOTE-1437.md) on [LIT-536](../literature.d/LIT-536.md), which is Unfetched,
and reported it as an inconsistency. The owner asked for a rule in the
luria config to flag that sort of thing.

The note is not an inconsistency. It is `Skimmed` and covers "the
Introduction alone", and the note-statuses vocabulary defines `Skimmed` as
"abstract, figures and selected sections". Eight notes in the record are
like it: each is a skim of a précis, an introduction or an excerpt of a
work whose full text was not reached. The contradiction the vocabularies
do define is narrower. `Read` is "the full text, closely enough to state
its assumptions and results exactly". `Unavailable` ([ADR-041](ADR-041.md)) and
`Unfetched` ([ADR-053](ADR-053.md)) both say the full text was not reached. A `Read`
note on such a work means one of the two records is wrong. So does a
`Read` note on a `Deferred` LIT, which lit-statuses defines as "not yet
read closely enough to place … or reading stopped at the abstract or a
skim".

## Decision

Three patterns are declared with `report: true` and listed in
`lint.fail_on`:

```yaml
read_but_unreached:
  match: "(n:NOTE {status: 'Read'})-[:paper]->(l:LIT {status: 'Unavailable'})"
read_but_unfetched:
  match: "(n:NOTE {status: 'Read'})-[:paper]->(l:LIT {status: 'Unfetched'})"
read_but_unreviewed:
  match: "(n:NOTE {status: 'Read'})-[:paper]->(l:LIT {status: 'Deferred'})"
```

They are three patterns because the pattern grammar matches one literal per
property. When one fires, the fix is one of two:

- The work was read in full. The LIT is placed: it returns to reading, as
  the Unavailable and Unfetched blurbs already say a supplied copy does,
  and its status note is updated.
- Only part was reached. The note is `Skimmed`, and its summary says which part.

A `pattern-ok:` directive in the note can excuse a match, as for the
[ADR-057](ADR-057.md) checks. It is not expected to be needed.

## Verification

The record as of this decision pairs note and LIT statuses as follows:
Read with Active, Proposed, Rejected and Superseded; Skimmed with Active,
Deferred (3), Unavailable (4) and Unfetched (4); Superseded with Active.
No `Read` note sits on an Unavailable, Unfetched or Deferred LIT, and
`pattern_rules.reported()` returns no match for any of the three patterns. In a scratch copy with [NOTE-1437](../notes.d/NOTE-1437.md) and [NOTE-832](../notes.d/NOTE-832.md) set
to `Read`, it returned `read_but_unfetched: (NOTE-1437, LIT-536)` and
`read_but_unreached: (NOTE-832, LIT-1004)`.

## Alternatives considered

- **Flag every note on an unreached work.** This was the first proposal. It
  flags eight skims that the vocabularies allow, and each would need a
  directive to say so.
- **A warning rather than a failure.** The [ADR-057](ADR-057.md) checks fail CI because they
  were at zero when adopted, and this is at zero too. A warning would
  let the first contradiction land.
- **Derive the LIT's status from its notes.** A LIT's status records a
  search for a copy, and a note doesn't. Neither can be computed from the
  other.

## Consequences

A filing that reads a work in full, but forgets to move its LIT off
`Unavailable`, `Unfetched` or `Deferred`, now fails `docs-check`. Skims are
untouched: a `Skimmed` note may sit on a LIT of any status.
