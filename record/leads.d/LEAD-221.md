# LEAD-221: Intervene on a record-keeping team's members and record one at a time, replacing a member while keeping the record, withdrawing the record while keeping the members, and giving one member justified dissent, to see whether team-level dispositions persist and how the team answers the dissent

## What to do

From A21 §5 of the exchange "Explain First Contact RL"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6b037165-3a1c-83ea-9d7c-311c7807acd3). Inspect
collaborators' private judgements before interaction, then:

| Intervention | What it tests |
|---|---|
| replace one collaborator while retaining the record | whether an organizational disposition persists beyond a particular participant ([QUESTION-118](../questions.d/QUESTION-118.md)) |
| keep the collaborators but withdraw the record | whether the record is causally important to sustained coordination ([CLAIM-1159](../claims.d/CLAIM-1159.md)) |
| introduce justified dissent in one collaborator | whether the organization can revise its distinctions without sacrificing coordination |

Code the dissent arm's outcome by [CLAIM-1128](../claims.d/CLAIM-1128.md)'s four responses:
fragmentation, conformity, centralized arbitration, adaptive integration.

## An arm added in filing

Cut or narrow one channel between two members (deny one of them access to a
part of the shared record, as A29 §8 suggested) and compare the team's
response with the members' responses measured in isolation. That is where
[CLAIM-1128](../claims.d/CLAIM-1128.md) says team-level dispositions part from isolated profiles.
(The assistant's; Claude, in this session.)

## Its relation to [LEAD-227](LEAD-227.md)

That lead compares conditions across teams; this one intervenes within a
team. The withdrawal arm is shared and can be run once.
