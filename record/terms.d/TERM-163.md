# TERM-163: adaptive inquiry readiness, of an institution for a declared family of future epistemic demands: the condition of meeting its current epistemic obligations while keeping admissible, affordable and sufficiently reliable pathways to investigate the relevant demands within their horizons; kept up through the institution's own activity, it is a viability condition

## Definition

A63 §8. Let S include an institution's "present scientific capabilities,
governing arrangements, and capacities for institutional revision", and 𝓓
"a declared family of possible future epistemic demands". The institution
is **adaptively inquiry-ready** when "it can meet its current epistemic
obligations while retaining admissible, affordable, and sufficiently
reliable pathways to investigate relevant members of 𝓓 within their
applicable horizons." The stronger condition, kept up through its own
activity, "can be expressed through a viability requirement: the
institution's dynamics should admit policies that preserve the conditions
through which present inquiry and future reconfiguration remain possible."

In the record's terms, an epistemic demand d ∈ 𝓓 with horizon H_d is met
when some pair (q_d, a) is brought into the joint inquiry relation of
[CLAIM-1190](../claims.d/CLAIM-1190.md) ([TERM-160](TERM-160.md)'s joint frontier, or [TERM-159](TERM-159.md)'s inquiry
frontier) by H_d. "Sufficiently reliable" is [TERM-115](TERM-115.md)'s readiness for that
contingency held above a declared level. The stronger condition is
[TERM-167](TERM-167.md)'s viability kernel, with the pathways to investigate the
members of 𝓓 in place of the routes to a viable alternative organization.

A63's limits: "Such a criterion would be conditional on the family of
contingencies, the available resources, and independently justified
standards of inquiry. It would not establish that preserving every
alternative perspective is valuable."

## What it is not

- **Not present research performance.** A63 §8's point is that the two come
  apart: "the institution may retain impressive present research
  performance while possessing diminished ability to respond to novel
  questions". It calls that "epistemic overconsolidation", which is the
  owner's overconsolidation ([CLAIM-758](../claims.d/CLAIM-758.md), from another exchange) carried from
  a learner to an institution, and the query-induced capability trap of
  [CLAIM-1054](../claims.d/CLAIM-1054.md) and [CLAIM-1003](../claims.d/CLAIM-1003.md) reached by funding. A63 also separates
  the two achievements by [CLAIM-099](../claims.d/CLAIM-099.md), which it cites correctly: maintaining a
  regime of effective action is not maintaining the capacity to govern among
  regimes.
- **Not richness.** [TERM-158](TERM-158.md)'s richness is a maintained plurality with
  no declared demands. This term is relative to 𝓓, so it does not say an
  institution is rich, only ready for the demands declared.
- **Not reorganization readiness.** [TERM-167](TERM-167.md)'s routes end in a viable
  alternative organization; these end in an answer to a declared demand.

## What it leaves open

"Relevant members of 𝓓" is not defined: whether readiness is required for
every declared demand, for a weighted share, or for those an evaluator marks
as relevant. With every member, the term is [TERM-167](TERM-167.md)'s kind of
worst-case condition; with a weighting, it is coverage in [CLAIM-1042](../claims.d/CLAIM-1042.md)'s
sense, linear in the weights. The two are different conditions, and A63
does not choose. Nor does it say who declares 𝓓, which [CLAIM-1040](../claims.d/CLAIM-1040.md)'s
question of who classes noise would ask of it. These points are the
assistant's (Claude, in this session).

## Standing

Proposed, A's. It has content of its own only as an instantiation: the
condition is [TERM-115](TERM-115.md)'s and [TERM-167](TERM-167.md)'s; what is new is the object, the
joint inquiry relation, and the declared family in place of A52's "rich".
