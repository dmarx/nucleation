# NOTE-tmp1yxz6: Ge, Xie, Sun, Fan, Zhang and Liu — Brief online mindfulness training and impulsivity

## Contribution

It measures three kinds of impulsivity in the same people before and
after brief mindfulness training, against a monitor-only arm and a
waiting arm, so that a change in response speed and a change in
discounting can be compared within one trial.

## Key insight

A week of monitor-and-acceptance practice changed how fast people
responded in conflict and inhibition tasks and did not change how they
discounted delayed money. Whatever the training moved, it was not the
discount curve.

## Assumptions

- **Faster is less impulsive.** "A higher error rate and longer average
  reaction time are generally believed to indicate poorer individual
  motoric impulse inhibition ability" (p. 453), and likewise for the
  Stroop task. The Go/No-go reaction time is the time to respond to Go
  stimuli.
- **Monitor and Acceptance Theory** (Lindsay and Creswell 2016): the
  effective element is acceptance joined to monitoring, so the monitor-only
  arm is the active control.
- **Discounting as the share of immediate choices** over fixed
  percentages and delays of 7, 15 and 30 days, split into "easy" and
  "difficult" items; no discount rate is fitted.

## Key results

- **Design (pp. 451-453).** 70 participants aged 18-26 from the lowest
  27% on the Philadelphia Mindfulness Scale, randomized to mindfulness
  (n = 24), monitor (23) or control (23); audio training adapted from
  Williams and Penman's "Mindfulness of Body and Breath", 10 minutes a day
  for 7 days; tasks at pre-test, post-test and 15 days later.
- **Manipulation check (p. 455).** Mindfulness scores rose in the
  mindfulness arm only, group × time F(4, 134) = 17.91, p < 0.001.
- **Stroop (pp. 455-456).** Accuracy above 95%, no group effect. Reaction
  time: group × time F(4, 134) = 4.37, p = 0.002; at post-test the
  mindfulness arm was faster than the monitor arm (p = 0.011) and,
  at trend, than the control arm (p = 0.077).
- **Go/No-go (pp. 456-457).** Accuracy above 95%, no effects. Reaction
  time: group × time F(4, 134) = 2.53, p = 0.043; at post-test the
  mindfulness arm "was significantly shorter than that of the monitor
  group and the control group (p < 0.05)"; no differences at follow-up.
- **Delay discounting (pp. 457-458).** No main effect of group or time;
  a group × time interaction, F(4, 134) = 2.608, p = 0.038, with "no
  significant difference in the immediate choice proportion between the
  mindfulness group, the monitor group and control group at pre-test,
  post-test, and follow-up conditions (p > 0.10)".
- **Abstract.** "At follow-up, all the results indicated that the brief
  mindfulness training did not have sustained effects." "The current
  study is not preregistered."
- **The authors on discounting (p. 459).** "The mindfulness training
  might actually be counterproductive to the goal of lowering rates of
  delay discounting which involves thinking about future consequences of
  behavior."

## Concepts

- **Cognitive, motoric and decision impulsivity**: the Stroop, Go/No-go
  and discounting tasks, after Winstanley et al. (2006).
- **De-automatization**: the authors' name for the effect on reaction
  times.
- **Monitor versus acceptance**: the two components of Monitor and
  Acceptance Theory; only their combination changed behaviour here.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-156.** The claim's first test asks whether choice under
  temptation and response inhibition improve by more than any change in
  discounting carries. Here discounting did not change and Stroop and
  Go/No-go reaction times did, so nothing that changed could have been
  carried by discounting. That is the pattern the claim predicts. But the
  changed measures are speeds with accuracy at ceiling: a shorter Go
  reaction time is not a better inhibition of a dominant response, and
  no measure of choice under temptation was taken. So the study does not
  show the claim's route; ARG-tmpc8jqe records the judgement, Rejected.
  The record's earlier summary ("some impulsive behaviours reduced,
  discounting not improved") is accurate to the abstract.
- **LIT-906 (Dixon et al.).** Dixon et al. report discounting lowered by
  five minutes of mindful breathing; this trial, with a week of practice,
  finds no change. The two disagree on the flow route, unread on one side.
- **No mediation test.** The trial does not ask whether discounting
  mediates anything, so the gap CLAIM-156 and LEAD-078 name stays open.

## Limitations

- Small arms (23-24), not preregistered, Bonferroni-corrected simple
  effects only; the abstract's "compared to the monitor group and the
  control group" for the Stroop is p = 0.077 against control in the text.
- Reaction-time reductions with unchanged accuracy may be a speed shift,
  not better inhibition; the paper does not report Stroop interference
  (incongruent minus congruent) or No-go commission errors apart from
  overall accuracy.
- Discounting is a raw share of immediate choices on a coarse grid
  against a fixed immediate £20; the paper does not say whether any
  choice was paid.
- The participants were chosen for low trait mindfulness, so the
  population is a narrow one.
