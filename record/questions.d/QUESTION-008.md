# QUESTION-008: Is pragmatic fidelity a form of approximate bisimulation between communicative systems, and which parts of their update structure must it preserve?

## Why it is a question

A35 §10: "Can we characterize pragmatic fidelity as a form of approximate
bisimulation between communicative systems, and then determine which aspects of
their noncommutative update structure must be preserved for that bisimulation to
hold?" A38 gave it a section (labelled transition systems, Milner, van
Glabbeek, Desharnais et al.) and in §11.4 already doubted it: "Consider whether
bisimulation is too strong and whether weaker trace equivalence is more
appropriate."

## Why it was set aside

Under U16, A39 put "observational equivalence at the center of the formal
theory, and treat[ed] intertwining, bisimulation, and quantum contextuality as
progressively stronger structures whose usefulness must be justified". A40
ordered the candidates as static response, finite-sequence observational,
trace, simulation and bisimulation, "noting that exact logical relationships
depend on the chosen system model", and closed: "The earlier version risked
jumping from *pragmatic framing* to *noncommuting operators* to *bisimulation*
before showing why that additional mathematical structure was necessary."

An answer would name a judgement that finite-sequence observational equivalence
([TERM-033](../terms.d/TERM-033.md)) gets wrong and bisimulation gets right. The one-way case survives as
[CLAIM-034](../claims.d/CLAIM-034.md).
