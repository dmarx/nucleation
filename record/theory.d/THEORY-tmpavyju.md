# THEORY-tmpavyju: Social ontology asks two separable questions about a social category: what grounds a fact of that kind, the conditions something must meet, and what anchors those conditions, the facts that put them in place; theories of the field, such as convention, collective acceptance and practice, answer the second, and a theory can be individualist about one and not the other

## Source

Brian Epstein (2018; substantive revision 2024), "Social Ontology",
*Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, [LIT-487](../literature.d/LIT-487.md), read in full (the 2024
revision) in [NOTE-393](../notes.d/NOTE-393.md), with the author's open-access précis of *The Ant
Trap* (*Journal of Social Ontology* 2(1):125–134, 2016), cited there as
"the précis". *The Ant Trap* itself ([LIT-478](../literature.d/LIT-478.md)) is not lawfully readable.

## What was actually shown

The entry is a survey with a thesis about how the field is organised.

- **The distinction (§§2.2, 4; the précis).** Take *x is kosher*. Whether x
  came from a pig grounds the fact. The *frame principle* gives the
  conditions for grounding facts of that kind across a frame, a set of
  possible worlds. What puts the frame principle in place is its *anchor*:
  perhaps religious authority, acceptance, practice or divine command.
  Likened to semantics and metasemantics.
- **The field sorted by it.** Psychologism, atomism, physicalism, holism
  and flat ontologies are answers about determination bases (§3).
  Agreement, Lewis's convention, Hart's accepted rules, Searle's collective
  acceptance, Millikan, Boyd and Hacking's pattern theories, practice
  theories and language are answers about anchoring (§4).
- **Two dimensions (§2.1).** Crowds are socially grounded but not
  constructed; phenotypic racial categories are constructed but not
  grounded; fiat money is both. The entry cites this to an "Epstein 2016"
  its bibliography does not list ([NOTE-393](../notes.d/NOTE-393.md), Corrections).
- **Anchors are heterogeneous (§4.5).** US legal categories are anchored by
  jury instructions, trial results, legislation, judicial interpretation
  and environmental regularities.
- **Individualism split (the précis).** Ontological individualism (the
  social is exhaustively determined by facts about individuals) and anchor
  individualism are separate claims. The précis says both are false and
  argues only the first in the book.

**What could have come out otherwise.** A theory the entry surveys could
have answered both questions at once in a way that resists splitting. The
entry places each on one side. That is the organising choice, so it is not
a test.

## What this does not say

- **That anchoring is a distinct relation.** That it is neither grounding
  nor meta-grounding is Epstein 2019b's view, reported as such. Haslanger
  treats anchoring as epistemic "scaffolding"; Hawley and Schaffer would
  reduce it to grounding; Schaffer models the split on structuring versus
  triggering causes (§2.2.2). Each dissent gets a sentence. The entry calls
  the sharpness of the distinction "a matter of some controversy".
- **That ontological individualism is false.** Reported as "recently come
  under criticism", with five critics cited and no argument given here
  (§3; C4).
- **That group attitudes outrun member attitudes.** Reported as a
  challenge to local dependence (Bird 2010, Epstein 2022, neither read).
- **That a group's agency or experience is anchored.** On the précis, a
  group is an agent by realising a system of practical activity, not by a
  status assignment. Whether experience could be anchored the texts do not
  say ([NOTE-393](../notes.d/NOTE-393.md)).
- **That the survey adjudicates.** It reports positions and objections and
  does not weigh them, except where its organising distinction is at
  stake.
