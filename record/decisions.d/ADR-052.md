# ADR-052: The record's own argument is stated ordinally first: orderings, partial orders and classes before scales, a numerical measure only with what it is cardinal in stated, and incomparability or partial identification reported as a result, not a gap

## Context

The record's own argument ([ADR-032](ADR-032.md)) has three lines that each turned
on whether a quantity was needed:

- **Will-organization.** The owner asked whether freedom of will comes in
  "gradations (perhaps even gradients/spectra?)". The answer filed was a
  partial order by inclusion of what an agent can viably reach, with
  incomparable pairs, and a gradient only of a declared measure
  ([CLAIM-354](../claims.d/CLAIM-354.md)).
- **Frustration.** The owner's correction, U37 of the exchange of
  2026-10-17: "We can do quite a lot with access to an ordering, even if
  it doesn't give us a true measure." Frustration became a complex of
  jointly satisfiable demands whose relief is enlargement, with
  reorganizations that relieve different conflicts incomparable
  ([CLAIM-347](../claims.d/CLAIM-347.md)).
- **Valence.** The owner reframed a common currency as a negotiation
  admitting an ordering ([CLAIM-440](../claims.d/CLAIM-440.md)). Two reviews of the pull request that
  filed it found the measures confusing ordinal and cardinal structure:
  a Hodge gradient that reverses a comparison the wins settle, a netted
  record that erases alternation, a baseline valid only for complete
  tournaments, an approximate fit read as a Lyapunov function, and a
  sign read as determinate where the record fixes only a class
  ([CLAIM-434](../claims.d/CLAIM-434.md), parts 4 to 11). The fix in each case was the ordinal
  statement first ([TERM-077](../terms.d/TERM-077.md), [TERM-078](../terms.d/TERM-078.md), [TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md)).

Nothing recorded the method as one. Each line found it separately, and
the valence line found it by being reviewed.

## Decision

For entries of the record's own argument (CLAIM, TERM, QUESTION, CASE,
ARG):

1. **Ordinal first.** A comparison is stated as an ordering, a preorder
   or a partial order when that suffices for what the entry claims. A
   scalar is introduced only when the claim needs one.
2. **State what a measure is cardinal in.** A numerical measure names what
   its differences mean (margins as intensities, costs, rates) and is
   checked against the ordinal facts it should respect. Where it can
   reverse one, as a least-squares fit can, the entry says so.
3. **Incomparability is a result.** Where two things are incomparable
   under the declared order, the entry says they are, and does not force
   a ranking.
4. **Report the class.** Where a record fixes only a class of orderings,
   potentials or representations, conclusions drawn from it are stated
   for the class, set-valued where its members disagree, and narrowed
   only by an intervention or further observation named in the entry.
5. **Scope.** The rule binds the record's own argument. Readings (LIT,
   NOTE, THEORY) report what their sources say, cardinal or not.

## Alternatives considered

- **Leave it to each claim.** The status quo. It worked where the owner
  intervened and failed where nobody did: the valence line's first
  measures were cardinal by default and needed two reviews to correct.
- **Scale first, ordinal as a fallback.** Most quantitative literatures
  default this way, and it makes measures easy to compute. It is the
  default that produced the errors above, because a scale supplies
  answers to comparisons the data never made.
- **A principle (DP) instead of a decision.** The record's design
  principles concern what the record is for; this is a working rule
  about how its argument is stated, which is a decision's business.

## Consequences

- Existing entries are not swept. An entry is brought under the rule when
  it is next revised; [CLAIM-434](../claims.d/CLAIM-434.md)'s parts 4 to 11 and the three potential
  terms already meet it.
- A review can cite this decision against a measure introduced without its
  cardinality stated.
- It costs some determinacy: entries will more often say "partially
  identified" or "incomparable" than give a number. That is the intended
  cost.
