# NOTE-tmp55ned: Plávala, General probabilistic theories: An introduction

## Contribution

The review introduces general probabilistic theories: operational theories that generalize finite-dimensional classical and quantum theory and also include more exotic ones, such as boxworld with its Popescu–Rohrlich boxes. It explains the basic concepts and elements of the framework in depth and proves several well-known results. It aims to be self-contained and consistent. Its main tool is convex geometry, supplemented by a diagrammatic notation used to write many equations.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 surveys GPT research directions with references: nonlocality, steering and PR boxes; uncertainty and incompatibility; noncontextuality; causal structure and interference; computation, resource theories and cryptography; entropy and thermodynamics; spectral decompositions; axiomatic reconstructions that usually land on Euclidean Jordan algebras. It notes the GPT/GPTs naming convention.
- Structure: §3 covers state spaces and effect algebras and their duality, order-unit and base norms, the no-restriction hypothesis (§3.7) and diagrammatic notation (§3.8). Then come classical theory as the worked example (§4), tensor products and entanglement (§5, with Theorem 5.21 characterising classicality via the minimal = maximal tensor product) and channels, measurements and instruments (§6).
- §7.1: a direct proof of no-broadcasting. Theorem 7.7 gives the equivalence of identity-is-measure-and-prepare, identity-is-self-compatible, existence of a universal broadcasting channel, and K being a simplex. Corollaries: every non-classical theory has incompatible channels, and not all its channels are measure-and-prepare.
- §7.3 covers incompatibility witnesses, steering and Bell nonlocality. §8–9 work quantum theory and boxworld as examples. Appendices A–C cover convex cones, duals and hyperplane separation, and tensor products.
