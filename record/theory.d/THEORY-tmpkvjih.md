# THEORY-tmpkvjih: On Sørensen and colleagues' scenario, an endosymbiont becomes an organelle in steps: the host first targets effector proteins into it; an imported host protein redundant with one of its functions then lets the endosymbiont's own gene decay, making it dependent; and a general import pathway, once evolved, brings in hundreds of proteins; this genetic integration arises only in permanent, vertically transmitted associations, and host effector control without vertical transmission, as in rhizobia, does not lead to it

## Source

Sørensen, Stiller, Kröninger & Nowack (2025), *The FEBS Journal*
292(12): 2992–3013, [LIT-1560](../literature.d/LIT-1560.md), read in full in [NOTE-tmpl32gd](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpl32gd.md) from the
PubMed Central copy. The rhizobia section's last paragraph, and the
fourth paragraph of "Conclusions and future directions".

## What was actually shown

Nothing was tested. The scenario is assembled from systems at
different stages:

- **Effectors without replacement.** The cereal weevil's peptide ColA
  enters its 20-million-year-old *Sodalis* symbiont and regulates its
  growth: "relatively recently captured endosymbionts can already be
  targeted by host effector proteins". Legume NCR peptides control
  bacteroid division and differentiation.
- **Single replacements.** RlpA4 in *Buchnera*, MurF in *Moranella*, two
  transaminases in *Nardonella* complete a function the symbiont lost.
- **Few targeted proteins.** Six in the diatom's spheroid bodies and
  seven in *Angomonas*'s symbiont, some at the symbiont's division site.
- **Hundreds.** The chromatophore and the nitroplast, each with a
  conserved targeting signal.

In the authors' words: "a eukaryotic host cell starts relatively early
on to target effector proteins into a bacterial endosymbiont ... Once a
nucleus‐encoded protein with functional redundancy to the endosymbiont
attains import capacity, deleterious mutations in the endosymbiont copy
can get fixed and—if the resulting process cannot be replaced by
metabolite import—the endosymbiont becomes dependent on the imported
host protein. ... As soon as a general import mechanism with a specific
targeting signal evolves, it likely spreads quickly".

On rhizobia: "we would not consider rhizobia as genetically integrated
organelles". They are not vertically transmitted, keep a soil life, and
have no function replaced. Effector manipulation is "an effective
alternative to the genetic integration that is always linked to a
permanent host/endosymbiont association with vertical transmission".

What could have failed: the systems could have shown no gradient, for
instance hundreds of imported proteins in a horizontally acquired
symbiont, or replaced functions with no effector stage anywhere.

## What this does not say

- **Not that any lineage went through these steps.** The stages are
  different organisms, ordered by the authors. A static gradient across
  systems fits several histories.
- **Not that effectors cause the later steps.** Effectors and replacing
  proteins are different proteins. Whether effector targeting builds the
  route later used for replacement is not argued.
- **Not that vertical transmission is sufficient.** *Buchnera* is
  vertically transmitted for over 100 million years and shows one
  imported protein.
- **Not the reason dependence is selected.** The scenario has mutations
  in the symbiont copy "get fixed" once the host copy covers them. That
  is consistent with drift under relaxed selection. The review names no
  selective advantage for the host or the symbiont.

## Connections

- **[QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md), the order of the dimensions.** Translated into the
  question's letters (the translation is mine): host control (C, as
  effectors) comes first; dependence (D) follows when a redundant
  imported protein lets the symbiont's gene go; the distribution of
  functions across genomes (G) shifts as import grows. Reproductive
  cohesion (R, vertical transmission) is a condition for the sequence to
  end in an organelle. Rhizobia are high C, no R and no genetic
  integration. This gives the question a definite order to test, and it
  is the reverse of [THEORY-1049](THEORY-1049.md)'s insect picture, where the symbiont's
  losses come first and force host supply.
- **[THEORY-1050](THEORY-1050.md) and [THEORY-1051](THEORY-1051.md).** Athreya et al. model D and R with no
  host control. This scenario makes host control the first step. The two
  are about different stages and do not conflict.
- **[LIT-1605](../literature.d/LIT-1605.md).** Frank's model of host control of transmission, unread,
  is the formal counterpart of R as a precondition.
