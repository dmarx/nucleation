# THEORY-919: Departments of the French Empire more shielded from British trade by the Napoleonic blockade added more mechanized cotton-spinning capacity during it and kept a larger industry through 1887, with more productive spinning firms in 1840 and higher industrial value added per capita in 1860 but not detectably from 1896

## Source

Juhász, [LIT-1489](../literature.d/LIT-1489.md), read in full in [NOTE-1122](../notes.d/NOTE-1122.md) from the working draft of
17 December 2015 (CIRJE copy): pp. 20–40 and Tables 1–5. The version of
record (American Economic Review 108(11), 2018) and the online appendices
were not read.

## What was actually shown

- **Short run.** Across 88 departments observed in 1803 and 1812, a larger
  rise in effective distance to London went with a larger rise in spindles
  per thousand inhabitants (coefficient 33.11, s.e. 9.78; Table 1). The
  interquartile difference in the shock predicts a gain about equal to mean
  1812 capacity. No pre-trend once market potential is controlled, and no
  effect on wool spinning, leather tanning, capital-labour ratios or machine
  vintage (Table 2).
- **Long run.** Instrumenting 1812 capacity with the shock, one more spindle
  in 1812 meant about 2–3 more in 1840 and 5–6 more in 1887 (Table 3);
  1840 firm productivity rose 0.2–0.5 standard deviations per standard
  deviation of 1812 capacity (Table 4); industrial value added per capita
  was higher in 1860 only (Table 5): "The point estimate decreases in size
  over time and from 1896 onwards, the coefficient is longer
  distinguishable statistically from zero" (sic, p. 38).

What could have come out otherwise: the blockade's northern industry could
have been Heckscher's "hothouse" growth and collapsed when British yarn
returned. It did not; the southern departments shrank instead.

## What this does not say

- **That a temporary tariff can be self-limiting.** No tariff was chosen:
  the protection was a side effect of a war blockade, which Napoleon did
  not intend for cotton. And it was not followed by free trade: "tariff and
  non-tariff trade barriers were put in place between the two countries"
  (p. 33). The within-France results compare more and less protected
  regions under continued national protection.
- **That protection pays.** The author says the evidence "does not imply a
  case for infant industry protection in similar settings" (p. 8), and the
  aggregate gain faded within about forty years.
- **That the instrument is strong.** "The Kleibergen-Papp F-statistic for
  the first stage is generally below 10" (p. 35).
- **That the mechanism is learning by doing.** That is suggested by firm
  entry, productivity and historical sources, not separated from pecuniary
  agglomeration.
