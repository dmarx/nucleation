# QUESTION-439: When rival communities use one expression with different application rules, inferences, practical responses and conditions of revision, is there one concept with context-dependent extension, several historically related concepts sharing an expression, or an organization of the rivalry among the uses; what observations tell these apart; and in which sense of individuation, if any, is the concept or the symbol individuated?

## Why it is a question

The owner's U19 of the exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and
Disposition" (https://chatgpt.com/share/6aee7a9b-f13c-83e9-a865-6a29c2c3ff7e):
"apply this individuation framework to examine individuation and
perpetuation of symbols and concepts themselves". The example was "anti-vax"
([CASE-222](../cases.d/CASE-222.md)).

U19 runs against [TERM-098](../terms.d/TERM-098.md) (Proposed), which says under "What it is not":
"Not a thing or entity. A thing is an individuated organization." "A
concept can be an entity in a theory without being a thing." A did not cite
that clause. U19 is the first owner turn found that tests it.

A41 §5 set out three options for one expression used by rival communities,
"Does that mean there are three concepts?":

1. a stable common core with context-sensitive extensions;
2. "distinct but historically related concepts sharing one expression";
3. the "system of relationships among the competing uses" as the most
   persistent structure ([CLAIM-621](../claims.d/CLAIM-621.md)).

It declined to choose: "Possibly. But we should not decide the question by
stipulation." A41 §11 proposed measuring first, with a concept-in-perspective
profile of application rule (A), inferential role (I), response structure
(R) and update organization (U): "The advantage is that we don't need to
resolve in advance whether they represent two separate concepts or two
perspectives on one concept." "We can measure the differences first."

**Which sense of individuation.** A41 §6 asked "When does “anti-vax” become
an individuated concept?" and answered with [TERM-098](../terms.d/TERM-098.md)'s marks of a concept.
In the record's terms being a concept ([TERM-098](../terms.d/TERM-098.md)) is not being individuated
([TERM-088](../terms.d/TERM-088.md)). The reader's qualification splits the question three ways:
whether a realization has conceptual organization ([TERM-098](../terms.d/TERM-098.md)); whether a
conceptual lineage persists by transmission ([CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md)); and whether
anything here is individuated in one of [TERM-088](../terms.d/TERM-088.md)'s senses. The plausible
candidate for the last is the expression as a recurrent pattern of use,
individuated descriptively or dynamically while sustained from outside.
A41 §10 found the fourth sense, organizational autonomy, "Not established".

## What bears on it

- A, I and U are [TERM-098](../terms.d/TERM-098.md)'s application, inferential role and
  corrigibility; R is efficacy ([TERM-073](../terms.d/TERM-073.md), [TERM-104](../terms.d/TERM-104.md)). Comparing profiles
  across communities needs a correspondence, so [CLAIM-249](../claims.d/CLAIM-249.md)'s relativity
  applies (the reader's qualification).
- Lineage is [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md)'s question, not this one.
- **A difference in revision alone does not decide for option 2.** The
  reader's qualification, from [TERM-098](../terms.d/TERM-098.md) and A54 of the second share
  (https://chatgpt.com/share/6aeea2bc-d280-83e9-ac1f-f6772adf263c):
  [TERM-098](../terms.d/TERM-098.md) "deliberately **does not make corrigibility part of being a
  concept**—so our earlier four-part concept profile should not treat
  different revision policies as automatically different concepts". A55
  §3 rewrites the profile as (A, I, R), with U, the "revision
  organization", kept apart: "**Two communities can realize effectively
  the same concept while having different capacities to correct its
  use.**" So the title's "conditions of revision" is no evidence of
  several concepts on its own. It may be a difference of perspective, as
  A41 §14 put corrigibility in the perspective ([CLAIM-620](../claims.d/CLAIM-620.md), layer 2),
  or of will-governance (A55 §4's CASE E, in [CASE-226](../cases.d/CASE-226.md)).
- **Two discriminators for option 2** (A55 §6, which re-asks it as
  "conceptual fission"): telling "historical descent from independent
  convergence" by "tracing transmission pathways", and not defining
  conceptual identity "by simple similarity of embeddings, symbol
  persistence, or any one numerical divergence threshold unless that
  criterion is independently defended".
- A41 §12's vignette design measures the profile by telling participants
  about cases, a do(I) in [CLAIM-130](../claims.d/CLAIM-130.md)'s sense, so it cannot reach the
  effects of the classification on those classified.

## What would count as an answer

For a stated expression, which option holds under a stated correspondence
between communities' profiles, and on what evidence; and, separately, which
of [TERM-088](../terms.d/TERM-088.md)'s senses, if any, the expression or the concept meets. A
difference in U alone does not decide for option 2 ([TERM-098](../terms.d/TERM-098.md)). The study
that would measure the profiles is [CASE-230](../cases.d/CASE-230.md).
