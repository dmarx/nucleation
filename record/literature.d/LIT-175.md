# LIT-175: Evidence in Medicine and Evidence-Based Medicine

John Worrall (2007), *Philosophy Compass, 2(6), 981–1022* — DOI-10.1111/j.1747-9991.2007.00106.x

## Key takeaways

- The EBM evidence hierarchy's privileging of RCTs lacks a sound rationale — none of the standard arguments for randomization (Fisher's significance-testing logic, control of unknown confounders, selection bias, observational exaggeration) shows that randomization adds evidential weight beyond balancing plausible confounders.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Medicine, like any rational activity, obviously has to rest on evidence; the interesting question is how general principles of evidence apply there. Worrall focuses on the Evidence-Based Medicine movement and raises difficulties for the rationales given for its evidence hierarchy, especially the privileged place of RCTs and meta-analyses of RCTs. He does not oppose a scientific approach to medical evidence. His aim is to show where philosophers of science can help implement that approach properly.
