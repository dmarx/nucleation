# CASE-170: The East Wing of the White House, 2025–2026: the wing demolished for a ballroom before the legal challenge to the project was decided, so that the remedy still available was to stop new construction, not to keep the old building

## The case

The owner, at U7, quoting A6 ("The means used to establish control can
therefore change the practical conditions under which that control can be
challenged"): "another instance of this is the destruction of the East
Wing of the white house and the courts throwing out the legal challenge to
it."

In October 2025 the East Wing of the White House was demolished to make
way for a ballroom, and the project was challenged in court. A7 corrected
the owner's premise with a court history this filing has not checked and
keeps as the exchange's: on 7 August 2026 the D.C. Circuit affirmed a
preliminary injunction against above-ground ballroom construction,
finding that the challengers had standing and were likely to succeed on
their statutory arguments, while allowing security-related work; on 31
August the Supreme Court stayed the injunction, finding the government
likely to prevail on standing and crediting declarations that the
ballroom's dimensions were needed to protect underground facilities and
that delay would create security problems. A7: "The Supreme Court did
**not** finally decide that the demolition or construction was lawful."

## What it shows

- **Physical irreversibility before review** ([CLAIM-464](../claims.d/CLAIM-464.md)). By the time any court
  ruled, the wing was gone. A7: "An order stopping the new building and an
  order restoring the old one are fundamentally different remedies." In
  A9's terms, t_action < t_loss < t_effective remedy: a fully successful
  later proceeding cannot preserve the interest that justified it.
- **The project as an argument for itself.** A7: "the architecture of the
  ongoing project became part of the argument for why construction should
  continue." It is the remedy problem changed by the act under review, of
  the kind A6 found at USIP ([CASE-176](CASE-176.md)), by a different mechanism: material
  rather than coercive.
- **A contrast for search** ([QUESTION-279](../questions.d/QUESTION-279.md)). A10: preserving the original fabric is
  infeasible after demolition, so here no remedy for that objective exists
  at all, where at CECOT ([CASE-175](CASE-175.md)) a remedy existed and was disputed. "A
  useful theory must distinguish a remedy that truly no longer exists from
  one that exists but cannot be discovered or activated."

## What it does not show

- **That the demolition was unlawful.** No final judgment on the merits is
  reported.
- **That the security concerns were invented.** A7: "We should not simply
  declare all the government's security concerns self-created or
  fictitious." The narrower point is that the state of the project entered
  the weighing.
- **Intent to foreclose review.** A8: demonstrating that costs rose "is not
  sufficient to establish deliberate obstruction".
- **Standing.** The demolition is documented; the appellate and Supreme
  Court rulings of August 2026 are the exchange's, after this filing's
  knowledge, and unchecked.
