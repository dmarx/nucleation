# THEORY-tmpk5cgl: Stern argues that Darwall's account, on which moral obligation consists in what persons have legitimate second-personal authority to demand of one another rather than in actual demands, cannot explain obligations to aid a sufferer who makes no demand, so that wronging, or exclusionary reasons, may suffice for obligation without second-personal authority

Stern (2014), LIT-tmp1wuw5, on Darwall (2006), LIT-tmpr2bbq.

## The account

Darwall: obligation "involves the exercise of legitimate authority over the
person obliged" (p. 2), and it holds if demands "could legitimately" be
made, whatever the "actual demands" (p. 3). Better guidance does not make
authority (p. 7). Stern: "(1) A person’s suffering does not give a
second-personal reason to act (2) Obligations require second-personal
reasons (3) The suffering of others can give us obligations" (p. 12);
Darwall must give one up, and Stern thinks it should be (2).

## What it does not show

- **The 2006 book's argument.** The sketch is short and the detail comes
  from the 2013 essays.
- **That wronging suffices.** Stern offers it as an alternative, not a
  proof.
