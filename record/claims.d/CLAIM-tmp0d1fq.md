# CLAIM-tmp0d1fq: Contracts include implicit ones: an unspoken understanding can form as expectations and assumptions built on consistently observed behaviour evolve into dependencies, with no agreement ever made

## The claim

The owner's words, at U16: "importantly, we should ensure we have  space
for characterizing implicit contracts, e.g. an unspoken understanding that
evolved through evolution of expectations and assumptions based on
consistently observed behaviors that evolved into dependencies".

The thesis is a formation route, in three steps the owner names:

1. **Consistently observed behaviour.** One party, or process, behaves in a
   regular way.
2. **Expectations and assumptions.** Another comes to expect it and to
   assume it in what it does.
3. **Dependencies.** The assumption becomes a dependence: the second
   party's activity would now be impaired if the behaviour stopped.

What results is an "unspoken understanding", a contract in the sense the
exchange had been building at U14 and A16 (a maintained relationship that
governs the participants' later activity), but one nobody stated or agreed
to. The owner asks that the account of contracts make room for it.

## What it does not say

- Not that every regularity, expectation or dependence is a contract. A20
  §3 keeps the distinction ("A plant depends on sunlight. That does not
  ordinarily constitute a contract between the plant and the sun"), and
  the owner's route ends in a contract only where the dependence comes to
  govern how the parties respond to its failure. Where that line falls is
  [CLAIM-tmpersgx](CLAIM-tmpersgx.md)'s, with its six probes (it supersedes [CLAIM-tmp1nubb](CLAIM-tmp1nubb.md)).
- Not that an implicit contract binds anyone morally. That expectation and
  reliance do not by themselves make anything owed is [CLAIM-tmp0i6c0](CLAIM-tmp0i6c0.md), on
  [CLAIM-738](CLAIM-738.md).
- Not that the parties exist, fully formed, before the contract. A20 §17
  draws the stronger consequence, that "Relationships can form first. Their
  participants can subsequently individuate through them"; that is A's, not
  the owner's, and is left to the account of individuation.

## Relation to existing work

The owner's route has neighbours the exchange names and the record holds in
part:

- **Relational contracts** in economics, sustained by the value of future
  dealings rather than by enforcement (Baker, Gibbons and Murphy 2002,
  registered as [LIT-tmp1xqzu](../literature.d/LIT-tmp1xqzu.md), not read).
- **Psychological and implied contracts** in organizational psychology
  (Rousseau 1989, [LIT-tmpwdxhm](../literature.d/LIT-tmpwdxhm.md), not read), which separate what a party
  perceives as owed from what the relationship implies.
- **Convention** (Lewis 1969, [LIT-473](../literature.d/LIT-473.md), Unavailable), coordination kept up
  by mutual expectation.
- **The Black Queen** route to dependence by adaptive loss ([LIT-1568](../literature.d/LIT-1568.md), read;
  [CLAIM-603](CLAIM-603.md)), which A20 §4 uses as a precedent "for the development of
  dependencies upon which stronger contractual arrangements might form".
- **Incomplete contracts** ([CLAIM-728](CLAIM-728.md)), where what the contract leaves
  unspecified is settled by whoever holds residual control.

The owner's addition to these is the direction: a contract reached from
below, by the history of expectation and dependence, among parties that
need not be agents able to agree.

## Standing

Proposed, the owner's. It extends the idea of contracts between processes
introduced at U14 (filed from that turn); the dependency is wired by the
filing's closing pass.
