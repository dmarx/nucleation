# ARG-tmpsh7he: Accounts of impersonal domination, Marcuse's administration, Han's seductive power and auto-exploitation and Benjamin's default discrimination, show that a dependence lacking anyone with consequential discretion can still be domination or unfreedom on serious accounts, so the domination threshold holds only as a test for domination by an arrangement's controllers

## The inference

1. [THEORY-tmpnydju](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpnydju.md): Marcuse holds that "domination no longer requires force or the presence of an authority figure"; "all domination assumes the form of administration".
2. [THEORY-tmpclnon](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpclnon.md): neoliberal power "tries to make them dependent", and "There is no person opposite"; [THEORY-tmp5hgen](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp5hgen.md): freedom and constraint coincide in auto-exploitation, though Han calls this "the disappearance of domination".
3. [THEORY-tmpo6ipc](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpo6ipc.md): "default discrimination", subordination by defaults and inherited data with no discretionary act now.
4. So a complete dependence can lack anyone holding consequential discretion and still be domination (Marcuse, Benjamin) or unfreedom (Han). Version 1's clause "is not domination, however complete" is false on those accounts.

## Critical questions

- **Do the sources say what the inference needs?** Marcuse and Benjamin, as read, call the constraint domination or subordination; Han denies it is domination and calls it unfreedom. So the readings refute the clause as a claim about domination on two accounts, and as a claim about freedom on all three.
- **Could the claim answer that administrative systems and defaults still have controllers?** In part: Benjamin's designers exercised discretion once, at design time; administrators exist. But the readings' point is that the constraint does not run through anyone's present discretion. The reply would make "discretion" cover design-time choice, which changes the claim more than narrowing does.
- **Are the sources reliable?** Marcuse is read through Farr's SEP entry, Han through one chapter and one essay, Benjamin through one chapter. Secondary for Marcuse.
- **Verdict.** Proposed. The claim is narrowed at version 2 to a test for domination by an arrangement's controllers, and the four readings now bound it.
