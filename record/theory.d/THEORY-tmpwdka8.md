# THEORY-tmpwdka8: Kosterlitz's Nobel lecture holds that the two-dimensional planar rotor and superfluid film have a finite-temperature topologically ordered phase without long-range order, in which winding-number sectors are separated by barriers growing as ln L, while the 2D Heisenberg model's invariant has a barrier of order unity and no ordered state; the phase ends when vortex pairs unbind at πK = 2, and a finite penetration depth, which cuts off the logarithmic interaction, frees vortices at any T > 0

## Source

Kosterlitz (2016), "Topological Defects and Phase Transitions", Nobel
Lecture, LIT-tmp3k6np, read in NOTE-tmp0si2r.

## The account

Long-range order is absent in 2D (p. 87), but "there can be a more subtle
type of order called topological order" (p. 89). Winding numbers "are
numbers defining a particular metastable state", with "an energy barrier
proportional to the logarithm of the system size" between them; in the
2D Heisenberg model the barrier "is of order unity", "which implies that
there is no ordered state" (pp. 90–91). A free vortex has
"∆F = ∆E − T∆S = kBT(πK0(T) − 2) ln(L/a)" (p. 92); the transition comes
"when πK(∞) = 2" (p. 93). A finite penetration depth "limits the range of
the logarithmic interaction between vortices", so in a charged film "the
vortices are always free at any T > 0" (p. 98).

## What would have come out otherwise

Had a vortex's energy grown faster or slower than its entropy, ln L
against ln L², there would be no temperature where they balance: always
bound or always free.

## What it does not say

- That the 1973 paper derived the line of fixed points or η(T): the
  lecture places the RG in Kosterlitz (1974).
- That the phase is critical in the sense of a tuned transition.
