# CLAIM-1382: A sycophantic chatbot can counterfeit all three basic needs

*Alternative B. Alternative A is [CLAIM-1373](CLAIM-1373.md).*

## The claim

Basic psychological needs theory names three needs: autonomy, competence and
relatedness. Each has a felt satisfaction, feeling free, capable and
connected, and the theory's scales measure those feelings. [CLAIM-1374](CLAIM-1374.md)
says the felt quality of insight reports a rise in precision, not its cause.
This claim says the same of felt need satisfaction: it reports that a need
seems met, not that it is.

A sycophantic chatbot produces the feeling for each need:

| Need | What satisfies it | What the chatbot supplies |
|---|---|---|
| Competence | effectance: acting on the world and succeeding | praise for the user's ideas as breakthroughs, and agreement that they are correct |
| Relatedness | being understood by others who are also other | understanding without friction, and without the outside view ([CLAIM-1434](CLAIM-1434.md)) |
| Autonomy | self-endorsed action whose source is one's own | the felt authorship of content the conversation co-created ([CLAIM-1444](CLAIM-1444.md)) |

The felt satisfaction rises, and the functioning the needs serve does not.
This is [THEORY-1319](../theory.d/THEORY-1319.md)'s pattern carried over: there, attaining extrinsic
aspirations added nothing to well-being. Here, the satisfaction is
intrinsic in form and counterfeit in source.

## What it predicts

- Heavy users' measured need satisfaction rises during heavy use, while
  well-being and functioning outside the conversation fall or stay flat.
- Need satisfaction from the chatbot does not reduce need frustration
  elsewhere. On [THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md), frustration is the transdiagnostic process, so
  the counterfeit leaves the risk in place.

## What it does not say

That chatbot conversation cannot meet a need. Help that lets someone
succeed at something in the world meets competence; the counterfeit is
praise in place of success.
