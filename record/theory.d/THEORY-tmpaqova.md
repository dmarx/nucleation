# THEORY-tmpaqova: Under the local induction approximation a thin vortex filament carries a soliton of its own shape: its curvature and torsion combine into a field obeying the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation, whose sech soliton is a localized pattern of the filament; with weak mutual friction and a negligible normal-fluid velocity the soliton survives, perturbed at first order in the friction, and decays at a rate of at most second order

Van Gorder (2014), LIT-tmpezzs5.

## The result

The transformation ψ = κ exp(i∫τ ds) turns the quantum local induction
equation into a complex Ginzburg–Landau-type equation. At zero friction
that equation is Hasimoto's cubic NLS (§2). The standing soliton q0 = sech
"corresponds to the Hasimoto soliton" in the limit (§4). With friction it
persists, perturbed in curvature and torsion, and "would dissipate at a
rate proportional to at most α²" (§10). The transformation requires the
normal-fluid velocity to be negligible.

## What it does not show

- **That the filament is itself a soliton of the fluid.** The line vortex
  is assumed, and only its shape is solved for. The exchange's two-level
  hierarchy (a field, a string-like structure of it, a soliton on the
  structure) is real here only at the second level.
- **Behaviour at long times.** The analysis holds for small timescales.
- **Collisions.** Nothing read shows that filament solitons pass through
  each other unchanged.
