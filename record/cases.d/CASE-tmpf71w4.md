# A proposed study of assisted learning: learners matched on starting skill practise a skill under an assistant that does the hard step for them or one that does only what they cannot yet do, with unassisted performance measured at intervals and after the assistance is withdrawn, to test whether the capability trap of learning by doing holds for a human skill

## The case

A17 §V:

> Consider an AI system assisting someone with intellectual work.
>
> One design immediately performs the difficult reasoning and supplies the
> answer.
>
> Another helps the person reason through the problem, offering assistance
> when necessary.

and: "if the relevant skill develops through exercise and declines through
disuse, the long-term consequences could differ."

**Arms.** Learners matched on unassisted skill at the start practise one
skill (a class of proof, a programming task, a language) for a fixed
period under one of:

- **substitutive assistance**: the assistant does the hard step whenever
  asked, as CLAIM-605's support P = min(τC, 1 − A);
- **capacity-first assistance**: the assistant gives only the part the
  learner cannot yet do, after the learner has tried, as P = min(τC, 1);
- **no assistance**, for the unassisted trajectory.

**Measures.** Unassisted performance on fresh items at intervals during
the period and at several delays after the assistance is withdrawn;
assisted output during the period; time on task.

**Held fixed.** Total practice time, the items, the starting skill.

## What it can show

CLAIM-605's model makes three predictions that a study of this design can
check against people: substitutive assistance keeps assisted output whole
while unassisted performance falls; beyond some duration, withdrawal no
longer restores unassisted performance (a threshold); capacity-first
assistance leaves unassisted performance at least where it was. The first
without the second would show substitution without a trap, and
CLAIM-tmp6wuql's point about evaluation would still stand for the period
of support. Neither result would show anything about AI in general, or
about skills that are not learned by doing.

## What it cannot show, given its standing

It is stipulated and unrun. Whether a skill decays with disuse at the rate
the model needs, and whether there is a threshold below which practice no
longer rebuilds it, are what it would find out; nothing here assumes them.
