# THEORY-tmp8m1e4: Crawford holds that attention has become a collective, cultural problem because shared spaces treat people as a captive audience whose mental space can be monetized, that distraction is a problem of political economy rather than of technology in itself, and that attention should be protected as an attentional commons by those who design shared environments

## The account

Crawford: "today attention has become a collective problem – a cultural one - of modern life"; "people are a captive audience and that their mental space could be monetized"; "distraction is not a problem of technology in itself. It is rather a problem of political economy." Smart cities, "controlled by what could be seen as a cartel of ICT companies", make citizens "captive and dependent" and remove the need for the skills that kept attention in use. The remedy is "an attentional commons".

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-tmpp5kql](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpp5kql.md), results 1–7.

## What it does not say

The book's positive account: skilled practice, the ecology of attention, jigs and affordances. Nor that any technology as such causes distraction; Crawford denies it.
