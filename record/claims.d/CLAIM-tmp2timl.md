# CLAIM-tmp2timl: Replacing publication count with a count taken from a maintained record, of claims, corrections, endorsements or dependencies, reproduces the proxy problem it replaces, because researchers will shape the record to the count; an accountable history of contributions is evidence for an evaluation and not a score computed from the record

## The claim

A21 of the exchange "Hyper article interpretation"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6b064f1b-9ea8-83e8-951e-634ed41675b6): "The
moment you reward researchers for maintaining the largest knowledge graph,
you're going to get enormous knowledge graphs full of individually
identified trivia. Likewise, rewarding the most corrections encourages
unnecessary revisions. Rewarding endorsements encourages popularity
contests. Rewarding downstream dependencies can encourage people to
construct elaborate networks of superficial references."

A24 §6 grounds this in two Active claims:

- [CLAIM-402](CLAIM-402.md): a category that institutions act on can produce the
  regularities that confirm it. A24: "The resulting proliferation of papers
  is not independent evidence that paper count was a good measure of
  scientific contribution. The measure helped produce the behavior it
  records." A count over a record is a category of the same kind.
- [CLAIM-266](CLAIM-266.md): probes that supply an optimized system's training signal
  cannot also certify what it preserves. A24: "the evaluations used to
  reward researchers should not rely exclusively on the same signals those
  researchers were incentivized to optimize."

A24's conclusion: "an accountable history of contributions is evidence for
evaluation, not a replacement for evaluation ... It shouldn't become an
automatic score calculated from the topology of the record."

## What it does not say

- Not that metrics are useless. A24: "The lesson isn't that metrics are
  useless."
- A24 adds [CLAIM-404](CLAIM-404.md)'s two routes, the evidence an evaluator receives and
  the criteria it weighs evidence by, as two vulnerabilities of a
  feed-based journal. That bears on the journal model (U4 of the same
  exchange), not on this claim.
- Both premises are stated for categories and for optimized systems
  generally. Applying them to research evaluation is this claim's own step,
  and is not shown by either.
