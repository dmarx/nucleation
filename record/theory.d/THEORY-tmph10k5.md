# THEORY-tmph10k5: Bovier, Eckhoff, Gayrard and Klein hold that for a reversible Markov chain with generically separated metastable points, minus the logarithm of the capacity between them is an ultrametric up to an additive ln 3, the mean time to leave a point's valley is the valley's mass over that capacity up to a factor 1 + o(1), and each metastable point owns one low-lying eigenvalue equal to the inverse of that time, with an eigenvector near the valley's indicator

## Source

Bovier, Eckhoff, Gayrard and Klein, LIT-tmpl98s1, read in NOTE-tmpjot4l from arXiv math/0007160v1 (26 Jul 2000).

## What was actually shown

Under Definitions 1.1 and 1.2: Corollary 2.6 and (2.27), the ln 3 almost-ultrametric; Theorem 1.3, exit times, eigenvalues and exponential laws; Theorem 5.1 and Lemma 5.3, the ordering of eigenvalues by nested exclusion sets.

## What this does not say

Nothing about finite rate ratios, commute times, or degenerate (non-generic) landscapes; it is not a small-noise ε^U result.

## Connections

THEORY-1366 (Freidlin and Wentzell, logarithmic asymptotics); THEORY-1370 (Berglund on Kramers' law, whose promote_when names a later paper by the same group); CLAIM-581 (the peak-cost ultrametric); THEORY-tmppuotc (Bachas and Huberman: dynamics on the tree).
