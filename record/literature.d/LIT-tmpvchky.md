# LIT-tmpvchky: World models, artificial general intelligence and the hard problems of life–mind continuity: toward a unified understanding of natural and artificial intelligence

Adam Safron, Michael Levin, Victoria Klimaj, Zahra Sheikhbahaee, Dalton Sakthivadivel, Adeel Razi, et al. (2026), *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A 384(2320), 20240533 (introduction to the theme issue "World models in natural and artificial intelligence")* — DOI-10.1098/rsta.2024.0533

## Key takeaways

- "World model" is used in senses that range from any information a system cognizes over, to explicit causal structure, to causal prediction in the service of agency. LLMs clearly meet only the first, and the editors argue that world models are context-dependent constructions shaped by embodiment, goals and social learning, not neutral abstractions.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

This editorial introduces a theme issue on how natural and artificial intelligences model the world, and what that reveals about cognition and the continuity of life and mind. The editors take no single definition. They survey causal, self-referential, goal-directed, collective and narrative forms of world modelling. A recurring question is whether AI trained on vast data captures the context-sensitive, temporally embedded, value-laden aspects of biological world modelling, or mainly exploits statistical surface regularities. Contributions also address embodiment, complexity, learning architectures and the social settings of world models. The aim is to clarify the conceptual landscape and to frame questions about grounded understanding, robust agency and human-like general intelligence.
