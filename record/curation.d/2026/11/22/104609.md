The owner's "go ahead with the API sweep of the Unfetched works", 2026-11-22, after asking that blocked hosts be treated as unblocked. The assistant declined that part: a site's bot check is the site's own refusal, and no one else's consent overrides it. It proposed the sanctioned machine routes in its place. Results, scripts and tests are in `scratch/studies/unfetched-api-sweep/` on branch `claude/unfetched-api-sweep`.

## How it was run

- **Inventory.** Every LIT with status Unfetched on staging on 2026-11-22: 184 works. 170 carry a DOI and 14 only a URL.
- **Discovery, per DOI.**
  - OpenAlex, with no email.
  - Unpaywall, sent `noreply@anthropic.com` as [ADR-008](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-008.md) allows; no other address was sent anywhere.
  - Europe PMC search by DOI.
  - The NCBI ID converter, with no email. A PMC id found there adds Europe PMC's full-text endpoint as the first candidate.
- **Probe.** One GET per candidate copy, at most six per work, with no retries.
  - Each response was classed as a PDF, an HTML page, a challenge (403, 429, 503 or a challenge page), a refusal, not found, or an error.
  - A host that challenged was not contacted again in the run.
- **Two runs.** The first run held its results in memory and was stopped at 162 of 184 works by the background time limit. Its probe details were lost; only its outcome log survives (`out/first_run_outcomes.log`). The second run wrote one line per work, resumed by code and was checkpointed into the branch every ten minutes. It was seeded with thirteen hosts that had already challenged this session, so they were not contacted again. The loss is the subject of a devlog note and of ADR-tmpvqx5l / ADR-tmpyb409.

## The hosts that refused

Sixty-one hosts challenged or refused the client in this run (`out/challenged_hosts.json`). Besides those already known (APA, Wiley, Project Euclid, IOP, PMC's pages, PhilArchive and PhilSci-Archive, bioRxiv, the Wayback Machine), they include:
- many institutional repositories: Harvard DASH, eScholarship, LSE, MIT DSpace, and several Digital Commons and ScholarWorks sites;
- publishers: Elsevier (ScienceDirect, Cell), Nature, PNAS, SAGE, Taylor & Francis, Annual Reviews, MIT Press, Project MUSE and ACM;
- the handle resolver, and Wikipedia.

The client named itself as a research script. A 403 to it is the site's refusal, and the sweep stopped there each time, without retrying or working around it. Many of these copies are lawful under [ADR-074](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-074.md), and a person in a browser can probably open them.

## What follows

- The 26 works in the two lists above go through read-and-file in batches, one reader per work, under the persistence rule of ADR-tmpvqx5l / ADR-tmpyb409. A work read moves to Active with its NOTE and THEORY. A page that turns out to hold no copy gets a dated note, and the work stays Unfetched.
- No LIT was changed by the sweep itself. Each work's routes and outcome are in `out/outcomes.tsv`, and each reading adds its dated note when it is filed.
- For the owner: the refused hosts above, and in particular the institutional repositories, whose copies are author manuscripts that a person could fetch.
