# SECTION-009: Objections and conclusion

## Manuscript text

**Does the proposal merely rename organizational closure?** The concepts of constraint, constraint maintenance, and closure have direct predecessors in Moreno and in Montévil and Mossio. The contribution claimed here is not priority for those mechanisms but an analysis of their evidential relation to dynamical coherence, boundary selection, and metaphysical identity. A fully revised manuscript must demonstrate this contribution against the mechanistic-constitution literature as well.

**Is provenance merely an artifact of a boundary chosen beforehand?** Internal and external are relative to a candidate boundary, but the presence and location of productive mechanisms are objective relative to that specification. Causal investigation may require revising the boundary. It cannot produce one uniquely privileged boundary independently of every kind and explanatory criterion.

**Why not identify individuals with the complete productive mechanisms?** Doing so remains a possible account for some kinds. But one productive arrangement can sustain several products, and a product can be maintained by distributed processes not themselves one individual. An identification between mechanism and individual therefore requires an additional constitutive principle, not merely a causal equation.

**Does pluralism proliferate arbitrary individuals?** The article distinguishes the objective satisfaction of specific organizational criteria from the stronger metaphysical thesis that each adequate criterion picks out a numerically distinct individual. The latter is not established by the present constructions. The former is compatible with genuine error: a proposed closure, maintenance mechanism, or historical transmission can fail to exist.

The resulting thesis is constrained. An organization's present state does not generally determine the provenance or resilience of the mechanisms maintaining it. Causal provenance is a distinct object of investigation, whose relevance to an individual's constitution must be defended rather than assumed. Recursive individuation names a significant form of productive organization; it is not a universal boundary test. To understand an individual may therefore require distinguishing the organization it currently exhibits from the organization of processes through which that unity is sustained, transformed, or lost.
