# LIT-135: Conscious artificial intelligence and biological naturalism

Anil K. Seth (2024), *Behavioral and Brain Sciences 49, e315 (target article; online 2025-04-21, volume 2026). First posted as a PsyArXiv preprint.* — DOI-10.1017/S0140525X25000032

## Key takeaways

- Conscious AI needs both computational functionalism and enough substrate flexibility to run consciousness-relevant computations in silicon, and both are doubtful. Seth argues that positive, non-vitalist reasons from predictive processing, the free energy principle and autopoiesis tie consciousness to being alive, so real AI consciousness is unlikely on current trajectories, while conscious-*seeming* AI is nearly inevitable and ethically pressing.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Seth asks whether advancing AI might become conscious as well as intelligent. He diagnoses the biases that make people expect it (anthropocentrism, human exceptionalism, anthropomorphism, and the resulting conflation of intelligence with consciousness). He questions whether computation suffices for consciousness and argues instead for biological naturalism, on which consciousness depends on our being living organisms. After surveying scenarios for artificial consciousness, he concludes that it is unlikely on current trajectories but grows more plausible as AI becomes more brain-like or life-like. He ends with the ethics of AI that is, or convincingly seems to be, conscious: "If we sell our minds too cheaply to our machine creations, we not only overestimate them – we underestimate ourselves."
