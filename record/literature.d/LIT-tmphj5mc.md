# LIT-tmphj5mc: The Misfortunes of the Dead

Pitcher, G. (1984). *American Philosophical Quarterly*, 21(2), 183–188. Reprinted in J. M. Fischer (Ed.), *The Metaphysics of Death* (pp. 157–168). Stanford, CA: Stanford University Press, 1993.

Not read. The summary is drawn from the abstract or from what the work is
standardly cited for. A reading should check it first.

## Why this is here

The classic case for interests that reach past one's death. If one can have interests in one's legacy, acting for it may be self-interested without any extended self; that is an alternative to the thesis's reading. For [QUESTION-tmpruhu4](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpruhu4.md).

## Read in its place

The work cannot be read here, so the record holds it at second hand.

Luper's SEP entry (NOTE-tmpl7p19) gives Pitcher's position in two places.
In §4.2 it credits him with priorism: a desire you have now can be
thwarted by a death months away, so the death may make you worse off now.
In §6.2 it gives his account of posthumous harm: posthumous events harm us
by making true things that thwart desires we had while alive, as slander
does a desire for one's good name, or a child's later illness one's desire
for her happy upbringing. The harm falls on the living person, indirectly,
without backward causation. It reports the replies of Velleman, Partridge,
Vorobej and Suits. Missing still: the ante-mortem / post-mortem person
distinction that the LIT summary names, Pitcher's own wording and
argument, the broken-promise example (the entry gives the deathbed promise
to Nagel), and correct page numbers (the entry's citation of Pitcher is
wrong).
