# SYNTHESIS-tmp5blqy: What a normative judgment about an organization needs: viability does not establish what ought to persist, a judgment is well-defined only once its relata are fixed and can then be false, some judgments of continuity, reform and obligation need histories, and a self-maintaining system's failure norm is proposed to be set by its own upkeep, while what fixes a signal's content is left open

*A part account of the organizational-normativity line ("Normative judgment about organizations"), filed at the synthesis review of the line on 2026-11-15. The line's account, SYNTHESIS-038, is built over it and three sibling parts. Its first nine components were mapped in SYNTHESIS-038 versions 1 to 3, and that mapping is kept here unchanged; the rest were filed from 2026-11-07 to 2026-11-12 and are new to any account. Where a component declares which sense of "organization" it means (TERM-074), the account says so; where it declares none, the account says that, and assigns none.*

## The account

**The limit.** CLAIM-075 (the paper's, Sections 6.10 and 7.3; disclaimed,
interpretive, Active): viability does not establish what ought to
persist; "An unjust institution may be highly viable." It complements
CLAIM-518, CLAIM-352, CLAIM-523 and CLAIM-547. Its note of 2026-11-12 says
two readings corroborate it "for sense-3 wholes (TERM-074)"; the claim
itself declares no sense. QUESTION-128 (the owner's U16 of 2026-10-17;
Open) asks whether the organizational account gives a path to normativity
from first principles: "can norms of function and failure be defined from
an organization's own dynamics, and what more do practical and moral
authority need?"

**Relata, then truth.** CLAIM-363 (the owner's U17 and U18; thesis,
interpretive, Active) rests on CLAIM-075 and CLAIM-152, uses TERM-059 and
complements CLAIM-405: a normative judgment is well-defined only once its
relata are fixed ("the organization or standpoint it concerns, the
standard, the scale and the context"), "and that dependence does not by
itself make it relative: once they are fixed, the judgment can be false".
TERM-059 (the owner's name; Active) states relational normative realism
and says its third condition, constraints on admissible perspectives, is
not established. QUESTION-129 (Open) refines QUESTION-128 and asks whether
such judgments can be objective about the standards and perspectives
themselves.

**Histories.** CLAIM-518 (the owner's U26 of 2026-10-31, narrowed the same
day and again on 2026-11-06; thesis, interpretive, Active) rests on
CLAIM-363 and CLAIM-350, bears on QUESTION-128 and QUESTION-256, and
complements CLAIM-075 and CLAIM-348: judgments about an organization's
continuity, reform or obligations can depend on diachronic relations that
present states and terminal outcomes do not show. Its note of 2026-11-12
says Thompson corroborates it "for sense-3 wholes (TERM-074)". ARG-043
(Proposed) concludes it from Bencherki's checklist and daycare (LIT-1901,
THEORY-1285). CLAIM-512 (the assistant's, A26 and A27; counter,
interpretive, Active) objects to CLAIM-518 and CLAIM-608 with a dilemma and
a distinction; CLAIM-518's version 3 concedes the broad horn, and the
clause it dropped is CLAIM-608 (the record's gloss; thesis, Proposed),
which rests on CLAIM-518 and CLAIM-363 and holds that histories add a
relatum only if counterfactual discrimination, transfer or causal
specificity is shown. QUESTION-256 (A26's, from the owner's case of Clive
Wearing; Open) asks which continuities self-governance needs.

**Corrigibility, not claimed.** CLAIM-511 (the owner's question; disclaimed,
interpretive, Proposed; first line distributed-agency) is the bare
proposition that a self-maintaining organization ought to preserve its
corrigibility. It bears on QUESTION-128, QUESTION-129 and QUESTION-087 and
complements CLAIM-462; its summary says the record's constructions do not
establish it and its value is conditional (CLAIM-520).

**A failure norm set by the system's own upkeep.** CLAIM-648 (the
assistant's, a reading across the filing of "Meaning Transmission
Explained"; thesis, interpretive, Proposed; also record-as-apparatus)
rests on the owner's CLAIM-649, grounds in Bickhard (THEORY-220, Proposed)
and CASE-241, and bears on QUESTION-128: a self-maintaining system that
anticipates an interaction will sustain it "holds an expected response
profile set by its own upkeep", the shortfall means by its own lights
that the interaction failed, and that norm and a designer's profile "come
apart where the system's persistence is at stake". ARG-083 (Proposed)
concludes it, and says the construction shows the two norms differ, not
that a simulated self-set norm is the system's own. CASE-241 (the
assistant's construction; fictional, Proposed; a variant of CASE-240, the
debugger's test) builds both foragers: they agree on a silent patch and
part when the yield falls but still pays and when the upkeep rises past
an unchanged yield. CASE-239 (a proposed study, stipulated, Proposed;
variant of CASE-241; bears on QUESTION-128 and QUESTION-285) is the forager
the review of #483 asked for, one that learns which distinctions predict a
loss of viability; its summary names the threat that "the designer still
chooses the learning rule and the viability variable". None of the four
declares a sense of "organization"; their subject is a self-maintaining
system.

**What a signal's content is answerable to.** CLAIM-650 (the assistant's,
revised at the review of #483 relayed on 2026-11-12; thesis, interpretive,
Proposed; also pragmatic-transport) grounds in Grice (THEORY-444) and the
owner's smoke detector (CASE-243) and bears on QUESTION-128: a designed
alarm's content can be false, its error lies in a non-factive content,
and what makes "fire" the content it is supposed to carry is "answered
for this designed alarm by its designer's intention and left open,
between selection history and organizational maintenance, for other
systems". It says CLAIM-648 takes up the second. ARG-082 (Proposed)
concludes the factivity half; ARG-081 (Rejected) is the step from
non-factivity to intentional origin, which "does not follow from
factivity alone".

**Surrogate authority.** QUESTION-454 (A's, A9 §5 of "Medical Ethics and
Media Enshitification", 2026-11-11; Open; also will-organization) asks
what makes one agent's authority to decide about another's body
legitimate when the other has standing but cannot decide: welfare,
earlier wishes, projected future wishes, or the relation. It cites
QUESTION-364, TERM-083, THEORY-464 and CASE-128, none of them in this
line, and stores no relation. Its note of 2026-11-12 (the assistant's,
accepted by the owner) sorts its cases by TERM-100's formation and
maintenance provenance, and says that if only maintenance provenance can
be read off a present organization, "a surrogate's authority cannot be
checked from the present arrangement alone, which is CLAIM-518's point
about histories", calling it "An interpretive mapping, not an answer".
