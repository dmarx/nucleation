# THEORY-1704: Boddy and colleagues (2015) argue that fetal microchimerism's paradoxical effects on maternal health reflect a mix of aligned and conflicting mother-offspring fitness interests, so fetal cells are a selected extension of the placenta "beyond the womb" that can both maintain and manipulate maternal tissues, provoking maternal immune countermeasures

## The account

Boddy and colleagues (2015) argue that fetal microchimerism's paradoxical effects on maternal health reflect a mix of aligned and conflicting mother-offspring fitness interests, so fetal cells are a selected extension of the placenta "beyond the womb" that can both maintain and manipulate maternal tissues, provoking maternal immune countermeasures.

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-1781](../notes.d/NOTE-1781.md); its key results are quoted there with locations.
