# CLAIM-tmplrpat: A successful corrective act, an executable procedure and a maintained organizational capacity are three different things, so an organization can record a correction, even rewrite its rules, without the corrected capacity being maintained

## The claim

The owner proposed two kinds of case at U23 and U25: "a near miss
involving a mayday on a fireground that triggered escalated response and
resulted in a change to institutional safety culture", and NASA's
"well structured mechanisms for documenting and learning from mistakes",
with "the events leading up to and following the Challenger disaster as a
classic example".

From Brookhollow ([CASE-tmpqq6jz](../cases.d/CASE-tmpqq6jz.md)), A24 separated three capacities,
"Constitutional", "Operative" and "Developmental", and concluded: "A
successful act, an executable procedure, and a maintained organizational
capacity are three different things." It added: "A change in the archive
of rules is not automatically a change in the collective dispositions that
those rules are meant to maintain." From Challenger and Columbia
([CASE-tmpv2pte](../cases.d/CASE-tmpv2pte.md)), A25: "Institutional learning is not complete when an
organization changes its rules." And from NASA's own lessons-learned
system, as GAO found it ([LIT-tmpdahyq](../literature.d/LIT-tmpdahyq.md)): "A correction is recorded ⇏ The
corrected capacity is maintained." It sorted the outcomes into no
correction, ephemeral correction and maintained correction.

So the claim has three objects:

1. **The act.** A rescue that worked, a launch that was stopped.
2. **The procedure.** A rule, a checklist, an office, a reporting system,
   written down and executable.
3. **The maintained capacity.** The organization's continuing disposition
   to recognize the condition the procedure is for, and to act on it with
   the authority the procedure assigns.

Each can exist without the others. A heroic rescue can succeed with no
procedure behind it. A procedure can be adopted, as NASA's
post-Challenger safety office was, and the capacity it was meant to
maintain can erode, as the Columbia board found ([THEORY-140](../theory.d/THEORY-140.md): reforms
undone, a safety organization dependent on the program it oversaw). A
recommendation can be made without the procedure being adopted, as A23
noted: "A near-miss review may *recommend* reform without demonstrating
that the institution actually changed."

It rests on [CLAIM-025](CLAIM-025.md), whose three objects, the operative regime, the
governing organization and the capacity to transform it, "can persist or
fail separately". It adds that the record of a transformation is a fourth
thing, and the one most easily mistaken for the third. [THEORY-145](../theory.d/THEORY-145.md)
(Argyris) holds the individual-level form: espoused theory against theory
in use. [CLAIM-414](CLAIM-414.md) gives the method for telling them apart: two
organizations alike in ordinary operation part under the intervention the
capacity is for.

## What it does not say

- **Not that procedures are idle.** A procedure is evidence of a capacity
  and often a cause of it. The claim denies only that it is the capacity.
- **Not culture change.** A23 kept "enacted procedural and training
  changes" apart from a changed safety culture, and the owner's premise at
  U23 was weakened accordingly. Whether a culture changed is a further
  question the claim does not answer for any case.
- **Not that NASA failed to learn.** It learned and then lost part of what
  it learned. The Columbia board's reading is [THEORY-140](../theory.d/THEORY-140.md), Proposed.
