# LIT-tmpyrsss: Rationality as the Normative Dimension of Speech Acts

Federica Berdini (2012), "Rationality as the Normative Dimension of Speech
Acts", *Phenomenology and Mind* 2. DOI 10.13128/phe_mi-19636.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpq3jbs.*

- **Rules do not fix the act.** With Bach and Harnish, "meaning never
  exhausts illocutionary force" (1979, p. 132): "The door is open" may be
  an assertion, an order, a threat or advice. So a rule-based account
  cannot say what makes an act the act it is, even in literal cases.
- **The inferential model.** The hearer infers the speaker's attitude from
  what is said, mutual contextual beliefs, and the linguistic,
  communicative and conversational presumptions. Communication is the
  speaker's expressing an attitude by reflexive intention (Bach and Harnish
  1979, p. 39, as quoted).
- **Bach and Harnish on deontology.** They call it "a moral question"
  whether illocutionary acts create a deontology: "at best they create
  mutual beliefs between speaker and hearer about rights and obligations"
  (1979, p. 124, as quoted). Berdini sides with Searle that deontology is
  internal to speech acts, but not because of rules.
- **Rationality as minimal normativity.** The presumption that one's
  interlocutor is rational, following Grice's late work on reason and
  value, sets expectations and constraints that might ground commitments
  and obligations. The paper offers this as a proposal for further
  research.
