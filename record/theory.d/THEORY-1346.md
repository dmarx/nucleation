# THEORY-1346: In rat cortical slices with synapses blocked, extracellular fields of the local field potential's size, applied by an electrode, shift the membrane potential of layer 5 pyramidal neurons by less than 0.5 mV at frequencies up to 100 Hz and, with spiking driven by current, phase-lock spike times without changing spike counts, most effectively for slow fields, and synchronize nearby neurons; the authors explain the effect by Kirchhoff's circuit laws and a fixed firing threshold

## Source

Anastassiou, Perin, Markram & Koch (2011), [LIT-1953](../literature.d/LIT-1953.md), pp. 217–222 and the
online methods; read in [NOTE-1516](../notes.d/NOTE-1516.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Subthreshold**, n = 23: membrane oscillations of 0.16 mV at 1 Hz and 0.14 mV
  at 100 Hz for I₀ = 100 nA, anti-phase with the extracellular potential (p. 218).
- **Spiking**, n = 25, each condition paired with a control: spike counts
  unchanged, phase locking rising with field strength, significant at 1 Hz from
  0.74 mV/mm (P = 6.5 × 10⁻⁵) and at 8 Hz (P = 0.019), and at 30 Hz only near
  5.6 mV/mm (p. 220).
- **Synchrony**: four simultaneously patched neurons clustered their spikes near
  270° under a common 1 Hz field (p. 221).

Each could have come out otherwise: the membrane response could have been
filtered at high frequency, as intracellular injection is, or the timing shift
could have come with a rate change that explained it. Neither happened.

## What this does not say

- **That this happens in vivo.** The fields were applied; the matched amplitudes
  are an argument.
- **That brain activity is not locally describable.** The authors' own
  explanation is circuit physics (p. 221).
- **Anything about attention or experience** ([LIT-388](../literature.d/LIT-388.md)).
