# CASE-177: Hodgkinia in cicadas: one endosymbiont splitting into two to dozens of interdependent lineages, with near-complementary gene sets, idiosyncratic fragmentation in Magicicada, and hosts transmitting up to six times as many cells to keep every lineage

## The case

Cicadas carry two nutritional endosymbionts, *Sulcia* and *Hodgkinia*.

- **Splitting ([LIT-1563](../literature.d/LIT-1563.md)).** In some *Tettigades*, *Hodgkinia* has split
  into two cytologically distinct species. Their gene loss and retention
  are almost perfectly complementary, so a three-member symbiosis became
  four. The authors suggest nonadaptive forces can build organismal
  complexity.
- **Repeated ([LIT-1586](../literature.d/LIT-1586.md)).** A single ancestral lineage split at least
  six independent times in *Tettigades* over four million years, giving two
  to six lineages per host. Together the complexes encode nearly the
  ancestral gene set.
- **Fragmentation ([LIT-1612](../literature.d/LIT-1612.md), [LIT-1602](../literature.d/LIT-1602.md)).** In *Magicicada tredecim*
  the genome fragmented into many chromosomes, together almost ten times
  the ancestral size. Every *Magicicada* species holds at least twenty
  circles, with little synteny between species and very different gene
  dosages.
- **The host's cost ([LIT-1590](../literature.d/LIT-1590.md)).** Rare but essential lineages risk
  being lost at transmission. Eggs still receive the full set, because
  hosts with more complex *Hodgkinia* transmit up to six times as many
  cells. The authors propose this as a cost of the fragmentation.

## What it can show

- **More parts, more dependence, no new function.** The complexes do what
  one lineage did. Integration measured by dependence rose with no evident
  gain, which is A2's "partly nonadaptive" dependence ([QUESTION-277](../questions.d/QUESTION-277.md)).
- **Host compensation at transmission.** The host's response raises
  reproductive cohesion (R) to rescue a collective made fragile by
  constituent changes.
- **Constituent change that costs the whole.** This is the converse
  direction of [CLAIM-060](../claims.d/CLAIM-060.md). What the constituents became is not what the
  collective needed.

Documented in works the record has not read. What it shows rests on their
abstracts until they are read.

## Note of 2026-10-27 (batch 2): the transmission paper read in full

[LIT-1590](../literature.d/LIT-1590.md) has been read in full ([NOTE-1235](../notes.d/NOTE-1235.md), [THEORY-1106](../theory.d/THEORY-1106.md)).
Corrections to "The host's cost" and "What it can show":

- **The six-fold rise is one species.** It compares *M. septendecim*
  (30,154 *Hodgkinia* cells per egg) with a single-lineage *Tettigades*
  (4,889). Only *M. septendecim* differs significantly. The six-lineage
  *T. chilensis* (about 12,000) does not differ from the single-lineage
  species. The study has four species and 5–6 eggs each, with no
  phylogenetic control.
- **"Eggs still receive the full set, because ..." is inference.** The
  full set was tracked with one or two marker genes per species, and the
  authors grant that rare losses could escape detection. The "because",
  and the order (splitting before titre), are the authors' inference.
- **The hundredfold abundance range** comes from the authors' earlier
  metagenomes (114-fold in rpoB), not from this paper.
- **Circles are not shown to be cells.** In *Magicicada*, genomic circles
  are not shown to be separate cell lineages. The simulation assumes they
  are, and the authors suspect some circles share cells.
- **The host maintains R; it does not raise it.** It keeps co-transmission
  complete as the parts multiply, by sending more *Hodgkinia* (not more
  *Sulcia*) through an unchanged route. The authors speculate that the
  larger transmitted population weakens bottlenecks and "decrease[s] the
  level of control that the host can exert", which could feed further
  splitting. If so, keeping R costs C and S.
- **Composition is inherited.** Mothers pass their own lineage proportions
  to their eggs, and egg nests differ significantly, so the make-up of the
  symbiont community is inherited at the host level.
