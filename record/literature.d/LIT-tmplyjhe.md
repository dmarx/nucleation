# LIT-tmplyjhe: How continuity distinguishes autonomy from agency in agentic AI

Kahl, P. (2026). How continuity distinguishes autonomy from agency in agentic AI. *Discover Artificial Intelligence* 6. DOI 10.1007/s44163-026-01675-5

## Key takeaways

Read in full ([NOTE-tmp0foxv](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp0foxv.md)).

- **Two senses of agency.** Thin operational agency-talk is accepted as
  engineering vocabulary; the target is its migration into claims about
  responsibility, authority and governance.
- **Capability does not locate evaluative authority.** Planning, tool use,
  memory, multi-agent coordination and autonomous action each leave open
  who authors the standards: "distribution of execution is not the same
  as unity of authorship."
- **Continuity pressure.** It "begins where an inherited evaluative frame
  no longer settles how future operation should proceed", and it is
  absent where revision is imposed from outside, escaped by rollback, or
  chosen among paths "already ranked by a fixed higher-order criterion".
- **The cases.** Tool-using agents are resettable; reinforcement learners,
  preference-trained assistants included, revise under an antecedent
  admissibility structure; workflows coordinate without authorship;
  institutional systems have "authority without authorship". Skill-library
  agents and continually learning clinical systems come closer and fail.
- **Group agents.** What separates a court from an orchestration is
  whether revision of the integration rule under breakdown is internal,
  not whether the rule was designed.
- **Governance.** Current frameworks treat deployed systems as non-agents,
  and misattributed agency displaces responsibility, most where affected
  people cannot exit.

## Why this is here

A11 asked whether a system is self-governing 'merely because authority allocation is internal, or must it retain responsibility for the standards by which its own decisions are evaluated'. Kahl answers the second: voting rules, ranking functions and the like "coordinate execution; they do not generate authorship of the governing frame"; only revision of the system's own standards under breakdown does ([THEORY-tmpbt4lq](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpbt4lq.md)). That supports [CLAIM-tmpzn7l2](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpzn7l2.md), by an argument from responsibility that cites no enactive work, and bears on [QUESTION-tmpt97t5](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpt97t5.md) and [CASE-tmpxtzj0](../cases.d/CASE-tmpxtzj0.md), where it sides with present organisation against origin. The filing's summary was right in outline. [QUESTION-tmpt97t5](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpt97t5.md) expected the paper to make "the scheduler's location beside the point"; it does, but it makes the location of revision the point. The paper is a necessary condition stated without a positive case, and its key phrase, settled under breakdown rather than by an antecedent rule, is not made precise. Flagged `anthology-candidate` because the anthology's agents-and-environments topic might hold a paper about AI agents; it is filed here for the question of evaluative authority ([ADR-005](../decisions.d/ADR-005.md)).
