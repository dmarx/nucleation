# LIT-1007: The Ethics of Manipulation

Noggle, R. (2018; substantive revision 2026). The Ethics of Manipulation. *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*. <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-manipulation/>.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmphtua8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmphtua8.md).*

- **Two questions.** What manipulation is (identification) and whether
  and why it is wrong (evaluation). The answer to the first constrains
  the second.
- **Every single-mechanism account has a counterexample.**
  - Covert influence (Ware, Strudler, Susser et al., [LIT-1002](LIT-1002.md)) misses
    peer pressure, nagging and guilt trips, which are open.
  - Covert intent misses the teenager whose nagging for a phone is
    "quite transparent".
  - Non-rational influence catches graphic smoking warnings and dressing
    up for an interview.
  - Trickery misses charm offensives, nagging and emotional blackmail.
  - Pressure misses pure misdirection.
- **What is left.** A disjunction (Rudinow: "deception or pressure or …
  playing on a supposed weakness"), a common factor (Noggle's 2025
  Mistake Account; Klenk's carelessness about whether a method reveals
  reasons; Baron's vice), or no definition (Ackerman; Cohen's cluster
  concept).
- **Wrongness.** Not wrong by definition or absolutely; the entry leans,
  with "perhaps", to prima facie and pro tanto. Harm cannot be the only
  wrong-maker, since beneficent manipulation seems wrong. The autonomy
  account faces autonomy-enhancing manipulation and S. Buss's argument.
  Renzo's account, on which the manipulator co-opts the target's own
  practical agency, is given most room.
- **Intent is open (§4.2).** Baron allows intent with recklessness,
  Manne manipulation without conscious intent. For an algorithm that
  selects interface variants which "had they been chosen deliberately by
  a human, we would label as manipulation", five answers are listed and
  none endorsed.
- **Ordinary, not global.** The free-will literature's manipulation is
  "radical programming or reprogramming", and "few people have explored
  the connections" with ordinary manipulation.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 at the owner's request with [QUESTION-070](../questions.d/QUESTION-070.md) and
[QUESTION-071](../questions.d/QUESTION-071.md), and read in [NOTE-tmphtua8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmphtua8.md). It is held as the record's map
of the analyses of manipulation, to which those questions appeal.

For [QUESTION-070](../questions.d/QUESTION-070.md), its §4.2 asks the question's first part for a
learning system and lists the candidate answers, including the two
branches the question names. It does not discuss a network with no
designer. For [QUESTION-071](../questions.d/QUESTION-071.md), it is about ordinary manipulation and says
that its relation to the global manipulation of the four-case argument
([LIT-1006](LIT-1006.md)) has hardly been studied. So it does not by itself supply what
that argument assumes. The 2019 paper by Susser, Roessler and Nissenbaum
([LIT-1002](LIT-1002.md)) takes its classification from the 2018 version and is quoted
in this one.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing here belongs in
the anthology.
