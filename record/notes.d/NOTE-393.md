# NOTE-393: Epstein — Social Ontology (SEP)

## Contribution

A field survey with a thesis about how the field is organised. The entry
treats the many theories of the social as answers to two different
questions. One asks what grounds social facts: their constituents or
determination base. The other asks what anchors social categories: what
sets up the conditions. It argues that much confusion comes from running
them together. That division is the author's, and it is the frame of The
Ant Trap ([LIT-478](../literature.d/LIT-478.md)).

## Key insight

For any social category there are two questions. Given that *kosher*
(or *foxtrot*, or *Supreme Court justice*) has the conditions it has,
what makes this thing satisfy them? And what put those conditions in
place? Collective acceptance, convention, practice and patterns are
answers to the second question. Individualism and its rivals are answers
to the first. A theory can be individualist about one and not the other.

## Assumptions

- **Survey stance.** The entry reports positions and the main objections
  to each. It does not adjudicate, except where its organising
  distinction is at stake.
- **Grounding** is taken in the standard metaphysics sense (Fine, Rosen,
  Audi), as non-causal determination, with the entry on metaphysical
  grounding as background (§2.3).
- **The two inquiries are separable.** The entry calls the sharpness of
  the distinction "a matter of some controversy" but uses it to organise
  §§3–4.

## Key results

- **Demarcation (§2.1).**
  - No agreed mark of the social: Gilbert locates sociality in joint
    commitment and hesitates over crowds; others deny there is a
    criterion.
  - "Epstein 2016" separates *socially grounded* from *socially
    constructed*. Crowds are grounded but not constructed, phenotypic
    racial categories constructed but not grounded, fiat money both.
- **Building relations (§2.2).**
  - Supervenience gave precise distinctions (local versus global
    supervenience of the Senate's properties) but has known shortcomings.
  - Grounding has displaced it.
  - Epstein 2015 treats determination by grounding and construction by
    anchoring. Schaffer treats both as grounding. Mikkola argues against
    grounding approaches, and Passinsky answers her.
  - Epstein 2019b argues anchoring is neither grounding nor
    meta-grounding. Haslanger proposes anchoring as epistemic
    "scaffolding". Hawley and Schaffer would reduce it to grounding.
    Schaffer models the distinction on Dretske's structuring versus
    triggering causes.
- **Ontology versus causation (§2.3).** Ontological determination can be
  diachronic, as a footprint depends on a past foot-strike and a
  president on a past election. Epstein 2015 and Schaffer favour
  diachronic determination; Bernstein opposes it. Searle's account is
  synchronic.
- **Determination bases (§3).**
  - Psychologism (Mill, Popper). Its local-dependence form says group
    attitudes depend only on members' attitudes (Gilbert, Tuomela &
    Miller, Bratman, Wray, Lackey); Bird 2010 and Epstein 2022 challenge
    local dependence.
  - Atomism; bodies, actions and relations (List & Pettit 2011 widen the
    base to actions and dispositions).
  - Material building blocks and physicalism. Ontological individualism
    "has recently come under criticism" (Epstein 2009, Hindriks 2013,
    Ylikoski, Elder-Vass, Haslanger 2022).
  - Mixed views (Pettit's externalist psychologism, institutional
    individualism), socially constituted individuals and practices,
    holism and flat ontologies (Actor-Network Theory).
- **Anchoring theories (§4).**
  - Individual mental states (Lockean nominal essences).
  - Community-wide mental states:
    - agreement;
    - Lewis's convention: solutions to coordination problems with
      several equilibria, needing no agreement, only beliefs,
      expectations and preferences;
    - Hart's accepted rules;
    - Searle's status functions, imposed by collective acceptance of
      constitutive rules ("X counts as Y in C") through declarations,
      with deontic powers. Searle differs from Hart in three ways:
      collective rather than individual attitudes, no requirement of
      behaviour, and one form put in place by declarations.
  - Pattern theories: functional roles, Millikan's reproductively
    established families, Boyd's homeostatic clusters, Hacking's looping
    kinds, Mallon.
  - Practice theories, and pluralism. Epstein 2014a: there are several
    "anchoring schemas", and anchors are heterogeneous.
  - Language: word meanings, speech acts, discourse.
- **Domains (§5).**
  - Groups (§5.1): sets, fusions, pluralities, structures, or entities
    constituted by members. Coinciding groups are a problem for the
    fusion view, and Epstein 2019a argues there can be distinct groups
    whose members necessarily coincide.
  - Group minds and agency (§5.2): Gilbert's joint commitment;
    Bratman's meshing intentions; Searle's primitive we-intentions;
    Tuomela & Miller's we-mode; the functionalist model of group agency
    as a realised "system of practical activity" (Bratman, List & Pettit,
    Theiner, Epstein 2022); critics who deny groups phenomenal states;
    Hutchins and Huebner on large complex groups.
  - Institutions (§5.3); race, gender and disability (§5.4); artifacts
    (§5.5); money (§5.6); law, with Hart's rule of recognition and
    Epstein 2015 ch. 7 (§5.7); language (§5.8).

## Concepts

- **Grounding (ontological determination)** — the non-causal relation
  between a social fact and the facts that make it obtain.
- **Anchoring** — the relation between a frame principle (the conditions
  for a fact of some kind to be grounded) and the facts that put it in
  place. The précis gives the example *x is kosher*: whether x came from
  a pig grounds it, and what anchors the condition might be religious
  authority, acceptance, practice or divine command.
- **Frame, frame principle** (précis) — a frame is a set of possible
  worlds; a frame principle says how a kind of social fact is grounded
  across it. Searle's constitutive rules are frame principles of one
  form.
- **Ontological individualism** — the social is exhaustively determined
  by facts about individual people. **Anchor individualism** (précis;
  the entry cites it only by the title of Epstein 2014b) — the parallel
  individualist claim about how frame principles are anchored. The
  précis says both are false and argues only the first in the book.
- **Local versus global dependence** — whether a social fact about some
  people depends only on them or on a wider population (§3.1.1).
- **System of practical activity** — the functional organisation of
  belief, intention, planning, deliberation and action that an agent
  realises, used for individual and group agency alike (§5.2).

## Connections

  reduce them to regulative rules held in equilibrium. On the

## Bearing on the record

**[THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md).** [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md) holds that the downward and upward
functionalist moves are one move. It finds that what separates them is
either an anti-nesting principle or an architectural criterion, and
that neither is yet principled. Both routes, as [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md) states them,
take the candidate whole to be fixed by its parts: Kammerer's condition
asks whether *members* consciously represent the whole, and List's low-Φ
argument looks at how *members* are coupled. The framework, as this
entry and the précis state it, bears on that framing in three ways.
These are my inferences from those texts, not claims either makes about
consciousness. Neither text discusses group phenomenal consciousness
beyond §5.2's two sentences.

- **What grounds a group's states is not only its members.** The entry
  reports a challenge to local dependence: group attitudes need not
  depend only on member attitudes (§3.1.1; Epstein 2022, not read). The
  précis says a group's actions and intentions can be grounded partly in
  non-members, infrastructure, powers and membership rules. If so, an
  architectural criterion computed over members and their links, as
  List's is, is computed over less than the system whose states are in
  question. That sharpens [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md)'s point that no one has computed
  the criterion for a group: it also has to be computed for the right
  group.
- **"The group" is not one candidate.** Distinct groups can have the
  same members, even necessarily (§5.1, Epstein 2019a, not read). That is
  the metaphysical version of the gap Schwitzgebel's reply opens at
  Kammerer's condition A: it is unclear which entity citizens would have
  to represent ([NOTE-381](NOTE-381.md), C2). The framework says the vagueness is not an
  artefact of the word "United States". Coinciding groups are a general
  feature of groups.
- **Anchoring is not a third route, but it is a reason to keep the
  question about experience separate.** On the précis's account, status
  assignments do not make a group an agent. A group is an agent by
  realising a system of practical activity. That sides with [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md)'s
  observation that agency survives both directions, and against any
  view that group agency is conferred. Whether experience could be
  anchored at all, rather than grounded, the texts do not say. Nothing in
  them suggests it.

Net: no bearing on `promote_when`, which asks for a criterion of
phenomenal consciousness. The framework adds a constraint on any such
criterion: it must say which of several coinciding wholes it applies
to, and it must count the non-member grounds of a group's functional
organisation. Reading Epstein 2022 ("Why Group Mental States are not
Exhaustively Determined by Member States", Philosophical Issues
32(1):417–433, not in the record) would test the first point directly.

## Limitations

- A survey: positions are reported in a sentence or two and objections
  are listed, not weighed. The anchoring dispute (§2.2.2) gets one
  paragraph for both sides.
- The organising distinction is the author's own thesis. Readers who
  reject anchoring as a distinct relation (Hawley, Schaffer) would
  organise §§3–4 differently.
- §5 is thin by design; each subsection points to other SEP entries.
- The 2024 revision was read. Whether the 2018 first version made the
  same claims was not checked.

## Open questions

- Is anchoring a distinct relation from grounding? The entry names the
  dispute and does not settle it (§2.2.2).
- Are group mental states exhaustively determined by member states?
  Epstein 2022 argues not; the record does not hold it.
- Can the anchoring–grounding split be applied to non-social kinds whose
  boundaries are set by practice, such as scientific kinds (§4.5 notes
  pluralism about scientific kinds)?
