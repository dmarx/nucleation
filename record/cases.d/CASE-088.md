# CASE-088: The repeated objection and the argument nobody reuses: filing pays for itself after a few recurrences in one case, and never in the other

## The case

**The repeated objection** (A11's CASE B). An objection costs 15 to
reconstruct and answer. After it has appeared twice it is filed, with its
strongest form, the answer and the answer's assumptions, for 25. Each
later encounter costs 3 to retrieve and check. Over three further
encounters: 3 × 15 = 45 against 25 + 3 × 3 = 34. Filing saves 11. The
break-even is n > 25/(15 − 3) ≈ 2.1, so three uses.

**The argument nobody reuses** (A11's CASE A). Filed for 20, reconstructible
for 10, never needed again. "The record's storage was successful. Its
amortization failed."

## What it can show

That filing an addressed objection can pay, which is the record's reason
for coding recurring moves ([ADR-032](../decisions.d/ADR-032.md)), and that an anchor can work as
[CLAIM-182](../claims.d/CLAIM-182.md) says and still not be worth making.

## What it cannot show

A11: "That the saved effort improves the argument's truth, or that the
objection remains answered when new evidence arrives."
