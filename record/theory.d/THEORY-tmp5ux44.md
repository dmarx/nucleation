# THEORY-tmp5ux44: Communication can be treated as transduction, the continuity of an action through processes that it transforms, rather than as exchange between parties; meaning is then the contribution an action makes, in retrospect, to a joint individuation, coexisting meanings mark concurrent individuations, and the problem of scaling agency from individual to collective is claimed to dissolve

## Source

Bencherki, Brummans and Vézy (2024), [LIT-tmp3mudg](../literature.d/LIT-tmp3mudg.md), pp. 1355–1358 and 1363–1365;
read in [NOTE-tmpfw3f7](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpfw3f7.md).

## What was actually shown

The paper argues four things.

- **Communication is transduction.** It is "the continuity of action
  through beings who are in a perpetual state of becoming", and "any
  communication also implies a transformation" (pp. 1356, 1364). Its
  examples include a synapse converting electrical to chemical signals,
  a hunger pang becoming a postponed meeting, and a piston that
  "communicates movement".
- **Meaning is retrospective.** "action contributes to informing a system
  that, retrospectively, gives that action its meaning" (p. 1357).
- **Organizations are not consensus.** They are not formed by reducing
  equivocality: "Coexisting meanings of the same action indicate that
  several individuations are ongoing" (p. 1358), against Weick.
- **Scaling up dissolves.** The problem of scaling from individual to
  collective agency "vanishes the moment communication is viewed
  eventfully" (p. 1364). Individuation is said to take place "concurrently
  at different 'levels'".

The last is asserted, with the MSF meeting as illustration. The
illustration shows the description can be given. It does not show that a
scaling-up account of the same meeting goes wrong.

## What this does not say

- **That information-theoretic communication is wrong.** It sets aside the
  transmission view of communication; it does not engage it.
- **How to bound the notion.** With pistons communicating movement,
  "communication" covers every causal continuity. Something must separate
  organizationally relevant transduction from causation in general, and
  the paper does not supply it.
