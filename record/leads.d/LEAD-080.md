# LEAD-080: Run the readiness simulator twice, with each period's budget set to the last period's peak spending and with carry-over

Run A's simulator in the two budget regimes.

Where the record names it:

- [QUESTION-394](../questions.d/QUESTION-394.md): "A's simulator is also the cheapest test of [CLAIM-577](../claims.d/CLAIM-577.md)."

its first organizational test.

## Note of 2026-11-22: run, with a corollary

<!-- inactive-ok-block: CASE-tmpvnozj CLAIM-tmpocsw4 CASE-200 — new or Proposed; filed for this lead, and CASE-200's rule run as filed -->
The simulator QUESTION-394 records from A was built and run under spend-matching and under carry-over, 300 runs of ten years per configuration (CASE-tmpvnozj; code, tests and outputs under scratch/studies/lead-080/). The lead's "what would count" is met: under spend-matching the heavy rescue squad is lost for good, and under full carry-over it is kept in every run. The study adds what makes a period quiet: a month in which nothing at all mobilizes the squad, so the dispatch policy and the drill schedule decide how long it survives the rule; that is filed as CLAIM-tmpocsw4, a corollary of CLAIM-577. Since the lead does not say which carry-over rule it means, two were run: rolling over what the peak rule leaves unspent, which holds the budget, and CASE-200's base-plus-balance rule, which never loses the squad but rations one used every month to every other month. What remains: QUESTION-394's own question, the policy ranking across congestion, geography, substitutes, accuracy and coverage cost, which this run does not sweep; and a budget kept in money or hours rather than at the period's peak, under which a cancelled dispatch would hold far less of it. The lead stays Open.
