# LIT-2616: nGPT: Normalized Transformer with Representation Learning on the Hypersphere

Loshchilov, I., Hsieh, C.-P., Sun, S. & Ginsburg, B. (2024). nGPT: Normalized Transformer with Representation Learning on the Hypersphere. ICLR 2025. arXiv:2410.01131v2. [ARXIV-2410.01131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01131).

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1971](../notes.d/NOTE-1971.md).*

- **The update** (§2.2.2, Eqs. 10–11): h ← Norm(h + α_A(h_A − h)) and
  h ← Norm(h + α_M(h_M − h)), with h_A = Norm(ATTN(h)) and h_M =
  Norm(MLP(h)); "We denote α as eigen learning rates".
- **Speed** (§3.1): "approximately 4x, 10x, and 20x faster at context
  lengths of 1k, 4k, and 8k tokens", in tokens, at 0.5B and 1B on
  OpenWebText; each step costs about 60–80% more time (A.5).
- **Learned steps** (§3.2): "the network learns to take only modest steps
  (20%-30%)".
- **Geometry** (§3.2): GPT's attention matrices "degenerate into
  lower-rank matrices"; nGPT's embeddings cluster and "are not uniformly
  distributed across the hypersphere".

## Standing in the record

Filed from A's turn on representations as trajectories on a hypersphere.
A's account holds: hidden states are kept on a unit sphere, attention and
MLP blocks propose points, and each layer moves toward them and
renormalizes; A's update rule matches the paper's in form, with two
imprecisions the reader notes (the block output is itself normalized, and
each layer makes two such steps with separate α_A and α_M). A rightly
says the paper does not show branched flow: it analyses no trajectories
across inputs. Its reading of each layer as an optimizer step is an
analogy by construction, not the in-context identity of [LIT-792](LIT-792.md). It
carries no claim about memory, superposition or circuits, and bears on
none of the codes the rest of this batch does. Its central account is
[THEORY-1881](../theory.d/THEORY-1881.md).
