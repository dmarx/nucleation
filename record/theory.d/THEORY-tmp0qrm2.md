# THEORY-tmp0qrm2: Large aid sustained over decades can weaken a recipient state's institutions: it substitutes for taxation, grants and not loans going with lower revenue, and it decouples the state's revenue from its performance, weakening its accountability to citizens

## Source

Moss, Pettersson & van de Walle (2006), [LIT-tmp4d1o2](../literature.d/LIT-tmp4d1o2.md); read in [NOTE-tmpd45i4](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpd45i4.md).

## What was actually shown

A review; the evidence is others', and mixed.

- **Substitution for tax**: the studies reviewed (pp. 12–13) run from
  insignificant (Leuthold; Teera and Hudson) to negative (Ghura; Brautigam:
  "71 percent of the African countries with aid/GDP above 10 percent in
  1995 had lower than expected tax effort"), with country cases both ways.
  Gupta et al., 107 countries, 1970–2002: "grants have a significant,
  negative effect on revenue while loans have a significant, positive
  impact".
- **The authors' own figure** (p. 11): over 1972–1999 "there are no
  incidences at all of high aid (>10% of GNI) and high tax (>18% of GDP)",
  thresholds they call arbitrary, with no causation implied.
- **Accountability**: argued from fiscal sociology and African case
  literature; the moral-hazard effect, revenue "not affected by government
  efficiency" (p. 15), is said to be borne out by Brautigam and Knack's and
  Knack's associations of high aid with lower governance quality.
- **Verdict** (p. 13): "Generally, thus, the literature finds a negative
  relationship between aid and revenue collection, but this is not a
  conclusive result."

## What this does not say

- That aid causes low tax effort: confounding by low income and
  industrialization is not excluded, and states may "choose not to seek
  revenues they have the capacity to collect" (p. 14).
- At what level aid becomes harmful: the authors relay Berg's 5 % and
  Clemens and Radelet's 15–45 % of GDP and commit to neither.
- That all aid does harm: debt relief, peacekeeping and regional or global
  public goods are held unlikely to.
- How a state is weaned off aid, beyond naming Korea, Taiwan and Botswana.
