# QUESTION-398: Under what conditions does a partial organization become independent of the intervention that originally established it?

## Why it is a question

A productive nucleus ([TERM-117](../terms.d/TERM-117.md)) may need support to form and keep
growing. A's question is when the growing structure's own relations take
over. A's measure:

  D(s) = q_supported(s) − q_withdrawn(s),

the committor with support kept minus the committor with support removed,
from the same intermediate state s. A expects D(s) to be large at first and to approach zero once enough
downstream organization has formed. A's analogy:
"temporary scaffolding used to construct a building", whose removal tests
construction without making the building "self-repairing".

The record separates assembling, persisting and regenerating ([CLAIM-566](../claims.d/CLAIM-566.md))
and asks when persistence makes an anchor ([CLAIM-185](../claims.d/CLAIM-185.md), [QUESTION-038](QUESTION-038.md)). It
does not ask when what was built ceases to need what built it.

## What the record has on it

- **DNA origami's supports.** Rothemund's and Douglas et al.'s staple
  excess and annealing programs, and Ni et al.'s thermal cycling and UV
  crosslink. Each is withdrawn after formation, and the objects persist:
  support-independence after completion, not a measured D(s) along the
  way.
- **Marras et al.'s closing strands** hold the compact state. Their
  displacement returns it to about 10 % compact, so there the support is
  never dispensed with.

## What would answer it

A system where support can be withdrawn at chosen intermediate states,
with the committor measured afterwards from each, in molecules or in a
model. A's rival to exclude: "The system merely completed before
withdrawal".
