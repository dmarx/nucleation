# QUESTION-029: What constitutes the identity of a relationally organized communicative act, how can it be recognized apart from any one representation, and under which transformations does it persist?

## Why it is a question

A203 X, at the end of the assistant's second crystallized argument (U56,
2026-10-10 02:52): "The final question is no longer simply *what does a
translation preserve?* It is: **What constitutes the identity of a
relationally organized object, how can that identity be recognized
independently of any particular representation, and under what
transformations does it persist?**" The October proposal ends on a shorter
form of the same question (A218, U60): "**How can a relationally constituted
kind remain identifiable and useful when the representation through which we
encounter it changes?**"

The change of question was announced, which the record's earlier changes of
thesis were not (workbench 025539 on [CLAIM-017](../claims.d/CLAIM-017.md) and [CLAIM-115](../claims.d/CLAIM-115.md)). It was not
argued. The owner's turns in between asked for the argument to be laid out
(U56) or tied together (U59), and for a new proposal (U60).

The question has three parts, and the record holds a different amount on each.

- **What constitutes the identity.** This is [QUESTION-021](QUESTION-021.md)'s question, now
  given a relational answer. A187 §3 proposed partial constitution by
  relations to participants, norms and uptake ([CLAIM-151](../claims.d/CLAIM-151.md)). The owner's
  U54 proposed relata with no identity beyond their relations
  ([CLAIM-136](../claims.d/CLAIM-136.md)). The record holds objections to both readings
  ([CLAIM-134](../claims.d/CLAIM-134.md), [CLAIM-138](../claims.d/CLAIM-138.md)).
- **How it can be recognized apart from a representation.** This is
  where characters, Yoneda and identifiability enter ([CLAIM-137](../claims.d/CLAIM-137.md),
  [CLAIM-142](../claims.d/CLAIM-142.md), [CLAIM-135](../claims.d/CLAIM-135.md)). The signature question
  [QUESTION-027](QUESTION-027.md) is its constructive half.
- **Under which transformations it persists.** This is [QUESTION-002](QUESTION-002.md) turned
  round: not what a translation keeps, but which transformations keep a kind.

An answer would say what the unit of identity is (the record files it as a
communicative kind, [TERM-040](../terms.d/TERM-040.md)), what independent evidence recognizes
it, and for which classes of transformation that evidence is stable. A thesis
that answers only the first part is metaphysics. One that answers only the
second and third is [QUESTION-002](QUESTION-002.md) again.

## How it differs from [QUESTION-002](QUESTION-002.md) and [QUESTION-021](QUESTION-021.md)

[QUESTION-002](QUESTION-002.md) asks what survives a translation, retelling or rendering. It
takes the transformation as given and asks about its output. This question
takes the kind as given and asks what makes it one kind and which
transformations it survives. Translation becomes one case. A203's opening says
so: "Translation was our starting point, but the underlying problem is more
general".

[QUESTION-021](QUESTION-021.md) asks whether there is a frame-independent act or only a family
of frame-relative realizations. A24 dropped the underlying act object and
refined that question into [QUESTION-001](QUESTION-001.md). This question reopens it with a third
option: a repeatable relational organization that numerically distinct events
realize (A218 §1: "We distinguish numerical identity of concrete events from
structural identity of repeatable communicative kinds").

## What it is not

It is not a question about objects in general. A203's preamble, A214 and A218
speak of "objects", but A203 §35's own ontological thesis is scoped to
"Communicative acts and other structured phenomena", and no turn argues the
generalization. A question about the identity of objects as such would need a
line of its own, and `lines` is closed by decision.

It does not presuppose that the answer is relational. That is the October
thesis's answer ([CLAIM-139](../claims.d/CLAIM-139.md)). The question stands if the thesis falls.
