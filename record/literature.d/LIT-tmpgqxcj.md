# LIT-tmpgqxcj: Protecting the Vulnerable: A Re-Analysis of Our Social Responsibilities

Robert E. Goodin (1985). *Protecting the Vulnerable: A Re-Analysis of Our Social Responsibilities*. University of Chicago Press. ISBN 9780226302997.

## Key takeaways

*Not read; as A describes it.* A12 §1: special responsibilities are grounded in another's vulnerability to one's actions and choices, rather than mainly in promises, agreements or prior roles, and need not be voluntarily assumed. A builds on it the proposal that a whole's control over what its constituents depend on generates responsibility toward them. Unverified until read.

## Note of 2026-11-12: unavailable; paratext and a restatement

Goodin's text was not reached. The publisher's description: "Since the range of people vulnerable to our actions or choices extends beyond those to whom we have made specific commitments (promises, vows, contracts), we must recognize a much more extensive network of obligations and moral claims." The contents include "Individual and Collective Responsibilities" and a chapter "Invulnerability as an Alternative", the two places that would decide CLAIM-tmpwn3wr. Carroll and Crutchfield (2022), open access, restate the model: "A has a duty to protect B just in case B’s interests are vulnerable to A’s actions", its strength varying "according to the degree of dependence", and a duty that can be shed "by transferring the child’s vulnerability to another person". That is a restatement, not Goodin, and it is cited here rather than registered: two paragraphs in an applied argument. Goodin's cases are families, friends, clients, compatriots, the state and future generations, sense-3 wholes and persons (TERM-074); none is a whole and its own parts.
