# NOTE-1057: Safron, Klimaj & Sheikhbahaee — IWMT and the Human Consciousness Hypothesis

## Contribution

A short position paper for the AAAI Spring Symposium. It summarises
Integrated World Modeling Theory (IWMT) and lines it up with the Human
Consciousness Hypothesis (HCH), which it attributes to the California
Institute for Machine Consciousness (CIMC). Its contribution is the
alignment and a compact statement of IWMT's commitments, above all that
consciousness needs embodied grounding. It offers no argument for IWMT
beyond what the earlier papers give, and those were not read.

## Key insight

On IWMT, an experience is the brain's best current estimate of the state of
an embodied sensorium, like a generated image but made of sensations, and
it is conscious because it is coherent in space, time and cause and can
both shape and be shaped by the agent's acting. Integration alone and
broadcast alone are each said to fall short; what they lack is a coherent
model tied to a body acting in a world.

## Assumptions

- **HCH's three principles** (p. 345): Genesis (consciousness emerges early
  as a learning algorithm), Coherence (a "cortical conductor" that
  minimises constraint violations) and Second-order perception (perception
  of perceptual processes, in synchrony with them). They are stated, not
  argued, and not referenced.
- **Marr's levels**, with a phenomenological level added (Figure 1).
- **Kant's categories** as preconditions of experience: coherence "with
  respect to space (as organized locality), time (as proportional changes
  in space), and cause" (p. 346).
- **The free-energy principle and active inference**, with IIT and GNWT
  taken as partly right (p. 345).
- **A minimal self.** "some degree of basic self-modeling and agency may be
  required for coherent subjectivity", though it "may potentially be
  extremely minimal" (p. 346).

## Key results

There are no new results. By section:

- **Abstract and introduction (p. 345).** IWMT's central proposal is that
  phenomenal consciousness is what it feels like to be "the spatiotemporally
  and causally coherent functioning of a probabilistic generative world
  model for an embodied agent". "Self-organizing harmonic modes" (SOHMs),
  synchronous neural complexes, implement it as maximum a posteriori
  estimates of sensorium states. "Critically, IWMT requires (body-)world
  models to be capable of both informing and being informed by
  action-perception cycles at behaviorally relevant timescales." It ends
  that consciousness could be realised in artificial systems "with
  appropriate recurrent dynamics and sufficient degrees (and kinds) of
  embodied grounding".
- **Integrating theories (p. 345).** IWMT draws mainly on GNWT, IIT and the
  free-energy principle, and claims compatibility with recurrent
  processing, predictive processing, dynamic core, temporo-spatial,
  higher-order and attention-schema theories, "While not necessarily
  endorsing all claims".
- **Consciousness as coherent modelling of self and world (p. 346).**
  World models let a system predict and simulate beyond what it observes.
  "IWMT suggests that without sufficiently rich physical or virtual
  embodiments, systems such as large language models are not likely to be
  able to generate the kinds of world modeling underlying robust
  general(izing) intelligence, nor consciousness". Experience is generated
  "either perception or imagination when respectively coupled to or
  decoupled from sense-data". No pre-existing theory alone is said to give
  sufficient conditions.
- **Coherence and minimal selfhood (p. 346).** Coherence in space, time
  and cause, and perhaps integration into a self-model, are necessary for
  "any kind of subjective experience whatsoever".
- **Embodiment (p. 346).** "IWMT is distinct from the theories it draws
  upon in that it is fundamentally cybernetic and embodied in its
  approach." Against IIT: systems could have "arbitrarily large amounts of
  integrated information and still not be conscious without embodied
  grounding". Against GNWT: systems "could instantiate functional global
  workspaces without entailing any kind of subjective experience" unless the
  workspace models a coherent, embodied world. With the enactivists,
  consciousness is "inseparable from embodied cybernetic action–perception
  cycles", but IWMT "diverges from strong enactivism in retaining
  brain-internal representations".
- **Learning to model the world (pp. 346–347).** The section opens with the
  question: "What are the necessary and sufficient conditions for
  coherently generating a stream of experience, organized according to
  coherently structured perspectival reference frames?" The answer given:
  consciousness is a mathematical object, an iteratively computed MAP
  estimate of system–world relations, informally "akin to a" deep fake of
  sensorium states, including interoceptive ones. The model is "organized by
  egocentric perspective". It is called an adaptation and a "digital twin".
- **Substrates (p. 347).** SOHMs emerge as metastable attractors and
  implement turbo coding by loopy message passing. Experiences are
  "causally efficacious with respect to agency". Alpha-band complexes in
  posterior cortex bind sensory states, theta-band coupling brings in
  frontal and hippocampal systems. IIT's posterior hot zone and GNWT's
  frontal network are both "proposed to be correct", for phenomenal
  consciousness and access respectively.
- **Figure 1 (p. 348).** A map of the brain at phenomenological,
  computational, algorithmic and implementational levels, with machine
  learning architectures on the algorithmic level and beta, alpha and theta
  complexes on the implementational level. Frontal cortex "might only
  indirectly contribute to consciousness".
- **Machine consciousness (pp. 347–349).** Against "wetness" arguments
  (Koch, Seth), "a simulation capturing the emergent properties of
  interacting water molecules would recapitulate the phenomenology of
  wetness within the simulated world" (p. 349). Yet realisation "may
  practically (and perhaps also in principle) require the parallelism and
  physical recurrence of neuromorphic and/or analogue computing systems",
  and "non-recurrent networks (such as transformer-based large language
  models) lack the functional closure, robustness, and efficiency required
  for realizing the computational properties that generate consciousness"
  (p. 349). A feedforward workspace "would be divorced from the
  environmental couplings and historicity by which adaptive predictive
  (world-)models are learned" (p. 349).
- **Conclusion (p. 349).** "Thus, there appears to be substantial
  convergence between IWMT and the HCH." Genesis and Coherence match
  IWMT's early, self-organised coherence; second-order perception matches
  deep temporal models in which high-level beliefs stabilise low-level
  ones, including body and attention schemas.

## Concepts

- **Integrated world modelling**: consciousness as coherent, embodied
  self-and-world modelling, across Marr's levels.
- **Self-organizing harmonic modes (SOHMs)**: synchronous neural complexes,
  metastable attractors that act as integrated cores and workspaces.
- **Embodied grounding**: the world model's capacity to inform and be
  informed by action–perception cycles at behavioural timescales, and the
  history of such coupling by which it was learned.
- **HCH**: Genesis, Coherence and Second-order perception (CIMC).

## Connections

It restates Safron 2020a, 2020b, 2021a, 2022 and 2023, and draws on
Thompson & Varela's radical embodiment, Metzinger's minimal phenomenal
experience, Cleeremans's radical plasticity thesis and Wiese's minimal
unifying model. The record holds Safron's editorial on world models
([LIT-195](../literature.d/LIT-195.md), [NOTE-136](NOTE-136.md)), which already cites IWMT as a possible
functional account. Laukkonen et al. ([LIT-1363](../literature.d/LIT-1363.md)) and Whyte et al.
([LIT-1364](../literature.d/LIT-1364.md)) both cite Safron's IWMT papers, the latter only to set
them aside as conceptual models. It cites COGITATE (Ferrante et al. 2025,
[LIT-973](../literature.d/LIT-973.md) in the record), and Seth & Friston 2016 for interoceptive
inference in feeling.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-413](../claims.d/CLAIM-413.md) (grounding), and a correction to the exchange.** The
  exchange cited this paper as requiring "embodiment and sensorimotor
  coupling, where the owner's thesis does not require current grounding,
  so the two part on that point". The reading changes where they part.
  IWMT does not require current sensory coupling: the model generates
  "either perception or imagination when respectively coupled to or
  decoupled from sense-data". It requires a capacity to inform and be
  informed by action–perception cycles, and the "historicity" of coupling
  by which the model was learned. So it agrees with the claim's occurrence
  half for current grounding, and parts from it on developmental grounding
  and on the capacity for coupling, the second and third of A6's four
  independence theses, about which the claim says it is silent. IWMT is
  then not an objection to the claim as stated. It is the enactive
  objection the claim leaves open, made specific. It also allows
  "virtual" embodiment, which the exchange did not mention.
- **[QUESTION-254](../questions.d/QUESTION-254.md).** The paper asks nearly the question itself: "the
  necessary and sufficient conditions for coherently generating a stream of
  experience, organized according to coherently structured perspectival
  reference frames" (p. 346). Its answer is the IWMT bundle: coherence in
  space, time and cause; egocentric organisation; embodied grounding;
  recurrence. The separations it draws from IIT and GNWT have the form the
  question asks for (integration, or broadcast, without the bundle is not
  enough). But the bundle is asserted, not derived, and embodiment is
  defined by degree ("sufficient degrees (and kinds)"), so it does not give
  the verdict on a firm or a language model that follows from the property
  rather than from intuition. On language models it gives one anyway.
- **[CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md) (constitution).** IWMT's phenomenal consciousness, what it is like to be the
  coherent functioning of a world model, is an identity of the claim's kind, with a
  different organization: a world model of an embodied sensorium, not
  interpretive organization in [TERM-073](../terms.d/TERM-073.md)'s sense. It is a rival
  candidate, and it builds in the embodiment the claim does not assume.
- **[TERM-072](../terms.d/TERM-072.md) (perspective).** IWMT makes egocentric, perspectival
  organization part of experience and suggests a minimal self-model may be
  required. That is an organizational and perhaps a represented
  perspective, against Laukkonen et al., who make a first-person
  perspective optional ([NOTE-1063](NOTE-1063.md)).
- **[THEORY-063](../theory.d/THEORY-063.md) (unity without a self-model).** IWMT hedges against its second
  clause: "some degree of basic self-modeling and agency may be required"
  (p. 346). It offers no case.
- **[QUESTION-255](../questions.d/QUESTION-255.md) (unity).** Unity on IWMT is coherence of one estimate,
  the MAP state. That is the single-assignment reading, and the paper does
  not discuss conflict held within one experience.
- **[CLAIM-411](../claims.d/CLAIM-411.md).** IWMT accepts that much modelling is not conscious
  (not every image in Figure 1 is "meant to indicate conscious
  experiences"), and its condition is coherence plus embodiment. It does
  not test that condition against blindsight or masked priming.
- **[THEORY-767](../theory.d/THEORY-767.md).** Interoceptive inference enters the sensorium estimate, after
  Seth & Friston 2016; nothing on ownership.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice. Its remarks on JEPA,
  transformers and neuromorphic hardware are about what might be
  conscious, not how to build models.

## Limitations

- A symposium summary: no data, no derivation and no new argument. Its
  claims rest on Safron's earlier papers, which were not read.
- HCH is described without a reference, so its principles cannot be
  checked against their source here.
- "Embodied grounding" is never given a threshold or a test; it comes in
  "sufficient degrees (and kinds)".
- The paper argues both that a simulation would recapitulate phenomenology
  within the simulated world and that consciousness may "in principle"
  require physical recurrence. It does not reconcile the two.
- The extracted text of the PDF is out of order because of the two-column
  layout and an abstract that runs into the introduction; one reference
  (Ferrante et al.) is broken across a column.

## Open questions

- What would show that a system lacks "sufficient" embodied grounding,
  rather than that it lacks experience for some other reason?
- Does developmental embodiment suffice, so that a world model learned in
  a body and then cut off stays conscious? IWMT's "historicity" suggests
  yes, which is [CLAIM-413](../claims.d/CLAIM-413.md)'s case.
- Where in HCH's own statement are the three principles argued?
