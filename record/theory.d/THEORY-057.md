# THEORY-057: Emotion categories are populations of variable instances grouped by the perceiver's concepts, not natural kinds with a shared neural or appraisal mechanism

## Source

Barrett (2017), [LIT-542](../literature.d/LIT-542.md), read in full in [NOTE-434](../notes.d/NOTE-434.md): "The theory of
constructed emotion", Table 1, the section on degeneracy, footnote 15 and
the list of claims the paper does not make.

## What was actually shown

The argument has three steps. None of them is a new experiment.

1. **Degeneracy.** Instances of one category, such as anger, are produced
   by dissimilar sets of neurons "in the same context" (the wording as
   corrected in nsx060). This is many-to-one mapping of structure to
   function, which Barrett says is "ubiquitous in biology".
2. **No consistent signature** (Table 1; C4–C5 in [NOTE-434](../notes.d/NOTE-434.md)). Cited
   meta-analyses find no region or intrinsic network that is consistent
   and specific for a category. Multivariate patterns that classify a
   category need not occur in any of its instances. Stimulation finds no
   emotion-specific receptive fields. Amygdala lesions have variable
   effects. Classifiers do not replicate across studies, and a classifier
   pattern is "a statistical summary of a highly variable set of
   instances". Taking it for the emotion's brain state is the
   "essentialist error".
3. **Population thinking.** After Darwin as read by Mayr, a category is a
   population of variable instances grouped "by 'a goal' rather than by
   similar features or a single, shared underlying cause". Its summary
   "need not exist in nature". An emotion is constructed when sensation is
   categorized with an emotion concept (C2).

The conclusion is that no emotion category has an essence, neither a
Platonic fingerprint in face, body or brain nor a Lockean mechanism, which
for appraisal theories is "a set of evaluative mechanisms". Descriptive
appraisals survive as products of categorization.

## What the philosophy holdings add

- **Degeneracy alone does not carry the conclusion.** Pernu ([LIT-147](../literature.d/LIT-147.md),
  [NOTE-091](../notes.d/NOTE-091.md)) shows the parallel mistake in arguments for mental causation.
  Many single-neuron patterns realise one cortical variable, and each
  intention corresponds to one value of that variable. So variability
  at the fine grain is micro over macro within the physical, and leaves
  identity at the coarser grain intact (C1, C7). Applied here, step 1 is
  consistent with a classical view that puts the emotion's mechanism at a
  coarser grain than neurons. The account therefore rests on step 2, the
  claim that no grain shows a consistent signature, and that step is
  cited from other papers that the record has not read. The application
  of Pernu is the record's own, and it is the reason `promote_when` asks
  about a variable at any grain.
- **Categories without definitions are coherent.** Minsky's frame memo
  ([LIT-410](../literature.d/LIT-410.md), [NOTE-337](../notes.d/NOTE-337.md), C5) argues that family resemblance "needs no
  definition" and can come from local similarity links. That is an
  existence argument, not evidence about emotions. It removes one
  objection: that a category with no shared feature could not be learned
  or used.
- **"Social reality" is narrower than it sounds.** Footnote 15 counts
  emotion categories among "the categories of social reality, such as
  flowers and weeds", whose functions are imposed on physically disparate
  instances by collective agreement, like currency. Searle ([LIT-482](../literature.d/LIT-482.md), [NOTE-388](../notes.d/NOTE-388.md)) makes such
  functions observer-relative (C1, C3). But his mark of institutional
  reality is a status function that carries deontic powers (C4), and
  Barrett names none for anger or fear. So her comparison is to
  observer-relative function in general, as with weeds, and not to
  institutions such as money. Epstein's split ([LIT-487](../literature.d/LIT-487.md), [NOTE-393](../notes.d/NOTE-393.md)) states
  what the theory needs. A particular instance is *grounded* in brain and
  body states. What makes it fall under "anger" is *anchored* in a
  community's concept. Neither paper discusses emotion, so this mapping
  is the record's own.
- **The rival has the same shape.** Frijda ([LIT-540](../literature.d/LIT-540.md), [NOTE-433](../notes.d/NOTE-433.md)) also lets
  instances vary. "When angry one can kick, shout insults, turn one's
  back", and he says that a motive is inferred "from observing a variety
  of equifunctional actions". Both accounts group variable instances by
  function. They disagree about *whose* function does the grouping:
  for Barrett it is the perceiver's goal-based concept, and for Frijda it
  is the agent's own readiness to change its relation to the object. That
  is the precise point of the rivalry with [THEORY-055](THEORY-055.md). It is held as
  `rivals`, not `corrects`, because neither paper cites or tests the
  other ([ADR-022](../decisions.d/ADR-022.md)).

## What this does not say

- **It does not say that emotions are unreal.** "I am not saying that
  emotions are illusions", Barrett writes, but that "emotion categories
  don't have distinct, dedicated neural essences". Instances are real
  brain events, and the categories are real in the way weeds are.
- **It does not deny affect.** Affect is felt allostatic state and is
  present whether or not any emotion concept is applied. That layer is
  [THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md), which this account sits on top of but does not need.
  Steps 1–3 do not use it.
- **It does not establish that appraisal theories are false.** Barrett's
  case is strongest against neural fingerprints. Her Figure 1 counts
  causal appraisal as a Lockean essence, but she does not engage any
  appraisal theory's predictions (Limitations in [NOTE-434](../notes.d/NOTE-434.md)). That is why
  the relation to the appraisal account is `rivals`.
- **Its denial of fear to the fly is perceiver-relative.** "Does the fly
  feel fear?" is answered "no" because the fly has no concept of fear.
  The fly "may feel affect".
- **It is not Dennett's real-patterns question.** Whether a category with
  no shared mechanism is still a real pattern in behaviour is a further
  question. The record's account of that criterion ([THEORY-036](THEORY-036.md)) finds
  that it admits almost every non-random pattern, so it would admit
  these categories without deciding anything between the rivals.
