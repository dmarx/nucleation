summary: >-
  Worrall (1989), DOI-10.1111/j.1746-8361.1989.tb00933.x. Worrall argues
  that the only position that honours both the no-miracles argument and
  the historical fact of radical theoretical change is Poincaré's
  "structural or syntactic realism". What survives theory change and
  explains predictive success is mathematical structure: equations carried
  over unchanged (Fresnel's reflection/refraction amplitude ratios,
  reinterpreted as electric-vector amplitudes in Maxwell's theory) or
  recovered as limiting cases under the correspondence principle (Newton
  from Einstein). The underlying ontology (elastic ether versus
  electromagnetic field) is not what survives. The support is one worked
  case and a general appeal to the correspondence principle. There are no
  formal definitions, and the paper says itself that "much clarificatory
  work needs to be done", above all on what it is for one theory's
  structure to approximate another's (p. 121).
