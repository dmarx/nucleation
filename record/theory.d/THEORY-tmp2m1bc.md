# THEORY-tmp2m1bc: No single-mechanism analysis of ordinary manipulation fits the clear cases: covert influence, covert intent, non-rational influence, trickery and pressure each misclassify a familiar tactic, so what unifies manipulation is unsettled

## Source

Noggle, "The Ethics of Manipulation", *Stanford Encyclopedia of
Philosophy* (2018; substantive revision 31 March 2026), [LIT-1007](../literature.d/LIT-1007.md), read in
full in [NOTE-tmphtua8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmphtua8.md): §2.1 (bypassing reason), §2.2 (trickery), §2.3
(pressure), §2.4 (disjunctive, hybrid and non-definitional views).

## What was actually shown

A survey's counterexamples, one or more per family of analyses.

- **Covert influence** (Ware, Strudler, Susser et al., [LIT-1002](../literature.d/LIT-1002.md)): fits
  priming, framing and gaslighting, but "Many manipulative influences,
  such as peer pressure, nagging, and guilt trips, do not seem covert."
- **Covert intent** (Belohrad; Whitfield, who needs only "plausible
  deniability"): the teenager nagging for a phone has a "quite
  transparent" intent.
- **Non-rational influence** (Raz): graphic portrayals of the dangers of
  smoking, the Golden Rule and dressing up for an interview are
  non-rational and not obviously manipulative. Literal bypassing
  (subliminal advertising, hypnosis) sets the bar too high to count the
  entry's ordinary examples.
- **Trickery** (Noggle 1996, Barnhill, Mills): charm offensives, peer
  pressure, nagging and emotional blackmail "do not seem to involve
  trickery".
- **Pressure** (Faden, Beauchamp and King; Kligman and Culver): misses
  pure misdirection; most of its proponents claim only sufficiency.
- **The conjunctive hybrid** (Greenspan) fails on pure pressure and on
  pure misdirection.

What is left (§2.4): a disjunction (Rudinow: "deception or pressure or by
playing on a supposed weakness"), whose cost is leaving unanswered "what
makes all forms of manipulation manifestations of the same phenomenon";
common-factor accounts (Noggle's 2025 Mistake Account, whose akrasia
claim the entry flags as unproven; Klenk's disregard for whether a method
reveals reasons; Baron's vice); and non-definitional accounts (Ackerman;
Cohen's cluster concept, "no single subset of which is necessary and
sufficient").

## What this does not say

- **It does not say manipulation has no unifying account.** Common-factor
  accounts locate the unity in a mistake induced or a manipulator's
  carelessness, not in one mechanism of influence, and the survey leaves
  them open. The claim is only that the five mechanisms each fail alone.
- **The cases are intuitions.** Each counterexample rests on finding a
  tactic clearly manipulative, or clearly not. A defender can bite the
  bullet, as Susser et al. do in calling open nagging a failed attempt at
  manipulation ([LIT-1002](../literature.d/LIT-1002.md), p. 5). The survey does not show such moves
  fail; it reports that they are costly.
- **It says nothing about global manipulation.** The entry is about
  ordinary tactics, and says that their relation to the "radical
  programming or reprogramming" of the free-will literature has hardly
  been explored (§1.1).
- **It does not settle whether manipulation needs intent.** §4.2 lists
  five answers and endorses none. That is a report of an open question,
  not a finding, and is not filed as one.
- **It does not settle why manipulation is wrong.** §3 leans, with a
  "perhaps", to prima facie and pro tanto wrongness and sets objections
  against harm and autonomy accounts. That is not filed either.

## Connections

No relations are declared.

- **[THEORY-tmppv6y2](THEORY-tmppv6y2.md) (covert influence and autonomy).** This account is why
  that one is filed of covert influence and not of manipulation as such.
  The two are compatible: one bounds the scope of the other.
- **[QUESTION-070](../questions.d/QUESTION-070.md).** Any answer must say what manipulation is before asking
  whether a whole with no intending member can do it. This account says
  that taking one mechanism as the definition will misclassify clear
  cases. Cohen's cluster concept, on which intent is one feature among
  several and none is necessary, is the option nearest a view on which a
  system without intent can manipulate.
- **[QUESTION-071](../questions.d/QUESTION-071.md).** The analyses here are of ordinary manipulation of
  single decisions. They do not by themselves supply what the four-case
  argument assumes.
