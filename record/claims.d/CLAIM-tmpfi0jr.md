# CLAIM-tmpfi0jr: The pragmatic identity of an utterance is partly constituted by the context-dependent transformations it induces, so pragmatic fidelity is an approximate structure-preserving correspondence between source and target dynamics

## The claim

A33 opened: "**A translation is pragmatically faithful to the extent that it
preserves the structure of the transformations an utterance induces in its
audience's interpretive state.**" A35 §10: "**The pragmatic identity of an
utterance is not exhausted by its propositional content or by a static
specification of its illocutionary force. It is partly constituted by the
structure of context-dependent transformations it induces in a communicative
system. Accordingly, pragmatic translation fidelity can be modeled as an
approximate structure-preserving correspondence between the interpretive and
performative dynamics of source and target utterances.**" The short form: "A
faithful translation preserves not merely what an utterance means, but how its
meaning acts upon—and is transformed by—the communicative contexts in which it
is encountered."

The move it made, A35 §5: "a shift from **invariance of attributes** to
**invariance of transformations and their composition**". Meaning, at A33, is
"what the utterance **does to possible interpretive states**", not a fixed
vector. A35 §10: "We've moved from comparing *representations* to comparing
*processes*."

## Why it was replaced

It presupposes interpretive states that can be put in correspondence. A35 §8
already weakened that ([TERM-tmpvpzy0](../terms.d/TERM-tmpvpzy0.md)), and A39 made observations primary ([CLAIM-tmpd81nk](CLAIM-tmpd81nk.md)).
The revision is [CLAIM-tmpnvxfj](CLAIM-tmpnvxfj.md). Its intertwining core survives in the manuscript as
[CLAIM-tmpx6akp](CLAIM-tmpx6akp.md).
