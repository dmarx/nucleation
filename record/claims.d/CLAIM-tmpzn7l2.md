# CLAIM-tmpzn7l2: A simulation whose operators each maximize an objective the modeller fixed cannot show self-constitution, because the norms the organization appears to maintain are then the modeller's, not set by the organization itself

## The objection

[CLAIM-tmpspyay](CLAIM-tmpspyay.md) is meant to be tested by building populations. A10 §7
proposes "**Sufficiency tests.** Construct systems satisfying the proposed
organizational conditions without adding predefined self-models or executive
controllers." A7's ideal first result is a population that develops
"specialized niches, persistent but revisable trust, and higher-level
governance structures solely through local transformation, temporally
discounted policy evaluation, and constrained endogenous endorsement."

In the model the exchange built, each operator is rewarded for something
the modeller wrote down. In A2 §6 it is J_i = E[Q_i(a*, c)] − λ_i Cost(E_i).
In A6 §1 it is discounted reward r_i. Barandiaran, Di Paolo and Rohde
([THEORY-071](../theory.d/THEORY-071.md), read in [NOTE-458](../notes.d/NOTE-458.md)) derive that "Systems optimizing an externally
fixed function are not models of agency" (C5), because normativity must be
"Regulation against a condition that can be failed, with the condition set
by the system's organization." Di Paolo (2005, [LIT-1017](../literature.d/LIT-1017.md)) makes the same
point about norms: they must be norms *for* the system, which it can use.

So a population of reward-maximizing operators that came to hold durable,
differentiated authority would show that endogenous governance can arise
from fixed local objectives. It would not show that the organization sets
the conditions it maintains, and that is the constitutive part of the
thesis. A10 §6.1 named the boundary case and left it open: "a system that
learns to maintain itself only because an external designer rewards that
behavior."

## What would answer it

- Operators whose objectives are themselves produced, weighted and revised
  by the organization, such as the viability of the coalition they belong
  to. A10 §3.2 moves this way: "Instead of imposing a global reward function
  defining what the system ought to do, ask whether its own organization
  generates conditions whose violation threatens its continued existence."
  [CLAIM-075](CLAIM-075.md) warns that viability alone does not settle what ought to
  persist.
- An argument that the modeller's objectives are only initial conditions
  that the organization then revises. A10 §6.1 calls this the difference
  between the historical origins of agency and its current constitutive
  organization.

## What it does not say

It does not say the simulations are worthless. They can test
[CLAIM-tmpf1063](CLAIM-tmpf1063.md) and [CLAIM-tmpgfyls](CLAIM-tmpgfyls.md), which make no constitutive claim.
