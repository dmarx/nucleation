# CLAIM-491: No action is selfless in the sense of aiming at a good that is no self's: what is called selflessness, collectivist or altruistic, is concern for the good of a self extended to include others or a group, so collectivism is self-interest on a larger self-identity

## The claim

The owner's position: there is no selflessness. A person who acts for a
group or for others acts for a self that includes them. The difference
between the "selfish" and the "collectivist" person is the size of the
self each identifies with, not whether each pursues their self's good.

The record holds four kinds of support:

- **Self-categorization theory** ([THEORY-1114](../theory.d/THEORY-1114.md)). Social identity is a
  self-categorization at a more inclusive level, as much a self as
  personal identity (Turner et al. 1994). Group-serving action is then
  action on that self's behalf.
- **Oneness and fusion.** Cialdini et al. ([LIT-1728](../literature.d/LIT-1728.md)) found helping
  tracked perceived self–other oneness, not empathy. Identity fusion
  (Swann et al., [LIT-1735](../literature.d/LIT-1735.md)) predicts self-sacrifice for the group from
  felt oneness of personal and group self.
- **Inclusive selves.** Self-expansion (Aron et al., [LIT-1706](../literature.d/LIT-1706.md)) and
  interdependent self-construal (Markus and Kitayama, [LIT-1703](../literature.d/LIT-1703.md)) give
  selves that include others.
- **Evolution.** Hamilton's rule ([THEORY-655](../theory.d/THEORY-655.md)) values others' fitness by
  relatedness, as if the self's genetic interest extended to kin.

## What it does not say

- **Not that people are self-centred** in the ordinary sense. The thesis
  locates collectivism within self-interest, not against it.
- **Not that the extended self is a group subject.** It is the
  individual's self-identification that extends.
- **Not settled by self-categorization theory.** That theory gives levels
  of the self, but its depersonalization says the personal self
  *recedes* when the social one is salient. That is a change of self, not
  plainly a larger one. Identity fusion and León's personalized "we"
  ([THEORY-1115](../theory.d/THEORY-1115.md)) fit "larger" better.
- **Its sharpest objections are two.** The empirical one: Batson et al.
  find empathic helping without self–other merging ([CLAIM-494](CLAIM-494.md)). The
  conceptual one: the thesis may be true by definition ([CLAIM-492](CLAIM-492.md)).
- **Two neighbouring views draw the line elsewhere.** Team reasoning
  (Sugden, [LIT-1782](../literature.d/LIT-1782.md)) treats "what is good for us" as a third mode,
  neither self-interest nor altruism. Akerlof and Kranton ([LIT-1752](../literature.d/LIT-1752.md))
  put identity in the utility function, which makes the thesis true of an
  economist's agent by construction.
- **Where the owner's question sits.** It is in both territories.
  Self-categorization theory supplies the mechanism of a larger self;
  the phenomenology of the "we" (Zahavi, [LIT-1758](../literature.d/LIT-1758.md); León) asks whether
  a "we" is a self at all, and León argues that intimate "we"s are built
  by individualization. Johnston ([LIT-1759](../literature.d/LIT-1759.md)) takes the same premise to
  the opposite moral: the good person's self is as large as humanity.

## Note of 2026-10-29: team reasoners and team benefactors

Salice and Miyazono ([NOTE-1266](../notes.d/NOTE-1266.md)) distinguish two people who both act
for a group.

- **The team benefactor** reasons in the first person singular about the
  group's good: "I intend that we J".
- **The team reasoner** has undergone self-transformation and intends as
  part of the group: "We intend to J".

The thesis fits the team reasoner best. Their good is the we's, and if the
we is their self, it is self-interest on a larger self. It fits the team
benefactor least: they care about a group without counting themselves in
it. Salice and Miyazono also offer what [CLAIM-492](CLAIM-492.md) asks for, a mark of
self-extension apart from caring: the activated social self, triggered by
group cues and not under direct rational control. A sharpened thesis would
say that collectivist action by team reasoners is self-interest on an
extended self, and leave the team benefactor's concern as the open case.

## Note of 2026-10-29: identity fusion, read

Swann et al. ([NOTE-1256](../notes.d/NOTE-1256.md)) has been read. It supports the thesis in two
ways and qualifies it in one.

- **A larger self, not a changed one.** Fused people keep a salient
  personal self and add the group to it. They say they would do more for
  the group than other members would, which is not depersonalization.
- **The prediction holds.** If the group is part of the self, defending
  the self should go through the group. Challenging fused participants'
  personal self-views made them readier to fight and die for Spain. It
  had no effect on the nonfused.
- **The self being served is a self-concept.** The authors call dying for
  the group "not in the individual's self-interest" and explain the effect
  by self-verification, the motive to keep one's self-views true. If the
  thesis recruits fusion, "self-interest" has to cover keeping a
  self-concept true, not only furthering one's welfare.

For [CLAIM-492](CLAIM-492.md), fusion is measured by perceived overlap, is specific
to one group and is separable from identification. But the paper never
measures caring separately, and its gloss on the fused is that they "care
as much about the outcomes of the group as their own". So it supplies a
candidate criterion without showing that the criterion comes apart from
caring. The outcomes are self-reported willingness, not acts.

## Note of 2026-10-29: Sugden, Stich and Johnston, read in place of unavailable works

Three works read in place of Sugden 2003, *Unto Others* and *Surviving
Death* each press the thesis to say more exactly what it claims.

- **A third shape of cooperation** (Sugden 2015, [NOTE-1288](../notes.d/NOTE-1288.md)). Bacharach's
  team reasoner pursues the team's collective good, which can outweigh a
  member's. That is close to self-interest on a larger self. Sugden's
  cooperative team reasoner intends each member's benefit, her own
  included, with no larger self in play. It is neither the thesis's larger
  self nor sacrifice. Sugden also rejects building pro-social motives into
  an individual's payoffs, which is what enlarging the individual does.
- **Two readings of the thesis** (Stich 2006, [NOTE-1282](../notes.d/NOTE-1282.md)). In the
  egoism debate's terms the thesis must choose. Either the desire for a
  child's or a group's good is instrumental to some good of the agent,
  which is classical egoism and meets Sober and Wilson and Batson. Or it is
  an ultimate desire whose object counts as part of the self. That is a
  redescription of what Sober and Wilson call altruism, and has content
  only with [CLAIM-492](CLAIM-492.md)'s independent criterion. Stich also shows that
  kin selection is silent about motive, so Hamilton's rule supports the
  thesis only by analogy.
- **Johnston has changed his mind** (Johnston 2023, [NOTE-1257](../notes.d/NOTE-1257.md)). The
  premise cited above, that the self's extent is fixed by one's deepest
  attitudes, is one he now rejects. His reason is that such selves
  multiply bearers of moral status. Whether an enlarged self inherits that
  problem is open.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the psychology, read in place of unavailable works

Works read in place of the unavailable psychology papers bear on the
thesis at five points.

- **The thesis in a precise form** (Miyazono and Inarimori, [NOTE-1277](../notes.d/NOTE-1277.md)).
  They read Cialdini's "merging" as group identification: empathizing with
  someone makes her welfare a constituent of "our" welfare. Helping is then
  altruistic at the individual level and egoistic at the group level. That
  is this claim, stated exactly, and it leaves Batson's individual-level
  altruism standing. On their reading empathy always brings identification
  with it, so the claim gains content only if identification can be found
  absent where empathy is present.
- **Two routes into a group self** (Otten, [NOTE-1301](../notes.d/NOTE-1301.md)). Projecting the
  personal self onto a new group (self-anchoring) is a larger self. Taking
  on the group's traits (self-stereotyping) is a changed one. Which
  operates depends on how much is known about the group. When participants
  could also pay themselves, they took the most for themselves and favoured
  the in-group with the rest: the personal self comes first.
- **Larger or switched** (Spears and Postmes, [NOTE-1280](../notes.d/NOTE-1280.md); Spears,
  [NOTE-1268](../notes.d/NOTE-1268.md)). The crowd member is not selfless: anonymity brings "the
  group in the self", and behaviour follows group norms more closely. But
  anonymity amplifies whichever identity is salient, which is a switch of
  self, not an enlargement. Identity fusion ([NOTE-1256](../notes.d/NOTE-1256.md)) stays the
  record's case for "larger".
- **Which larger self** (Brewer, [NOTE-1290](../notes.d/NOTE-1290.md)). Close relationships and
  groups both extend the self, in different ways: the relational self takes
  in particular others, the collective self a group. Brewer separates
  relational collectivism, obligation to specific persons, from group-based
  collectivism. The thesis should say which it means, or that it means
  both.
- **Helping follows the helper's category** (Dovidio et al., [NOTE-1286](../notes.d/NOTE-1286.md)).
  Recategorizing two groups as one removed in-group bias in helping: at a
  stadium, White fans complied with a Black interviewer's request 59% of
  the time when he wore their university's hat, against 36% when he did
  not. Shared identity raised empathic concern, which carried part of the
  effect. But White students helped a Black student who called himself a
  Black Colgate student no more than one who called himself Black. If
  helping follows an extended self, it is the helper's version of the
  shared category.

The same extension in time, legacy, is [QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md).

## Note of 2026-10-29: the thesis in time

The readings on legacy and on obligations to the future ([QUESTION-301](../questions.d/QUESTION-301.md),
[QUESTION-299](../questions.d/QUESTION-299.md)) bear on the thesis in four ways.

- **Its shape holds for prudence** (Hershfield, [NOTE-1259](../notes.d/NOTE-1259.md)). Felt
  overlap with one's future self predicts patience, so prudence is regard
  scaled by overlap. The thesis and its mirror, "no prudence is purely
  self-interested", are claims about one scale.
- **Scheffler is not the counterexample he was filed as** ([NOTE-1298](../notes.d/NOTE-1298.md)).
  He reads our concern for humanity's survival as dependence, not
  altruism. The self served would be one's standing as a valuer, a third
  sense of "self-interest" the thesis would have to adopt explicitly.
- **Where the far future is concerned, the evidence points elsewhere**
  (Schubert et al., [NOTE-1270](../notes.d/NOTE-1270.md); Caviola et al., [NOTE-1267](../notes.d/NOTE-1267.md)). Lay
  concern reaches the far future through a picture of it as good, an
  impersonal valuation that no measured self carries. That is the hardest
  case the record now holds, and it is thin.
- **Bostrom grants the thesis its prediction** ([NOTE-1321](../notes.d/NOTE-1321.md)). Concern
  for existential safety is undersupplied because people capture only
  their own share of it: concern reaches as far as the self's stake.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the thesis backward in time

The intergenerational-trauma readings ([QUESTION-297](../questions.d/QUESTION-297.md)) test the thesis's
reading that a descendant's grief over an ancestral injury is
self-regarding. They support its shape and move its boundary.

- **The self an ancestral injury extends to is a category, not a line of
  descent** (Eyerman, [NOTE-1246](../notes.d/NOTE-1246.md)). Slavery became the trauma of black
  Americans "whether or not they directly experienced slavery or even had
  ancestors who did" (p. 14). "Ancestral" grief, if self-regarding, is
  regard for a category's past.
- **What joins the self is an image, felt as grievance** (Volkan,
  [NOTE-1250](../notes.d/NOTE-1250.md)). A chosen trauma is a shared image of an ancient injury,
  "real, fantasized or even mythologized", called up when the group's
  present identity is threatened. That fits a self-regarding reading
  well. Grief for particular, recent dead is the case Volkan keeps apart,
  and the thesis's open one.
- **Family transmission is not where the effect is** (Fridman, [NOTE-1291](../notes.d/NOTE-1291.md)).
  Survivors' daughters did not differ from daughters of families who
  shared the same national memory. If an ancestral injury is carried as
  part of a self, it is carried by the group, not handed down in the
  family.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the remaining sources on the self, read

Four works the thesis was filed against have now been read. They agree on
one point: what group membership changes is the self's content or
self-image, and none of them shows a self enlarged to take in the group's
good.

- **Markus and Kitayama ground the premise, not the conclusion**
  ([NOTE-1275](../notes.d/NOTE-1275.md)). For interdependent selves, "others are included
  *within* the boundaries of the self" (p. 245), and the authors name the
  thesis's middle position, "an alternative to selfishness … besides
  selflessness" (p. 247). But this "does not result in a merging of self
  and other" (p. 228). The interdependent self is made of relations and
  roles, and its in-group may be narrower, not larger. The authors read
  the resulting action as possibly "genuine, other-oriented, altruistic"
  (p. 248). They also split collectivist motive in two (p. 229):
  - others' goals taken as one's own, which is the thesis;
  - others' goals met as the price of watched reciprocity, which needs no
    extended self.
- **Akerlof and Kranton make it true only in the trivial sense**
  ([NOTE-1311](../notes.d/NOTE-1311.md)). Identity-driven sacrifice maximizes a utility that
  includes identity. The identity is a self-image, kept by following one's
  categories' prescriptions. No group's welfare enters anyone's utility,
  and a fellow member's deviance is a loss one may relieve by harming
  them. Their criterion of the self's extent, category assignment partly
  fixed by others, could come apart from caring ([CLAIM-492](CLAIM-492.md)). But its
  weights are free parameters read off the behaviour they explain.
- **Zahavi: a "we" is "not an enlarged I"** ([NOTE-1289](../notes.d/NOTE-1289.md), p. 10). It keeps
  the plurality it unites. Identification changes how one apprehends
  oneself, as one of us seen through another's eyes: a changed self more
  than a larger one. His account covers only the face-to-face "we".
- **The self-esteem hypothesis: benefit without motive** (Rubin and
  Hewstone, [NOTE-1278](../notes.d/NOTE-1278.md)). This is social identity theory's own version
  of the thesis. Favouring the group tends to raise self-esteem (18
  experiments for, 13 against), but a need for self-esteem rarely drives
  it (7 for, 28 against). A benefit that follows group-serving action
  without motivating it does not show the action aimed at the self's good.
  The group-level motive was nearly untested; the two studies that
  measured it both support it.

The thesis survives these readings only as the claim that the self served
is a self-concept. That is the third sense of "self-interest" the note on
Scheffler named. On that reading, [CLAIM-492](CLAIM-492.md)'s demand for a criterion
apart from caring is still unmet.

## Note of 2026-10-30: what holds it back

Two objections are unanswered. [CLAIM-494](CLAIM-494.md), Batson et al.'s empathic helping
with self–other merging controlled, is Proposed, and nothing in the record
answers it. [CLAIM-492](CLAIM-492.md) is conceded: the thesis has content only with a
criterion of the self's extent that comes apart from caring, and the
readings offer candidates (fusion, the activated social self) but show
none coming apart from caring. Its best evidence, Cialdini et al.
([LIT-1728](../literature.d/LIT-1728.md)), is Unavailable and read only at second hand.

## Note of 2026-10-30: filed with its concessions

[CLAIM-492](CLAIM-492.md) has been written into this claim's defeated_if since filing:
the thesis is answered only if it is tied to a criterion of the self's
extent, such as perceived overlap, fusion or salient
self-categorization, that can come apart from caring. That conditional
is conceded. The claim has not yet named such a criterion and shown it
coming apart from caring, and until it does it says nothing that caring
alone does not.
