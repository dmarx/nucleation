# TERM-030: frame (communicative situation)

## Definition

A38 §2.3 restored the tuple f = (S, A, K, N, G): speaker position, audience,
common ground, operative norms, genre. A frame "must be more than an analyst's
interpretive label", and changing "the *description* of an utterance" differs
from changing "the *communicative situation*". A39 §3.1 folded the utterance and
the interaction history in, c = (u, s, a, k, n, g, h), and §3.2 defined "a
pragmatic frame as a configuration of relevant social and interpretive
conditions". It extends A24's measurement context ([TERM-031](TERM-031.md)) with norms and
history, and is no longer an index on judgements only.

## What it is not

Not an act on a reader: that is a framing intervention ([TERM-018](TERM-018.md)). A39: "A friend
speaking is a property of a communicative situation."

## Note of 2026-11-24: the exchange 'Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity', A32

A32 §3 reads the frame's components as contractual: speaker position as
standing and authority, audience as participants and affected parties,
common ground as presupposed assumptions, operative norms as commitments,
genre as an established interaction protocol, history as formation and
maintenance provenance. "A pragmatic frame is not necessarily a contract.
But it can express or provide access to contractual organization." A
framing intervention can then change which established relationship
governs or help form one, which is reflexive control by frame: "The
request *help me* acquires different practical significance when framed
as an emergency, professional obligation, reciprocal commitment or
discretionary favor." An application of the term, not a revision; the
claims it serves are [CLAIM-tmp80ny9](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp80ny9.md) and [CLAIM-tmpro73c](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpro73c.md). The wording of this note is the assistant's (Claude, in this session), reporting A's (ChatGPT's) text; adopting what it reports is the owner's call.
