# NOTE-2025: Ensign, Friedler, Neville, Scheidegger and Venkatasubramanian (2018) — Runaway Feedback Loops in Predictive Policing

## Contribution

A proof, in a stylized model, of why a predictive-policing system that is retrained on what its own deployments discover runs away from the true crime rates, a quantitative link between how far it runs and the disparity between regions, and a black-box remedy that filters the system's inputs.

## Key results

1. Abstract: such systems are "susceptible to runaway feedback loops, where police are repeatedly sent back to the same neighborhoods regardless of the true crime rate".
2. §1: "Decisions made by the system influence the data that is fed to it in the future."
3. §2.1, n.1: "PredPol— critically — conflates amount of crime and incident data": it predicts "where incidents will be reported or discovered", not where crime happens.
4. §3.2, Lemma 2: with equal crime rates the limiting share is a Beta draw fixed by the initial counts; "the prior belief coupled with the lack of feedback about the unobserved region" stops the system learning that the regions are alike.
5. §3.3, Lemma 4: with unequal rates the urn goes to the higher-rate region with probability 1; at 10% against 11% it will "settle on region B with probability 1", a "go with the winner" problem.
6. §3.4.1: reported incidents help only if discovered ones are down-weighted or the rates are nearly equal, yet "it is precisely when crime rates are different that predictive policing is of value".
7. §3.5: the remedy is rejection sampling, "we should simply drop the incident record" in proportion to how often police are sent there, "a rejection-sampling variant of Thompson-Horvitz estimation".
8. §4–§5, Figs 1–2: on Lum and Isaac's Oakland data, the urn and a PredPol simulation "converge to only sending police" to the top region; with the remedy both settle near the true ratio.
9. §6: "reporting is noisy and skewed by area and by type of crime", and without the true-rate assumption "the solutions might not continue to work".

## Assumptions

One officer and two regions; only counts are kept; patrols discover and residents report crime at its true rate (Assumption 3.3, "Truth in Crime Data", which the authors call generous); deployment is a coin toss on current statistics. Policing does not change the crime rate: the loop runs through where evidence is gathered, not through any change in the people policed.

## Concepts

Runaway feedback loop; discovered against reported incidents; generalized Pólya urn; "go with the winner"; observation selected by the decision; rejection-sampling (Horvitz–Thompson) correction; black-box input filtering.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-277](../claims.d/CLAIM-277.md)**: corroborates its second clause, that the loop "entrenches whatever gained influence early", in an independent formal model: under equal rates the outcome is fixed by the initial counts (result 4). Under unequal rates the loop turns a slight true difference into total allocation (result 5), which is entrenchment of the leader and not only of the early.
- **[CLAIM-406](../claims.d/CLAIM-406.md)**: corroborates its form. The deployment decides where incidents can be found, and only being patrolled supplies incidents, as only being sought supplies indicators of competence. The paper says nothing about injustice.
- **[CLAIM-707](../claims.d/CLAIM-707.md)**: no bearing. The paper does not discuss blame, credit or compensation; the LIT's link to it was A19's analogy.
- **The exchange**, A19 §6: its description of the paper is accurate. Its extension, that algorithms "convert effects of institutional treatment into apparent properties of the people being treated", is A's: in this model treatment changes nothing about the people (Assumption 3.3).

## Limitations

Stylized two-region model; the remedy needs the deployment probabilities and the true-rate assumption; behavioural responses to policing are not modelled; the PredPol results are simulations on one dataset with estimated drug-use rates.

  is the selector. It qualifies the claim's remedy: independent evidence
  (reported incidents) mixed with self-selected evidence only
  "attenuate[s]" the loop, and the authors' fix reweights the self-selected
  stream by the probability of selection rather than only adding
  independent evidence. Corroborates.
