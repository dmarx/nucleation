# THEORY-1490: Hankins, Hoekstra and Skiba find that Florida lottery winners of 50,000 to 150,000 dollars were about half as likely as winners of under 10,000 dollars to file for bankruptcy in the two years after winning but more likely to file three to five years after, so that over five years no difference in bankruptcy was detected, and that the large winners who filed had similar net assets and unsecured debt, from which the authors conclude that large cash transfers postpone rather than prevent bankruptcy

## Source

Hankins, Hoekstra & Skiba (2011), [LIT-2184](../literature.d/LIT-2184.md); read in [NOTE-1673](../notes.d/NOTE-1673.md)
from the 2009-02-16 working draft. Page and table numbers are the
draft's.

## What was actually shown

- **Design.** 34,987 first-time Florida Fantasy 5 winners of $600 to
  $150,000 (1993–2002), linked by name and county to every Florida
  Chapter 7 and 13 filing. Given a win, its size is as good as random:
  prize size does not predict filing before winning once year effects and
  a game-structure control are included (Table 3). The result could have
  come out otherwise: a lasting fall, or no fall at all.
- **Timing** (Table 4, col. 4). Against winners of under $10,000, winners
  of $50,000–150,000 were 1.63 points less likely to file within two years
  (a 50% decline) and 1.21 points more likely in years 3–5 (se 0.0062);
  over five years −0.42 points (se 0.78). "These declines are offset,
  however, by increases of 0.5 and 1.21 percentage points three to five
  years after winning".
- **Size** (p. 7). The median large prize, $65,000 pre-tax, exceeded the
  $52,000 unsecured debt of the most distressed players.
- **Balance sheets** (Table 6). Large-winner filers within five years (47,
  against 103 small) had net assets and unsecured debt not significantly
  different: "whatever the recipients did with their cash, they did not
  use it to either pay down debt or increase their assets."

## What this does not say

- That the money has no effect: it halves filing for two years.
- That the five-year effect is zero: the 95% interval is about −1.9 to
  +1.1 points on a rate near 5.5%.
- Why the money was not kept. Strategic consumption, high discount rates,
  mental accounting and low financial literacy are listed (p. 1), none
  tested.
- That the same holds for other recipients, other forms of transfer, or
  people far from the bankruptcy margin; the authors say the results "may
  warrant some skepticism about the long-term efficacy of such a bailout",
  not that it fails.
- Anything about labour supply, earnings, consumption or distress short of
  filing, which are not measured.
- That a windfall cannot cross a threshold. The contrast with
  [THEORY-1465](THEORY-1465.md), where a one-off productive asset did, and with
  [CLAIM-601](../claims.d/CLAIM-601.md) and [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md), is the record's reading, not the
  authors'.
