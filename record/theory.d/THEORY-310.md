# THEORY-310: On a forward-looking justification of blame, a counterfactual capacity condition calibrated to what makes blaming effective is redundant: fixing its parameters presupposes that blaming such agents is good policy, so freedom is ascribed because of blameworthiness rather than grounding it

## Source

Harry Harland (2020), *The Journal of Ethics* 24:401–425, [LIT-991](../literature.d/LIT-991.md), read
in full in [NOTE-847](../notes.d/NOTE-847.md): §3.1 (Vargas's test) and §3.2 (the redundancy
and inversion arguments), with §4.

## What was actually shown

**The target.** Vargas's agency cultivation model justifies praise and
blame by their effects on a valuable form of agency, responsiveness to
moral considerations. Its free-will condition is counterfactual: an agent
who failed to respond to a moral consideration M in circumstances C had
the capacity if she would detect M, and act on it, "in a suitable
proportion" of worlds "relevantly similar" to C (Vargas 2013: 213–214).
Relevance and proportion are fixed by what is "at least co-optimal for
the cultivation of moral considerations-responsive agency" (2013:
214–221). And responsibility is "a function of what the justified norms
of moral influence say" (2013: 103).

**The redundancy argument.** To apply the test to Jones, one must give
its parameters a value. They have no value until one decides "whether it
is good policy to blame similar people for similar wrongdoings". Once
that is decided, the model already says whether to hold Jones
responsible, so "the work necessary to give the counterfactual test
meaning makes that very test redundant". Harland works it for
psychopaths, kleptomaniacs, disruptive pupils, religious bigots and
alcoholics: in each, the verdict follows from how the class responds to
blame.

**The inversion.** So "a wrongdoer's blameworthiness is logically prior
to his free will". On the model, the statement that he has free will is
"a roundabout way of saying" that the model gives "good
(freedom-independent) reasons to hold him responsible". Harland diagnoses
the source: the test is asked both to track who is improved by blame and
to preserve the folk view that responsibility needs the ability to do
otherwise.

**What could have come out otherwise.** The argument depends on a
reading of Vargas: that the parameters are fixed only by the system's
aims. Footnote 7 records that McGeer read Vargas the other way, with
capacities "ontologically prior to, and conceptually independent of"
their role in the system, and that Vargas rejected her reading. If he
had accepted it, the test would have content of its own and the
argument would fail.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say that freedom is irrelevant to holding people
  responsible.** The abstract says so unconditionally. The body restricts
  it: "if we embrace ACM, enquiries into an agent's counterfactual freedom
  will become superfluous". On accounts where avoidability is a
  metaphysical condition, as in [THEORY-118](THEORY-118.md) and [THEORY-119](THEORY-119.md), the argument
  has no grip.
- **It does not say that forward-looking accounts are false.** It says
  one such account cannot also have a free-will condition that does
  independent work. Harland's case that the model is no advance on the
  older moral influence theory (§2) is a separate argument and not part
  of this account.
- **It does not show that the test can never sort individuals.** The
  argument proceeds class by class, with each class's response to blame
  known in advance. If the test is what sorts individuals into classes,
  or the parameters are calibrated once and then applied by facts about
  each agent, the test keeps work to do. This is the reader's objection;
  Harland does not consider it.
- **It rests on Harland's Vargas.** Building Better Beings is not held
  here. Every quotation of Vargas is Harland's.
- **It says nothing about ownership.** Whose the will is does not come up
  in the paper.

## Connections

- **[QUESTION-068](../questions.d/QUESTION-068.md) (Deferred; bears on it, does not answer it).** The
  question asks whether accountability needs conditions, such as
  avoidability, that ownership does not supply. This account says that on
  an influence-based justification an avoidability condition calibrated
  to the practice is not a further condition: it is fixed by the ethics
  of holding responsible. So the question's avoidability option has
  content only on accounts that ground it outside the practice.
- **[THEORY-118](THEORY-118.md) and [THEORY-119](THEORY-119.md).** Both treat the ability to do otherwise
  as a metaphysical condition on blame, for and against. This account
  concerns a view on which it is not, and leaves their dispute where it
  is.
- **[THEORY-122](THEORY-122.md).** Harland's alternative to Vargas's notion of agency,
  reflective scrutiny of one's convictions, is a normative-competence
  condition of [THEORY-122](THEORY-122.md)'s kind. Harland raises it as a dilemma, not as
  his view.
- **[THEORY-147](THEORY-147.md).** Harland separates the reasons to hold someone
  accountable from her moral agency. That fits [THEORY-147](THEORY-147.md)'s independence
  of accountability from other kinds of responsibility, without adding a
  positive account.
