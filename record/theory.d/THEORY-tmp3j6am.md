# THEORY-tmp3j6am: A protest performed bodily inside a civic ritual can contest the ritual in its own grammar, keeping the ritual's reverence for its ideal while rebuking its present object, which is incivility in the Confucian sense of disruption aimed at harmonization; the reading of the gesture and the claim that its uptake makes it sagely are asserted

## Source

Walsh (2021), "The Phenomenology of Ritual Resistance: Colin Kaepernick as
Confucian Sage", [LIT-209](../literature.d/LIT-209.md) (*Dao*), read in full in [NOTE-164](../notes.d/NOTE-164.md) from the accepted
manuscript.

## What was actually shown

An interpretive argument; no data.

- **The register (§2).** Critics grant Kaepernick the right and charge
  disrespect. On Walsh's reading that shows "widespread implicit approval of
  a basic principle of Confucian statecraft": using ritual to inscribe
  values on the body politic. Rights-talk is the wrong register for the
  dispute.
- **The licence (§3).** Classical Confucianism endorses remonstrance
  (Analects 13.15, 14.22; Mengzi 4B:3, 5B:9) and incivility aimed at
  harmonization (he 和) rather than mere agreement (tong 同) (Analects 13.23;
  Kim 2011). The reading rates this strong as textual exegesis. The concept
  is Kim's, not Walsh's.
- **The extension (§§3–4).** Existing scholarship treats only discursive
  incivility. Kaepernick's case shows incivility "functioning at the bodily
  level within ritual itself" (p. 11). It also "democratizes" incivility,
  which the classical texts reserve for ministers, and has "mimetic force",
  being readily imitated at the visual centre of the ritual.
- **The mechanism (§5).** Habits are normative, a "tension that oscillates
  around a norm". Ritual meaning is sedimented and can carry oppressive
  norms. Style allows modification within mastery. Ritual action is
  "motivated", between "absolute mechanism and absolute voluntarism".
- **The gesture (§§6–7).** Kneeling keeps reverence, deference and humility,
  unlike the raised fist. It honours the American ideal as one "we
  necessarily will fail to live up to".

What could have come out otherwise: the Confucian texts might have licensed
only verbal remonstrance by officials. They do license remonstrance; whether
they reach a bodily gesture by anyone is Walsh's extension.

## What this does not say

- **That kneeling means reverence.** That is an interpretation of how the
  gesture reads. The paper reports critics who read it as disrespect and
  gives no way to settle between the readings beyond Kaepernick's stated aims
  ([NOTE-164](../notes.d/NOTE-164.md), limitations).
- **That uptake makes a gesture sagely.** §5.5 says sagely authority "just is
  his power to institute new body language that others take up". Walsh also
  cites Analects 13.24 against popular approval as a measure. No criterion
  separates sagely citational force from virality. The constraints that do
  the work, aiming at harmonization, fidelity to what is central, reverence,
  are asserted of this case (§§3, 6).
- **That Kaepernick is a sage in the full sense.** "Sage" is deflationary:
  Walsh is "certainly not arguing" that Kaepernick embodies perfect
  morality (§1, p. 3). The gesture is sagely because it institutes a new
  ritual form.
- **That patriotism is valuable.** Walsh takes no position (§1, p. 2). The
  claim of compatibility with American ideals rests on MacIntyre's account
  of patriotism.
- **That sagehood is a collective, diachronic project.** Walsh adopts
  Nivison's and Hutton's reading of Xunzi while conceding it is "not
  definitive" (§4), and uses the case both to apply it and to support it.
- **The empirical claims.** The post-9/11 ritualisation of sport and the
  Department of Defense spending rest on journalism and a Senate report
  whose year is cited two ways (2015 and 2017).

## Connections

- Bunton ([LIT-174](../literature.d/LIT-174.md)) treats the same protest as speaking out and asks when a
  celebrity's speech is justified. On this account the act is not speech but
  bodily ritual, and its force comes from that difference.
- Archer & Robb ([THEORY-tmpg3y0n](THEORY-tmpg3y0n.md)) concern the audience's response when a
  public figure changes a presented self. Walsh's critics are such an
  audience, and on that account their refusal of uptake would be a wrong.
- The phenomenology of habit as normative, and of action "between absolute
  mechanism and absolute voluntarism", bears on accounts of embodied,
  habitual agency elsewhere in the record. The paper does not engage them.
