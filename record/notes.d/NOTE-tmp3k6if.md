# NOTE-tmp3k6if: Howard, Gagné and Bureau (2017) — Testing a continuum structure of self-determined motivation

## Contribution

The first meta-analytic test of the continuum (simplex) assumption across
domains and scales, with recommendations on scoring: no evidence for the
relative autonomy index's weights; introjection is mixed; intrinsic
subscales should not be used; integrated regulation needs removal from the
theory or reconceptualization.

## Key results

1. **The continuum (pp. 19, 22–23).** The matrix "generally conforms to a
   simplex pattern"; "Results largely supported the presence of a continuum
   ordering of motivation". One dimension: normalized stress .00246; a
   second adds ".071%".
2. **Integrated against identified (p. 19; Tables 1–2).** Pooled corrected
   r = .913 (k = 98); integrated–intrinsic .818, identified–intrinsic .853.
   "The exception to this trend was integrated regulation which correlated
   with regulations in much the same direction and strength as identified
   regulation"; "confidence intervals of integrated regulation almost
   always overlapped with neighboring regulations, suggesting a lack of
   differentiation" (p. 26).
3. **The verdict (pp. 28–29).** "integrated regulation may not have a place
   on the continuum", "practically indistinguishable from identified
   regulation"; "there needs to be a revision of the theory to exclude this
   form of regulation or new ways of conceptualizing and operationalizing
   integrated regulation need to be developed."
4. **A second dimension? (pp. 24–25).** Identified and integrated may differ
   somewhat on it; "It is currently unclear whether this second dimension
   is theoretically important" or noise.
5. **Introjection (p. 28).** "relatively equidistant from both external and
   identified regulations", correlating .603 with identified and .313 with
   intrinsic.
6. **Intrinsic subtypes (p. 29).** "it is recommended that intrinsic
   subscales are not used".
7. **Amotivation (p. 29)** fits as "a negative counterpoint to intrinsic
   motivation".
8. **The index (p. 31).** Nothing "provide[s] evidence for the weights used in
   the RAI", though a single self-determination score is defensible.
9. **Heterogeneity (pp. 20–22).** I² from 98.13% to 99.75%, unexplained by
   domain, scale, country, occupation, age or gender.

## Assumptions

- The correlational structure of self-report subscales is evidence about
  the structure of motivation.
- Reliability correction, with imputed alphas where missing.
- Publication bias dismissed partly by argument (inter-factor correlations
  are not selected for significance), plus funnel plots.
- English-language reports only.

## Concepts

Relative autonomy continuum; simplex; internalization; external,
introjected, identified and integrated regulation; intrinsic motivation;
amotivation; relative autonomy index; metric multidimensional scaling;
heterogeneity.

## Bearing on the record

- **On the LIT's characterization.** A2's "does not find integrated
  regulation as a distinct factor" is right, with two refinements: the
  paper runs no factor analysis but finds integrated regulation's
  correlations and intervals indistinguishable from identified
  regulation's, and it leaves open a small second dimension. LIT-1996's
  history gloss, "partly because too few studies measured integration", is
  not the paper's reason: integrated regulation has k = 84–98 samples, and
  the paper puts the problem in the construct and its measures.
- **THEORY-054**: first-hand evidence on its first promote_when clause: no
  validated scale to 2016 separates integrated from identified regulation.
  It supports the graded half and undercuts the "by integration" half as
  measured.
- **THEORY-1327**, **NOTE-1500** and **LIT-1967**: the meta-review's report
  is confirmed.
- **THEORY-120**: identified regulation is the analogue of endorsement, so
  the data cannot tell a coherence account from an endorsement account.
- **NOTE-448**, **NOTE-442** / **LIT-559**, **NOTE-1513**, **NOTE-436**,
  **NOTE-431**, **NOTE-441**, **THEORY-075**: dated notes on each.
- **No bearing on needs or their thwarting**: the paper studies the
  structure of regulations, with need satisfaction only as background
  (p. 6).
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- Correlational and cross-sectional self-report: it shows how participants
  perceive the regulations, not how motivation is structured.
- Disattenuated correlations near .9 may overstate overlap if reliabilities
  were underestimated.
- With heterogeneity near 99%, the pooled matrix may describe no actual
  population, as the authors concede.
- The scaling is exploratory; alternative structures, such as a two-cluster
  autonomous and controlled model, are not tested against the simplex.
- Integration's non-separability is put down to the measures or to the
  theory in turn, with no evidence deciding between them.
- Small inconsistencies between Tables 1 and 2; a manuscript, not the
  version of record.
