# NOTE-tmpcenk3: Massimi (2023) — Perspectival realism, a primer

## Contribution

A short statement, by its author, of the view of Perspectival Realism
(LIT-1228), written against the "follow the science" politics of the
pandemic: an image of science as situated and collective that is still
realist, offered as an antidote to both deference to "Science" and
distrust of experts.

## Key results

1. **Perspective (§"Scientific perspectives and windows on reality").** "I
   think of a scientific perspective in broad terms as the historically and
   culturally situated scientific practice of a community at a given
   historical time", its theories, instruments and "general
   methodological-epistemic principles".
2. **No self-certification (same section).** "No particular community
   can sanction the reliability of their own claims of knowledge or
   justify such reliability by their own lights." Reliability comes from
   "the ability of this plurality of communities to compare data, to enter
   in dialogue, and to make relevant and appropriate inferences from data
   to the phenomena".
3. **Intersecting and interlacing.** Perspectives "methodologically
   intersect" in present work, with models as "inferential blueprints"
   that let communities "come together, compare their data, and make
   reliable inferences"; and they "historically interlace", trading tools
   whose history forms a "historical lineage" (the geomantic compass
   becoming the mariner's).
4. **What is real (§"Modally robust phenomena and natural kinds with a
   human face").** "Phenomena are stable events indexed to a particular
   domain (depending on the context of inquiry) and modally robust across
   a variety of perspectival data-to-phenomena inferences." Natural kinds
   "are nothing over and above historically identified and open-ended
   groupings of modally robust phenomena"; the view "does not make natural
   kinds any less real".
5. **Epistemic injustice (§"The right to enjoy the benefits of scientific
   progress").** "Epistemic severing is the almost surgical excision of
   the contribution of particular communities ... from narratives about
   scientific knowledge production"; trademarking is presenting the
   result as one community's. These mechanisms are "often ... implicit
   and structural rather than intentional".
6. **To rights.** Seen as a cosmopolitan right, the right to enjoy the
   benefits of scientific progress carries obligations: "Where there are
   rights, there are obligations."

## Assumptions

- That reliability is secured by intersection and interlacing is stated,
  not argued, here; the arguments are in the book.
- The realism is about phenomena; the primer does not say what makes
  phenomena mind-independent, only that they are modally robust across
  perspectives.

## Concepts

Science sub specie aeternitatis; scientific perspective; methodological
intersecting; historical interlacing; historical lineage; inferential
blueprint; perspectival modelling; modally robust phenomena; natural kinds
with a human face; epistemic severing; epistemic trademarking; non-classist
scientific cosmopolitanism.

## Connections

How far it stands in for LIT-1228. It restates the book's theses and its
vocabulary (perspective, intersection, interlacing, inferential
blueprints, modally robust phenomena, natural kinds with a human face,
severing and trademarking), with pointers to chapters 5, 10 and 11. It
carries none of the book's arguments: not the case studies (nuclear
physics, climate science, developmental psychology), not the treatment
of incompatible models, and not the account of how intersection secures
truth-conduciveness. A18's reason for naming Massimi, a "model for
cross-perspectival accountability", rests on result 2, which the primer
states and the book would have to argue.

## Bearing on the record

- **QUESTION-129**: a candidate for the "Partial" answer, in the domain of
  knowledge (dated note there). Massimi's constraint on admissible
  perspectives is procedural: no community certifies itself, and
  reliability is what survives comparison across communities. That is
  A22's "Openness to justified criticism and comparison across affected
  standpoints" stated for science. It rules some claims out (those no
  other community can take up) without ranking the rest, and it is
  epistemic: it says which claims are reliable, not which standards bind.
  Whether it carries to normative standards is the question's, not the
  primer's.
- **CLAIM-363**: a parallel, in epistemology. Knowledge "indexed to a
  particular domain (depending on the context of inquiry)" and still not
  arbitrary is the claim's two parts for scientific claims. Analogy only;
  no edge (dated note there).
- **QUESTION-456**: a parallel. Epistemic severing, excluding a
  community's contribution from the account of how knowledge was made,
  "implicit and structural rather than intentional", is the knowledge-side
  form of a demand set shaped by the governance that should be judged
  against it. Analogy only; no note.
- **The constitutivism test (CLAIM-tmp8g26d)**: Massimi's "no
  self-certification" is the epistemic counterpart of the premise reading
  (a) says the dynamics cannot supply: a standard that binds a community
  from outside its own lights. She supplies it by procedure, not by
  constitution.
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.**

## Limitations

- **A primer**: eight pages, no arguments, written for a general
  scientific audience with a political aim.
- **A preprint**: the journal text was not compared.
- **About knowledge, not norms**: nothing in it says that standards of
  action, as distinct from claims about phenomena, can be adjudicated
  across perspectives.
