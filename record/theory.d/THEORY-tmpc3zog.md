# THEORY-tmpc3zog: The unity that makes a movement an agent's action is achieved by conformity to the constitutive norms of agency, not given by the causal unity of a mind, so collective agents are agents in the same sense as individual ones

## Which unity this is about

The record keeps four unities apart ([ADR-024](../decisions.d/ADR-024.md)). This account is about the
unity of **agency**, and it locates that unity in **self-governance**:
self-determination by principles the agent takes as law. It explicitly
denies that the unity is `behavioral-integration`, the causal unity of
control systems acting as one. It also separates it from `phenomenal-unity`.
Korsgaard grants that a natural individual is "the seat of a unified
consciousness", and says that this unity "sets you the task" of building a
unified agency and does not supply it ([LIT-tmpnjho3](../literature.d/LIT-tmpnjho3.md), ms. p. 40). So on this
account phenomenal unity and unity of agency are different things, and the
first does not entail the second. Narrative unity plays no part.

## What was actually shown

**Agency needs unity.** An action is "a movement, or the effecting of a
change, that is backed by the agent as a whole", attributed "not just to his
eyebrow like the twitch, or to his feet like the slip, but to the agent
himself" (ms. p. 8). Attribution carries two implications: the agent was
active, and the action expresses who she is, which is why love, gratitude
and resentment are fitting responses to it (§1.2; [NOTE-tmpcjj10](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpcjj10.md), C1).

**The unity is made by following norms.** "To act is not just to cause an
end, but to make yourself into the cause of the end." Conforming to the
categorical imperative makes one self-determined, and conforming to the
hypothetical imperative makes one efficacious. Together they give action
"the form of genuine activity" (ms. pp. 15–18). The material of the maxims
chosen under that form makes one "an effective friend, teacher, parent,
citizen", which is one's practical identity (ms. pp. 18–19). The activity
half does not depend on origin: "It is the content of the principles we
follow, not the metaphysical story about how we came to follow them, that
explains the activity implication" (ms. p. 17).

**The given unity is lost and rebuilt.** Animals perceive "teleologically",
things "to-be-eaten" and "to-be-fled" (ms. pp. 29–31). Humans are aware of their own
attitudes as attitudes, and that awareness leaves "a kind of heap of desires
and fears and impulses", so "it is up to us to put ourselves back together"
(ms. p. 35). The laws of reason "are the laws of mental activity"
([NOTE-tmpcjj10](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpcjj10.md), C5). The Tanner Lectures give the same structure as the
"reflective structure" of the mind, which "needs a reason" and so needs a
law the agent takes as her own ([LIT-tmp18qr5](../literature.d/LIT-tmp18qr5.md), Lecture III).

**Groups.** "The unity of agency is not given with the unity of the mind of
the natural individual." Since a unified mind is not the only thing that
sets the task of unification, and talking and acting together set it too,
"we constitute ourselves the joint agents of the enterprise by observing the
rules of normative unification together". "Unification is an achievement,
not a given", so "nothing ... prevents people from acting together in the
most literal sense of the word" (ms. p. 40).

The chapter argues each step but borrows its central premises. That the
categorical imperative has determinate content is supposed "as Kant supposes
we can" (ms. p. 17). The account of reason as unification is compressed from
other papers. The collective conclusion is one paragraph, and how a group
observes the rules together is not worked out.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say how a natural mind is unified.** Korsgaard declines to
  "advance a view" about that ([NOTE-tmpcjj10](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpcjj10.md), Limitations). The account is
  about agency only, and is compatible with any account of behavioural
  integration or of phenomenal unity.
- **It does not say a group has a mind or experiences.** The collective
  claim is about agency, achieved by members' joint conformity to norms. It
  needs no group mind and says nothing about group consciousness.
- **It does not say manipulated agents are inactive.** By design, a
  manipulated agent who follows the right principles is active. Korsgaard
  concedes that for "deep" responsibility "it does matter how (and whether)
  it comes about that we think thoughts about self-determination", and
  concludes "responsibility is not that deep" (ms. p. 19, n. 15). This is
  the choice [THEORY-040](THEORY-040.md) describes, taken for activity and declined for deep
  responsibility.
- **It does not escape the problem of bad action.** If activity is
  conformity to the categorical imperative, wicked conduct is less than
  fully action. The chapter treats a deviant success as a failure of agency
  ([NOTE-tmpcjj10](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpcjj10.md), Assumptions). Self-Constitution, unread, is where the
  "defective action" reply is said to be.

## Connections

- **The natural conception, and Bratman.** The rival the chapter names is
  the natural conception of agency, action as movement caused by the right
  mental state. No THEORY in the record states it. Korsgaard measures it
  against her view through Bratman (§1.4). She reports asking him whether an
  intention installed by post-hypnotic suggestion would be any less the
  person's own, and that he said no (ms. p. 21), which reads him as a
  naturalist. But she also reads his Lockean claims as saying that "by
  conforming to the norms consequent upon intention, we constitute our
  identities" (ms. p. 23), and concludes that the naturalist can explain the
  identity implication only by "adopting the self-constitutionalist view".
  So against Bratman's account of self-governance over time
  ([THEORY-tmpxn1af](THEORY-tmpxn1af.md)) the disagreement is narrower than a rivalry. It is
  whether activity needs conformity to the categorical imperative, a
  question about agency at a time, and it is not a disagreement about what
  links an agent's times. No `rivals` is declared, and the converse reading
  is in that account's Connections.
- **[THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md)** (minds of agents, built downward and upward). That account
  finds that the readings agree on agency in both directions: Block, List
  and Levin all grant a whole made of agents its agency, on functional
  grounds. This account grants collective agency on different grounds.
  What makes a group an agent is joint conformity to norms, not functional
  organisation, and it draws no conclusion about experience, which is where
  [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md) locates the dispute. It agrees with [THEORY-043](THEORY-043.md)'s verdict on
  agency and gives a reason that does not turn on architecture.
- **[THEORY-029](THEORY-029.md)** (the regress). The stopping point here is not an attitude
  but conformity to norms that constitute agency. That is specified by the
  norms of agency themselves, so it is a candidate for [THEORY-029](THEORY-029.md)'s
  second horn. [THEORY-029](THEORY-029.md)'s Connections take it up.
- **[THEORY-tmptybsy](THEORY-tmptybsy.md)** (obligation and practical identity) is the Tanner
  Lectures' half of the same view: there, practical identity is the source
  of obligation; here, it is what the content of chosen maxims constitutes.
