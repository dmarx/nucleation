# LIT-2614: The stimulus-evoked population response in visual cortex of awake monkey is a propagating wave

Muller, L., Reynaud, A., Chavane, F. & Destexhe, A. (2014). The stimulus-evoked population response in visual cortex of awake monkey is a propagating wave. *Nature Communications* 5: 3675. doi:10.1038/ncomms4675.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1963](../notes.d/NOTE-1963.md).*

- **Waves on most single trials.** The authors found "stimulus-evoked
  propagations over the imaging array in 32 of 40 trials", and none on
  blank trials.
- **At horizontal-fibre speed.** The speeds were 0.25–1.35 m/s (median 0.57), "in close correspondence to the
  observed range of horizontal conduction speeds in monkey V1".
- **Averaging hides them.** "on the trial-averaged level, distal responses
  are heavily attenuated as a result of the averaging".
- **Fixed delays.** "the cross-trial phase variability of the waves does
  not increase with distance from their source", as a network with fixed
  conduction delays and jittered input reproduces.

## Standing in the record

A's characterization, that "visual stimuli evoke population responses that
propagate across cortex as waves", holds for a small, brief stimulus in
awake V1 and V2. It leaves out what the paper flags: the waves stay within
an area, show only on single trials in a 5–20 Hz band, and their
latency–distance correlations are "significant, but not overly strong".

- **[THEORY-088](../theory.d/THEORY-088.md)**: no bearing either way. The waves are radial, from a point
  source, at millimetre scale, with no spiral or phase singularity. Its
  promote_when asks for spirals in a non-BOLD recording, and this paper
  does not supply them.
- **[LIT-647](LIT-647.md)** (Muller et al. 2018, review, Unavailable): this is the same
  first author's primary result for awake cortex, and a partial neighbour
  for that claim of the review only.
