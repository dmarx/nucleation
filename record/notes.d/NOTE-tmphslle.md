# NOTE-tmphslle: An Appraisal-Driven Componential Approach to the Emotional Brain

## Contribution

A perspective article that argues the major theories of emotion are
complementary once emotion is seen as multi-componential. It then
proposes to organize affective neuroscience around five functional
networks, with the appraising brain as the driver. It updates the
authors' earlier syntheses (Sander, Grandjean & Scherer 2005; Grandjean
et al. 2008; Sander 2013) and reports no new data.

## Key insight

If emotion is a pattern across components, then what needs locating in
the brain is not an emotion but each component's network, and the
coupling between them. On this account the coupling is the emotion's
signature.

## Assumptions

- That there is "consensus among major theories of emotion that the many
  facets of emotion can be grouped into five components" (p. 221).
- That appraisal is constitutive of emotion, not only antecedent to it,
  as encoding is to memory (Figure 1 caption, after Sander 2013).
- That communication through coherence (Fries 2005) is the right model
  of how distributed networks share information.

## Key results

A framework, not results:

- **Table 1** maps theory families to levels of explanation and candidate
  neural mechanisms (basic, dimensional, appraisal, adaptational,
  constructivist).
- **Synchronization** is split into within-component synchronization
  (neuronal populations serving one component) and between-component
  synchronization (the distant networks of different components) (p. 222).
  "Synchronization of neuronal activity can take place at different levels
  including close neuronal assemblies interacting at high frequencies, and
  more distant regions synchronized at lower frequencies (see Grandjean et
  al., 2008)" (p. 222).
- **Each network** is reviewed. Expression has three levels (reflex,
  habit, voluntary) that can inhibit one another through the
  subthalamic hyperdirect pathway (p. 223). The action tendency network
  includes the approach–withdrawal asymmetry, and the feeling network the
  insula, somatosensory cortex and vmPFC.
- **Sequential appraisal** is supported by EEG studies of novelty,
  pleasantness, goal conduciveness and coping (p. 227).

## Concepts

- **within- and between-component synchronization**: coupling inside
  one component's network, and coupling across the networks of different
  components.
- **elicitation network**: the appraising brain, centred on the amygdala
  for relevance detection.
- **control precedence**: the priority some action tendencies take over
  others during an episode (Frijda).

## Connections

A restatement for neuroscience of the CPM that Scherer (2009) gave for
modellers (LIT-tmpfjacn, NOTE-tmpsx1uk). Its five networks are what
Mohammadi, Van De Ville and Vuilleumier try to recover from data
(LIT-tmp9qnrb, NOTE-tmpocnqb), and their latent
variables do not line up one per component. Takes control precedence
from Frijda (THEORY-055).

## Bearing on the record

**A per-episode measure of synchronization?** No. The paper says what
synchronization should be in the brain: phase synchrony within a band,
or one region's theta phase organizing another's gamma bursts. It gives
no index, no data and no procedure, and it closes by calling the
architecture "and how they interact and synchronize" "a major challenge"
(p. 228). It names the methods that would be needed, "excellent
resolutions at the spatial and temporal scales" such as intracranial
recording (p. 225). For QUESTION-tmpsqwu5 that is a sharper lead than
the 2009 paper gave: a phase-locking or cross-frequency index computed
over an episode's window is a per-episode quantity. But it would still
be unsigned. It would say how coupled the networks are, not which of
them is in charge.

**Is it signed, or does it say which tendency prevails?** The coupling
is not signed. What does say which tendency prevails is a different
sentence: "some action tendencies with control precedence take priority
over other potential action tendencies" (p. 223). That is the record's
ascendancy (TERM-tmp8gbrt) in the CPM's own words: a tendency winning
the conflict with its rivals. The paper does not connect it to
synchronization or measure it. In the CPM, then, ascendancy and
synchronization are separate notions. Synchronization is a degree of
coherence across components, and precedence is a ranking among the
tendencies within one of them. That supports the reading in
NOTE-tmpsx1uk that synchronization is at most the analogue of the term's
intensity clause, and it adds that the CPM has its own name for the
ordering clause, without a measure for it.

**Threshold for feeling.** On Scherer's suggestion, "feeling is dependent on the processes’ degree
of synchronization in the other components surpassing a certain
threshold" (p. 225). This is a threshold on an unsigned degree, so it is
a hypothesis about when an episode is felt, not about how it is
resolved. It sits beside CLAIM-tmptd5us, felt when the system models
its own ascendancy, as a rival with a different variable: degree of
coupling rather than a self-model. It is the same identity-with-degree
view that NOTE-tmpsx1uk set against the self-model reading (closer to
THEORY-101). It also does not bear on CLAIM-tmp83ge2's continuity
question, which is about trade-offs in choice, not about entry into
consciousness.

**Reciprocal inhibition in expression.** The three expression levels
"can be subject to reciprocal inhibition", with the subthalamic
hyperdirect pathway inhibiting prepotent responses "to shift into a more
controlled behavior" (p. 223). This is a mechanism for one response
overriding another, the override regime in CLAIM-tmp83ge2, but here it
works inside one component. It is mentioned in passing.

Candidly, the paper is a map of where to look. It does not help the
record measure anything.

## Limitations

Programmatic and largely a literature synthesis. The synchronization
proposal is not operationalized, and its key citations (Grandjean et
al. 2008; Dan Glauser & Scherer 2008) were not read here. The claim of
consensus on five components is argued from quotations, and the
constructivist and basic-emotion authors quoted might not accept that
their "components" are the CPM's.

## Open questions

Whether Grandjean, Sander and Scherer (2008, Consciousness and Cognition
17:484–495) or Dan Glauser and Scherer (2008, Brain Topography
20:224–231) compute a synchronization index per episode, and whether
either says which component leads. Whether control precedence, as a
ranking of action tendencies, has ever been measured in the CPM
programme.
