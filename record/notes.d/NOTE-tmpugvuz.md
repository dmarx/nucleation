# NOTE-tmpugvuz: McAuliffe et al. — The developmental foundations of human fairness

## Contribution

A review that defines the signatures of fairness as costly choices against
an equality benchmark, dates them in childhood, and proposes neural
mechanisms.

## Key results

1. **Benchmark.** "Equality acts as an objective benchmark in
   investigations of fairness" (MS p. 4).
2. **The norm at 3.** "by three years of age children will state
   explicitly that equality is a norm that should be followed in
   distribution tasks" (MS p. 6).
3. **Knowing versus following.** "in Dictator Games children as young as 3
   years recognize an equal split as a norm but keep more for themselves,
   suggesting that young children do not view fairness norms as
   obligatory" (MS p. 12).
4. **Getting less, at 4.** "Children as young as four rejected allocations
   that placed them at a disadvantage relative to a peer" (MS p. 6).
5. **Punishment, at 6–8.** "the first clear signs of children being
   willing to pay a cost to punish unfair treatment around 6-8 years of
   age" (MS p. 7).
6. **Getting more, at 8.** "by 8 years of age children begin to reject
   advantageous allocations in the Inequity Game" (MS p. 8), and this
   aversion "is tied to local norms of fairness and expectations of child
   behavior that vary by culture" (MS p. 9).

## Assumptions

Fairness measured by costly behavioural-economic choices against an
equality benchmark; mechanisms inferred from adult brain data.

## Concepts

Disadvantageous and advantageous inequity aversion; second- and
third-party punishment; knowing versus following a norm.

## Bearing on the record

- **[THEORY-495](../theory.d/THEORY-495.md)**, a contrast that sharpens it: with windfalls,
  three-year-olds know the equal norm and do not follow it; after
  collaboration they do. So collaboration bears on following the norm, not
  on knowing it.
- **[NOTE-185](NOTE-185.md)**, a parallel: supplies the baseline without collaboration
  that its sharing studies are read against.
- **[THEORY-tmpqxwyk](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpqxwyk.md)** (Schelling), a parallel: equality as an "objective
  benchmark" fits its salience, though the paper argues nothing about
  salience.

## Limitations

Only resource division, only costly fairness; no collaborative contexts;
neural claims from adults; ages depend on task.
