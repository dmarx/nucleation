# THEORY-1623: Ord reports that since The Precipice existential risk from climate change has fallen, risk from nuclear war has risen, and risk from pandemics and from AI has moved in no clear direction, so that none of the book's rounded per-century estimates has moved far

## The account

Risk estimates change in two ways: "Sometimes we have made the risks lower
(or higher), sometimes we've discovered that they always were" (Climate
Change).

Climate risk is down: emissions are "headed somewhere between the lower two
paths", and the IPCC's likely climate sensitivity narrowed from 1.5–4.5°C to
2.5–4°C. Nuclear risk is up: the war in Ukraine, New START's coming expiry,
and funding for the field halved. Pandemics are mixed: Covid showed weak
institutions and a unity that was "quite a temporary phase — you get a few
months of that", but vaccines came within a year. AI is mixed: language
models are not inherently agents, racing among the largest companies is bad,
governance has improved.

The book's estimates, 1 in 1,000 per century for climate and for nuclear, 1
in 30 for pandemics and 1 in 10 for unaligned AI, have not "moved that far"
at their rounding (Conclusions). The longer work is "establishing the moral
seriousness of existential risk", then norms, treaties and governance.

## Evidence

Ord's judgement on public events, with unsourced standard charts. The talk
is dated to February 2024 ([NOTE-1317](../notes.d/NOTE-1317.md), Limitations).
