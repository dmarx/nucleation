# QUESTION-537: Is the relationship an act invokes, a friendship, an employment or an emergency, a communicative kind in the record's sense, a repeatable relational organization partly constituted by roles, norms and expected uptake and recognized only relative to the probes used, so that invoking a relationship can be misread, translated and transported as a communicative kind can?

## Why it is a question

[CLAIM-1243](../claims.d/CLAIM-1243.md) (A30 and A32 of "Memes, Contracts, and Systemic
Normativity") holds that when an act could invoke more than one
relationship, "The requested action may be identical across these
interpretations. But the contractual consequences differ substantially",
and sorts the uncertainty into epistemic ambiguity, relational
disagreement and constitutive indeterminacy. [SYNTHESIS-068](../syntheses.d/SYNTHESIS-068.md) holds that a
communicative kind is "a repeatable relational organization that distinct
events realize, proposed as partly constituted by relations to roles,
norms and expected uptake and recognized through probes that fix it only
relative to the family used". The two read alike: a request is one act
with several candidate relational organizations behind it, and which one
it realizes is fixed by roles, norms and uptake, not by the act's
content. [CLAIM-151](../claims.d/CLAIM-151.md) holds the second half for social acts. [CLAIM-044](../claims.d/CLAIM-044.md)'s
contrast between too many global readings and none is the formal form
A32 §4 adapts to the parties' specifications.

What is not stated is whether the relationship invoked *is* a
communicative kind, or only resembles one. If it is, [SYNTHESIS-068](../syntheses.d/SYNTHESIS-068.md)'s
results carry over to invocation: identification underdetermines
constitution, so no probe family settles which relationship was invoked
except relative to that family; sharing a kind is neither transport nor
descent, so two parties can invoke "the same" relationship without either
having learned it from the other; and a translation, which changes the
audience, can change the relationship invoked and not only its wording.
If it is not, the resemblance is a shared form (an act with candidate
organizations behind it) and Article 3's third section borrows the
pragmatic-transport line's vocabulary without its results.

[QUESTION-027](QUESTION-027.md), [QUESTION-393](QUESTION-393.md) and [QUESTION-541](QUESTION-541.md) ask about signatures and
identity, not about invocation, so none holds this.

## What would count as an answer

- **Yes**, if the probes the record uses to recognize a communicative kind
  (competent uptake as a defeasible standard, a declared probe family)
  also recognize which relationship an act invokes, and the three kinds of
  uncertainty in [CLAIM-1243](../claims.d/CLAIM-1243.md) map to cases [SYNTHESIS-068](../syntheses.d/SYNTHESIS-068.md) already
  distinguishes (several kinds compatible with the probes; incompatible
  uptake; no kind yet fixed).
- **No**, if the relationship invoked has a component a communicative
  kind lacks, such as a standing obligation that persists when no act
  invokes it ([CLAIM-1280](../claims.d/CLAIM-1280.md)'s contract formed by resolving an ambiguity),
  so that invocation selects among arrangements that exist apart from any
  communicative event.
- **Partly**, if the act's kind is a communicative kind and the
  relationship it invokes is a further object the kind refers to; then
  the question becomes how the two are individuated together.
