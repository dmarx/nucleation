# QUESTION-tmpgciew: Can one organism impose its goals on another: do parasites that redirect their hosts' behaviour, such as Toxoplasma gondii and the ant-manipulating fungi, show an organism setting the ends that another's own machinery pursues, and in what sense of goal?

## Why it is a question

The owner's CLAIM-166 holds that a lower-level collective, the gut
microbiome, can drive a person's behaviour and experience. A17 granted the
routes and denied that the microbes "independently formulate goals". The
owner moved the test to cases where the influence is stronger and the
influencer is a separate organism with its own evolutionary interests:
Toxoplasma, which makes infected rodents less averse to cat odour, and the
fungi that make ants climb and clamp before dying.

What would count as an answer: a sense of "goal" fixed in advance
(selected-for outcome, causal control, represented end), and for each, the
evidence that would show a parasite meets it. Behaviour that serves the
parasite is not enough by itself: infection also changes behaviour through
the host's own defences and through mere illness (A21 §4).

## Bearing

- QUESTION-033 (who drives?) asks whether causation can be apportioned
  across levels; this asks whether the driver's ends, and not only its
  effects, can be another organism's.
- CLAIM-404 (an organization governed from outside through the evidence
  and criteria its own procedures use) supplies the shape A19 reads the
  parasites through.
