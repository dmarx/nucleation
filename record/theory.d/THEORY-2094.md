# THEORY-2094: Kuhn holds that a scientific community's paradigm, learned from exemplary problem-solutions as a rule-free similarity relation, makes later puzzle-solving tractable and restricts which problems can be asked, that its rigidity makes anomalies visible until they bring crisis, and that a revolution changes the field's problems, standards and some learned similarity relations, with losses as well as gains

## The account

A paradigm, an exemplary achievement open enough to leave problems to solve, gives a community a criterion for problems assumed soluble; working its exemplars gives a tacit similarity relation by which new puzzles are seen as like solved ones. The same commitment narrows what can be asked and resists revision; its precision makes anomaly visible; persisting anomaly blurs the paradigm and loosens its rules; a revolution reconstructs the field, changing problems, standards and some similarity relations, with losses.

## Evidence

Selected cases from the physical sciences, read interpretively; no measurement.
