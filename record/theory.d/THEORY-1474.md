# THEORY-1474: Heins, Unrau and Avram, following Mauss, hold that a gift carries no legal guarantee of return yet obliges one, and that an unreturned gift leaves the recipient inferior and poisons the relation; they report Dillon's reading of the Marshall Plan in France, whose recipients, unable to repay, responded with resentment, and his proposal that circular (kula-like) reciprocity that lets the recipient give back is the better design for aid between nations

## Source

Heins, Unrau and Avram (2018), [LIT-2178](../literature.d/LIT-2178.md); read in [NOTE-1679](../notes.d/NOTE-1679.md).

## What was actually shown

Nothing was tested; the article is an introduction and a synthesis.

- **The definition** (p. 127): a gift is a transfer "without any (legal
  or contractual) guarantee of reciprocation" that yet demands a return,
  and "The failure to return a gift changes its very character."
- **The inferior receiver** (p. 131), Mauss quoted: "The unreciprocated
  gift still makes the person who has accepted it inferior".
- **The case** (p. 131), on Dillon (1968): "the French felt the
  obligation to repay gifts but did not have the possibility to fulfill
  their obligation and thereby to become accepted as equals", and many
  "responded instead with resentment and anti-Americanism".
- **The remedy** (pp. 131–132): Dillon proposes the kula ring "as a
  useful heuristic analogy for the design of better models", faulting the
  Plan "for not having allowed Europeans to respond in a psychologically
  gratifying way to the obligation to repay".

## What this does not say

- That an unreturnable gift harms the recipient's capacity. The cost is
  in standing and in the relation, not in what the recipient can do.
- That reciprocity must be bilateral or between equals: the kula is
  circular, and Mauss's exchange works "without being based on formal
  equality or symmetry" (p. 138).
- That circular reciprocity was ever tried in aid and worked. Dillon's
  kula is a proposal.
- That it is Hattori's (2001) argument, or that it reaches development
  aid after the Marshall Plan.
