summary: >-
  Beck & Cohen (2003), Superstatistics, Physica A 322, 267-275 (DOI 10.1016/S0378-4371(03)00019-0); arXiv cond-mat/0205097. A letter. A nonequilibrium system made of cells, each in local equilibrium at its own inverse temperature β, with β fluctuating slowly compared with local relaxation, has as its long-run statistics the Boltzmann factor averaged over the density f(β), B(E) = ∫f(β)e^(−βE)dβ, a Laplace transform. A gamma (χ²) density gives Tsallis's q-exponential (1 + bE)^(−c) exactly, with 2c the effective degrees of freedom; uniform, two-level, lognormal and F densities give other statistics. At small variance all share the first correction e^(−β₀E)(1 + σ²E²/2), so q = ⟨β²⟩/⟨β⟩² is defined for any f; they differ at third order and at large E, which "strongly depends on the function f(β)". The paper never says "power law": only the gamma case gives one, and a uniform density bounded away from zero keeps an exponential tail. Here as the general form of the mixture route to heavy tails (exchange "Rich Get Richer Power Laws", A5 §4).

## Note of 2026-11-22: Beck and Cohen, read

Read in NOTE-tmpj8ro5. The registration's "heavy-tailed, Tsallis-like
distributions" was right about the construction and half right about the
result: the gamma case gives (1 + bE)^(−c), tail E^(−c), but the paper's
main result is that Tsallis statistics hold universally only at small
variance, and "for large variances quite different behaviour from Tsallis
statistics can be generated" (p. 1). "Heterogeneity across a population
alone yields a power law" overstated it twice: the setting is one system
whose parameter fluctuates in space and time, and only some spreads give
a power law (CLAIM-tmpf9qt9).
