# QUESTION-tmpnpl4u: Can a macro description of a known mechanism have interventional autonomy, an effective-information advantage over its micro description, without organizational closure or self-individuation?

## Answer

Yes. [THEORY-222](../theory.d/THEORY-222.md) (Active), from Hoel, Albantakis & Tononi (2013), read in
[NOTE-726](../notes.d/NOTE-726.md).

- **The advantage.** Effective information is effectiveness times the log
  of the number of states, under uniform interventions. A macro always
  loses size and can gain effectiveness. In four constructed systems the
  gain wins, by 0.40 to 2.92 bits. The note of 2026-10-20 gives a
  four-state construction with a gain of about 0.189 bits.
- **No closure.** None of the systems maintains the constraints of its
  own functioning. They are logic gates with a fixed transition matrix.
- **No self-individuation.** The coarse-graining is chosen by an
  analyst's search. The paper's "self-definition" means the maximum of
  effective information, not a boundary the system draws.

[THEORY-222](../theory.d/THEORY-222.md)'s Connections reads this as A10 §4.1's interventional autonomy
met with neither of the last two criteria.

## What it leaves to [QUESTION-093](QUESTION-093.md)

The advantage depends on imposing a uniform distribution separately at
each level. Whether it survives one distribution at both levels is
[QUESTION-204](QUESTION-204.md). The answer concerns effective information, not predictive
autonomy from observed data. It says nothing on whether closure or
self-individuation can be had without the other criteria, or on which of
the four, if any, makes a higher-level agent.
