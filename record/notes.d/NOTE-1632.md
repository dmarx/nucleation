# NOTE-1632: Majikes et al. — Thermodynamics of a single DNA origami fold via affine transformations

## Contribution

A measurement-methods paper on the smallest unit process of origami
folding: one staple closing one fold of the M13 scaffold. The Rothemund
rectangle involves "≈ 600 reversible hybridization reactions, of typically
6–24 bases in length" (Introduction), and bulk methods cannot single one
out. The paper offers four things: a FRET reporter whose sequence
environment is the same across every fold, so that differences between
samples can be put down to the fold; an affine curve-collapse baseline
correction in place of the subjective two-window fit, with the collapse
error as an uncertainty; a scaffold whose apparent persistence length can
be tuned with coating oligos; and measured loop-entropy and
bending-enthalpy trends for one fold, with ΔCp shown to matter.

## Key insight

Because a staple already anchored to the scaffold closes a fold without
consuming a free strand, "the folding (unfolding) of the scaffold neither
consumes (generates) a free staple strand, and is effectively unimolecular
in nature" (Methods). Its stability is then set by architecture, the fold
distance and the scaffold's stiffness, not by concentration, and the cost
of the loop is what a short fold saves. The authors read from this an
order of folding: "Among the sub-sequences on a staple, the longest binds
first in a bimolecular reaction, consistent with the nucleation seed
subsequence concept" (Discussion).

## Assumptions

- **Melt curves are equilibrium curves.** Implicit and untested: the ramp
  rate is not varied and the anneal is not recorded.
- **The signal is an affine image** of one intrinsic curve, plus separately
  measured background and baseline; the quenched state has negligible
  temperature dependence.
- **Two-state fold.** The anchor domain stays bound through the melt of the
  10-base melt domain: the reporter's domains are predicted to melt near
  70 °C against 35 °C for the melt sub-sequence, so that "the fluorophore
  is unquenched only when the melt sub-sequence dehybridizes" (Methods).
- **Geometry cancels.** The reporter's non-Holliday-junction geometry is
  taken to cancel in comparison with the control.
- **Polymer models** (Jacobson–Stockmayer, worm-like chain) serve "only as
  a rough guide".

## Key results

- **Cooperativity named, not measured (Introduction).** "As each staple
  binds, it reduces the entropic penalties for further folds and provides
  base stacking for its neighbors." But: "Here we only explore zeroth order
  cooperative effects, i.e., changes in conformational entropy."
- **Temperature range and ΔCp (Introduction).** Over "the large thermal
  processing range for DNA nanofabrication (≈ 25°C to 80°C)" the
  temperature dependence of ΔH and ΔS cannot be neglected.
- **Protocol (Methods).** Denature at 80 °C, anneal 75 to 15 °C, then
  "finally melting (15°C to 75°C, 0.61°C steps, 3.5 min hold) in which the
  fluorescence was measured": about 0.17 °C per minute (the reader's
  arithmetic), fluorescence on the melt only. Buffer 12.5 mM Mg acetate;
  scaffold 45 nM, fold and coating strands fivefold (Table 1).
- **Orthogonal check (Table 2).** For melt peak α, nearest-neighbour
  prediction 54.9 °C, DSC 51.0 ± 2.3 °C, fluorescence 54.4 ± 0.25 °C, on a
  truncated 100-base analogue at much higher concentration.
- **Unimolecular against bimolecular (Results, Fig. 4A).** "The
  hybridization-only sample is less stable than the others as it involves
  a bimolecular reaction, at low concentration, rather than a unimolecular
  reaction."
- **Fold distance and stiffness (Results, Fig. 5).** "Increasing loop
  distance depresses the Tm, as does increasing persistence length,
  primarily at short fold distances"; "the increase in melt temperature due
  to a short versus a long fold distance can be > 10°C". The blocked
  scaffold "has a higher Tm for the large folds, suggesting that some
  competition with native secondary structure has been removed".
- **ΔCp (Results, Figs. 6–7).** Curved van't Hoff plots, read as "evidence
  of the non-negligible effect of ΔCp in DNA melting"; ΔCp does not vary
  with topology within error.
- **Resolution (Results, Fig. 7).** "we are able to measure a <10% change
  in ΔS(T)", and short folds show a ΔH dip read as a bending penalty. For
  the medium and long persistence-length scaffolds "their slightly higher
  uncertainty propagates aggressively through the van’t Hoff analysis";
  better "would require equipment with a temperature control precision
  better than 0.3°C, and with a data sampling density greater than one
  measurement per 0.6°C."
- **The inferred pathway (Results; Discussion).** Long domain first,
  bimolecularly, then the others unimolecularly, short folds favoured:
  "These short folds further reduce the loop entropy for nearby staples and
  the system assembles progressively." Loop entropy being sequence-agnostic,
  "this would explain why qualitative experimental work has found origami
  folding to proceed like a traditional nucleation and growth system with
  predetermined nucleation sites". Untested: "although confirming this
  process will require additional testing in systems with multiple folds".
- **What other methods cannot separate (Introduction; Discussion).** UV-Vis
  and DSC "measure signal from all DNA in the sample"; "there is little to
  no gel mobility shift associated with a single staple fold of the
  scaffold"; with intercalating dyes "the melt temperature, Tm, is taken to
  be the only reliable parameter"; concentration-series van't Hoff "is not
  usable for scaffold folding itself"; imaging of binding has "only been
  performed post mortem, on assembled structures" and gives "purely
  qualitative measurements of yield". The affine method itself works best
  "when only one physical process dominates the measured signal".

## Concepts

- **Unimolecular against bimolecular hybridization**; the concentration
  independence of a fold.
- **Loop (configurational) entropy**, Jacobson–Stockmayer extrapolation;
  **bending enthalpy** in the worm-like chain; **apparent persistence
  length**.
- **ΔCp**, and curvature of the van't Hoff plot as its signature.
- **Affine curve-collapse baseline correction.**
- **Structural against functional yield**: an assembled shape against every
  needed staple present.
- **Nucleation seed sub-sequence**; nucleation and growth with
  predetermined sites; competition from scaffold secondary structure.

## Connections

It draws on Rothemund's rectangle for the scale of the problem, on
nearest-neighbour thermodynamics, and on the authors' companion
best-practices paper on the affine analysis (its reference 27, not read).
The nucleation-and-growth picture it invokes is the classical frame of
[LIT-924](../literature.d/LIT-924.md) (Unavailable). Against the held DeLuca et al. simulation, [LIT-2081](../literature.d/LIT-2081.md)
([NOTE-1561](NOTE-1561.md), [THEORY-1398](../theory.d/THEORY-1398.md)), it is complementary: experimental evidence for
local entropic cooperativity, short folds first and each lowering later
loop costs, which fits locally ordered growth; nothing on global traps;
and it anneals, which that model omits. Within this batch it is the
single-fold counterpart to the whole-origami folding measurements of
Sobczak et al. (2012, read through its neighbour) and the pathway
modelling of Dannenberg et al. (2015) and Dunn et al. (2015), which it does
not test; its distinction of structural from functional yield bears on
Wagenbauer, Wachauf and Dietz (2014). [CLAIM-185](../claims.d/CLAIM-185.md)'s nucleation threshold has
a physical analogue in the seed sub-sequence.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-557](../claims.d/CLAIM-557.md) and [CLAIM-574](../claims.d/CLAIM-574.md) (observing budget refines): supports,
  concretely.** What can be told apart grows with the measurement budget:
  replicates, 0.3 °C control, a sample every 0.6 °C, and a reporter that
  isolates one domain. Bulk methods cannot single out one fold; at the
  paper's budget persistence-length differences show in Tm but not in ΔH,
  ΔS or ΔCp. Nothing on the order of probe costs ([CLAIM-592](../claims.d/CLAIM-592.md)) or on a
  shared budget ([CLAIM-572](../claims.d/CLAIM-572.md)), beyond a loose resonance: the DSC check needed
  a scaffold so short and a concentration so high that it was no longer the
  measured system.
- **[CLAIM-569](../claims.d/CLAIM-569.md) (construct, maintain, transition): partial support, on the
  maintain side.** A fold's Tm and ΔG are measured separately and depend on
  fold distance and stiffness; construction and transition are not
  measured.
- **[CLAIM-567](../claims.d/CLAIM-567.md) (feasible, reachable, reliably reached): weak
  qualification.** The paper measures the equilibrium stability of one
  fold and frames yield as a matter of "limiting thermodynamic equilibria";
  it says nothing about reachability or reliability.
- **[CLAIM-184](../claims.d/CLAIM-184.md) (anchors entrench): weak support, inferred only.** The
  first-bound long domain lowers the cost of later nearby folds, early
  structure biasing later growth; no lock-in or blocked alternative is
  shown.
- **[CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md) (scale separation makes constraints): weak support.** The
  reporter rests on a separation of more than 30 °C in predicted Tm
  (anchor near 70 °C, melt domain 35 °C), so the anchor is a fixed
  constraint over the window in which the fold melts.
- **[QUESTION-380](../questions.d/QUESTION-380.md) (small compatible nucleus): directly relevant, as a
  hypothesis.** The seed sub-sequence and the entropy a short fold saves its
  neighbours are a mechanism by which a small nucleus helps, predicted from
  an architectural quantity (loop distance, stiffness) rather than
  sequence, at a size of more than 10 °C in Tm.
- **[CLAIM-571](../claims.d/CLAIM-571.md), [CLAIM-583](../claims.d/CLAIM-583.md), [CLAIM-591](../claims.d/CLAIM-591.md): leave open.** With no anneal curve and
  no hysteresis the paper measures only the holding side; the same melt
  domain in a different fold geometry differs in stability, not in
  reachability; the inferred one-way order is not measured. [CLAIM-581](../claims.d/CLAIM-581.md) and
  [CLAIM-566](../claims.d/CLAIM-566.md): nothing, beyond the structural/functional yield distinction.
- **[THEORY-136](../theory.d/THEORY-136.md) and [CLAIM-463](../claims.d/CLAIM-463.md).** No hysteresis is reported, so the paper
  supplies no evidence of alternative stable states or of a capacity not
  restored with its conditions.
- Filed: [THEORY-1454](../theory.d/THEORY-1454.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice;
  nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **One fold, one melt domain.** Full-origami behaviour and the pathway are
  inferred.
- **No anneal, no hysteresis, no kinetics, no test of equilibrium.**
- **ΔH, ΔS and ΔCp resolved only for the short persistence-length
  scaffold.**
- **Rough polymer models**; the persistence length of the composite
  constructs is unknown.
- **DSC on a truncated analogue** at other concentrations.
- **The supplement was not read**, so the affine procedure in detail, the
  fits and the medium and long persistence-length data are unverified.
