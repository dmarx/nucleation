# CASE-tmp0st1e: Contextuality tests on human meaning and judgement data: a held null, and positive results that miss pragmatic content or power

Found on 2026-10-10 by a search the owner asked for: documented tests of
contextuality proper on elicited pragmatic or meaning judgements, for
[CLAIM-tmp1rlu5](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1rlu5.md), whose concession rests on [NOTE-235](../notes.d/NOTE-235.md)'s "none" and on one paper's
data sets.

## The case

The conditions a study must meet to bear on the fixed-scenario form of
[CLAIM-105](../claims.d/CLAIM-105.md) are four: (a) elicited judgements, not corpus counts; (b) no
signalling within tolerance, or contextuality after a
Contextuality-by-Default (CbD) correction; (c) enough observations per
context; (d) pragmatic or meaning content, not perception. Four bodies of
data were checked against them.

**The held null: Dzhafarov, Zhang and Kujala (2016), [LIT-264](../literature.d/LIT-264.md).** Read in
[NOTE-235](../notes.d/NOTE-235.md). Under CbD, none of the re-analysed data sets is contextual:
73 question-order pairs, mostly Pew and Gallup split ballots of several
hundred respondents per order; Bruza et al.'s 23 primed word combinations,
with ΔC from −2.882 to −0.418; conjoint choices; psychophysics. It meets
(a), (b) and (c). For (d) its content is attitudinal and lexical, not
pragmatic in the sense of a speaker, an act and its uptake.

**A Bell-type null on meaning judgements: Wang's thesis (2024).** D. Wang,
*A Quantum-Inspired Analysis of Human Disambiguation Processes*, UCL
thesis, arXiv:2408.07402. 400 noun–verb phrases were rated by 25 MTurk
workers per batch on a 0–7 plausibility scale for each meaning
combination. The rank-4 result, p. 105: "none of the empirical models
obtained in the corpus or human judgment dataset were contextual (in either
framework)". Among the rank-2 systems, ten are listed as CbD-contextual
(Fig. 3.4b, from 0.232 for file/admit down to 0.001). No significance test
or bootstrap is given for any of them, and the "probabilities" are
averaged normalised ratings, not frequencies of outcomes (§3.1.2), so CbD's
sampling reading does not plainly apply. Lo, Sadrzadeh and Mansfield
(2024, arXiv:2402.04505, §1) report "4.5% of the phrases (13 out of 290)"
as contextual, attributing human judgements to Wang et al., the papers the
record holds as corpus studies ([LIT-842](../literature.d/LIT-842.md), [LIT-851](../literature.d/LIT-851.md)). The two counts are not
reconciled here.

**The strongest positive, off content: Cervantes and Dzhafarov (2018),
"Snow Queen".** *Decision* 5(3): 193–204, arXiv:1711.00418. About 450 MTurk
respondents per context, four contexts, each choosing a character from
Andersen's *The Snow Queen* and a characteristic, under an instruction that
the pair match the story. The marginals differ by context (P(Kind) .841
against .374 in the correct-response system), yet the CbD criterion is
violated. p. 6: "the left-hand side of Inequality 2 equals 0.452 … the
99.99% bootstrap confidence interval … is [0.226, 0.668]". p. 9, of the
working hypothesis that behavioural context-dependence is direct influence
only: "This hypothesis is now falsified." Basieva, Cervantes, Dzhafarov and
Khrennikov (2019, *J. Exp. Psychol.: General* 148(11): 1925–1937,
arXiv:1807.05684) found five of six further instructed-choice systems
contextual. Yearsley and Halliwell (2019, arXiv:1905.12570) reply that "it
is … possible to construct a non-contextual model which explains the
experimental data via direct influences", and they "cast doubt on the idea
that any experimental data in psychology could provide conclusive evidence
for contextuality". No reply to the comment was found (one search).

**The right content, underpowered: Lo, Sadrzadeh and Mansfield (2023).**
Their generalised Winograd schema, EPTCS 384: 187–202, doi:10.4204/EPTCS.384.11,
arXiv:2308.16498. A story with two ambiguous pronouns, each resolved under
one of two words: "A and B belong to the same [cannibalistic / herbivorous]1
species of animal. … After a while, one of them2 is no longer [hungry /
alive]2." Coreference from world knowledge is meaning content of the
pragmatic kind. Of 410 MTurk responses 348 were valid, about 87 per
context. §4.4: "The model violates the Bell-CHSH inequality by 0.192 with a
standard deviation of 0.176", and "87% of them exhibited a positive
violation". No signalling holds by construction: "all symmetric models are
non-signalling … By considering only symmetric models, we thereby avoid the
complications of dealing with non-signalling models", and responses that
broke the symmetry were discarded. The authors also concede the story
"contains several incorrect uses of English", noticed after data
collection.

## What it can show

That [CLAIM-tmp1rlu5](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1rlu5.md)'s concession stands on the documented evidence, read
wider than the one paper it cites. No published study of elicited
*pragmatic* or *meaning* judgements finds contextuality proper at an
adequate sample size. The one that meets all four conditions finds none
([LIT-264](../literature.d/LIT-264.md)). The one Bell-type test on meaning judgements finds none at
rank 4 (Wang). The one test on pragmatic content finds a violation that
cannot be told from zero (Lo et al.). So the fixed-scenario form of
[CLAIM-105](../claims.d/CLAIM-105.md), which needs a contextual model on one side, still has no case but
a constructed one ([CASE-037](CASE-037.md)).

It also corrects the scope of the concession. "None" in behavioural data
([NOTE-235](../notes.d/NOTE-235.md)) is out of date: since 2018, instructed choice tasks have been
reported CbD-contextual with high reliability, though the verdict is
disputed in print. The defensible sentence is "none in pragmatic or meaning
data adequately tested". The Snow Queen data are also a documented case
for [CLAIM-037](../claims.d/CLAIM-037.md): CbD finds contextuality in heavily signalling data, where
a test that presupposes no signalling cannot be applied at all.

## What it cannot show

- **Condition (d) is met only partly, and (c) only where (d) fails.** The
  adequately powered studies are attitudinal, lexical or instructed
  matching; the pragmatic one has about 87 responses per context. So the
  case documents an *absence of adequate tests* as much as a null. It does
  not show that pragmatic judgements are noncontextual. [CLAIM-tmp1rlu5](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp1rlu5.md)
  itself says it does not say contextual pragmatic data cannot exist.
- **The strongest reply.** A defender of [CLAIM-105](../claims.d/CLAIM-105.md)'s fixed-scenario form
  would say the null is uninformative where it matters. The one pragmatic
  test points the right way (87% of resamples positive), was run once, in
  faulty English, and its forced symmetry threw away the signalling that
  [LIT-842](../literature.d/LIT-842.md) and [LIT-851](../literature.d/LIT-851.md) find to be the norm in language. Wang's ten
  rank-2 positives, untested, point the same way. On this reply the
  concession is premature: the right study has not been done. The reply is
  fair as to evidence, and the case does not refute it. It answers only
  that the claim's concession was conditional on "the evidence the record
  holds", and the wider evidence does not change it.
- **The positives do not transfer.** Snow Queen's correlations are made by
  the instruction to match the story, not by what an utterance does; it
  gives [CLAIM-105](../claims.d/CLAIM-105.md) no pragmatic contextual model. Whether its CbD verdict
  survives richer direct-influence models is disputed (Yearsley and
  Halliwell), and this case does not settle it.
- **What would close the gap.** A powered rerun of Lo et al.'s design,
  without forced symmetry and analysed under CbD, with pragmatic items
  (whose uptake, which act, which relation). As a rough figure, computed
  here from their reported standard deviation and not by them: a violation
  of 0.19 at three standard deviations needs about 0.064, so about seven to
  eight times their responses, some 650 valid per context. Such items could
  sit in the discriminating study ([CASE-tmp1uzfn](CASE-tmp1uzfn.md)) as its contextuality arm,
  run on source and rendering alike. A positive result there would reopen
  the fixed-scenario form; a powered null would make the concession
  evidence of absence.
