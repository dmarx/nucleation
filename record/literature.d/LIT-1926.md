# LIT-1926: Re-Engineering Philosophy for Limited Beings: Piecewise Approximations to Reality

William C. Wimsatt (2007). *Re-Engineering Philosophy for Limited Beings: Piecewise Approximations to Reality*. Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press. DOI-10.2307/j.ctv1pncnrh.

## Key takeaways

*Seeded from the citing paper, not read.* Griesemer and Shavit ([LIT-188](LIT-188.md)) take two concepts from it. Generative entrenchment: a core element that later-evolved ones "depend on for their existence and character, so that perturbations of it will tend to be catastrophic to the whole system while changes in the later interactions tend to lead to tolerable system changes" (§2). Aggregativity: contributions that sum without depending on their organization, the mark of coordination in a collection (§1). Wimsatt's paper on aggregativity is held as [LIT-707](LIT-707.md), Unavailable.

## Standing in the record

Registered on 2026-11-01 from the re-reading of [LIT-188](LIT-188.md) ([NOTE-173](../notes.d/NOTE-173.md)). The record's entrenchment claims ([CLAIM-184](../claims.d/CLAIM-184.md), [CLAIM-185](../claims.d/CLAIM-185.md)) state the idea without this source. The record's question for a reading is whether Wimsatt's entrenchment is an acyclic dependency order or allows the core to be maintained by what depends on it, since only the second can give closure ([CLAIM-531](../claims.d/CLAIM-531.md)).

On 2026-11-22 ([LEAD-176](../leads.d/LEAD-176.md)) the book was looked for and no lawful copy found. Wimsatt and Schank 2004 ([LIT-3138](LIT-3138.md), [NOTE-2365](../notes.d/NOTE-2365.md)) stands in for its definition of generative entrenchment, which there is downstream reach in a directed causal graph of the organism and relevant environment, graphs with cycles allowed. That answers the question above as far as the definition goes: entrenchment is a dependency order that may or may not contain a cycle, so it does not by itself give closure, which is [CLAIM-531](../claims.d/CLAIM-531.md). Whether the book states it differently is unverified until the book is read.
