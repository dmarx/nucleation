# LIT-2149: Why Do People Stay Poor?

Balboni, C., Bandiera, O., Burgess, R., Ghatak, M. & Heil, A. (2022). Why Do People Stay Poor? *The Quarterly Journal of Economics* 137(2): 785–844. doi:10.1093/qje/qjab045. First circulated as NBER Working Paper 29340, October 2021, doi:10.3386/w29340.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1654](../notes.d/NOTE-1654.md). Page numbers are the NBER working paper's.*

- **A threshold, located.** The asset transition "rejects the null of
  global concavity with p < 0.01" (p. 13), and the unstable point, about
  9,309 BDT, "falls exactly in the low-density range of the baseline
  distribution of assets" (p. 14).
- **Divergence on either side.** Below it, households lose 14% of assets
  in four years; above it they gain 16% (p. 15, Table II). Over 11 years
  those above gain about 10,700 BDT in productive assets, mostly land,
  and 3,464 BDT a year in consumption (Table IV).
- **Effort rises, it does not fall.** Those above "were thus not only
  able to sustain and expand their livestock, they were also able to work
  more" (p. 23); at baseline the ultra-poor women already worked the most
  hours of any wealth class (Table I).
- **Not ability.** "It was not their ineptness that previously excluded
  them from taking up a better occupation but their inability to make a
  large enough initial investment." (p. 23)
- **No guarantee.** Among younger women, "almost half of those who start
  above the threshold end up at least retaining the value of the transfer
  in 2018 while only 30% of those below do" (p. 23).
- **Open mechanisms.** Escapers "might also be healthier or experience
  less stress", and the authors "cannot know whether they are causes or
  consequences of poverty, or both" (p. 16).

## Standing in the record

Filed as a counterpoint to the record's dependency claims. It is about
households holding a private, productive, indivisible asset, not about
states or projects under repeated aid, and it never tests assured or
repeated help, so it does not refute the Samaritan's-dilemma mechanism of
[CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md) and [THEORY-1480](../theory.d/THEORY-1480.md) where that mechanism applies. It does
cut against reading persistent low position as low effort: the poorest
worked the most, and support raised their work. It supports, at the
household level, the ignition threshold of [CLAIM-604](../claims.d/CLAIM-604.md) and the
self-sustaining condition of [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md), adds a failure that is
neither dependence nor safe withdrawal (decay of a seed too small), and
qualifies [CLAIM-602](../claims.d/CLAIM-602.md), since here the amount relative to a threshold
decided the outcome. It answers [QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md) in part. Filed from it:
[THEORY-1465](../theory.d/THEORY-1465.md).
