# CLAIM-425: A perspective governs itself with respect to social judgement to the extent that it keeps access to others' judgements of it while making their authority over its conduct depend on their grounds, not on their visibility, repetition or attributed plurality; neither absorbing every judgement nor resisting every one meets this, since both let the judgements set the terms of response

## The claim

A39 ended its examination of the phrase with a fourth reading: "I can
recognize the judgment, investigate its grounds, and refuse to let its
apparent popularity substitute for its actual credibility." And its
thesis: "The capacity to distinguish social visibility from justified
authority is therefore central to perspectival self-governance."

The claim has three parts.

1. **Keep access, condition authority.** [CLAIM-349](CLAIM-349.md) separates access to a
   signal, the signal's authority over action, and action on it, and
   calls adaptive the regulation that keeps the first while making the
   second conditional on evidence. Here the signal is a judgement of
   oneself. A39, in its section "Who gets to tell me who I am?": "When
   someone tells me who I am, I can understand their judgment without
   endorsing it. I can acknowledge that it contains evidence about how I
   affect others without accepting their complete characterization."
2. **Visibility, repetition and attributed plurality are not grounds.**
   These are what [CLAIM-415](CLAIM-415.md)'s false audience trades on: a judgement seen
   often, heard repeatedly, or taken to speak for everyone. The record
   draws the same line for itself, where repetition is kept apart from
   independent corroboration ([ADR-044](../decisions.d/ADR-044.md)). A39: "Repeated visibility,
   numerical prevalence, and independent corroboration are different
   things."
3. **Neither absorbing nor resisting meets it.** A39: "someone who
   deliberately resists every social judgment may be no less governed by
   the judgments than someone who obeys every one. In both cases, the
   external judgments can set the terms of their response." [CASE-142](../cases.d/CASE-142.md)
   is the resistance route; [CASE-141](../cases.d/CASE-141.md), where the label works through
   others' treatment, is a reminder that the capacity governs one's own
   response, not what others do with the label.

It complements [CLAIM-424](CLAIM-424.md), reflexive governance of an organization's
perspectival arrangements. That claim is about governing the arrangements
that decide which signals count; this one is the same achievement for the
judgements others hold of a person.

## What it does not say

- **Not immunity.** A39: "A person incapable of being affected by others'
  perspectives wouldn't necessarily be autonomous. They might simply be
  unresponsive to relevant information." The claim requires access.
- **Not that the capacity is common or easy.** It names what
  self-governance would need here; how often people have it is empirical.
- **Not that judgements carry no evidence.** A characterization can be
  true, and an apparently popular one can be popular because it is true.
  The claim is that popularity is not the reason to grant it authority.
- **Not that the capacity is enough.** Which descriptions become
  consequential "depends on relationships of trust, social power, history,
  context, and the person's own practical organization" (A39). Others'
  attributions act through their treatment of the person whatever the
  person's response ([TERM-075](../terms.d/TERM-075.md), sense 1).
