# LIT-tmpgh72l: Conscious emotional experience emerges as a function of multilevel, appraisal-driven response synchronization

Didier Grandjean, David Sander & Klaus R. Scherer (2008), *Consciousness
and Cognition* 17(2):484–495 — DOI-10.1016/j.concog.2008.03.019

## Key takeaways

- **The thesis** (abstract, p. 484). "Consciousness of emotional reactions
  emerges when lower levels of processing are not sufficient to cope with
  the event and regulate the emotional process, particularly when the
  degree of synchronization between the components reaches a critical
  level and duration".
- **Feeling monitors** (p. 486). "Subjective experience serves a
  monitoring function, integrating all information about the continuous
  patterns of change in all other components, as well as their coherence,
  and then building an integrative conscious representation." Only part
  of that representation (circle B of the Venn diagram) becomes conscious.
- **Levels and stopping** (p. 489). Appraisal runs in parallel at several
  levels and stops at a conclusive result, so "higher, more costly—and
  more conscious levels—are only recruited in cases in which lower-level
  processing does not achieve conclusive results."
- **Sequence** (pp. 489–491). "Novelty > Intrinsic pleasantness >
  Goal/Task relevance > Goal conduciveness > Coping potential >
  Compatibility with internal and external norms or standards". EEG
  evidence: novelty and intrinsic pleasantness early and automatic, goal
  conduciveness later, "on a conscious, controlled, and possibly
  propositional level" (p. 491).
- **Why synchrony brings consciousness** (p. 492). Lasting high
  synchronization "is an expensive operation for the organism's resources
  and can probably not be regulated automatically", so regulation such as
  reappraisal must take over, and it needs "high-level representational
  operations".
- **Two mechanisms** (p. 493), "necessary and sufficient": synchronization
  of the components, and neuronal synchronization, local and distant,
  that integrates their representations into a conscious feeling.
- **Measures** (pp. 488–489). EEG gamma and beta activity differs between
  episodes with and without a reported feeling (Dan Glauser and Scherer
  2008). Phase coherence between assemblies (Fries's communication through
  coherence) predicts amygdala–orbitofrontal synchrony.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-23 for the record's candidate condition for felt valence
([CLAIM-tmptd5us](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmptd5us.md)). [NOTE-tmpt8ltj](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpt8ltj.md) sets out where Grandjean et
al.'s account rivals it and where it agrees. In short: it agrees that
feeling is a representation used by further regulation; it differs on
what triggers the representation (the regime's own cost) and on what is
represented (coherence, not which ordering wins).
