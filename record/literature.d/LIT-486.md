  Ritchie (2018 online; PPR 100(2), 2020). Social structures are networks
  of relations constitutively, not merely causally, dependent on social
  factors, and can be overt or covert, intentionally or unintentionally
  created, internal or external to what realizes them. Organized social
  groups (teams, courts, families) are structured wholes with internal
  social structures whose nodes people occupy; feature social groups
  (women, racial groups, classes) are social kinds, nodes in external
  social structures. Both meet a "Goldilocks Constraint": intuitive and
  explanatory groups in, arbitrary collections out. Silent on agency.

# LIT-486: Social Structures and the Ontology of Social Groups

Katherine Ritchie (2020), *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 100(2):402–424 (online 20 September 2018) — DOI-10.1111/phpr.12555

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, as one of the works filling out
the record's coverage of social ontology under the topic [ADR-017](../decisions.d/ADR-017.md) added.

[NOTE-389](../notes.d/NOTE-389.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-02, and it placed the work:
**Active**. Its value to this record is the distinction it draws between two
kinds of social group, which the record's two social-ontology strands have so
far taken one at a time. The group-agency and group-mind works (List & Pettit
[LIT-391](LIT-391.md), List [LIT-401](LIT-401.md), Schwitzgebel [LIT-159](LIT-159.md), Kammerer [LIT-409](LIT-409.md), Huebner
[LIT-426](LIT-426.md), and Tollefsen [LIT-483](LIT-483.md) filed beside this) are about organised
groups. The
social-construction works (Haslanger, Ásta) are about categories like women
and races. Ritchie's framework puts both in one ontology and says what
separates them: an organised group is a concrete structured whole whose
members occupy roles in its own (internal) structure; a feature group is a
kind, a position in a structure that relates it to other positions (external).

**What the distinction implies for the group-agency and group-mind works.**
Ritchie never discusses agency or minds; "agent", "agency" and
"consciousness" do not occur in the paper's text. What follows is this record's application, from the paper's
definitions.

- **Only organised groups are candidates.** On Ritchie's view a feature group
  is a kind, "(clusters of) properties" or a class-like entity (p. 420), not a
  concrete whole. A kind does not act and is not a subject. So the question
  List ([LIT-401](LIT-401.md)) and List & Pettit ([LIT-391](LIT-391.md)) ask, whether a group can be an
  agent, and the one Schwitzgebel ([LIT-159](LIT-159.md)) asks, whether one can be conscious,
  apply to organised groups only. List's own examples (a firm, the FBI, a
  university, a board; [NOTE-340](../notes.d/NOTE-340.md)) and the "companies, churches, and states" of
  List & Pettit's publisher description are organised social groups in
  Ritchie's sense. Talk of what "women" or "the working class" wants is then
  either about an organisation formed from its members (Ritchie's n. 25: a
  Black Lives Matter chapter, not African Americans) or not literal.
- **Schwitzgebel's United States is not an organised social group in her
  sense.** [NOTE-381](../notes.d/NOTE-381.md) records his definition: "a spatially distributed, concrete
  entity with people as some or all of its parts", which his best guess
  extends to artefacts and computers. Ritchie requires every node of an
  organised group to be occupied by people, social creatures or structured
  groups of them (condition (ii), p. 413), and treats a whole with stadiums
  and bank accounts among its occupants as a different social entity
  (p. 411). So the entity Schwitzgebel asks about falls outside her
  group category, in the wider class of structured social entities she
  gestures at; the US government and Congress fall inside it.
- **Nesting.** Ritchie lets organised groups have organised groups as members
  (Congress with the House and Senate as members; n. 24). That is nesting of
  groups in groups, with no claim about minds, so it bears on [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md)'s
  anti-nesting question only as a reminder that group composition already
  nests.
- **[THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md) does not move.** Ritchie's structures are networks of social
  roles and power relations, not causal or informational architecture, and she
  offers no criterion for agency, awareness or experience.

**Other connections.** Durkheim ([LIT-484](LIT-484.md)) is cited for the group as more
than a "mere sum of individuals", from a later chapter than the one filed here.
Haslanger ([LIT-480](LIT-480.md)) is quoted accurately (her pp. 38–39) and reinterpreted
as identifying *women* with a node. Ásta's conferralism is reinterpreted from
her 2011 and 2015 papers, not from her book ([LIT-491](LIT-491.md)). The 2013 paper
([LIT-479](LIT-479.md)) is the source of the organised-group view, here amended (n. 22).

**Topic.** `social-ontology` is primary. `metaphysics` holds the structuralism
and the constitutive-dependence account of the social; `mereology` holds
organised groups as structured wholes with groups as possible members;
`individuation` holds the identity condition and coincidence.
