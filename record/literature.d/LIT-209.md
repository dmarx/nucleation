# LIT-209: The Phenomenology of Ritual Resistance: Colin Kaepernick as Confucian Sage

Philip J. Walsh (2021), *Dao: A Journal of Comparative Philosophy, 20(1), 1–24* — DOI-10.1007/s11712-020-09759-4

## Key takeaways

- Kaepernick's kneeling is Confucian "ritual resistance": it subverts the anthem ritual from within while staying faithful to the ideals the ritual expresses, and so founds a new bodily form of patriotism that liberal rights-talk cannot capture.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Walsh reads Colin Kaepernick's 2016 anthem protest — first sitting, then, after consulting veteran Nate Boyer, kneeling — through Confucian thought and phenomenology together. The Confucian account of ritual (li) and sagehood explains what the gesture means, and phenomenology describes how ritual works through the body. He argues that the gesture subverts the anthem ritual from within while remaining faithful to its ideals, and institutes a new bodily expression of patriotism compatible with both American and Confucian ideals.
