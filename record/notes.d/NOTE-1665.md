# NOTE-1665: Evans — State-society synergy: complementarity and embeddedness

## Contribution

Against a zero-sum view of state and civil society, Evans draws from five
case articles (Lam on Taiwanese and Nepali irrigation, Heller on Kerala,
Ostrom on Brazilian sewers and Nigerian schools, Fox on Mexican peasant
organisations, Burawoy on China and Russia) and Tendler's Ceará a single
account, "state-society synergy", with two parts that are themselves
complementary. He then asks whether synergy depends on endowments or can
be constructed, and answers that it can be constructed at small scale.

## Key insight

The useful support is a dependable public input the community cannot
produce, delivered through continuing contact between officials and
citizens. It is not support that withdraws and leaves the community to
it: the model is a stable joint arrangement, not a hand-over. "Active
citizens are hamstrung unless their governments dependably supply them
with inputs that they cannot produce on their own" (p. 204).

## Assumptions

- **Shared interests.** Officials and constituents are often taken to want
  the same outcome; Evans flags this himself (p. 196).
- **Chosen cases.** The cases are positive examples, and the
  generalisations "have to be considered exploratory" (p. 179).
- **Ostrom second-hand.** What is said here of Ostrom (1996) is Evans's
  summary in his own framing; it carries none of her numbers.

## Key results

- **Complementarity is the precondition (p. 187; per Evans, from Ostrom's
  production functions).** "When public agents and citizens have
  sufficiently different (but equally necessary) kinds of inputs, they can
  produce more efficiently by combining their efforts than by either
  producing everything privately or everything publicly."
- **The sewer split (p. 187, per Evans).** "The technology of producing
  trunk sewer lines was beyond the collective efforts of local
  neighborhoods", while "public sector fiscal problems put the resources
  necessary to deliver the entire system out of reach of the government."
- **Start-up costs (p. 187, per Evans).** The organisational start-up
  costs "of setting up neighborhood meetings, explaining options, and
  mediating conflicts required substantial time and effort on the part of
  public officials."
- **After construction (pp. 187–188, per Evans).** "monitoring and
  maintenance required continuous face-to-face interaction" between a
  familiar set of agency staff and the neighbourhoods.
- **Nepal (p. 183, from Lam).** "Direct involvement of the state
  bureaucracy in the operations of local systems undercut the development
  of the collective institutions that farmer-managed systems depended on";
  Taiwan's "enmeshment" did not, and Taiwan keeps construction and upkeep
  in separate organisations so that upkeep does not become a
  stepchild "in a construction-dominated bureaucracy" (p. 188).
- **Nigeria as anti-synergy (p. 195, per Evans).** "To begin with, there
  is no complementarity. Bereft of resources itself, the state provides
  little in the way of tangible support to local public institutions";
  bureaucracy means "a uniform, simplistic application of inflexible rules
  which leave no room for initiative".
- **The signals problem (p. 196, Evans quoting Ostrom).** "Public sectors
  typically rely on incentive systems that send very weak signals about
  performance to staff."
- **Endowments (p. 191).** "prior endowments of social capital are not the
  key constraining factor"; "The limits seem to be set less by the initial
  density of trust and ties at the microlevel" than by the difficulty of
  scaling up. Recife's neighbourhoods are not "known as
  historically exceptional repositories of civic engagement."
- **Transplantation (p. 203, per Evans).** "Ostrom notes, for example, that
  the effectiveness of condominium sewer systems is not confined to
  Northeast Brazil but has transplanted well to Kenya, Paraguay, and
  Indonesia."
- **Cheap to create (pp. 204–205).** Citizens "can also contribute their
  time at implicit wages that public employees should not be forced to
  match"; synergy "magnifies the socially valued output that can be
  derived from existing tangible assets but requires minimal material
  resources in its own creation".
- **Decay (p. 206, n. 10).** Vorkuta miners' mobilisation "did slow the
  pace of decline" as public institutions decayed.

## Concepts

- **State-society synergy**: active government and mobilised communities
  enhancing each other's developmental efforts.
- **Complementarity**: each side supplies inputs the other cannot, from
  the rule of law and publicity to dams and trunk sewers.
- **Embeddedness**: everyday ties between public officials and citizens.
- **Coproduction**: per n. 9, close to synergy but focused on outputs of
  goods and services, where synergy looks to their social and
  institutional consequences.
- **Endowments against constructability**; **scaling up**; **soft
  technologies** of organisation; the **signals problem** (Ostrom's).

## Connections

The coproduction model is Ostrom's (1996), [LIT-2140](../literature.d/LIT-2140.md), unread, and
Lam's; Evans's sewer case is Ostrom's, whose field source is presumably
Watson (1995), [LIT-2190](../literature.d/LIT-2190.md), read in [NOTE-1659](NOTE-1659.md). Embeddedness extends
Evans's own earlier work on the state. Lam's Nepal–Taiwan contrast
parallels the self-governing irrigators of [LIT-737](../literature.d/LIT-737.md) and [THEORY-149](../theory.d/THEORY-149.md)
(Ostrom's Governing the Commons) and [LIT-719](../literature.d/LIT-719.md) (her Nobel lecture):
coproduction extends self-governance to goods that need a public input
the users cannot supply. It sits beside Ostrom, Gibson, Shivakumar and
Andersson's Sida report, [THEORY-1480](../theory.d/THEORY-1480.md), on donor-built assets and
upkeep.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md) (when transient support is safe to withdraw):
  qualifies.** In coproduction the supported party does its share
  throughout, yet the support, the complementary input and officials'
  contact, is not meant to be withdrawn. At most the officials' start-up
  effort is transient.
- **[CLAIM-604](../claims.d/CLAIM-604.md) (ignition threshold, collapse, hysteresis): none.** No
  threshold or hysteresis evidence.
- **[CLAIM-602](../claims.d/CLAIM-602.md) (support that does the work crowds out capacity):
  supports, with a qualification.** Nepal's bureaucratic operation
  undercut farmers' institutions, while support confined to inputs farmers
  "cannot provide for themselves" (p. 183) did not. Taiwan qualifies it:
  deep official involvement coexisted with strong farmer organisation
  because it was embedded, not substitutive.
- **[CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md) (supplied upkeep never becomes optional): supports by
  analogy.** The lumpy public input must be supplied "dependably"
  (p. 204), and nothing suggests it becomes optional.
- **[CLAIM-603](../claims.d/CLAIM-603.md) (one-way against reciprocal supply): supports.**
  Coproduction is reciprocal supply, each side giving what the other
  cannot; the anti-synergy case, Nigeria, has a missing input.
- **[QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md) (independence from the founding intervention):
  qualifies.** For public services the target is a stable joint
  arrangement, not independence from the founding agency; the transient
  parts are the founding entrepreneur and the start-up work.
- **[THEORY-919](../theory.d/THEORY-919.md) (temporary protection, Juhász): none directly.**
  Constructability parallels persistence after temporary support but is
  not about withdrawal.
- Filed: [THEORY-1487](../theory.d/THEORY-1487.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice;
  nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- A secondary synthesis of five articles plus Tendler, with no data of its
  own beyond a Vietnamese credit anecdote (n. 6).
- Positive cases, chosen; the author calls the generalisations
  exploratory.
- No measure of persistence after support ended.
- The copy read is apparently the 1997 reprint; the World Development text
  may differ in wording or pagination.

## Open questions

- Does the World Development version make the same two claims, the dual
  structure and constructability limited by scaling up? Reading it would
  tell.
- What does Ostrom's own article say of the sewers' upkeep after
  construction, and of her formal conditions? Only her text can say.
- Does synergy built at small scale persist when officials' contact is
  cut? A case followed after withdrawal would tell; Watson's Recife and
  Cuiabá are a start.
