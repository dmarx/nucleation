# CLAIM-tmphjm0q: There is no optimal regulation until the demand specification, the instruments, the horizon and the trade-offs among demands are declared, and a regime is better judged by a profile of possibility, realization, resilience, accountability and cost than by a scalar, so steering an industrial ecosystem optimally is relative to commitments the mathematics does not select

## The objection

A31 §6 gives a candidate regime: the one that minimizes compliance and
administration costs plus a weighted fragility risk, subject to
P_g(Γ_H ∈ ⋂_j V_j) ≥ 1 − ε. It then says that this is one specification
among many, and that it depends on estimates of costs, probabilities and
fragility. A33 §8 declines the scalar altogether. "There is no generally
justified conversion from, say, a reduction in ecological risk to a
particular quantity of industrial profit or from increased competition
to diminished worker protection." A33 also credits the record with a
"preference for compatibility complexes and partial orders over
prematurely imposed scalar objectives". That is [CLAIM-347](CLAIM-347.md)'s first
clause, that frustration "is an ordinal fact before it is a quantity".

So the owner's "optimally" holds only relative to declared commitments.
Where none are declared, the most the profile gives is a set of regimes
no other dominates.

## Note of 2026-11-22: why the possibility dimension alone is already partial (the assistant's)

[CLAIM-347](CLAIM-347.md) says reorganizations that relieve different conflicts are
incomparable. Two regimes whose complexes each contain a face the other
lacks are unordered under inclusion, so even before realization,
resilience and cost are added, the regimes need not have a best element.
This fills a step A33 asserts without showing. The wording is the
assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it is the owner's call.

## What it does not say

It does not say regulation cannot be optimized. Once the objective and
the hard constraints are declared, A31 §6's programme is well posed. It
does not say every demand is negotiable: A33 §8 allows non-negotiable
constraints.
