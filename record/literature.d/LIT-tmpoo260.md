# LIT-tmpoo260: Fluid Concepts and Creative Analogies: Computer Models of the Fundamental Mechanisms of Thought

Douglas R. Hofstadter and the Fluid Analogies Research Group (1995), *Fluid
Concepts and Creative Analogies: Computer Models of the Fundamental
Mechanisms of Thought*, New York: Basic Books (Library of Congress 93044294,
call number BF311 .H617 1995; ix, 518 pp., illustrated, references pp.
[493]–501, index). The Library of Congress record spells the title *Fluid
concepts & creative analogies*; the publisher spells it with "and". The
hardback ISBN is 0-465-05154-5, as Bruce Burns's review in *AI Magazine*
16(3), Fall 1995, gives it with "Basic Books, New York, 1995, 518 pp."
The paperback (ISBN 978-0-465-02475-9) carries the same 1995 copyright in
its catalogue record, and the publisher gives its on-sale date as 22 March
1996, at 528 pages. No source I reached gives a day or month for the 1995
hardback, so `published:` carries the year only, as the first of January.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the publisher's description, the catalogue record and one
review, not a reading. What follows is what the book, its cataloguers and
its reviewer say about it.*

- The publisher's description (Basic Books), verbatim: "From the Pulitzer
  Prize winning author of Gödel, Escher, Bach, a stunning exploration of the
  mechanisms of intelligence through computer modeling. ... Readers of
  earlier works by Douglas Hofstadter will find this book a natural
  extension of his style and his ideas about creativity and analogy; in
  addition, psychologists, philosophers, and artificial-intelligence
  researchers will find in this elaborate web of ingenious ideas a deep and
  challenging new view of mind."
- The Library of Congress subject headings: analogy; artificial
  intelligence; cognitive science; computer simulation.
- Contents, from the contents note in the paperback's catalogue record
  (cataloguing source DLC), with the authors it gives. Chapters 2 to 10 are
  each preceded by a preface the note does not attribute:
  - Prologue: "The When, the Where, and the Who of This Book" (the note
    garbles it as "Why, the when, the where, and the who of this book").
  - 1, "To Seek Whence Cometh a Sequence" (Hofstadter).
  - Preface "The Unconscious Juggling of Mental Objects"; 2, "The
    Architecture of Jumbo" (Hofstadter).
  - Preface "Arithmetical Play and Nondeterminism"; 3, "Numbo: A Study in
    Cognition and Recognition" (Daniel Defays).
  - Preface "The Ineradicable Eliza Effect and Its Dangers"; 4, "High-Level
    Perception, Representation, and Analogy: A Critique of
    Artificial-Intelligence Methodology" (Chalmers, French and Hofstadter;
    the note misspells Chalmers as "Chambers").
  - Preface "Conceptual Halos and Slippability"; 5, "The Copycat Project: A
    Model of Mental Fluidity and Analogy-Making" (Hofstadter and Mitchell).
  - Preface "Two Early AI Approaches to Analogy"; 6, "Perspectives on
    Copycat: Comparisons with Recent Work" (Mitchell and Hofstadter).
  - Preface "Retrieval of Old and Invention of New Analogies"; 7,
    "Prolegomena to Any Future Metacat" (Hofstadter).
  - Preface "Analogy-Making in a Coffeehouse"; 8, "Tabletop, BattleOp,
    Ob-Platte, Potelbat, Belpatto, Platobet" (Hofstadter and French).
  - Preface "The Knotty Problem of Evaluating Research in AI and Cognitive
    Science"; 9, "The Emergent Personality of Tabletop, a Perception-Based
    Model of Analogy-Making" (Hofstadter and French).
  - Preface "The Intoxicating World of Alphabets and Their Styles"; 10,
    "Letter Spirit: Esthetic Perception and Creative Play in the Rich
    Microcosm of the Roman Alphabet" (Hofstadter and McGraw; the note has
    "MGraw").
  - The note stops there. A third-party transcription of the contents
    (egodeath.com, with section headings) agrees with this order and adds
    an Epilogue, "On Computers, Creativity, Credit, Brain Mechanisms, and
    the Turing Test". That transcription is not the publisher's, and the
    epilogue is the only thing taken from it here.
- Burns's review (*AI Magazine* 16(3), 1995, pp. 81–83, read in full from
  AAAI's own PDF) is the one account of the content I read. It says the
  book is a collection of articles, many already published, given "a
  coherent vision" by Hofstadter's prefaces. It names two underlying ideas:
  a computational one, that cognition can be modelled by a "parallel
  terraced scan", in which codelets chosen probabilistically from a
  coderack build and break structures while a falling temperature makes
  coherent structure harder to tear down, "without the need for any
  top-level control"; and a psychological one, that "cognition is
  recognition", high-level perception interacting with the processing of
  what is perceived. Burns recommends the book but reports two criticisms:
  every model works in a microdomain (letter strings, table settings, word
  puzzles, letter fonts), and the critiques of rival programs lean on
  analogy, rhetoric and intuition. On one point the review and the
  catalogue differ: Burns calls the evaluation preface "Preface 5", while
  the contents note places it before chapter 9. Which is right needs the
  book.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-01 at the owner's request for the Fluid Analogies Research
Group's programme that analogy-making is the core of cognition, Copycat and
its successors. This is the programme's main collection. No anthology topic
holds it, and the anthology does not hold it (checked by grep). It carries
no instruction for present machine-learning practice. Its nearest approach
is the methodological argument of the Eliza-effect and evaluation prefaces,
about when a program's apparent success is credited to the program; an
anthology reader on evaluation might want those, and that is a judgement for
the anthology, not a tag here.

`Deferred` because it was not read. This record files a book from its

Its parts that the parallel filing covers, named in prose because those
works have no codes yet:

- Chapter 4 has the title and authors of Chalmers, French and Hofstadter's
  1992 critique of AI methodology, which is being filed at the same time.
  Whether the chapter reprints it unchanged is for a reading.
- Chapters 5 and 6 are on Copycat, the program of Mitchell and
  Hofstadter's 1990 paper, also being filed now.
- The book's own claims about analogy as the core of cognition are carried
  further in *Surfaces and Essences*, in this batch, and in Hofstadter's
  2001 essay of that title in the parallel filing.

The publisher calls the book "a natural extension" of Hofstadter's earlier
ideas about creativity and analogy. That is the publisher speaking, and no
`extends:` is declared to *Gödel, Escher, Bach*.

Tags. `complex-systems` is there because, per the review and the contents
titles ("The Emergent Personality of Tabletop", Copycat's "statistically
emergent" behaviour in the transcription), the architecture is about how
coherent behaviour arises from many small, locally acting parts. Rizi's
proposal that emergence is a coarse-graining that stays predictive
([LIT-150](LIT-150.md)) is the record's nearest reading on what "emergent" should mean;
the pairing is mine, and a reading should check whether the book's
"emergent" means anything more than "not explicitly programmed".
