# ARG-219: The workbook's exemplar postmortem, which names a staffing limit among what went poorly and assigns its fourteen action items, all technical, to engineers and none to staffing, as evidence, fits the prediction that a blameless review's action items stop at or below the reviewing teams' authority

## The inference

1. [CLAIM-750](../claims.d/CLAIM-750.md) predicts that in postmortems commissioned inside the
   organization, action items "cluster at or below the reviewing team's
   authority", and that the contributing causes rarely exceed the
   commissioning body's.
2. The workbook's good postmortem ([CASE-418](../cases.d/CASE-418.md)) names, among what went
   poorly, ""The installer" has limited staffing". Its fourteen action
   items are all technical, each owned by an engineer, and none addresses
   staffing.
3. Staffing is decided by whoever allocates headcount, which the
   engineers who own the action items do not.
4. So in the practice's own exemplar, the one named condition that no
   engineer could fix went without an action item: an instance of the
   predicted pattern, with the cause named and the repair not routed.

## Critical questions

**Is the instance what the claim is about?** In part. It is an internal
blameless review, governed inside the organization, which is the claim's
domain. Premise 3 is the weak point: the chapter does not say whose
authority covers the installer's staffing, and "the installer" may be a
peer team (the case's alternative reading). If it is, the instance is
about lateral reach, not upward, and does not bear on the claim.

**Does one exemplar count as evidence for a prediction about rates?**
Weakly. The claim predicts a tendency across reviews; one document can
fit or fail to fit it, not establish it. What makes this one worth
recording is that it is the exemplar the practice chose: a pattern in the
model of good practice is not a lapse. [CLAIM-1062](../claims.d/CLAIM-1062.md) adds a further
doubt, that the practice puts upstream questions in reviews across
incidents, so the document may be the wrong unit.

## Verdict

Proposed. It fits the prediction, as one more instance beside the pilot
([CASE-335](../cases.d/CASE-335.md)), and is not independent of the claim's framing: the filer
read the exemplar knowing the prediction.
