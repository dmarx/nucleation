# LIT-1392: Walking through doorways causes forgetting: Situation models and experienced space

Radvansky, G. A., & Copeland, D. E. (2006). Walking through doorways causes forgetting: Situation models and experienced space. *Memory & Cognition*, 34(5), 1150–1156. doi:10.3758/bf03193261.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1083](../notes.d/NOTE-1083.md). Pages are the journal's.*

- **The cost falls on the carried object.** In Experiment 2, errors on the
  carried object rose from .05 to .14 after a doorway; the object just set
  down was unaffected. "Thus, making a spatial shift disrupted processing
  of currently relevant information" (p. 1154).
- **The authors' explanation** is situation-model updating: "entering a new
  room is a major change in the current situation, and this requires a
  person to update their situation model of the environment" (p. 1155).
- **Thin evidence.** 5 no-shift trials per probe type per person; rooms on
  either side of a doorway differed in size and wall pattern; the task is
  recognizing two colour–shape labels.

## Why this is here

The owner's U37 proposed that the record's account of perspective and grounding gives at least a partial explanation of the doorway effect. A37 cited this as the classic finding. The case is [CASE-136](../cases.d/CASE-136.md).

**From the reading, 2026-10-22.** The paper concerns recognition of two
object labels, not intentions, and the doorway's cost fell on the carried
object only. See [NOTE-1083](../notes.d/NOTE-1083.md) and the Proposed [THEORY-888](../theory.d/THEORY-888.md).
