# LEAD-266: Read Octopus and the research-assessment statements DORA and CoARA as prior art for maintained, attributable research records, to see what the hyperarticle proposal adds to them

## What it would do

Register and read three works that A21 of the exchange "Hyper article
interpretation" cites without the record holding them:

- the San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment (DORA);
- the Agreement on Reforming Research Assessment of the Coalition for
  Advancing Research Assessment (CoARA);
- Octopus, the platform that publishes research as linked units (problem,
  hypothesis, method, results, analysis, interpretation, review), with its
  design documents.

For each, the reading asks whether it has:

- addressable units below the paper;
- revision of a unit with its history kept;
- standing apart from inclusion;
- credit for maintenance and retirement;
- a procedure for revising its own categories, which is [CLAIM-469](../claims.d/CLAIM-469.md)'s last
  clause and [QUESTION-458](../questions.d/QUESTION-458.md)'s coupling.

## What would count

A table of the three against those properties, filed as readings. A21's
claim that the proposal adds "a more explicit theory of maintenance" is
then confirmed, narrowed or answered. The record's reading of
micropublications ([THEORY-1758](../theory.d/THEORY-1758.md)), done for [CLAIM-469](../claims.d/CLAIM-469.md), is the model: it found
that the "referable object" half was not new and put the weight on revision
through use.
