# NOTE-154: Allori — Wave-functionalism

## Contribution

Allori proposes treating the wave function as a non-material, non-epistemic, non-representational entity, defined functionally by the roles it plays in quantum theory. The view shares features with the nomological account of the wave function and with pragmatist and epistemic approaches. She argues that it avoids the main objections each of those faces.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §2 (pp. 2–9): four rival views are reviewed. Wave-function realism (a material field, with the configuration-space problem). Primitive ontology (ψ is nomological, facing the objections that it evolves in time and is not unique). Epistemic readings (constrained by PBR). Pragmatist quantum realism (Healey), which is non-representational but explains by principle rather than constructively.
- §3.1 (pp. 10–12): the functionalist strategy is borrowed from philosophy of mind: "the wavefunction is as the wavefunction does." This lets ψ be ontic but non-material, which bypasses the no-go theorems.
- §3.2 (pp. 12–14): the roles are empirical (recover measurement results, via the Born rule), nomological (govern the primitive ontology, or serve as "information bridges" for pragmatists) and explanatory.
- §3.3 (pp. 14–15): ψ is multiply realizable, as other mathematical objects can play the same roles, and the view is not reductionist. The realizers are part of the laws God "created".
- §3.5 (pp. 17–21): five objections answered. The configuration-space objection is answered by putting nonlocality in the law rather than in material ontology, which she finds more palatable, especially for a Humean.
- §4 (pp. 21–22): ψ has a nomological role without being a law, so time-dependence and non-uniqueness do not matter. Its explanation is tied to laws, so it is not restricted to principle-theory explanation. Being non-epistemic, it bypasses no-go theorems.

## Open questions

- A distinct option in Chen's taxonomy ([LIT-204](../literature.d/LIT-204.md)). It is close to his weak nomological reading, so check whether it escapes the "too cheap" objection Chen raises there.
- It contrasts with Gao's ψ-ontic RDM view ([LIT-121](../literature.d/LIT-121.md)) and Dorato's dispositional reading ([LIT-176](../literature.d/LIT-176.md)). A deeper reading should ask whether "ontic but non-representational" is a stable position.
- Its functionalism parallels Wallace's math-first strategy ([LIT-151](../literature.d/LIT-151.md)) of defining theoretical content by role and structure.
