# LIT-tmpgntir: Top-down causation without top-down causes

Craver, C. F., & Bechtel, W. (2007). Top-down causation without top-down causes. *Biology and Philosophy*, 22, 547–563 (online 2006) — DOI-10.1007/s10539-006-9028-8

Read in full in [NOTE-tmp6uiie](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp6uiie.md).

## Key takeaways

- **Levels are levels of mechanisms.** X is at a lower level than S if and
  only if X is a component in the mechanism for some activity of S
  (p. 548). Such levels are "defined locally, within the context of a given
  type of mechanism" (p. 550), and are not tiers across nature.
- **Part and whole cannot be cause and effect.** They are not distinct
  events, and they share their space-time paths. Interlevel "influence" is
  simultaneous and symmetrical, while causation is taken to be asymmetrical
  (pp. 551–553).
- **A mechanistically mediated effect** is an intralevel causal chain plus
  one or more claims of constitution. Bottom-up cases are components'
  interactions that make up a state of the whole. Top-down cases are parts
  "carried along for the ride", or, in the interesting cases, "enlisted in
  the ride" (pp. 555–561).
- **The experiments are symmetrical.** Lesion, stimulation, activation and
  correlation experiments run both ways between part and whole. They share
  a form with Mill's methods, and they establish componency only prima
  facie (pp. 553–554, nn. 10–11).

## Standing in the record

The record's constructions put a maintaining process inside one system and
outside another ([CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md), [CLAIM-414](../claims.d/CLAIM-414.md), [CASE-189](../cases.d/CASE-189.md)). Whether the difference is
one of constitution is the question this paper's framework poses, and its
own experiments leave open. Read for that, not for the debate about
downward causation.
