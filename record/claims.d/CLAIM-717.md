# CLAIM-717: Blame and repair do not substitute for each other: assigning blame for creating a harmful condition does not discharge the remedial duties of those now responsible for it, and assigning present remedial duties does not show that those who bear them were to blame for it, so that treating one guilty official as a repair of the system is blame displacement, and loading the repair on whoever is nearest is remedial scapegoating

## The claim

A110 §8 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b):

> **Assigning blame for the creation of a harmful condition does not discharge the remedial duties of actors presently responsible for that condition; assigning present remedial duties does not establish that those actors were blameworthy for creating it.**

It guards against "two opposite mistakes". "The first is *blame
displacement*: an institution identifies one guilty official and treats the
identification as a substitute for repairing the system. The second is
*remedial scapegoating*: an institution assigns a frontline worker
responsibility for correcting a failure whose effective causes and
necessary remedies lie outside that worker's control."

The principle comes out of A's responsibility graph for an institution that
denies warranted claims because its reporting system cannot tell eligible
from ineligible cases. Seven roles (frontline official, system designer,
governing authority, oversight body, resource controller, affected
claimant, institutional successor) bear different possible
responsibilities, in "a **many-to-many responsibility structure**". "And
the assignment of present corrective duties need not mirror the allocation
of historical blame." A's examples: "A successor government may owe a
remedy for an ongoing wrong it did not initiate." "An independent court may
be authorized to order correction without being responsible for the
original violation." "Conversely, a system designer may be culpable for
creating a known defect while no longer possessing the authority or
resources required to correct it." The claimant "need not be responsible
for repairing the institution".

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-742](CLAIM-742.md)** (the owner's) is what it rests on: the grounds of
  correction are not those of blame. This claim says what follows when the
  two are assigned: neither assignment discharges or certifies the other.
- **[CLAIM-707](CLAIM-707.md)**, filed from A17 earlier in the exchange, holds the
  moral crumple zone, where retrospective blame lands on the person nearest
  the failure while control over its conditions lies upstream. That is the
  mechanism by which remedial scapegoating happens. This claim is the
  principle that names it a mistake, with its mirror image.
- **[CASE-151](../cases.d/CASE-151.md)** (Active): at Wells Fargo leadership disciplined individual
  employees for what the board's investigation traced to the sales model.
  It is a documented instance of blame displacement: identifying culpable
  employees stood in for repairing the system.
- **[THEORY-207](../theory.d/THEORY-207.md)** (Proposed, Floridi) allocates responsibility in networks
  without fault by default; it is a reading of the second half, remedial
  assignment without a finding of blame.
- **[CASE-284](../cases.d/CASE-284.md)** (A110 §11's Case B) tests the second half: blameless
  decision-makers, the same wrong, and remedial duties assigned by
  capability and jurisdiction without inventing blame. [CASE-151](../cases.d/CASE-151.md) serves for
  the first half.
- **[QUESTION-476](../questions.d/QUESTION-476.md)** asks whether blameless post-incident practice keeps
  the upstream provenance blame supplies. A blameless review is designed
  against remedial scapegoating; whether it falls into the mirror failure,
  no one blamed and nothing upstream repaired, is the question's open part.
- **[QUESTION-470](../questions.d/QUESTION-470.md)**, from the first share of the same exchange,
  asks when remedial responsibility comes apart from culpability. This
  claim bears on it: it says the two do not substitute for each other, so
  that finding a culprit does not discharge repair and assigning repair
  does not show blame, without saying when repair survives where no one is
  to blame.

## What it does not say

- Not who bears which duty in a given case. The graph's entries are
  "Possible responsibility", and A conditions the resource controller's
  duty: "if established".
- Not that blame is idle. Blame has its own grounds and uses
  ([CLAIM-733](CLAIM-733.md)); the claim is only that it does not do repair's work.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Miller's capacity connection, through Shaw-Young: repair without fault
<!-- inactive-ok-file: NOTE-tmps8s9k THEORY-tmp3xr2l LIT-2755 — Skimmed, new or Unavailable; the reading filed on 2026-11-18 and the unread article it stands in for -->

Shaw-Young 2008, read in part ([NOTE-tmps8s9k](../notes.d/NOTE-tmps8s9k.md), [THEORY-tmp3xr2l](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp3xr2l.md)), standing in weakly for Miller 2001 ([LIT-2755](../literature.d/LIT-2755.md)). The THEORY now `corroborates` this claim, which does not rest on it.

Miller, as quoted from his 2007 statement, separates outcome from remedial responsibility and assigns the latter through six connections, "moral responsibility, outcome responsibility, causal responsibility, benefit, capacity, and community" (thesis p. 20). Capacity alone suffices: "if I am the only person walking along a river bank when a child falls in, then it is my responsibility to rescue the child" (p. 29, Miller 2007 p. 103). A remedial duty on an agent who did nothing wrong is the claim's second half: "assigning present remedial duties does not show that those who bear them were to blame". The reading says nothing about the first half, blame displacement, and it reports the 2007 book, not the 2001 article the exchange cited.

No status changes.
