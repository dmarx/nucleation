## The apparatus, briefly

Each perspective ranks the system's states by their bearing on its own concerns ([CLAIM-437](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-437.md)). A conflict is a pair of states the perspectives rank differently; the negotiation's win on it is the state that prevails. The system has valence over a window only if (1) some conflict is contested (nonvacuity), (2) the wins admit an ordinal potential, which on a finite set is exactly that no chain of wins cycles ([CLAIM-434](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-434.md)), and (3) that potential ranks states by their bearing on the system's maintenance ([TERM-076](../../record/terms.d/TERM-076.md)'s kind clause). Sign: ahead of or behind a reference course along the potential. Intensity: how far decisions follow it rather than a rival ordering. A perspective's emotion is its claim to precedence; the system's valence is read on grants ([CLAIM-442](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-442.md)).

## Two perspectives

1. **Dominance.** P1 wins every contest. The potential is P1's ranking, and the system's valence is P1's. P2 claims and is not granted: its precedence is frustrated ([CLAIM-283](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-283.md)'s p − w). *What it shows:* system valence can be positive while a part is persistently negative, the separation [CLAIM-527](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-527.md) states, with no cycle and no confusion. An organization content at the top and distressed in a function is this case.
2. **Agreement.** P1 and P2 rank every pair alike. No pair is contested, so valence is *undefined*, not positive ([TERM-076](../../record/terms.d/TERM-076.md)'s nonvacuity clause). *Pressure point:* a harmonious, thriving organization has no valence on this account. Either that is right, and what such an organization has is viable reach and no affect, or the account owes valence to uncontested improvement. This is the scenario most likely to look like a defect to a reader.
3. **Jurisdiction.** P1 wins contests in domain X, P2 in domain Y ([TERM-017](../../record/terms.d/TERM-017.md)'s jurisdiction). If the domains do not interact, the union of wins is acyclic and has a potential that is neither perspective's ranking: a composite, "nobody's ranking until it is shown to be one" ([TERM-076](../../record/terms.d/TERM-076.md)). Valence is then the system's in a strong sense. Whether the kind clause holds depends on whether the jurisdictions were drawn by bearing on maintenance.
4. **Context switching.** P1 wins when a threat cue is present, P2 otherwise. Within each context there is a potential; over a window mixing both, wins can cycle. Valence is window-relative, and the switch is mood-like: a change of which ordering governs, not a change of position along one. [CLAIM-446](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-446.md)'s threat that "grants defensive processes authority" is this switch, held in place.
5. **Deadlock.** Both claim, neither is granted; a default state persists. Contested pairs exist but carry no wins, so the potential is trivial and valence is undefined, while both perspectives are maximally frustrated. *What it shows:* frustration without valence, the strongest case for [CLAIM-368](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-368.md)'s making frustration, not valence, the primitive.

Two perspectives suffice for a cycle when wins are split across pairs: P1 wins b over a and a over c, P2 wins c over b. Each perspective is consistent; the negotiation is not.

## Three perspectives

6. **Majority cycle.** Each pair of states is settled by a majority of P1, P2, P3 with Condorcet-cyclic rankings. Wins cycle, there is no potential, and the system has no valence, though every perspective has its own. The organizational signature is churn: every state is improved on, decisions are reversed. This is stronger than [CLAIM-437](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-437.md)'s ambivalence ("strong saliences of opposite sign with no potential absorbing them"): it is structural, present at any intensity.
7. **Coalition.** P1 and P2 ally against P3. The coalition's composite is the potential; P3 is frustrated as in (1). The difference from dominance is fragility: the coalition can break, and valence can change regime abruptly without any change in the world.
8. **Arbiter.** P3 has no first-order concerns and grants authority between P1 and P2. The claim/grant gap is then localized in one place: P1 and P2 claim, P3 grants, and the system's valence is the ordering P3's grants follow. The kind clause holds if P3 grants by bearing on maintenance. [CLAIM-283](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-283.md)'s "It does not say the grant is made by a separate regulator" and [QUESTION-054](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-054.md) ask whether real organizations are like (8) or like (6) and (7), where granting is distributed among claimants.
9. **Filtered contest.** P3's contests never reach the arbiter ([CLAIM-448](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-448.md)'s legibility). The recorded wins are acyclic and show a potential, but it is the admitted negotiation's, and the valence read off it is the governors' picture. This is [CLAIM-439](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-439.md)'s mis-felt valence at Wells Fargo: the board's model of what wins was made from filtered reports.

## What the scenarios suggest

- Valence is a property of the *pattern of grants*, and it can fail to exist in three different ways: no conflict (2), no wins (5), cyclic wins (6). These are different conditions. An account that called them all "neutral" would lose them.
- Frustration and valence come apart in both directions: (1) has positive system valence with a frustrated part; (5) has frustration and no valence.
- Where granting sits, one arbiter (8) or distributed among claimants (6, 7), decides whether emotion-as-claim has a single addressee. That bears on the owner's proposal that emotion is a communicative act ([CLAIM-1437](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-1437.md)): in (8) the claim has a hearer; in (6) the claimants are each other's hearers.

## Note of 2026-11-26: unobserved is not absent

The owner corrected "What the scenarios suggest": "there is a difference between valence not existing, and it not being *observed*." Filed as [CLAIM-1411](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-1411.md), which objects to [CLAIM-440](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-440.md)'s "No contest, no valence". On that claim, the three failures above are not alike. Scenario 2 (agreement) leaves valence *unobserved*: no contest arose to show which ordering the negotiation would follow. The candidate "three failures of valence" is withdrawn as stated.
