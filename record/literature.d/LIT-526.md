# LIT-526: Life as we know it

Karl Friston (2013), *Journal of the Royal Society Interface*
10(86):20130475 (received 27 May 2013, accepted 12 June 2013, published 6
September 2013; CC BY 3.0).

The brief's citation (J. R. Soc. Interface 10(86):20130475) is correct.

## Key takeaways

- **The lemma.** "Any ergodic random dynamical system that possesses a
  Markov blanket will appear to actively maintain its structural and
  dynamical integrity." With the flow written in the Helmholtz form f =
  (Γ + R)·∇ln p(x|m), the flow of internal and active states averaged over
  external states climbs the marginal ergodic density of the blanket and
  internal states (Eq. 2.6). Choosing the variational density q(ψ|λ) equal
  to the posterior over external states makes that same flow a descent on
  the variational free energy F (Eqs. 2.7–2.8).
- **The inversion.** Earlier arguments said living systems *must* minimize
  free energy or their sensory entropy would grow without bound. This
  paper turns that round: "any system that exists will appear to minimize
  free energy and therefore engage in active inference." The word "appear"
  carries the claim.
- **Four marks of the lifelike.** Ergodicity, a Markov blanket, active
  inference and autopoiesis, used as tests on the simulation.
- **The simulation.** 128 subsystems with Lorenz "electrochemical" states
  and Newtonian motion, coupled within distance one. The principal Markov
  blanket of the time-averaged adjacency matrix encloses 8 internal
  subsystems. Internal dynamics predict the motion of 5 of the 82 external
  subsystems above the largest value of a time-reversed null. Lesioning
  active, sensory or internal subsystems makes the structure disperse
  ("oscillator death").
- **Demarcation.** "A candle flame cannot possess a Markov blanket, because
  any pattern of molecular interactions is destroyed almost instantaneously
  by the flux of gas molecules from its surface." Then, in the conclusion:
  "there are probably an uncountable number of Markov blankets in the
  universe. Does this mean there is lifelike behaviour everywhere?" The
  answer offered is a conjecture: the blankets of living systems have
  minimum entropy, but not so little as "a petrified stone".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, as part of filling out the
record's coverage of the "life as dissipation" literature and the
philosophy that tries to mark living systems off from merely dissipative
ones. No anthology topic holds it. The anthology does not hold this paper
or any paper on the free energy principle, and the paper carries no
instruction for machine-learning practice. It borrows the variational
bound from machine learning (Hinton & van Camp, Beal, the Helmholtz
machine) but uses it to describe organisms. So it is not filed there, and
[ADR-013](../decisions.d/ADR-013.md) dual holding does not arise.

[NOTE-421](../notes.d/NOTE-421.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-02, and it placed the work:
**Active**. It is the text later Markov-blanket accounts of life cite, and
it is short and complete. As an argument it is weaker than its title. The
"proof" is a redescription that holds because the variational density is
set equal to the posterior. The posterior depends on sensory and active
states as well as internal ones, which the paper's q(ψ|λ) does not allow
for. The four marks of life are read off one simulation run.

Where it bears on what the record holds:

- **The demarcation question ([LIT-192](LIT-192.md), [NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md)).** Nahas & Sachs report
  that closure-of-constraints accounts face the candle-flame problem, and
  that Deacon & Cashman fault autopoiesis for lacking tools to tell living
  teleology from dissipative self-organization. Friston addresses the
  candle flame by name and says it is excluded. His criterion is that a
  Markov blanket needs a set of states whose couplings "change slowly with
  respect to the states per se". That is a principled line in form,
  statistical persistence of a conditional-independence structure, but
  the paper asserts it of the flame in one sentence and shows it for
  nothing else. His own conclusion then gives the line away: blankets are
  everywhere, and what marks life is a conjecture about blanket entropy.
- **Autopoiesis.** Bruckner ([LIT-120](LIT-120.md), [NOTE-093](../notes.d/NOTE-093.md)) uses a loose, behavioural
  autopoiesis to ground welfare subjecthood. Here autopoiesis is
  operationalized as dispersion after lesioning blanket states in a
  simulation. That is a third use, closer to a test than either Bruckner's
  or the organizational accounts [NOTE-094](../notes.d/NOTE-094.md) surveys.
- **Seth ([LIT-135](LIT-135.md)).** Seth's biological naturalism runs from predictive
  processing through the free energy principle to autopoiesis. It takes
  thermodynamic and variational free energy to be "in some physical sense
  … the same thing" ([NOTE-169](../notes.d/NOTE-169.md)). This paper, which Seth's line of argument
  draws on, computes no thermodynamic quantity. Its free energy is the
  variational bound of Eq. (2.7), and its only contact with the second law
  is the introduction's sentence that bounding sensory entropy "enables
  biological systems to resist the second law". So this paper does not
  supply the equivalence Seth relies on (his C5).
- **Still et al. ([LIT-327](LIT-327.md)) and [THEORY-026](../theory.d/THEORY-026.md).** Both tie a system's internal
  states to prediction of what drives it. Still makes this a physical
  identity, dissipated work equals nonpredictive memory, for a system
  driven *without* feedback. Friston's systems are built on feedback:
  active states change external states, a "circular causality". That is
  the regime where [THEORY-026](../theory.d/THEORY-026.md) says (from [LIT-041](LIT-041.md)) prediction is no longer
  what efficiency requires. Friston's prediction is also not priced in
  work; his "inference" is a description of how the dynamics look. The two
  should not be read as one result in two vocabularies.
- **Levin ([LIT-439](LIT-439.md)) and nested selves.** Friston's remark that "the Markov
  blanket of an animal encloses the Markov blankets of its organs, which
  enclose Markov blankets of cells" is the same nesting Levin's Selves
  have ([NOTE-341](../notes.d/NOTE-341.md)). It also bears on the anti-nesting question in
  [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md), though Friston does not take it up.
- **Artime & De Domenico ([LIT-021](LIT-021.md)).** That review cites this paper for its
  claim that life is emergent in any random dynamical system with a
  Markov blanket, given a "heuristic theoretical and computational proof".
  [NOTE-007](../notes.d/NOTE-007.md) left the claim unassessed (its C8). This reading assesses it:
  the claim is the paper's own, stated as "(almost) inevitable", and its
  support is the lemma (a redescription) plus one simulation run.
- **"What Lives?" ([LIT-211](LIT-211.md)).** Friston's account sits across two of its
  clusters ([NOTE-109](../notes.d/NOTE-109.md)), Cognitive Autonomy (apparent inference, action) and
  Dissipative Self-Organizing Systems (the soup "is dissipative at two
  levels").
