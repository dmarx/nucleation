# CASE-tmpo33kl: Risk messages passed down diffusion chains: the evaluative signal kept and amplified while the content decays (Moussaïd, Brighton and Gaissmaier; Jagiello and Hills)

Found on 2026-10-10 by a search the owner asked for: transmission-chain
studies that measure a stance at each generation, the literature
[CLAIM-tmpi6n8k](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpi6n8k.md) found absent from the record.

## The case

**Moussaïd, Brighton and Gaissmaier (PNAS 2015).** Fifteen chains, of 7 to 12
people, analysed to the tenth position. The first person read six media
articles on the antibacterial agent triclosan, then discussed it face to
face with the next person, "in open, unstructured discussion", who then
discussed it with the next. Participants waited in one room and were told
not to interact. It is, the authors
say, the paradigm "pioneered by Frederic Bartlett" ([LIT-768](../literature.d/LIT-768.md)).

The transcripts were coded twice, on two schemes that the paper calls
independent of each other.

- **Content.** 61 units of information, each tracked from position to
  position and marked "distorted" when it changed.
- **Signal.** Every sentence of the informed speaker labelled Positive,
  Negative or Neutral by the assessment of triclosan it expresses:
  "Sentences that highlight the suspected dangers of Triclosan or express a
  negative judgement about it received a 'Negative' label". Two coders with
  the same instructions; "The results were then averaged." No agreement
  coefficient is reported.

The results, in the authors' words (arXiv preprint): messages "tend to become
shorter, gradually inaccurate, and increasingly dissimilar between chains.
In contrast, the perception of risk is propagated with higher fidelity"
(abstract). Positive statements decayed faster than negative ones, so the
negative share rose down the chain. Speakers differed: the direction in
which a speaker changed the signal correlated with that speaker's risk
perception (c = 0.25, p = 0.019), so "the direction of the 'mutation' of the
message corresponds to the opinion of the speaker" (p. 7). A receiver's
change in risk perception tracked the signal received (c = 0.55). The
discussion: "changes in risk perception occur less as a result of the
message content, and more as a result of its overarching, subjective
signal" (p. 10).

**Jagiello and Hills (Risk Analysis 2018).** Fourteen written chains of
eight. The first person read four documents balanced by construction, 21
positive and 21 negative statements, on nuclear energy (high dread) or food
additives (low dread). Each later person read only the previous message and
wrote their own. At position 6 the chain forked: one branch saw the original
documents again, one did not. Each participant's knowledge and perceived
risk were measured by questionnaire before reading. All 154 messages were
coded for negative and positive statements twice, by the experimenter and
by five confederates blind to the hypothesis: "Cronbach's alpha indicates
high interrater agreement for both negative (α = 0.961) and positive
statements (α = 0.974)" (§2.2).

- "the more a message was transmitted the more negative statements it
  contained. This was highest for the high dread topic" (abstract). Chain
  position had a main effect on the proportion of negative statements,
  F(10, 154) = 5.506; the low-dread chains "were not significantly affected
  by chain position" (§3.1).
- Re-exposure "did not restore negativity to its level in position 1" in the
  high-dread chains (§3.1.2).
- Across both conditions, a person's output negativity was predicted by the
  negativity received (β = 0.565), by their initial risk perception
  (β = 0.188, p = 0.005) and, negatively, by their knowledge (Table I).
  Within either condition alone, initial risk was not significant.

## What it can show

**For [CLAIM-068](../claims.d/CLAIM-068.md): drift along a stance dimension, measured.** The claim quotes
A50's "communicative drift along a stance dimension, not merely lexical
degradation". Here the evaluative signal and the content were coded
separately, at every link, by different retellers, and they behaved
differently: the content decayed and diverged while the evaluation was kept
and pushed one way. Jagiello and Hills add a start fixed by construction
(balanced), a drift away from it, and a failure of the source to pull the
chain back. The drift depends on the topic, as C1 Appendix D's
qualification expects: the endpoint is "a property of the specified
transmission mechanism".

**For [CLAIM-090](../claims.d/CLAIM-090.md): filtering by measured biases.** The claim's defeat condition
asks for a chain whose output tracks the ancestor "rather than any measured
prior of the transmitters". Here the transmitters' risk perception was
measured on them, not assumed, and it predicts the direction of the change,
in both studies. Kalish et al. ([LIT-820](../literature.d/LIT-820.md)) took their prior from earlier
studies.

**For [CLAIM-tmpi6n8k](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpi6n8k.md).** One item of the absent literature is now documented.
The objection's list of what the record lacks narrows; see the note there.

## What it cannot show

**Footing, or any interpersonal stance.** The stance coded is evaluation of a
hazard, the evaluation vertex of Du Bois's triangle ([LIT-779](../literature.d/LIT-779.md)). Its object is
a chemical or a power source, not the addressee. Nothing in either study
codes who stands behind a statement, whether it teases or reproaches, or
alignment between speakers. The closest work the search found on footing,
Altay, Claidière and Mercier's study of source attribution in rumours
(Evolutionary Human Sciences 2020, DOI 10.1017/ehs.2020.53), is a single
transmission step, with the chain simulated. A52's extension of iterated
learning "to speaker footing" still has no study under it.

**Convergence from different starts.** [CLAIM-068](../claims.d/CLAIM-068.md)'s defeat condition asks
whether chains "started from different stances" converge to a few
conventional ones. Every Jagiello and Hills chain started balanced, and the
Moussaïd chains all started from one set of articles. The chains are 8 and
10 long and are not stationary. Moussaïd's simulations end "strongly
positive or strongly negative" depending on the group's initial perception,
which reads as several attractors, as in Kalish et al.; but those are
simulations, not data.

**A clean prior.** [CLAIM-090](../claims.d/CLAIM-090.md) is met only partly. In Moussaïd the
correlation with the direction of change uses the risk perception the
subjects reported "in questionnaires conducted after the experiment", and,
since nobody knew triclosan, it was measured indirectly, on "chemical use in
food safety and cosmetics" generally. A post-experiment measure is
contaminated by the message received. Jagiello and Hills measured risk
before reading, which is cleaner, but the received message predicts output
three times as strongly, and the prior is significant only when the two
topics are pooled.

**Reliability.** Moussaïd reports no agreement statistic for the signal
coding. Jagiello and Hills report Cronbach's α between two passes on counts,
which is lenient for coding.

**The defender's reply.** A defender of the objection, that no reading
measures stance under transmission, would say this is content selection
under another name. A "Negative" sentence is one that "highlight[s] the
suspected dangers", and its count is a count of which propositions about
harm survived. The finding is then a negativity bias in recall, the kind of
content bias Mesoudi and Stubbersfield study, not a stance kept apart from
content. The reply has force: the two coding schemes are independent as
schemes, but the signal is computed over statements whose content is
evaluative. It does not reach the whole result. The signal was kept while
the information units carrying it changed and diverged between chains, and
Moussaïd's receivers changed their own risk perception with the signal
rather than with the content. But the case cannot separate an evaluative
stance from the valence of retained content.

**What would close the gap.** A chain study of footing. Seed chains with one
utterance under different attributed speakers, such as [CASE-018](CASE-018.md)'s sentence
as a friend's tease and as a judge's condemnation, and with different
starting stances, so that convergence can be tested. Have each generation's
retelling coded by blind raters for footing and alignment, on a definition
and response format validated first, which is Stage 0 of the discriminating
study, [CASE-tmp1uzfn](CASE-tmp1uzfn.md), and the question [QUESTION-tmpt0ou9](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpt0ou9.md) asks. The
intralingual retelling arm of [CASE-tmp1uzfn](CASE-tmp1uzfn.md) is the natural place to run it,
and these risk-message designs give its procedure.
