# THEORY-1907: Muller, Reynaud, Chavane and Destexhe show that in awake macaque V1 the evoked response to a small, brief stimulus is, on most single trials, a wave travelling outward from the input site at horizontal-fibre speeds, whose cross-trial variability is onset jitter alone and which trial averaging hides

## Source

Muller, Reynaud, Chavane & Destexhe (2014), [LIT-2614](../literature.d/LIT-2614.md); read in
[NOTE-1963](../notes.d/NOTE-1963.md).

## The result

VSD imaging of V1 and V2 in two awake, fixating macaques, analysed by phase
latency on single, unsmoothed trials. For a small Gaussian stimulus the
authors "found stimulus-evoked propagations over the imaging array in 32 of
40 trials", and none on blank trials. Speeds were 0.25–1.35 m/s, median
0.57. "the cross-trial phase variability of the waves does not increase
with distance from their source". A spiking model reproduces this with
jittered input and fixed conduction delays, and not with variable speeds.
Averaging attenuates the distal response until it looks like a stationary
pulse. Each area makes its own waves, and they do not cross the V1/V2
border. Any of these could have come out otherwise: no waves on single
trials, speeds off the horizontal-fibre range, or variability growing with
distance.

## What it does not show

- That cortex in general computes with waves. The paper suggests only a
  "consistent spatiotemporal frame for further neuronal interactions".
- Rotating waves or phase singularities ([THEORY-088](THEORY-088.md)). None is reported.
- A mechanism beyond the 1-D model's exclusion of variable-speed accounts.
  The detection criterion itself requires a speed of 0.05–0.8 m/s.
- Anything outside a 5–20 Hz band, the superficial layers, or two animals.
