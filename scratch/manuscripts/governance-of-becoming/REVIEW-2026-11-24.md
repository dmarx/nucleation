# Review of the first draft, 2026-11-24

# Part A. The main review

Main reviewer's report, under the write-manuscript skill's step 7. I did not draft this text. I read `README.md`, `00-overview.md` and Articles 1 to 3 in full, along with the motivating draft (`motivating/A49-article-series.md`), ADR-tmpenzcn, the audit (curation entry `record/curation.d/2026/11/24/205621.md`, section "U31 and A49" and after), the verbatim exchange "Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity", and every entry I cite below. Standings are from the entries' frontmatter on 2026-11-24. I worked read-only. I wrote no file except this one and ran no git or `luria` command. Each quotation was checked by a script against the entries cited in the same paragraph, then against the exchange, and every miss was checked by hand (section 5 gives the counts). I reproduced the two models the draft reports, and I recomputed its arithmetic. I did not judge whether the joins should be admitted. Where a join misstates its components, I say so.

## Verdict

The draft is careful at the level of the single step. Almost every one of about 570 quotations is exact, all 15 block quotations are exact, and all 166 standings given in the form "(CODE, Active/Proposed, warrant)" match the entries. Both models the draft reports reproduce: A20's dependency model gives 0.42 and 0.34, and A22's norm model gives equilibria 0.048, 0.689 and 0.993, with collapse just below b = −4.33. The draft corrects A49 well, and usually on the record's own grounds. Examples are the state-intervention flaw in Article 1's design, "only if" against "because" in Article 2, and the unpriced invocation and the undefined R_i in Article 3. It keeps the owner's voice, A's and the assistant's apart better than A49 does.

Four things keep it from being ready for a fact-check.

1. **It is out of step with the record its own pull request changed.** In the same work the drafters fixed the wording of entries and filed five gap entries. The articles were not brought back into line. As a result, some sentences are now false. Article 2 says "No lead holds A49's four-system comparison", but LEAD-tmpl9349 holds it. Two quotations give an entry's wording from before its correction: CLAIM-tmpisfi1's title, which the draft cuts so that it loses the clause that was added, and CLAIM-569's note.
2. **Article 1's abstract and design move scope.** The abstract and §5.3 turn CLAIM-522's ideal-controller result, and CASE-191's single fictional construction, into unconditional "only"s. In two of the design's five steps, the expected result holds by the definition of arm (c), so those steps cannot fail.
3. **The circularity disclosure is incomplete in the ways the precedent review found.** Two "accepted" entries Article 1 rests on were made Active the same day at the assistant's discretion. Many older Active entries were promoted at status reviews whose rulings the owner delegated. And some of the record wording the draft cites was written by this draft's own process.
4. **The three articles and the overview are not consistent with each other.** The join labels collide, section numbering differs, one owner thesis is credited in one article and not in another, and two of the overview's standing counts are wrong.

The revision is editing. None of it needs new research.

### 1.2 Scope moved in Article 1's abstract and design (Article 1 abstract, §§5.3, 5.5)

- **The ideal controller is dropped.** Abstract item 3 says matched organizations differ in ways that "Passive observation cannot show … Neither can interventions on their state. Maintenance shows only under interventions on the maintaining mechanism". CLAIM-522 (Active, demonstrated) is stated for "an outside controller that measures the constraint capacities and supplies the internal production law as state feedback". §5.2 keeps that scope ("The claim is scoped to the ideal controller") and cites CASE-332, where a non-ideal service *was* told apart by an intervention on state. The abstract has to carry the same condition.
- **One construction becomes "only".** Abstract: "Formation shows only in a record kept during formation, or in an intervention made then". §5.3: A49's design "cannot reach formation provenance at all, unless it intervened during formation or kept a record then". TERM-100 says less. Its table gives "what can test it", and its body says "after formation no knockout tells it from a self-formed one", of CASE-191, which is a fictional construction. No entry says that formation leaves no trace after the fact other than a record. The claim the record holds is about knockouts. Keep "no knockout after formation", and say "in CASE-191's construction".
- **Two of the design's steps cannot fail.** §5.5 defines arm (c) as CLAIM-522's ideal case, "so that no state probe can tell (c) from (a)". Step 2's falsifier is "an ideal controller that state probes separate … CLAIM-522 says this cannot happen, so such a result would mean the controller was not ideal." Step 5 expects that state interventions do not separate (a) from (c) "under the ideal controller". Both hold by the definition of (c), and the skill says not to count what holds by construction as evidence. Step 3 (formation) and step 4 (withdrawal) carry the design's empirical content, along with the question whether any real system realizes arm (c). Say so in §5.5.
- **A milder case.** §3: "So reciprocal production is the minimal form of an internally maintained organization". CLAIM-516 says that "a cycle is necessary and not sufficient". "The minimal structure a persisting capacity needs" would keep that.

### 1.4 Expectations before results (Article 2 §§2.3, 2.4, 2.6, 2.10; Article 3 §2.2)

- **The expectations come after the results.** Article 2's three "*Expectation(s), before the result*" blocks were written after the assistant's runs had been filed (CLAIM-tmp18v1o, CLAIM-tmpjg5gx and CASE-tmptpna1 all carry the results). Label them as expectations reconstructed against a known result, not as stated in advance.
- **One expectation holds by definition.** Article 2 §2.6: "they should give the same schedule when L holds no lock H needs". Priority inheritance acts only on contention, so this holds by definition. CASE-tmptpna1 does not simulate it either.
- **One expectation is never graded.** Of §2.4's three expectations, the second ("a practice should reach the high state only if it starts above the threshold") is stated and not graded.
- **One expectation contradicts its chosen instance.** Article 2 §2.10 expects that "mere coupling and unilateral dependence should show no correction" under interruption. The instance chosen for mere coupling is the assistant's two controllers, which §2.2 says "meet conditions 1 to 3", including "Differentiated responses to satisfaction and violation". - **Results with no expectations.** Article 3 §2.2 reports CASE-326's results with no expectations stated first. Its §3.4 and §7.4 do it right.

### 1.6 Joins that misstate their components

The joins themselves were not judged. These three misdescribe what an entry says:

- **J3.5 and Article 3 §6.3.** "The record attributes this directedness to TERM-116" and "the record grounds the contract asymmetry in TERM-116's additive distances". The record, as corrected on 2026-11-24, says the opposite. CLAIM-569's note reads "TERM-116 defines relational profiles, not a revision cost, and no entry defines R(C → C′)", and CLAIM-tmpsf9hp says the same. TERM-116 is also not additive by definition. It says that "Under additive costs either profile recovers d". So J3.5's contrast between "additive distances" and "peaks" is drawn against a predicate TERM-116 does not hold.
- **Article 2 §2.7 (a citation, not a join).** CLAIM-tmpisfi1 is quoted without its scope clause (correction 2.1).
- **J2's component.** J2's component is CLAIM-tmphq6hp, an operative norm, "in which a discrepancy recruits correction rather than revision". The claim's title says that which response a discrepancy gets, "adaptation, enforcement, accommodation or revision, is the work of normative governance", and §2.4 quotes that itself. "Rather than revision" belongs to the norm/prediction contrast, not to what counts as governing a norm. State the component as the title does.

For the joins reader: SYNTHESIS-tmp4tvbr item 8 states CLAIM-tmphq6hp as "An expectation is a norm when discrepancies are met by enforcement". That narrows the claim's four responses to one. J3 rests on that item ("holds the norm side only").

## Article 1

### J1.1: MISSTATES TERM-100. A corrected replacement passes by derivation.

The history half is held. TERM-100's "Why formation provenance matters" already lists THEORY-1118, the ground CLAIM-364 cites, and CASE-128, which "differs from an imposed restriction only in how it came about". So "history tests formation provenance" restates TERM-100.

The intervention half misreads TERM-100. Its maintenance provenance is *whose* process keeps the arrangement in being: the agent's own cycle, or an outside supply. Removing the supervisor is a knockout of an outside upholder. CASE-191 says an upheld capacity "is told apart by knockouts afterwards". So supervisor removal is exactly the maintenance-provenance test, and A45 found that it does *not* discriminate, because compliance persists. That makes both arrangements own-maintained. The tests that do part them are four:

- a novel demand;
- a change to the dominant policy;
- evidence that the coordination is mistaken;
- a perturbation of the coupling.

These probe *which* internal process holds the arrangement and how it answers dissent, not whose process holds it. TERM-100's own note of 2026-11-24 (A13's decisional and reflexive dimensions) names what they probe, and says those dimensions have no separating cases yet.

The draft says the supervisor test is "the same mistake as §5.2's", which was removing an initiating support. That is defensible as an analogy only. The supervisor upholds the arrangement; it did not initiate it.

**A corrected sentence passes by derivation.** It is not a narrowing; it replaces the join. It is valid from CLAIM-364 (compliance persists without the supervisor), TERM-100 (maintenance is whose process; it is tested by knocking out upholders) and CASE-191 (upheld arrangements are separated by later knockouts). It is nontrivial, because it supplies the separating pair that TERM-100's note lacks, between maintenance and decisional independence. No CLAIM holds it.

- **Title:** *Conditioned and negotiated coordination share their maintenance provenance, since both persist when the supervisor is removed, so the interventions that part them test how the constituents' own processes answer dissent, not whose process keeps the arrangement, and removing the supervisor fails to discriminate because it is the one maintenance-provenance test among them*

- **defeated_if:** conditioned compliance that, once the supervisor is removed, is upheld by another outside supply (a second enforcer, or a standing external sanction), and that knocking out that supply separates from negotiated coordination.
