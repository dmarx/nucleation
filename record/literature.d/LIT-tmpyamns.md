# LIT-tmpyamns: On the minimal theory of consciousness implicit in active inference

Whyte, C. J., Corcoran, A. W., Robinson, J., Smith, R., Moran, R. J., Parr, T., Friston, K. J., Seth, A. K. & Hohwy, J. (2026). On the minimal theory of consciousness implicit in active inference. *Physics of Life Reviews* 56: 4–28. doi:10.1016/j.plrev.2025.11.002. arXiv:2410.06633.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, NOTE-tmpjgegs. Page numbers are arXiv v2's.*

- **A theory read off the models.** Starting from active inference, not
  from a property of consciousness, the paper argues that the existing
  models of consciousness paradigms share a "hard core". It is set out in
  Lakatos's terms, with most of the explanatory work left to the
  "protective belt" (p. 28).
- **The core.** (1) Minimising variational and expected free energy, which
  covers "both conscious and unconscious" behaviour (p. 25). (2) Conscious
  contents correspond to the posterior over hidden states, so no content
  changes without a change in the posterior. (3) Conscious access occurs at
  "the discrete-continuous interface" (p. 26): a posterior is conscious
  when it has "the capacity (in a counterfactual sense)" to inform policy
  selection at the right timescale and precision (p. 27).
- **Its scope.** Held "necessary and sufficient" for content to become
  conscious in humans and similar animals; the authors "remain agnostic"
  about systems "vastly different to ourselves" (p. 27). The third part is
  "an inference to the best explanation" (p. 27).
- **The models reviewed.** Rivalry and Troxler fading as attention-driven
  precision, hemineglect as lost novelty, access and the P3b, a no-report
  model whose prefrontal and P3b-like responses drop while the stimulus
  stays reportable, homeostatic and interoceptive models, emotion concepts,
  valence and meta-awareness (pp. 13–22).
- **New here.** A rivalry model that fits Levelt's laws (Appendix 1); a
  predicted reward violation of them (Appendix 2); and a sleep model in
  which lower transition precision removes the P3b-like response and spares
  the MMN, which "does not in-itself explain why participants lose
  consciousness" (p. 24).
- **Predictions.** Reward should trade off against stimulus precision in
  rivalry, and "To See is to Look": a stimulus given precision by an
  attention or saccade policy should reach awareness sooner than a
  retinally matched one (pp. 29–30).

## Why this is here

The owner's exchange of 2026-10-21, "Experience and interpretation", asked whether experience is constituted by interpretation or produced by it. The exchange cited this paper for extracting a minimal, testable theory of consciousness from what active inference models have in common. It took the paper as one route from computational organization to stated conditions on experience, which is the step the thesis needs. Bears on [QUESTION-tmpc4j7p](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpc4j7p.md) and [QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md).

**What the reading changes.** The exchange's description is accurate as
far as it goes. What it leaves out is that the conditions are conditions
of conscious access in humans. The paper does not claim to say which
organizations are experiential, and it grants that non-conscious creatures
may run active inference (p. 26). For QUESTION-tmpc4j7p it is a candidate
on the organizational side only, and its criterion is tied to report, so
it does not meet CLAIM-tmp1lvwf's defeater. It bears on QUESTION-048
through its no-report model.
