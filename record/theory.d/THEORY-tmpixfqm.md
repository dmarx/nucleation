# THEORY-tmpixfqm: Silverberg and colleagues show that the square-twist origami, whose crease pattern admits no rigid folding motion, folds by bending its facets, and that the ratio of bending to crease stiffness, together with the plane angle, sets a critical transition between monostable and bistable folding

## Source

Silverberg, Na, Evans, Liu, Hull, Santangelo, Lang, Hayward & Cohen,
"Origami structures with a critical transition to bistability arising from
hidden degrees of freedom", Nature Materials (2015), LIT-tmptep4g; read in
NOTE-tmp0l0mn.

## What was actually shown

- **No rigid motion.** "An analysis of the geometric constraints reveals
  the pattern is isostatic."; rigidly, "the square twist allows two
  isolated states corresponding to the fully unfolded and folded
  configurations".
- **Folding by bending.** The hidden deformations "are essential for
  foldability as they enable the structure to access otherwise
  geometrically forbidden configurations."
- **The transition.** In paper, "below a critical plane angle
  φc = (25 ± 2.5)◦ , the distinction between folded and unfolded
  configurations is not observed; the structure is monostable"; in
  simulation, monostable for all geometries at k_b/k_c ≤ 1 and bistable
  for all at k_b/k_c ≥ 10³.
- **Design.** In the gel twist, "the addition of these creases removes the
  hysteresis (Fig. 4d)."

## What this does not say

- Anything about tessellations or coupled cells, which are proposed only.
- That the energy separation is a time-scale separation. The paper's
  scales are stiffnesses.
- That the critical angle is universal: it shifts with k_b/k_c, and the
  measured ≈25° is for one paper.
