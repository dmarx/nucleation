  selective-accessibility measure, turned up in the search for the 1999

  between samplable separations and one-way functions without proving it.
  Filed at the owner's request. Unavailable: Goldreich posts it, but neither his server nor the Internet Archive could be reached from here.
- Hutchins's "How a cockpit remembers its speeds" ([LIT-1066](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1066.md)), which
  he posts himself and which is closer to the EMT case ([CASE-085](../../record/cases.d/CASE-085.md))
  than the book. Registered and read ([NOTE-867](../../record/notes.d/NOTE-867.md)) at the owner's
  request. It adds to [TERM-055](../../record/terms.d/TERM-055.md) and [CLAIM-296](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-296.md) a factor they lack:
  retrieval done before the request and left in the environment.

**What the readings changed.**

- [CLAIM-302](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-302.md): the separation is unconditional (the book's
  Proposition 3.3); only an efficiently samplable witness needs an
  assumption. Its defeat condition overstated.
- [CLAIM-304](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-304.md) and [CASE-083](../../record/cases.d/CASE-083.md): wording tightened to what the book
  proves; postprocessing is an exercise there, not a theorem.
- [CLAIM-298](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-298.md): the selective-accessibility account is theory with
  indirect support, its most direct measure does not replicate, and its
  defeat condition no longer assumes retrieval and evaluation separate.
- [CLAIM-294](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-294.md): its full-belief counterpart, Hansson's Abe and Bob.
- [QUESTION-105](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-105.md): narrowed. Bases and orderings are one-step answers,
  and neither is provenance.
- [LIT-1050](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1050.md) and [LIT-1072](../../record/literature.d/LIT-1072.md): the first had Hansson's point, the
  second phrases that are A10's paraphrase.

**A second batch, for [QUESTION-095](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-095.md).** At the owner's request after the
cockpit reading, eight works on offloading and amortization were
registered and read the same day, all Read: Kirsh and Maglio 1994,
Kirsh 1995, Ballard et al. 1997, Risko and Gilbert 2016, Gray and Fu 2004,
Dasgupta et al. 2018, Lieder and Griffiths 2020, and Gershman and Goodman
2014 (no DOI; eScholarship). Sources: authors' own posted copies, except
Gray and Fu, from a Drexel course copy of the typeset article. Each
reading corrected its LIT summary, which had been written from how the
work is standardly cited. A citation search for work joining the two
literatures found pieces, listed on the question, and no joint cost model.

**A third batch.** At the owner's request: four works for the SDT question
([QUESTION-103](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-103.md)) and three from the citation search on [QUESTION-095](../../record/questions.d/QUESTION-095.md),
all Read. Geisler 2011 from the NIH manuscript on PubMed Central; Håstad et
al. 1999 from the authors' typescript on Håstad's KTH page; Dwork et al.
2021 and Hébert-Johnson et al. 2018 from arXiv, both flagged as anthology
candidates; Kirsh 1996 from his own posted scan; Gray et al. 2006 from a
University of Colorado course copy of the typeset article; Dasgupta and
Gershman 2021 from Gershman's lab site. The readings narrowed the SDT
question (ideal-observer analysis bounds the input, not the tests) and
settled Goldreich 1990's statement (generators, not one-way functions).
