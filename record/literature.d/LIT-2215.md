# LIT-2215: Weaning from mechanical ventilation

Alía, I. & Esteban, A. (2000). Weaning from mechanical ventilation. *Critical Care* 4(2): 72–80. doi:10.1186/cc660.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1701](../notes.d/NOTE-1701.md). Sections are the review's.*

- **Most can stop at once.** Ventilation "can be discontinued abruptly in
  approximately 75% of mechanically ventilated patients whose underlying
  cause of respiratory failure has either improved or been resolved"
  (introduction).
- **Abrupt beats gradual after a passed trial.** "immediate extubation
  after successful trials of spontaneous breathing expedites weaning and
  reduces the duration of mechanical ventilation as compared with a more
  gradual discontinuation of ventilatory support" (abstract).
- **After failure, the method matters.** Both trials found "that SIMV is
  the least efficient technique of weaning" (patients failing the first
  attempt).
- **The abstract contradicts the body.** It says SIMV "is the most
  effective method of weaning"; the conclusion says "SIMV is the most
  ineffective method of weaning those patients". The 2006 correction,
  unread, probably fixes the abstract.

## Standing in the record

Filed as the stand-in for Boles et al. (2007), [LIT-2213](LIT-2213.md), on the trials
the consensus drew on for initial weaning and for weaning after a failed
trial; it predates the consensus and does not stand in for its
classification or its graded recommendations. For the record it says how
to schedule withdrawal once readiness is tested: where the party can do
the work, abrupt removal beats a taper, which qualifies [CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md) on
schedule. Its treatment of rest with ventilation as the cure for fatigue
partly contradicts [CLAIM-602](../claims.d/CLAIM-602.md) in this setting, and is the doctrine later
studies of disuse atrophy reversed. Filed from it: [THEORY-1512](../theory.d/THEORY-1512.md).
