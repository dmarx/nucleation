# CLAIM-027: Which of an agent's available regimes becomes effective depends on maintained relations — accessibility, precedence, persistence, jurisdiction, suspension and transformation — that are not reducible to the contents of the competing regimes

## The claim

Section 1: "Agents may possess multiple action-generating regimes whose
practical efficacy depends upon maintained relations governing their
accessibility, precedence, jurisdiction, persistence, suspension, and
transformation."

It has two independent lines of support, [ARG-009](../arguments.d/ARG-009.md) (Northstar-A/B) and
[ARG-005](../arguments.d/ARG-005.md) (the reluctant writer), which is what lets the paper claim
the role spans scales. Its standing objections are [CLAIM-010](CLAIM-010.md) (answered)
and [CLAIM-089](CLAIM-089.md) and [CLAIM-024](CLAIM-024.md) (both still open).

## What it does not say

That the dimensions are a complete taxonomy (Section 7.5), or that the
mechanisms are new ([CLAIM-021](CLAIM-021.md)).

## Note of 2026-10-18: a proposed manuscript that rebuilds the essay

At U12 and U15 of the exchange of 2026-10-17 the owner asked for a
manuscript proposal. A12, revised at A15, proposed *The Geometry of
Effective Volition*. Neither cites this claim's essay, *The Organization of
Will* (`organization-of-will`), and the proposal rebuilds much of it:

- Its opening case (A12 §1), "an agent reflectively endorses completing a
  difficult project but repeatedly acts otherwise", is [CASE-013](../cases.d/CASE-013.md).
- A12 §2.4, "Introduce the Nucleation account of will-organization", with
  "accessibility, precedence, jurisdiction, persistence, suspension, and
  transformation", is this claim.
- A12's Proposition 2, "Equivalent observed actions need not imply
  equivalent governance", and A15 §3.5 are [CLAIM-122](CLAIM-122.md) and [ARG-005](../arguments.d/ARG-005.md).
- A12's "reliable execution of an endorsed commitment need not imply its
  revisability", and A15 §6.6, "Volitional efficacy can increase while
  revisability decreases", are [CLAIM-099](CLAIM-099.md), [ARG-007](../arguments.d/ARG-007.md) and [CLAIM-025](CLAIM-025.md).
- A12 §2.2, "Distinguish endorsing a motive, assigning it priority,
  successfully enacting it, and maintaining its priority over time", is
  [CLAIM-047](CLAIM-047.md).
- The thermostat objection (A12 §9.1) and A15 §9.4, "Does efficient
  behavior establish autonomy?", answered "Reject the inference", are
  [CLAIM-116](CLAIM-116.md).
- A12: "the paper should not claim novelty for the existence of multiple
  kinds of freedom, hierarchical control, or control-energy distinctions."
  That is [CLAIM-021](CLAIM-021.md).
- The limit that the account does not settle authorship or
  responsibility is close to [CLAIM-022](CLAIM-022.md).

What the proposal adds is the graded comparison ([CLAIM-354](CLAIM-354.md),
[CLAIM-345](CLAIM-345.md)), the Sen connection ([CLAIM-352](CLAIM-352.md)) and the nonlinear
constructions ([CASE-112](../cases.d/CASE-112.md)). It also conflicts with the essay's
supposition, [CLAIM-080](CLAIM-080.md): "the account asks how a given agent's practical
efficacy is organized, not what makes something an agent". The proposal
makes individuation a main section: A12 §3, "Individuating the agent
whose will is at issue", opening with "Why a system boundary cannot simply
be assumed", and A15 §4, "Individuation: whose control is it?". So the
manuscript would be the essay with its supposition lifted, and it should
say so. Whether it becomes a work of its own is the owner's decision
(workbench entries of 2026-10-18).

## Note of 2026-10-21: the essay found, and a companion manuscript

Unlike the exchange of 2026-10-17, this one found the essay. A15 of the
exchange of 2026-10-20: "the will paper's central Proposed thesis"; A17:
"I would write it as a companion to *The Organization of Will*". A17's
manuscript uses the six relations as the explanatory target and proposes
a formal adequacy criterion for descriptions of them, filed as
[CLAIM-407](CLAIM-407.md) and [CLAIM-409](CLAIM-409.md), with [TERM-067](../terms.d/TERM-067.md). A15's five
dimensions (capability, accessibility, practical authority, governance,
revisability) and A16's five stages (represented, accessible, authorized,
effective, revisable) restate the six relations with [TERM-055](../terms.d/TERM-055.md) and
[CLAIM-296](CLAIM-296.md), and are not filed.

## Note of 2026-10-27: status review

The status review of this date ([ADR-057](../decisions.d/ADR-057.md)) took this claim first. It has 19
dependents, more than any other Proposed CLAIM. It stays Proposed. Its
premises, [CLAIM-047](CLAIM-047.md) and [CLAIM-080](CLAIM-080.md), and the TERMs it uses are
Active, and nothing in the record meets its defeater. Of its two open
objections, one is answered and one still holds it back:

- **[CLAIM-089](CLAIM-089.md), the aggregate revealed preference.** It now has a
  reply, [CLAIM-tmpnp8w6](CLAIM-tmpnp8w6.md), on [CLAIM-434](CLAIM-434.md)'s result: an aggregate
  preference exists only where the wins never cycle, and then it is fitted
  to one regime of authority and does not predict a change in it. The
  owner held the reply the same day, and [CLAIM-089](CLAIM-089.md) is Rejected. The
  objection survives only for an organization with a persistent potential
  across a change in authority, which is where the reply says it is right.
- **[CLAIM-024](CLAIM-024.md), nothing new.** [CLAIM-159](CLAIM-159.md) is a partial reply. It
  places will-organization against the expected value of control
  ([THEORY-070](../theory.d/THEORY-070.md)) and Ainslie ([THEORY-067](../theory.d/THEORY-067.md)) as a claim about how the
  drive profile changes, and not about who drives. The comparison with
  planning theory and with institutional theory is still not made, and that
  is the missing result.
