# LIT-968: Is This the Subspace You Are Looking for? An Interpretability Illusion for Subspace Activation Patching

Aleksandar Makelov, Georg Lange and Neel Nanda (2023), *arXiv:2311.17030* (NeurIPS 2023 Workshop on Attributing Model Behavior at Scale).

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-782](../notes.d/NOTE-782.md).*

- **The mechanism (§3, Eq. 3).** Patching along
  v = (v_disconnected + v_dormant)/√2 changes the MLP output by
  ½(v_disconnectedᵀact_A − v_disconnectedᵀact_B) W_out v_dormant. The
  intervention converts variation the model cannot use into a push along
  a direction it does not use on the data. Equal weights are optimal
  (Lemma A.1).
- **IOI in GPT-2 Small (Table 1).** A DAS direction in MLP8 gives 46.7%
  FLDD and 4.2% interchange accuracy; without its null-space part, 13.5%;
  the whole MLP patch, −8%. MLP8 does not write the feature on the data
  (Figure 4), and a random MLP gives an even stronger direction (B.5).
- **The success case (§5).** The residual-stream direction after layer 8
  gives 140.7% FLDD and 74.8% accuracy, keeps 127.5% without the part the
  name movers cannot read, and is written only by the S-inhibition heads.
- **Factual recall (§6).** DAS fact patches in GPT2-XL MLPs depend on the
  null space of W_out. Any such patch has a rank-1 edit with the same
  output at that token (Lemma D.2), and ROME edits are approximated by
  subspace interventions in layers 20–35 (§6.3).
- **Prevalence (§7).** Argued, not measured: any unused MLP between two
  components that communicate a feature should contain such a direction.
- **Its own limits (§8).** No definition of a ground-truth explanation; a
  cancelling-subspaces reading of MLP8 is rejected on parsimony; the
  verdict depends on treating heads and MLP layers as the units of
  explanation, and in a rotated basis the toy's illusory direction becomes
  the faithful one (A.3).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 for [QUESTION-030](../questions.d/QUESTION-030.md), and read on 2026-10-14 with the
reply ([LIT-961](LIT-961.md)) and Huang et al. ([LIT-987](LIT-987.md)) as one dispute, in
[NOTE-782](../notes.d/NOTE-782.md). The registration said the paper shows that "interventional
success does not identify the constituting relation". The reading
narrows that: it shows that interventional success does not identify the
mediating variable, and it gives a test that tells the two apart in an
MLP. It says nothing about constitution. For [QUESTION-030](../questions.d/QUESTION-030.md) it is a
worked case, in a network, of an intervention that creates the relation
it was meant to detect by setting a variable to values the system never
takes. That is a caution for do(S) in [CLAIM-130](../claims.d/CLAIM-130.md)'s sense, and a concrete
instance of the underdetermination premise of [CLAIM-150](../claims.d/CLAIM-150.md).

Filed from the reading: [THEORY-282](../theory.d/THEORY-282.md), the illusion; and, from the point
this paper and the reply share, [THEORY-243](../theory.d/THEORY-243.md), that alignments related
by a change of basis can tie under interchange interventions.

The paper's closing advice to interpretability practitioners (search in
bottlenecks, validate beyond end-to-end effects) is an instruction for
machine-learning practice and is not filed here. An anthology topic on
interpretability could hold the paper, so it keeps the
`anthology-candidate` flag; it is held here for its own question under
[ADR-013](../decisions.d/ADR-013.md).
