# NOTE-tmphwo5s: Iranian NEWSCASTER social-media espionage (SecurityWeek, 2014)

## Contribution

SecurityWeek reports a campaign that the intelligence firm iSIGHT Partners called NEWSCASTER. More than a dozen fabricated personas on Facebook, Twitter, LinkedIn, Google+, YouTube and Blogger, backed by a fake outlet (NewsOnAir.org), connected with targets such as US military, diplomatic and congressional staff, journalists, think tanks and defence contractors. The connections were then used for spear-phishing and credential harvesting, with some unsophisticated malware. iSIGHT attributed the operators to Iran, but said there was no direct evidence of government sponsorship.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Method: low-tech but patient social engineering. Personas "connected, linked, followed, and friended" targets, then sent links to fake login pages (iSIGHT quote; Stephen Ward quote).
- Attribution evidence as reported: working hours matching Tehran, a lengthy lunch break, half days on Thursday and little activity on Friday (the Iranian weekend), plus the choice of targets and unspecified technical indicators.
- Response: iSIGHT coordinated with the FBI and notified the platforms. Ward said the personas had been removed from Facebook and LinkedIn.
- Industry commentary (Zscaler, Invincea, ZeroFOX) frames social media as a vector outside organisational control, and notes the risk of password reuse.
