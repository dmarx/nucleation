# LEAD-243: Build a multi-agent environment in which cooperation can change the participants' capacities and dependencies, implicit contracts can form and governance can be revised, and compare regimes matched on joint production and ordinary behaviour by their responses to withdrawal, disagreement, renegotiation and unforeseen contingencies

## The plan

A22 §21 and A24 §23 propose the same testbed from two sides. Agents learn
one another's behaviour, specialize, and can lose capacities they stop
using; relationships can come to govern later activity; and some agents can
revise the terms. The regimes to compare, merged from both turns:

| Regime | What distinguishes it |
|---|---|
| Independent / fixed-capacity cooperation | Interaction changes strategies but not independent capacities |
| Reciprocal specialization | Benefits and maintenance costs are shared |
| Unilateral specialization | One agent becomes dependent on another |
| Unaccounted subsidy | One agent compensates for another's failures |
| Fixed norms | Acquired commitments cannot readily be revised |
| Revisable norms / reflexive inquiry | Agents can investigate and renegotiate commitments |
| Protected contestability | Revision channels cannot be disabled unilaterally |

Measures (A24 §23): joint production, maintenance expenditure, independent
capacity, dependency structure (consequences of interrupted supply),
control over revision, demand compatibility, recognition of novel
failures, robustness after perturbation. A22 adds cost allocation and
compliance.

What it would test:

- **[CLAIM-1219](../claims.d/CLAIM-1219.md).** Regimes matched on ordinary behaviour should part
  under the perturbations that claim lists. A22's control: "compare
  genuine accommodation against suppression of conflicting demands".
- **[CLAIM-1284](../claims.d/CLAIM-1284.md).** Norm stability should rise with dependent investment
  while independent capacity falls (A22: "norm stability can increase
  while independent capacity declines").
- **[CLAIM-1244](../claims.d/CLAIM-1244.md).** In the unaccounted-subsidy regime, regulatory
  savings should accrue to the non-maintaining agent while measured harmony
  matches the reciprocal regime (A22: "exploitation-like arrangements can
  persist through successful performance").
- **[CLAIM-1316](../claims.d/CLAIM-1316.md)**, in one respect only: whether cooperation scores
  alone fail to fix the relational properties a moral evaluation would
  need. A24: it "would not demonstrate that the computational processes
  themselves have moral standing, or that the model has derived moral
  norms from first principles."

## What it needs before it is specified

- a capacity dynamics (how unused capacity decays and can be rebuilt),
  such as A20 §6's update rule;
- a definition of when a relationship counts as an implicit contract in
  the environment, by [CLAIM-1219](../claims.d/CLAIM-1219.md)'s probes, fixed before the runs;
- the matching criterion for "ordinary behaviour", stated in advance;
- a perturbation schedule fixed before the comparison, as in the
  record's other matched-behaviour designs ([CLAIM-364](../claims.d/CLAIM-364.md)).
