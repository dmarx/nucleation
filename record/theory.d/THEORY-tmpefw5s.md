# THEORY-tmpefw5s: Pembrey, Saffery and Bygren report that in the Överkalix cohorts a paternal grandparent's mid-childhood food supply was associated, sex-specifically, with grandchildren's mortality, and argue from the exposure's sensitive period that cultural or genetic inheritance alone is unlikely to explain it, while granting that replication is needed

## The account

In three Överkalix cohorts (born 1895, 1905 and 1920, about 100 probands
each), grandsons' mortality was linked only to the paternal grandfather's
food supply in mid-childhood, granddaughters' only to the paternal
grandmother's. Against moderate supply, rate ratios were 1.67 for good and
0.65 for poor supply in grandsons, and 2.13 and 0.72 in granddaughters. The
association held for the grandparent's "slow growth period" before the
prepubertal spurt, not later. In ALSPAC, sons of fathers who began smoking
before 11 had more body fat from 13.

The timing is the authors' argument against culture: the associations are
"most unlikely to be due to 'cultural' inheritance/social patterning or
genetic inheritance in isolation", and "assuming cultural inheritance
automatically when results make genetic inheritance implausible (and vice
versa) is no longer acceptable". Against multiple testing they cite "clear
prior hypotheses (see table 1) and coherence between different
studies/populations", and grant that "further replication is needed". On
mechanism they stay agnostic, listing maternal and paternal routes other
than the germline epigenome, and hold that effects beyond the second
generation need germline involvement.

## Evidence

The paper ([NOTE-1320](../notes.d/NOTE-1320.md)): historical harvest records linked to small cohorts
in one population, with some confirmation across cohorts, ALSPAC smoking
data, and one German discussion paper. The case against culture is argued
from the pattern of timing and sex, not from measured cultural variables;
the one adjustment is for the grandchild's early-life circumstances.
Several of the priors in Table 1 were set after earlier Överkalix results.

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-297](../questions.d/QUESTION-297.md), the germline route. Överkalix, cited there as a germline
trauma case, is a nutritional association in the male line, in which good
supply, not poor, went with higher grandchild mortality. The authors
measured no family culture and leave the mechanism open, so the case does
not choose between the germline, culture and other non-genetic channels.
