# NOTE-tmp2cx1i: An Organizational Account of Biological Functions

## Contribution

Before this paper the function debate faced a dilemma. Etiological
(selected-effect) theories explained why a trait exists and grounded
malfunction, but said nothing about what the trait does now. Dispositional
theories said what it does now but could not say which contributions are
proper functions. The paper offers one definition that does both, by
grounding teleology and normativity in the current organization of a
self-maintaining system, and it adds a criterion, differentiation, that
keeps functions out of flames and hurricanes.

## Key insight

Circular self-maintenance makes a process's existence depend on its own
effects, which gives teleology and norms without history. But every
dissipative structure is circular in that sense. Functions start only when
the system produces several distinct structures that each contribute in a
different way, so that one can ask what each part is for.

## Assumptions

- **Self-maintenance as far-from-equilibrium closure.** A macroscopic pattern
  exerts "at least one 'top-down' constraint that contributes to the
  maintenance of the FFE flow of energy and matter enabling the persistence
  of the microscopic dynamics" (p. 8, citing Ruiz-Mirazo 2001).
- **Context dependence.** Self-maintenance holds "with respect to a given
  environment and context" (endnote v), so the functional/indispensable and
  primary/secondary distinctions are context-relative too.
- **Classes and regimes.** Self-maintaining systems fall into classes, each
  admitting several regimes of self-maintenance; functions are relative to
  a regime (§5.1).
- **Biological traits, not artefacts.** Artefacts are deferred; endnote xi
  suggests they are produced by, but not maintained by, an organization.

## Key results

- **Closure grounds teleology and normativity (§4.1).** "To the question
  'Why does X exist in that class of systems?' it is legitimate to answer
  'Because it does Y'". The system's conditions of existence "are the norms
  of its own activity".
- **Closure is not sufficient (§4.1–4.2).** Bénard cells and a candle flame
  are worked examples of closure (pp. 8–9). Their components have no
  functions because they make "a unique contribution" through one pattern.
  Material complexity (many molecular species, autocatalytic sets) does not
  change this; endnote viii shows how a single-catalyst autocatalytic set
  becomes functional once it produces a membrane.
- **Definition (§5).** C1 contribution to O; C2 produced and maintained
  under constraints of O; C3 S differentiated. The heart pumps blood: C1
  through circulation, C2 because the organism makes and keeps the heart, C3
  because the organism makes many other contributing structures.
- **Indispensable versus merely functional (§5.1).** The heart is
  indispensable in every human regime; the eye's light transduction is
  functional but not indispensable (blind people survive by shifting
  regime).
- **Primary and secondary functions (§5.2).** The nose's primary function is
  to warm and humidify air; supporting eyeglasses is secondary, subject to
  closure only in a more complex (socio-technical) regime.
- **Useful, dysfunctional, irrelevant, accidental (§6.1–6.2).** Useful: C1
  and C3, not C2. Dysfunctional: C2 and C3, not C1. Irrelevant effects: C2
  and C3, no C1, not interfering with function. Frequency is irrelevant.
- **Against selected-effect theories (§6.3).** Primary functions usually
  coincide with proper functions, because basic regimes are evolutionarily
  older, but diverge for traits selected and now useless (SE: function; OA:
  had one) and for current contributions with no selection history (OA:
  function; SE: none).
- **Reproduction (§6.4)** is conceded not to be functional for the
  individual. It may be functional in a second-order self-maintaining
  system (lineage or ecosystem), which the authors say "still lacks
  adequate justification".

## Concepts

- **Organizational closure.** "A circular causal relation between some
  macroscopic (or higher-level) pattern or structure and the microscopic (or
  lower-level) dynamics and reactions" (p. 9). Note: this is broader than
  the closure of constraints of [LIT-tmpksq6n](../literature.d/LIT-tmpksq6n.md).
- **Organizational differentiation.** The system "produces different and
  localizable patterns or structures, each making a specific contribution
  to the conditions of existence of the whole organization".
- **Regime of self-maintenance.** A specific organization a member of a class
  can adopt without ceasing to exist or leaving the class.
- **Primary function.** The contribution subject to closure in the most basic
  regime of self-maintenance that produces T.

## Connections

The paper's targets are the systemic approach (Cummins, Craver, Davies),
the goal-contribution approach (Boorse, Adams), the propensity view
(Bigelow & Pargetter) and selected-effect theories (Wright, Millikan,
Neander, Godfrey-Smith). Its allies are Christensen & Bickhard (2002) and
McLaughlin (2001), whom it claims to improve on by giving explicit
conditions. Its physics is Glansdorff & Prigogine and Nicolis & Prigogine,
represented in the record by [LIT-516](../literature.d/LIT-516.md). Montévil & Mossio ([LIT-tmpksq6n](../literature.d/LIT-tmpksq6n.md))
replace this paper's broad closure with closure of constraints and keep the
function thesis. Barandiaran et al. ([LIT-tmp2zh8b](../literature.d/LIT-tmp2zh8b.md)) cite it for
organism-generated normativity.

## Bearing on the record

- **The demarcation question ([LIT-192](../literature.d/LIT-192.md), [NOTE-094](NOTE-094.md); [LIT-526](../literature.d/LIT-526.md); [LIT-516](../literature.d/LIT-516.md)).** [NOTE-094](NOTE-094.md)
  records that closure accounts face the candle-flame problem. This paper
  does not deny the flame closure; it denies it functions. So the line
  between flame and cell, in the 2009 account, is differentiation. In the
  2015 account ([LIT-tmpksq6n](../literature.d/LIT-tmpksq6n.md)) closure itself is narrower: each constraint
  must be both produced by and productive of another constraint in the set,
  with direct dependence, and a cycle of processes such as the water cycle
  is said not to qualify. Whether a flame's single pattern can meet that is
  not discussed in either paper; my reading is that a single macroscopic
  constraint cannot form a closed set by itself, so the 2015 closure likely
  excludes the flame where the 2009 closure did not. That is an inference,
  not a result of either paper.
- **Baedke ([LIT-167](../literature.d/LIT-167.md))** argues closure is too permissive to individuate
  organisms. This paper never uses closure to individuate; it uses it to
  ground functions. The objection bears on [LIT-tmpksq6n](../literature.d/LIT-tmpksq6n.md) §6, not here.
- **THEORY suggested** (reported to the batch, not filed): what separates
  organisms from dissipative structures is closure together with
  organizational differentiation, not closure alone.
- **Biological Autonomy ([LIT-536](../literature.d/LIT-536.md))** chapter 3 should contain the book's
  version; a reader of it could check whether differentiation survives.
- No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- Artefacts and reproduction are deferred, and reproduction is the obvious
  counterexample to a self-maintenance account of function.
- "Differentiation" is not given a measure; "different and localizable" is
  left informal.
- The relation between primary functions and selected effects is said to
  need "an independent paper".

## Open questions

- Is there a quantitative criterion of differentiation that a model could
  be checked against, so that a flame, a protocell and an autocatalytic set
  are sorted without intuition?
- Does the 2015 closure of constraints make differentiation redundant as a
  demarcation criterion, or are both still needed?
