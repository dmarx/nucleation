# CLAIM-1371: A purpose can govern its own reconsideration

## The claim

A16 §3, "A purpose must not acquire sovereignty over the institution that
pursues it": an adopted objective creates a process that "can subsequently
acquire influence over" which evidence is relevant, which activities are
resourced, which participants hold authority, "Which forms of criticism
are considered legitimate" and "Which alternative purposes remain
intelligible". "This is a form of organizational closure." The remedy it
states: "maintain the distinction between **authority to pursue a purpose
and authority to determine whether that purpose should continue to
govern.**"

A16 §6 gives the inquiry this needs, orders of question from "How can we
accomplish our objective?" to "Is our purpose still appropriate?" and "Are
the mechanisms by which we determine appropriateness themselves
adequate?": "The higher orders cannot always be evaluated according to
the objectives of the lower orders." "an AI safety team investigating
whether a technology should be deployed cannot be independently evaluated
solely by its contribution to successful deployment." Hence "**purpose-directed
inquiry** a first-class institutional function, with independently
governed resources and protected authority to raise questions".

A16 §7 and §11 sketch the apparatus: a "purpose ledger" recording each
purpose's normative grounds, constituents, "Authorized subordinate
objectives", "Jurisdictional constraints" and "Conditions requiring
reconsideration", so that it becomes possible to discover "that a
subordinate objective has gradually displaced the purpose it was
originally authorized to serve", and a "**purpose-governance protocol** analogous to an architecture decision
record process", which "could allow
an institution to retain the argument for its purposes rather than merely
retaining the purposes themselves".

## How it stands to the record

- [CLAIM-1252](CLAIM-1252.md): an objective that admits others only through their
  contribution to it excludes their standing. This claim is the
  reflexive case: the objective admits proposals about itself only
  through their contribution to it.
- [CLAIM-1287](CLAIM-1287.md): a narrow optimizer values weakening the constraints on it;
  here the constraint weakened is its own review.
- [CLAIM-1340](CLAIM-1340.md): shareholder-value belief binding "beyond the law" is A16's
  example as an empirical claim.
- [LEAD-280](../leads.d/LEAD-280.md) asks whether corrective processes stay effective against the
  interests of the authority that controls them; this claim says why
  they may not when that authority is the purpose under review.
- [QUESTION-271](../questions.d/QUESTION-271.md), evaluation that decides authority without being captured
  by it, is the general form.
- A16 §12 turns the claim on the platform itself: "If the platform itself
  acquired authority to decide which institutional purposes are
  legitimate, we would have constructed another sovereign optimizer, only
  this time operating at the constitutional level."

## What it does not say

That purposes should be easy to revise. A16 §8: "An institution whose
purposes change too easily cannot sustain commitments", and the design
problem is "**differentially governed revisability**", [CLAIM-1104](CLAIM-1104.md)'s
hierarchy of revisable constraints.
