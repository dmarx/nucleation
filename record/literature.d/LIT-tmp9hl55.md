# LIT-tmp9hl55: Ontic Structural Realism and Economics

Don Ross (2008), *Philosophy of Science 75(5), 732–743 (December 2008), in the PSA 2006 symposium proceedings. The preprint is PhilSci-Archive item 3045.* — DOI-10.1086/594518

## Key takeaways

- Ross argues that OSR, usually motivated only by fundamental physics, is also the right ontology for a mature special science, economics. The progress of economic theory from Jevons through Hicks–Allen (1934), Samuelson (1947), Debreu (1959) and Arrow (1965) consists in steadily removing psychological, individual-level hypotheses. Its basic objects are optimisation problems, above all games, which are "mathematical structures … whose relata are distinguished as such by the mathematical representations … just like quarks and bosons" (§3). So OSR, joined with an assumption that science is unified, gets consilient support from outside physics. Individuals, in physics and in economics, are "book-keeping devices".
