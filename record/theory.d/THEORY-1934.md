# THEORY-1934: Umpleby reports that Lefebvre's algebraic model of ethical cognition generates two ethical systems, in the first of which a bad means may not serve a good end and in the second of which it may, and that Lefebvre took the first to dominate in the West and the second in the Soviet Union

## Source

Umpleby (2016), [LIT-2693](../literature.d/LIT-2693.md), a secondary account read as a stand-in for [LIT-2697](../literature.d/LIT-2697.md) and [LIT-2663](../literature.d/LIT-2663.md). The reading is [NOTE-2017](../notes.d/NOTE-2017.md).

## What was actually shown

Umpleby reports the two systems and their cultural reading (pp. 65-66), and that in the first system the compromiser is the saint while in the second the confronter is (p. 66). His evidence for the cultural reading is anecdote and audience polls. Nothing of the model's mathematics is shown.

## What this does not say

- That the cultural differences are established: they are illustrated, not tested.
- The exchange's table (A11 §5), which assigns the systems the other way round.
- Anything about manipulation, or about organizations.

## From the second reading (2026-11-17)

The second reading adds the generating rule ("Depending on whether the
combination rule for the exponents is addition or multiplication, two
quite different ethical systems are generated", p. 65) and three limits:

- **The model.** The algebra and its derivations are not reproduced; the
  means/ends gloss is Umpleby's.
- **The cultural claim.** It rests on anecdote; A's own caution that these
  generalizations "should not be accepted as established psychological
  facts" fits how thin the evidence is.
- **A link to manipulation.** Reflexive control appears only as a keyword,
  so A's "bridge" to the manipulation of moral reasoning ([CLAIM-703](../claims.d/CLAIM-703.md)) is
  not supported by it.
