# NOTE-2020: Harsey and Freyd (2023) — The Influence of DARVO and Insincere Apologies on Perceptions of Sexual Assault

## Contribution

Experimental evidence that a perpetrator's DARVO statement (deny, attack,
reverse victim and offender) moves third parties' judgments of
believability, abusiveness and punishment in a sexual-assault scenario,
replicating Harsey and Freyd (2020), which used dating violence, and
extending it to punishment.

## Key results

- **Definition** (Abstract): "When using DARVO, perpetrators deny their
  involvement in wrongdoing, attack their victims’ credibility, and argue
  that they are the real victims."
- **Sample**: the abstract has "Data from 230 undergraduate students"; the
  Method has "Participants were 235 undergraduates".
- **Main effect** (Results): "Scores on all variables except victim
  responsibility (H1b) were statistically different between DARVO and No
  DARVO conditions." In the abstract, perpetrator less abusive ηp² = .09,
  victim less believable ηp² = .08.
- **Punishment** (Discussion): "Endorsement of perpetrator punishment was
  82% among individuals not exposed to DARVO but only 60.7% among
  individuals exposed to DARVO." "Belief that the victim should not be
  punished was endorsed by 92.9% of individuals who were not exposed to
  DARVO but endorsed by only 66.9% of individuals who were exposed to
  DARVO."
- **Responsibility is the small effect** (Limitations): for perpetrator
  responsibility, partial η² = .021, "a value that is generally categorized
  as a small effect size". Victim responsibility did not move; the authors
  suspect a floor effect.
- **Apologies** (Abstract): "Insincere apologies had minimal impact on
  ratings."
- **The authors' reading** (Discussion): "The argument made by DARVO—that
  the perpetrator not only did nothing wrong, but is also a victim of a
  false accusation—is clearly effective when it comes to decisions about
  punishment."

## Assumptions

- A short written statement stands in for DARVO; the authors note it was
  "a short, single-paragraph statement containing only a few phrases
  representing DARVO".
- The victim's statement was the same in every condition, so the ground
  truth reads as assault.
- The No DARVO/No Apology cell had no statement from the professor:
  "Professor Smith has chosen not to make a statement on the matter". The
  pooled No-DARVO condition therefore mixes silence with an apology-only
  statement.

## Concepts

- **DARVO** (Freyd 1997): deny, attack, reverse victim and offender.
- **Insincere apology** ("ifpology").
- **Third-party observers**' judgments of believability, responsibility,
  abusiveness and punishment.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-726](../claims.d/CLAIM-726.md)** (manufacturing culpability): the LIT registered this
  work as evidence for the claim, that "how responsibility is narrated
  changes how it is assigned". The study carries less. Its DARVO is a
  denial that casts the accusation as false; the claim is about a true
  accusation, of a wrong the accuser arranged, used before an audience.
  The experiment has no arranged wrong and no accusation by the stronger
  party. What it shows that is near the claim's third move is only that a
  party's narration of an accusation shifts observers' judgments of
  credibility and punishment; it shifts rated responsibility only a
  little. It neither corroborates nor bounds the claim, and no relation is
  written.
- **[TERM-127](../terms.d/TERM-127.md)**: the study's "responsibility" item is culpability in
  the term's sense.
- The study offers no test for recognizing DARVO from statements alone,
  which is consistent with the caution A5 §4 states and [QUESTION-071](../questions.d/QUESTION-071.md)
  quotes.

## Limitations

The authors name a homogeneous undergraduate sample, few men, small
effects on some outcomes, a high-status perpetrator never varied, broad
punishment items and no measure of rape-myth acceptance. Not named by
them: the silent control cell and the 230 against 235 discrepancy.

## From the reading of 2026-11-16

*Quotations here are carried from the reading of 2026-11-16, whose filer verified them against its copy; they were not rechecked at the reconciliation.*

- **Against the earlier study** (Introduction): in the authors' 2020
  dating-violence experiment, "Contrary to predictions, DARVO penalized"
  the perpetrator's believability; here it raised it.
- **Guilt by stipulation**: participants read about "an incident of sexual
  abuse", so the study measures how DARVO moves observers given a guilty
  speaker and cannot say whether DARVO-like statements are evidence of
  guilt.
- **The exchange** (A5 §4) reports the study accurately in direction.
  Corrections: 235 participants (230 in the analysed data the abstract
  reports), one scenario in four variants, small effects. A's own caveat,
  that such studies "do not provide an independent test for identifying
  which party is lying", is borne out by the design.
- **[THEORY-327](../theory.d/THEORY-327.md)** (Fricker): DARVO's attack on the accuser's credibility
  is a deliberate manipulation of credibility, which Fricker distinguishes
  from testimonial injustice as "a related but distinct wrong"; this study
  is experimental evidence that such manipulation moves third-party
  credibility judgments.
- **[THEORY-317](../theory.d/THEORY-317.md)**: men in the sample rated the victim less believable than
  women did, the kind of deflated credibility judgement it describes; the
  study does not show that prejudice is the cause.
- **[CLAIM-709](../claims.d/CLAIM-709.md) and [CASE-279](../cases.d/CASE-279.md)**, which the registration named: no
  bearing; the study is about accusation and response, not the
  distribution of control in a relationship.
- **Limitation**: the tables are not in the copy the first reading used.
