  Nisbett & Wilson (1977). Reviews evidence that people may have little or no direct introspective access to higher-order cognitive processes: they can be unaware of a stimulus that influenced a response, of the response, or of the influence. Reports on one's own processes rest on a priori, implicit causal theories about what plausibly caused what, so they are accurate when the influential stimulus is salient and a plausible cause, and not otherwise. Its test is that observers given only a description of the situation predict the same effects the subjects report.
