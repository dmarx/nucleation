# LIT-tmp6nn9d: Individuality Through Ecology: Rethinking the Evolution of Complex Life From an Externalist Perspective

Pierrick Bourrat, Peter Takacs, Guilhem Doulcier, Matthew C. Nitschke, Andrew J. Black, Katrin Hammerschmidt, Paul B. Rainey (2024). "Individuality Through Ecology: Rethinking the Evolution of Complex Life From an Externalist Perspective." *Ecology and Evolution* 14(12): e70661. DOI-10.1002/ece3.70661.

## Key takeaways

Internalist explanations of transitions in individuality take Darwinian properties at the higher level for granted; ecological scaffolding explains how they arise from outside, internal changes being "follower" factors. Endogenization is "An evolutionary process during which higher‐level collectives gain the capacity to persist even in the absence of an ecological scaffold" (Box 1). It needs two equilibria in the unscaffolded environment, the scaffold removing the one that favours independent cells (Figure 2). In a constructive variant, cells' waste makes the patches, and what counts as internal or external shifts during the transition.
