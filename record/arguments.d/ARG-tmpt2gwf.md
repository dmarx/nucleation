# ARG-tmpt2gwf: Saussure's value, as the record reads it, supports transport of relational identity across languages

## The inference

[CLAIM-082](../claims.d/CLAIM-082.md) is the manuscript's structuralist premise, and [CLAIM-115](../claims.d/CLAIM-115.md) rests on
it: "A sign's communicative significance is fixed by its contrasts within a
system rather than by correspondence to a referent." The record corrects
how it states Saussure ([ARG-tmpv7dh8](ARG-tmpv7dh8.md)): say value, not significance, and
drop the referent contrast. [CLAIM-072](../claims.d/CLAIM-072.md)'s last paragraph then drew this
inference: "It does not undercut the manuscript's use. Relational identity
survives the correction, and is arguably stronger for it: a term's value,
not its signification, is what the manuscript's observables track."

## Critical questions

- **Does the work say this, read in context?** In part. [THEORY-159](../theory.d/THEORY-159.md) holds
  that a term's value is fixed by its relations to coexisting terms, and is
  distinct from signification. It does not say anything about transport.
- **Is it said within the scope the claim needs?** No. This is the first
  question that fails. [CLAIM-254](../claims.d/CLAIM-254.md): "Value is a fact of the language system,
  the *langue*. [CLAIM-115](../claims.d/CLAIM-115.md)'s observables, 'stance, footing, alignment,
  expected uptake', are facts of utterances in use. Nothing in the line
  argues that a relational account of the language system carries over to
  utterances." A search of the claims on 2026-10-10 found *langue* only
  inside [CLAIM-072](../claims.d/CLAIM-072.md)'s quotation of Saussure, and *parole* nowhere.
- **Does the claim lean on the reading for more than the reading holds?**
  Yes, on [CLAIM-254](../claims.d/CLAIM-254.md)'s reading. [THEORY-159](../theory.d/THEORY-159.md): "The account concerns the
  relations inside a language state". Its first example: "French *mouton*
  may share a signification with English *sheep* but not its value,
  because English also has *mutton* for the meat." A translation moves a
  text into another language state, so every value is fixed afresh. "If
  the observables track value, as [CLAIM-072](../claims.d/CLAIM-072.md) says, then the premise
  predicts that what they track is what a change of language changes."
  What can cross is signification, or a correspondence supplied from
  outside both systems, which [CLAIM-082](../claims.d/CLAIM-082.md)'s own qualification from relative
  representations ([THEORY-175](../theory.d/THEORY-175.md)) already requires: "for transport between
  systems they work only once something ties the anchor in one to the
  anchor in the other."

## The reply

[CLAIM-242](../claims.d/CLAIM-242.md), 2026-10-10, grants the premises and denies the conclusion. What
the premise rules out is identity of value across languages, which the line
does not claim ([CLAIM-106](../claims.d/CLAIM-106.md)); "values do not cross between languages, which
is why fidelity is graded transport through an anchored correspondence
rather than identity of value". At the level of use, the counterpart of
value is an utterance's contrast with the alternatives its speaker could
have used, the rational-speech-act premise ([THEORY-172](../theory.d/THEORY-172.md)) and Bergen, Goodman
and Levy's finding ([LIT-809](../literature.d/LIT-809.md)). Saussure becomes precedent, not premise.
[CLAIM-242](../claims.d/CLAIM-242.md) could be the premise of a second line to [CLAIM-115](../claims.d/CLAIM-115.md). It is not
filed as one here.

Proposed, as [CLAIM-254](../claims.d/CLAIM-254.md) left it: "whether that use-level counterpart is the
structuralist premise restated or a replacement for it is arguable." The
third point, that value is a fact of *langue* and the observables are facts
of use, is conceded on both sides. What would settle it is an argument that
values in two language states can correspond without passing through
signification, a correspondence fixed independently of the fidelity it
measures (the τ of [QUESTION-005](../questions.d/QUESTION-005.md)), or a restatement of [CLAIM-082](../claims.d/CLAIM-082.md) at the
level of use with a reading that supports it there. Nothing here says
relational identity is false, nor that [CLAIM-115](../claims.d/CLAIM-115.md) needs Saussure: [CLAIM-082](../claims.d/CLAIM-082.md)
calls itself "a motivating premise, not a result the manuscript relies on
formally".
