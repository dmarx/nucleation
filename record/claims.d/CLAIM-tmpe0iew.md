# CLAIM-tmpe0iew: An ordering serving as the negotiation's potential, as the potential account of valence defines it, tracks 'wanting', not 'liking': sensitized incentive salience wins a system's conflicts, persists and resumes while hedonic impact measured by taste reactivity stays flat or falls, so the regime the account calls hedonic valence is motivational valence, and hedonic valence, if it is anywhere in the account, is elsewhere

## The objection

[TERM-tmp8gbrt](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8gbrt.md) defines potential by Frijda's three marks: priority in
conflicts, persistence, resumption after interruption. Sensitized
'wanting' has all three. Lateral-hypothalamic stimulation made rats "'want'
to eat at least four times more than normal amounts" while it "failed to
enhance 'liking' reactions to sweetness", and in addiction cue-triggered
'wanting' can grow "even if the drug became less pleasant" ([THEORY-tmp0bbxk](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp0bbxk.md)).
The ordering that wins is strengthened; the hedonic impact, measured by
taste reactivity without report, is not.

[CLAIM-tmpz8lli](CLAIM-tmpz8lli.md) names this as a defeater: "intensity that does not track
control precedence". Here it does not.

**The rescues tried, and why none is clean.**
- **The kind clause.** The potential ordering must rank the system's own
  states by their bearing on its maintenance. But hunger's 'wanting' is
  about maintenance, and it can be dissociated from 'liking' too.
- **The sign clause.** Tantalus, who wants and never gets, is behind his
  course, and his negative valence fits. The addict who gets the drug and
  enjoys it less is ahead of the course the 'wanting' sets, with strong
  potential and low 'liking'. The sign clause gives the wrong answer.

**So the regime the claim defines is motivational valence.** It is what
Berridge calls 'wanting', and Berridge says it is valenced: "Motivational
salience is never neutral, but its valence is not fixed." Hedonic
valence, 'liking', is not this regime.

**Where it might be instead.** Hesp et al. ([LIT-tmpvgq87](../literature.d/LIT-tmpvgq87.md), [NOTE-tmpsj4fq](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpsj4fq.md))
put valence in the system's inference about its own action model:
"agents infer their valence state based on the expected precision of
their action model" (p. 399). They concede that the dopaminergic signal
in their account is closer to 'wanting' than to pleasure (p. 430). If
hedonic valence is anywhere in the record's account, [CLAIM-tmptd5us](CLAIM-tmptd5us.md)'s
self-model is the candidate place. But [CLAIM-tmptd5us](CLAIM-tmptd5us.md) makes the
self-model what makes valence felt, not what makes it hedonic, and
Berridge notes that 'wanting' can itself be appraised as pleasure, so a
model of potential may yield felt motivation rather than felt pleasure.

## What it does not say

- That the potential account is wrong about motivational
  valence. The objection grants it there.
- That 'liking' cannot be ordering-potential of some other ordering. It
  says the ordering that wins the system's conflicts is not, in the
  dissociation cases, the one 'liking' tracks.

## What would answer it

A reading of 'liking' as potential of a different ordering than the one
'wanting' sets, for instance an ordering over the system's consummatory
states rather than its pursuits, with taste reactivity tracking that
ordering's potential in the dissociation cases; or a revision of
[CLAIM-tmpz8lli](CLAIM-tmpz8lli.md) and [TERM-tmp8gbrt](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8gbrt.md) from hedonic to motivational valence,
conceding the objection, with hedonic valence left to another account.
The second is the owner's decision.

## Conceded, 2026-10-23

At the owner's direction the objection is conceded: the claim it objects
to and its term are revised from "hedonic" to "goal-conduciveness"
valence ([CLAIM-tmp5lpdu](CLAIM-tmp5lpdu.md)). Active, in the record's convention for a
conceded counter.
