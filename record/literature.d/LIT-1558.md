# LIT-1558: Dominant contribution of Asgard archaea to eukaryogenesis

Tobiasson, V., Luo, J., Wolf, Y. I. & Koonin, E. V. (2026). Dominant contribution of Asgard archaea to eukaryogenesis. *Nature* 650(8100): 141–149. doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09960-6.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1242](../notes.d/NOTE-1242.md).*

- **Asgard first, by likelihood weight.** In 4,290 well-resolved clusters,
  Asgard takes 50% of the weight and leads in nearly every functional
  class. Information processing is "almost exclusively of Asgard descent"
  ([THEORY-1104](../theory.d/THEORY-1104.md)).
- **The mitochondrion's share is small and coherent.** Alphaproteobacteria
  lead only in oxidative phosphorylation, ubiquinone, 4Fe-4S assembly and
  mitochondrial translation.
- **Other bacteria are diffuse.** About a third of families point elsewhere,
  with no pathway from one donor apart from a narrow Myxococcota signal. The
  authors read this as transfer, with "no indication of another symbiotic
  event". That is an interpretation.
- **Stem length is not a clock.** The mitochondrion-late stem-length signal
  reproduces only for long stems and reverses for short ones, and
  co-acquired genes scatter widely ([THEORY-1101](../theory.d/THEORY-1101.md)).
- **Mitochondrion-late all the same.** The authors favour an Asgard ancestor
  with a cytoskeleton and endomembranes before the endosymbiont, inferred
  from gene ancestry, not timed.

## Why this is here

A1 ranked it sixth; A2 sets it beside [LIT-1566](LIT-1566.md) and [LIT-1561](LIT-1561.md) as one side of a live disagreement on whether eukaryogenesis was one two-partner merger or a distributed history of associations.

The reading corrects that placement in part. This work is the two-partner side only on symbiosis: it accepts many bacterial gene donors before and after the endosymbiosis and denies only that any was a further partner. On the timing of the mitochondrion it agrees with [LIT-1566](LIT-1566.md) as the record summarizes it. Its disagreement with [LIT-1561](LIT-1561.md) is about whether the non-alphaproteobacterial genes are diffuse or come from a few donors in waves, and whether stem lengths can time them ([NOTE-1242](../notes.d/NOTE-1242.md)).
