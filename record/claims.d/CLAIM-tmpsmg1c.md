# CLAIM-tmpsmg1c: When a choice about a technology or an institution could substantially and durably narrow the possibilities later open to those it affects, the burden of justifying it should rise with the size and the irreversibility of the narrowing

## The claim

A13 §VIII, "The reversibility principle":

> One particularly useful addition would be a preference for reversible
> arrangements under conditions of significant uncertainty.

and its formulation:

> When a technological choice could substantially and durably narrow
> future human possibilities, the burden of justification should rise
> with the magnitude and irreversibility of the change.

A adds the counterweight in the same section: "Not every innovation can be
fully tested before implementation. And excessive caution can preserve
harmful arrangements." The claim generalizes "technological" to choices
about institutions, which A's examples (education, employment, healthcare,
political participation) already include.

## Why irreversibility, and where the reason runs out

The reason the claim needs is uncertainty with learning. [CLAIM-559](CLAIM-559.md) shows
that where reassessment is informative, activating a capability
provisionally and cancelling it later can both be optimal under one
objective; an irreversible choice forecloses the second step. Where nothing
will be learned, irreversibility adds no reason of its own, and the
defeater says so. The claim is therefore a rule for choices made before
the evidence is in, which is the situation of [CLAIM-tmpbypt7](CLAIM-tmpbypt7.md)'s ratchet,
whose point of no return is where restoring the alternatives costs more
than any participant can bear.

## What it does not say

- Not a presumption against novelty. A13 §X: restraint "should not become
  a universal presumption against novelty".
- Not who bears the burden or who judges it. A pairs the principle with
  "pilot programs, independent evaluation, meaningful appeals, and
  opportunities for revision"; which institutions those are is left open.

## Note of 2026-11-13: the readings of 2026-11-13

- **Postman** ([THEORY-tmp8mi75](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp8mi75.md)): the consequences of technological change are "always vast, often unpredictable and largely irreversible", and he counsels caution. Corroborates the premise; he states no burden rule.
- **Moen** ([THEORY-tmpzuniv](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpzuniv.md)): control of technological progress faces "both an epistemic problem and a coordination problem", the uncertainty with later learning this claim's reason needs. Corroborates the premise.
- **Rosa** ([THEORY-tmp0dvpy](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp0dvpy.md)): a modern society can be slowed "but only at the cost of its institutions getting in crises", the cost of reversing a structural ratchet at the scale of the whole. A point of contact, no relation.
