# LIT-266: Contextuality for preparations, transformations, and unsharp measurements

R. W. Spekkens (2004), *Physical Review A 71, 052108 (2005)* — [arXiv:quant-ph/0406166](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0406166)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-748](../notes.d/NOTE-748.md).*

- **The definition.** "A noncontextual ontological model of an operational
  theory is one wherein if two experimental procedures are operationally
  equivalent, then they have equivalent representations in the ontological
  model." Equivalence quantifies over every procedure of the other kinds
  (Eqs. 1–3). The context is whatever the equivalence class leaves
  unspecified.
- **The old notion inside the new.** For sharp measurements with
  idempotent indicator functions, independence from the fine-graining of
  the PVM "is just the traditional notion of noncontextuality" (§III).
  Fine-grainings of non-trivial PVMs do not exist in dimension two, so the
  traditional proofs need dimension three.
- **A qubit is preparation contextual (§IV).** Six pure states give five
  decompositions of I/2. Orthogonal states have disjoint supports, and
  preparation noncontextuality then forces every distribution to vanish.
  No measurement assumption is used.
- **And transformation contextual (§VI)**, by the same structure on six
  rotations about one axis. It is also contextual for unsharp measurements
  if sharp measurements are outcome-deterministic (§V).
- **The bridge (§VIII.A).** Preparation noncontextuality implies outcome
  determinism for every sharp measurement. The proof uses every density
  operator's membership in some decomposition of I/d. So Kochen–Specker
  proofs refute universal noncontextuality.
- **Measurement noncontextuality alone is consistent with quantum theory
  (§VIII.B).** The Beltrametti–Bugajski model shows it: "one cannot
  confine all the contextuality into the measurements" (§IX).
- **Limits it states itself.** Exact equivalence may never hold under
  finite precision (§IX). That classical theories are always noncontextual
  is asserted in a footnote.
