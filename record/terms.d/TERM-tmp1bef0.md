# TERM-tmp1bef0: geodesic and orbit, of a route through the record's views: a geodesic takes a reader from an accessible problem to a deeper result in few conceptual transitions; an orbit returns to its starting question with the reader equipped to understand it differently

## The definition

A61: "A **geodesic** takes the reader from an accessible problem to a
deeper result through a relatively short sequence of conceptual
transitions. An **orbit** returns to an earlier question, but with the
reader now equipped to understand it differently."

A61 names four kinds of route a generator might seek: the "Shortest
explanatory route: minimize the number and difficulty of conceptual
prerequisites"; the "High-surprise route: maximize the distance between
the reader's starting topic and the eventual payoff, while retaining
support at each step"; the "Critical orbit: begin with a claim, pass
through its limitations or objections, then return to a narrower and more
defensible version"; and the "Reflexive orbit: begin with a practical
tool or institution, examine its underlying theory, and return to
evaluate the tool itself".

## What it is a route through

The views over the record ([TERM-tmpjn06u](TERM-tmpjn06u.md)'s hyperarticles, the record's
manuscripts and generated indexes), crossing them by the entries they
share. A route is the reader's, not the record's: it is published, not
maintained as part of the argument.

## What it does not fix

A metric. "Short" and "distance" need one, and A61 concedes the routes are
"not yet computed". [LEAD-tmpx9p54](../leads.d/LEAD-tmpx9p54.md) proposes one. The critical orbit is the
record's dialectic chain read as a route (objection, then revision); an
orbit's return "changed" is formally a loop with nontrivial holonomy, an
analogy [CLAIM-tmp6xzjy](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp6xzjy.md) notes and does not rest on.
