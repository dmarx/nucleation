# NOTE-tmp4gf0a: van der Weele (2021) — Four Paradigm Cases of Dependency in Care Relations

## Contribution

As a stand-in: it restates Kittay's dependency work, the dependency worker, and the step from dyadic care to socially organized responsibility. It does not restate the 1995 article's analysis of the Family and Medical Leave Act.

## Key results

1. p. 340: Kittay "takes as her paradigm case of dependency a situation of complete and unidirectional dependency".
2. p. 346: "If care no longer flows naturally from dependency, it becomes incumbent to organize it socially."
3. p. 347, quoting Kittay 2001: "To advocate for my daughter without also advocating for those who are entrusted with her well-being is at once unjust and uncaring toward the caregiver".
4. p. 347: "For these writers, care becomes a social responsibility in addition to a moral demand on individuals."

## Assumptions

Paradigm cases shape theories (after Nauta); Ruddick's triadic relation of worker, charge and provider.

## Concepts

Paradigm case; inevitable and temporary dependency; dependency worker; the dependency critique of liberalism; triadic care relations.
