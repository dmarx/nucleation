# LIT-508: A mutualistic approach to morality: The evolution of fairness by partner choice

Nicolas Baumard, Jean-Baptiste André, Dan Sperber (2013), *Behavioral and
Brain Sciences* 36(1), 59–78; open peer commentary and authors' Response,
78–122 — DOI-10.1017/S0140525X11002202

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 in the morality-as-cooperation batch ([ADR-018](../decisions.d/ADR-018.md)). No
anthology topic holds it. It carries no instruction for machine-learning
practice; its economic games are human experiments.

The record already holds the later work of two of its authors: Fitouchi,
André and Baumard's moral disciplining ([LIT-101](LIT-101.md), read in [NOTE-132](../notes.d/NOTE-132.md)), which
extends the same mutualistic programme to puritanical norms. The Response
here (R4.4) is the first sketch of that move. It answers the Haidt,
Koller and Dias chicken case by arguing that sexual restraint is a
contribution to a monogamous arrangement, so promiscuity is free-riding.
[NOTE-132](../notes.d/NOTE-132.md) reports how far [LIT-101](LIT-101.md) took the argument.

**Its bearing on morality-as-cooperation**, set out in [NOTE-407](../notes.d/NOTE-407.md):

- **Same premise, opposite cut.** Both theories place morality in the
  evolution of cooperation and both use game theory. Morality-as-
  cooperation ([LIT-047](LIT-047.md), read in [NOTE-034](../notes.d/NOTE-034.md)) counts seven kinds of
  cooperation as moral domains, including helping kin and helping one's
  group. This paper counts one, fair distribution, and says kin altruism and
  friendship are "not intrinsically moral" because they rest on
  favouritism (p. 60). [LIT-047](LIT-047.md)'s seven are, on this view, mostly
  non-moral dispositions that are moralized by fairness where they are
  moralized at all.
- **Fairness is [LIT-047](LIT-047.md)'s rarest domain.** In [LIT-047](LIT-047.md)'s sixty societies,
  "dividing disputed resources" was observed in 9 (15%), against 47 for
  kin and 54 for property. That is awkward for this paper, whose whole of
  morality it is. [LIT-047](LIT-047.md)'s narrow coding of fairness may explain it, as
  [NOTE-034](../notes.d/NOTE-034.md) notes.
- **Property.** The Response says mere possession is not moral and becomes
  a right only by mutual advantage (R3.4). Tummolini, Scorolli and Borghi's
  commentary argues the opposite, the view [LIT-047](LIT-047.md) takes: respect for
  possession is a separate adaptation.
- **Purity and harm.** The commentaries put the objections Bloom later put
  to [LIT-047](LIT-047.md). The Response answers in two voices, because André and
  Baumard favour a broad view (fairness regulates every moralized norm)
  and Sperber a narrow one (purity and in-group norms rest on other
  evolved dispositions and are not moral proper) (R4).

Graham et al. 2013 ([LIT-499](LIT-499.md)) and the MFQ-2 paper ([LIT-500](LIT-500.md)) cite
it as fairness monism.

[NOTE-407](../notes.d/NOTE-407.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-02, and it placed the work:
**Active**. With its commentaries it is the most complete debate in this
batch on whether cooperation exhausts morality. Its own evidence fits the
theory without discriminating it from rivals, as Kirkby, Hinzen and Mikhail
argue, and its key formal result lives in another paper.
