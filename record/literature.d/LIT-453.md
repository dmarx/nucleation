# LIT-453: Philosophical conceptions of the self: implications for cognitive science

Shaun Gallagher (2000), *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 4(1):14–21, January
2000 — DOI 10.1016/S1364-6613(99)01417-5. Commented on by Cole, Sacks and
Waterman, "On the immunity principle: a view from a robot", *TiCS* 4(5):167
(DOI 10.1016/S1364-6613(00)01459-5), with Gallagher's reply, *TiCS*
4(5):167–168 (DOI 10.1016/S1364-6613(00)01475-3); neither was read.

**On the citation.** The brief's details are all right: author, title,
journal, 4(1), pp. 14–21, 2000.

## Key takeaways

- **Two concepts of self.** The "minimal self" is "a self devoid of
  temporal extension", "limited to that which is accessible to immediate
  and present self-consciousness". The "narrative self" is "extended in
  time to include memories of the past and intentions toward the future".
- **Ownership and agency.** In voluntary action the sense that it is my
  body moving (ownership) and the sense that I am causing it (agency)
  coincide; in involuntary movement they come apart. Ownership is
  ecological, from sensory feedback; agency comes from what "precedes
  action", a forward model comparing efference copy with intention.
- **Misidentification and Frith's comparator.** Thought
  insertion is a candidate exception to immunity to error through
  misidentification (Campbell). On Frith's model it is a failure of
  forward monitoring: the thought is in my stream (ownership) but not felt
  as self-generated (agency). If so, the mechanism of the failure is the
  mechanism of the immunity.
- **Embodied or reflective minimal self.** Neonatal imitation (Meltzoff
  and Moore) suggests an embodied, pre-conceptual self from birth.
  Strawson's reflective "bare locus of consciousness", momentary and
  without history, is the other direction; Tani's robot builds a momentary
  "self" that arises only when its top-down and bottom-up dynamics
  conflict (Box 1).
- **Narrative self, two models.** Dennett's is an abstract "center of
  narrative gravity". Gallagher, extending Ricoeur, proposes the self as
  "the sum total of its narratives", "decentered, distributed and
  multiplex", which allows conflict and self-deception and fits
  Gazzaniga's left-hemisphere "interpreter".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request to fill out the record's
coverage of selfhood, as the paper that set the minimal/narrative
distinction in cognitive science. No anthology topic holds it, it carries
no instruction for machine-learning practice, and the anthology does not
hold it.

[NOTE-377](../notes.d/NOTE-377.md) is the reading, and it placed the work: **Active**. It is a
review of positions and models, not an argument for one; its value is the
taxonomy and the ownership/agency split, which later empirical work on the
sense of agency took up. Its own positive proposal is brief.

Where it meets the record:

- **Dennett** ([LIT-442](LIT-442.md)). The review restates the centre of narrative
  gravity accurately (an "abstract and movable point where the various
  stories ... meet up") and proposes its alternative against it.
- **Damasio.** The concluding remarks rest on Damasio's 1999 core and
  autobiographical selves, the book behind the views the record holds as
  [LIT-387](LIT-387.md) and [LIT-388](LIT-388.md). Gallagher reads Damasio as saying that neuroscience
  can offer "at best, informed speculation" on how the two relate.
- **Machines.** Box 1 and the published commentary "a view from a robot"
  put robot selves in the record's self cluster in 2000. Tani's robot has
  a self only when its models conflict. That is a machine self-model of
  the kind Minsky ([LIT-432](LIT-432.md)) and Metzinger ([LIT-450](LIT-450.md)) discuss, and no
  claim about experience: dropping Strawson's conscious subject is the
  price Gallagher names.
- **Strawson** ([LIT-452](LIT-452.md)). The minimal self here is Strawson's
  earlier metaphysics; "Against Narrativity" is its phenomenology.
