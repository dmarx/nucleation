# LIT-tmpcsgn6: Morality as Cooperation: A Problem-Centred Approach

Oliver Scott Curry (2016), in T. K. Shackelford and R. D. Hansen (eds.), *The
Evolution of Morality*, Evolutionary Psychology series, Springer, pp. 27–51 —
DOI-10.1007/978-3-319-19671-8_2

## Key takeaways

- Morality is identified with the solutions to recurrent problems of
  cooperation, so there is one moral domain per type of cooperation game:
  kin allocation, coordination, exchange, hawk–dove contests (two domains),
  division and possession.
- The chapter's testable predictions (pp. 39–40) include two the later tests
  never ran: non-cooperative behaviour will be judged morally neutral, and no
  culture's morality will be about something other than cooperation.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, to fill out the record's
coverage of morality-as-cooperation ([ADR-018](../decisions.d/ADR-018.md)). This is the statement the
whole programme cites as "Curry 2016". The record already held its main
empirical test, Curry, Mullins and Whitehouse's sixty-society coding
([LIT-047](LIT-047.md)), and the reading of that test ([NOTE-034](../notes.d/NOTE-034.md)) judged it weakly
diagnostic.

[NOTE-tmppnzy8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmppnzy8.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-02, and it placed the work
**Proposed**. It matters to the record for one reason above the rest. It
states the falsifying prediction in its own words: "behaviour that has
nothing to do with cooperation … will be regarded as morally neutral", and
"there will be no cultures where morality is about something other than
cooperation — say, aesthetics or nutrition" (p. 40). [LIT-047](LIT-047.md)'s design coded
only the seven cooperative behaviours, so it could not test this. The
criticism in [NOTE-034](../notes.d/NOTE-034.md) that no comparison domain was coded is therefore not a
complaint from outside: it is this chapter's own prediction, left unexamined
by the test meant to support it. The same page also says religious morality,
food taboos included, "will turn out to be" cooperative, which is the move
Bloom's falsifiability question in [NOTE-034](../notes.d/NOTE-034.md) is aimed at.

The questionnaire that followed ([LIT-tmp0moou](LIT-tmp0moou.md)), the combinatorial extension
([LIT-tmpps55m](LIT-tmpps55m.md)) and the machine-read replication ([LIT-tmpvrv55](LIT-tmpvrv55.md)) all start from
this chapter's seven domains. Fitouchi, André and Baumard's "Moral
disciplining" ([LIT-101](LIT-101.md)) is the record's strongest cooperative account of the
purity norms this chapter sets aside as having "no apparent connection to
cooperation" (p. 42).
