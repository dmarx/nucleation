# THEORY-154: Whether an expected tangible reward lowers or raises later intrinsic motivation depends on how tightly its stated contingency ties it to performance and success

## Source

Cameron, Banko & Pierce (2001), [LIT-562](../literature.d/LIT-562.md), read in [NOTE-587](../notes.d/NOTE-587.md): the
procedural coding (Table 1, p. 12), the results by contingency (pp. 16–20,
Figures 1–4), the discussion (pp. 21–26) and Appendix B.

## What was actually shown

Cameron, Banko and Pierce re-coded each study by what participants were told
the reward was for, in seven categories, instead of in cognitive evaluation
theory's four. On interesting tasks, expected tangible rewards then split.

**What undermines free choice (pp. 16–17):**
- rewards offered for doing the task: K = 57, −0.35 before outliers, still
  negative after them;
- rewards offered for doing well, with no standard stated;
- rewards offered per unit solved, but only where some participants earned
  less than the maximum: −0.22 (K = 14), against −0.03 (K = 6) where all
  earned it in full (pp. 19–20).

**What does not:**
- rewards unrelated to the task;
- rewards for finishing;
- rewards for surpassing a score.

**What enhances free choice:** rewards for meeting or exceeding others'
performance. On self-reported interest, every contingency except
task-noncontingent rewards and rewards for doing the task was positive
(p. 18).

The authors' summary is that "positive effects are obtained when the rewards
are explicitly tied to performance standards and to success; negative
effects are produced when rewards signify failure or are loosely tied to
behavior" (p. 23). They read it through social cognitive theory: rewards tied
to a standard the person meets raise self-efficacy, and with it interest and
time on task (p. 26).

This could have come out otherwise. Cognitive evaluation theory predicts that
performance-contingent rewards are on balance controlling and undermine. Had
the score- and norm-based categories come out negative, as Deci, Koestner and
Ryan's pooled performance-contingent category did ([NOTE-438](../notes.d/NOTE-438.md)), this account
would have failed on the paper's own data.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say rewards are harmless.** Rewards for doing an interesting
  task undermine free choice on this analysis as on its rival's. That effect
  is [THEORY-046](THEORY-046.md), and this account includes it as its "loosely tied" case.
- **It does not show the mechanism.** Self-efficacy and competence are
  inferred from which contingencies help; nothing in the paper measures
  them.
- **It does not isolate the key contrast.** Control groups told the
  performance objective and given feedback were pooled with those that were
  not (p. 13). The comparison that separates this account from CET, a
  performance-tied reward against a control given the same feedback, is
  reported only from Eisenberger, Pierce and Cameron (1999, [LIT-754](../literature.d/LIT-754.md)),
  which is filed but unread.
- **It does not establish the size of the positive effects.** The
  per-contingency values after outliers are in figures with no text layer,
  and the authors call every effect small (p. 25).
- **It does not cover low-interest tasks**, where rewards raised free choice
  on this analysis (0.28). That is a separate claim.

## Connections

- **Rivals [THEORY-049](THEORY-049.md).** Cognitive evaluation theory explains the same
  reward effects by whether an event is felt as controlling or as
  information about competence, and a strict reading predicts that
  performance-contingent rewards undermine. The paper's finding that
  "rewards specifically tied to level of performance (surpassing a score,
  exceeding others) do not undermine measures of intrinsic motivation is
  incompatible with the claims of these theories" ([LIT-562](../literature.d/LIT-562.md), p. 26). The
  authors allow that CET could be modified to let competence information
  override control, so this is a rival rather than a correction. Neither
  account has the mediation test that would settle it.
- **[THEORY-046](THEORY-046.md)** is the effect both accounts explain, and on which both
  meta-analyses agree.
