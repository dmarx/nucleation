# LIT-080: Understanding brain states across spacetime informed by whole-brain modelling

Jakub Vohryzek, Joana Cabral, Peter Vuust, Gustavo Deco, Morten L. Kringelbach (2022), *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society A 380 (2022), art. 20210247; theme issue "Emergent phenomena in complex physical and socio-technical systems: from cells to societies"* — DOI-10.1098/rsta.2021.0247

## Key takeaways

- The authors argue that healthy waking brain states sit in a metastable regime between order and disorder. On this view, depression is an overly rigid landscape with dominant attractors and psychedelics flatten the landscape. Whole-brain models, meaning connectome-coupled nonlinear oscillators near a Hopf bifurcation, are the mechanistic tool for explaining these spatiotemporal signatures and for finding perturbations that move between states.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Adaptive behaviour depends on whole-brain activity patterns that balance stability (order) with enough instability for flexibility, and this balance over space and time defines distinct brain states. Depression shows overly rigid, ordered dynamics, while psychedelics produce more disordered and sometimes over-flexible states. The review surveys neuroimaging and whole-brain modelling work that uses dynamical-systems principles to characterize these states. It suggests that the resulting spatiotemporal descriptions may eventually inform treatments that rebalance brain states in disease.
