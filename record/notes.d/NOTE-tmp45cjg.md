# NOTE-tmp45cjg: 49 U.S.C. § 1154 — the bar on NTSB reports in damages actions

## Contribution

The legal footing of the NTSB's separation from liability. The Board
determines probable cause and contributing factors, and the law keeps that
determination out of the suits for damages that follow, so that the
Board's finding is not a finding of liability.

## Key results

- **§ 1154(b)**, "Reports": "No part of a report of the Board, related to
  an accident or an investigation of an accident, may be admitted into
  evidence or used in a civil action for damages resulting from a matter
  mentioned in the report."
- **Sources** (Historical and Revision Notes): § 1154(b) restates § 701(e)
  of the Federal Aviation Act of 1958 (Pub. L. 85–726), § 6(d) of
  Pub. L. 89–670 (1966) and § 304(c)
  of the Independent Safety Board Act of 1974 (Pub. L. 93–633); "the words
  "civil action" are substituted for "suit or action"".
- **Which report** (49 CFR 835.2): a "Board accident report" is "the
  report containing the Board's determinations, including the probable
  cause of an accident"; a "factual accident report", the investigator's
  results, is different: "The Board does not object to, and there is no
  statutory bar to, admission in litigation of factual accident reports."
- **Why** (49 CFR 835.3(a)): the sections reflect Congress' "strong * * *
  desire to keep the Board free of the entanglement of such suits", and
  "serve to ensure that the Board does not exert an undue influence on
  litigation." Board employees may testify only to facts, never "expert or
  opinion testimony" (835.3(b)).
- **No blame** (49 CFR 831.4(c)): "NTSB investigations are fact-finding
  proceedings with no adverse parties ... not conducted for the purpose of
  determining the rights, liabilities, or blame of any person or entity, as
  they are not adjudicatory proceedings."
- **The Tempe report** (LIT-2664, notice page) quotes an earlier wording
  of 831.4, without "blame", says "The NTSB does not assign fault or blame
  for an accident or incident", and cites § 1154(b). Its recommendations
  (p. 60) go to NHTSA, the state of Arizona, the American Association of
  Motor Vehicle Administrators and Uber ATG; none goes to the operator.

## Assumptions

Federal law as currently codified; the regulations as the LII gives them.
How courts read the bar, and whether it is ever pierced, is not covered.

## Concepts

- **Board accident report** against **factual accident report** (835.2).
- **Probable cause** and contributing factors: the Board's determinations,
  which the bar covers.

## Bearing on the record

Filed as THEORY-tmpq12rp.

- **The lead's citation, checked.** The lead filer recalled "the statute
  barring NTSB reports from civil litigation" as 49 U.S.C. § 1154(b). The
  section is right. The recollection is broader than the text in two ways:
  the bar covers the Board's report of its determinations, not the factual
  record, and it reaches civil actions for damages, not civil litigation
  generally. Nothing read mentions criminal proceedings.
- **CASE-292** and **CLAIM-tmpx4kjt.** The NTSB that traced the Tempe
  operator's distraction to Uber's safety culture and Arizona's oversight is
  no-blame by regulation and insulated from damages suits by statute. Its
  probable cause cannot be used to win damages from anyone it names. That
  is the institutional condition under which CLAIM-tmpx4kjt says
  provenance can be carried without blame. Grounds.
- **QUESTION-476.** The statutory model of the question's first answer
  ("keeps correction and provenance"): a body that traces cause upstream
  and recommends repair there, with its findings fenced off from
  liability.

## Limitations

- Statute and regulations only; no case law, and nothing on how often
  litigants use the factual reports instead.
- It shows the separation in law, not its effect on what investigations
  find.
