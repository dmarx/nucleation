# NOTE-tmp1ov5o: Nahas & Sachs — What's at stake in naturalizing teleology

## Contribution

Recent teleological naturalism treats organisms as intrinsically goal-directed. The authors argue that both its supporters and its critics have missed that the view is used to address quite different problems. One problem is biological: whether an organism-centred account of teleological ascriptions would improve biological descriptions and explanations. The other is philosophical: how naturalized teleology would change our conception of nature and of ourselves as natural beings. Neglecting this metatheoretical difference has produced confusion, which the paper tries to clear up.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §2: surveys organizational approaches (Kantian self-organisation, organizational closure, e.g. Mossio & Bich, Deacon's teleodynamics) and behavioural or ecological approaches (Walsh's organisms-as-agents, with goals, affordances and repertoires).
- §3.1–3.2: two motivations — the perceived limits of the Modern Synthesis, and the philosophical debates over naturalism.
- §3.3: a philosophical account need not be operationalisable for biologists; scientific models trade truth for tractability, so an account's biological usefulness and its metaphysical correctness can come apart.
- §4.1–4.2: applies this to the demarcation of teleological systems from dissipative structures, and argues García-Valdecasas's (2021) critique of the Organizational Approach in favour of Deacon makes the same metatheoretical slip.
- §5: suggests a plurality of teleology accounts suited to different contexts — Deacon for the naturalism debate; Walsh and the Organizational Approach for advancing biology.
