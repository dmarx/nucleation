# NOTE-146: Winkielman et al. — The coherent and fluent mind

## Contribution

Philosophical accounts often hold that one function of consciousness is to produce a unified subjective experience that links separate perceptual streams. Standard explanations locate this unity in integration of specific stimulus features at perceptual or decisional levels. The authors propose an additional route that runs through processing dynamics. Different modalities affect a common, global experience of processing quality, and that integrated feeling then feeds decisions about its source and relevance. The account therefore combines "experiential" and "decisional" processes.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Background: classic cross-modal effects (McGurk, double-flash, bouba/kiki, synaesthesia) concern representational content. The authors propose influences via general representational quality, going back to James's "fringe consciousness" (feelings of familiarity, rightness, coherence).
- Evidence: fluency and familiarity misattribution across formats and modalities (Jacoby et al. 1988; Whittlesea et al. 1990; Curran & Dien 2003). In Ziembowicz et al. (2013), unrelated coherent background patterns in one modality made targets in another modality seem more "regular", "familiar" and "possible".
- The authors concede they lack direct evidence that the integration is experiential and causal. They cite self-reports, physiological markers of fluency-linked positivity, and the disappearance of effects when a person is given an explanation that lets them discount the feeling (misattribution paradigms).
- Mechanism: global, non-specific, "free-floating" quality signals like those in connectionist models. The example is a Hopfield network with a feedback-controlled "order parameter" (Lewenstein & Nowak 1989), which reproduces mere-exposure asymmetries. The authors link this to global prediction error (Friston).
- Open questions: whether blending is pre-experiential or experiential, and whether fused experiences keep information about their origin. Integration can make the world seem more, or less, unified than it is.

## Open questions

- Offers a content-free "global quality signal" as a unifying mechanism, a candidate analogue to confidence or coherence signals in multimodal models. That is an analogy only; the paper does not discuss ML.
- The philosophical claim (unity via processing experience) goes beyond the evidence, which the authors admit. Check follow-up work testing the experiential-causal link.
- Check how this sits against Bayne's phenomenal-unity thesis, which the paper cites but does not engage in detail.
