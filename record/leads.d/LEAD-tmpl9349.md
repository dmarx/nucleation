# LEAD-tmpl9349: Build four systems matched on the ordinary course, mere coupling, unilateral dependence, coordinated adaptation and maintained conditional commitment, and test whether the probes for implicit contracts part them as predicted

## What it would do

Build four systems of two or three processes, matched on the ordinary
course (the same behaviour when nothing goes wrong):

1. **Mere coupling**: the assistant's two controllers sharing a room, with a
   deadband and a fixed rule that heating yields ([CLAIM-tmp7hqtl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp7hqtl.md)'s note).
2. **Unilateral dependence**: A20's dependency model ([CLAIM-tmp18v1o](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp18v1o.md)).
3. **Coordinated adaptation**: two processes that each give up a capacity
   the other supplies.
4. **Maintained conditional commitment**: the arbitration rules of
   [CASE-tmptpna1](../cases.d/CASE-tmptpna1.md), with a mechanism that revises them.

Apply [CLAIM-tmpersgx](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpersgx.md)'s six probes to each: interruption, a substitute
provider, competing demands, removal of an enforcer, withdrawal of a
discretionary contribution, a request to renegotiate.

## Expectations, stated before any run

- Under interruption, mere coupling and unilateral dependence show no
  correction, only a fall in functioning.
- Coordinated adaptation shows both parties' functioning fall.
- Maintained conditional commitment recruits correction toward its terms.

## What would count against

- The probes do not part the four systems, or part them as well on the
  ordinary course.
- The two controllers respond to a change of terms with the processes kept
  exactly as a maintained commitment does. [CLAIM-tmp7hqtl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp7hqtl.md)'s filter would
  then have no test behind it.

## Note of 2026-11-24: the first expectation corrected

The manuscript review of 2026-11-24 found that the first expectation
contradicts its chosen instance. The two controllers meet [CLAIM-tmp7hqtl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp7hqtl.md)'s
second mark, differentiated responses to satisfaction and violation, so
under interruption they do respond: by their fixed rule. Corrected
expectation for mere coupling: under interruption it responds by its fixed
rule only, and that response does not change when the terms are changed
with the processes kept. Unilateral dependence shows a fall in functioning
and no correction. What parts mere coupling from maintained commitment is
then the change-of-terms probe, which is [CLAIM-tmp7hqtl](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmp7hqtl.md)'s proposed fifth
condition. The wording is the assistant's (Claude, in this session).


## Note of 2026-11-24: what the record already does, from U32 and A51 of the exchange 'Memes, Contracts, and Systemic Normativity'

A51 §3 does not take this study up by name, but its method is held: arrangements matched on their ordinary trajectories that part under intervention ([CASE-161](../cases.d/CASE-161.md), Active), and diagnostic probes that identify which of a known catalogue of mechanisms applies and predict withheld interventions ([CASE-163](../cases.d/CASE-163.md)). What is not held is the catalogue this lead needs, the four systems, and A51 §7 keeps the question open ("What differentiates implicit contracting from regulated co-adaptation? ... their proposed contractual integration needs a discriminating criterion"). Reframe the lead as [CASE-163](../cases.d/CASE-163.md)'s design run on these four systems: the same probe battery, a predictor fitted on one system and tested on the others, and identification accuracy against no probe as the measure, which also gives "What would count against" a number. The owner's U32 holds that the series' proposed experiments are "largely already satisfied by prior work" ([CLAIM-tmpyp0ju](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpyp0ju.md)). The note is the assistant's (Claude, in this session); whether to narrow, lower or close the lead is the owner's call. No status change.

  governing rule while keeping the processes, which is the shape of the
  change-of-terms probe (CLAIM-tmp7hqtl's note: "change the terms, keep
  the processes, and see whether behaviour follows the terms"). They test
  what restores the rule, not whether behaviour follows changed terms, so
  the probe is not done; CASE-161's design is the nearest held one, and
  the probe's arm here can be built on it.
