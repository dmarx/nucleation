# LIT-tmph5vta: Ironies of automation

Bainbridge, L. (1983). Ironies of automation. *Automatica* 19(6): 775–779. doi:10.1016/0005-1098(83)90046-8.

## Key takeaways

*Unread. Nothing here is drawn from the article, and nothing is put in
Bainbridge's name from her own words.*

- What the article is usually cited for is in the summary, in Ahn's
  paraphrase (LIT-tmp09gwf), unverified until read.
- Her remedies (practice, simulators, keeping the operator in the loop, as
  the reading brief guessed) are attributed to her by none of the three
  neighbours read, and are not recorded as hers.
- No neighbour gives a rate of skill decay under automation. The rates the
  record holds are Tatel's (LIT-2079), which CLAIM-558 already cites.

## Standing in the record

Registered for the gap between support that does the work and the party's
own capacity: here the support withdraws itself at its own failure, at the
hardest case, so the moment of withdrawal is chosen by the support and not
by the party's readiness. That is a different regime from the planned exit
of CLAIM-600, and a sharper case of the order CLAIM-602 states.

The neighbours stand in poorly for her text:

- **Ahn (2025)**, LIT-tmp09gwf, is the closest. He restates three ironies
  and the out-of-the-loop problem in his own words, quotes no sentence of
  hers, and gives no page references. Two short phrases he puts in quotation
  marks, "deskilling" and "worst possible time", could not be located in her
  text, which was not read. His remedies are his own.
- **Simkute, Tankelevitch et al. (2024)**, LIT-tmpaw2id, cite her for the
  general irony, that "the more advanced an automated system is, the more
  important the human operator may be", and for workflow restructuring.
  Their finding that automation makes easy tasks easier and hard ones harder is credited to others, not to
  her. Nothing on skill.
- **Macnamara et al. (2024)**, LIT-tmpbg80l, do not cite her. They carry the
  skill-decay irony and the aviation evidence on it, reporting Casner et al.
  (2014) second-hand.

When a lawful copy is reached, the reading should check her wording against
Ahn's paraphrase, and say whether she gives remedies and numbers.
