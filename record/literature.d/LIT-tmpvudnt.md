# LIT-tmpvudnt: Meaning

H. P. Grice (1957), *The Philosophical Review* 66(3):377–388 —
DOI-10.2307/2182440

## Key takeaways

- **Two senses of "mean".**
  - Natural meaning, "Those spots mean (meant) measles": x means that p entails p,
    and nobody meant anything by it.
  - Non-natural meaning (meaningNN), "Those three rings on the bell (of
    the bus) mean that the 'bus is full'": it does not entail p, and it
    supports a conclusion that somebody meant by x that p.
- **The causal theory fails.** Stevenson's account, a tendency to produce
  an attitude dependent on conditioning, lets the tail coat mean a dance.
  Saved by "in communication", it is circular. It also gives no account of
  what a speaker means on one occasion. Grice takes that occasion meaning
  to be fundamental.
- **The analysis, in three steps.**
  - An intention to induce a belief is not enough: the handkerchief left at
    the scene of a murder means nothing.
  - An intention that the audience recognise that intention is not enough
    either: Herod showing Salome the head, the child letting its mother see
    how pale it is.
  - The recognition must play a part in producing the effect. Showing Mr.
    X a photograph means nothing, because the photograph would convince him
    if left by accident. Drawing him a picture means something.
- **The general form.** "A meantNN something by x" is roughly "A intended
  the utterance of x to produce some effect in an audience by means of the
  recognition of this intention". The effect can be an action as well as a
  belief: pointing to the door, a policeman waving.
- **Insult.** Cutting someone in the street counts as meaningNN, because
  "I could not reasonably expect him to be distressed … unless he
  recognized my intention to affect him in this way". Failing a candidate
  does not, even when the distress is intended and meant to be recognised.
- **Intentions are read publicly.** "An utterer is held to intend to convey
  what is normally conveyed". Context and relevance to an evident purpose
  decide between candidate intentions.
