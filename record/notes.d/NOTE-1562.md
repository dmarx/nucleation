# NOTE-1562: Freidlin & Wentzell — Random Perturbations of Dynamical Systems, front matter

*Only the front matter (pp. i–xxviii) was read. Every statement below is
from the prefaces or the Introduction's outline; none is a theorem checked
in the body.*

## Contribution

By the authors' account, "This book was the first monograph in which
large-deviation theory for stochastic processes was presented." (Preface
to the Second Edition, p. vii). The quasi-potential is their notion,
"which we introduced more than forty years ago." (p. v), and they note
that "many of them were later rediscovered in applied papers." (p. v).
The book develops limit theorems for a deterministic system ẋ = b(x)
perturbed by small noise, either ẋ = b(x, εψ) with a random process ψ or
white noise εσ(x)ẇ, and uses them for exit problems, the limit of the
stationary distribution, averaging and stability under random
perturbations.

## Key insight

Small noise changes long-run behaviour qualitatively, and what changes it
is rare events. The Introduction admits the oddity: "It may seem that such
digging into extremely rare events contradicts the general spirit of
probability theory, which ignores events of small probability." (p. xix).
Yet ranking which unlikely events on a finite interval are more and less
improbable "serves as a key to the question of what the behavior, with
probability close to 1, of the process" will be on an infinite interval
(p. xix).

## Assumptions

- **Small perturbations.** All results are asymptotic as the noise
  parameter ε (or h) goes to zero.
- **Rough asymptotics only.** "Furthermore, in this book we only consider
  rough results on large deviations," that is, up to logarithmic
  equivalence (p. xxiv); the authors argue one can deduce more interesting
  rough consequences from rough theorems than sharp ones from sharp
  theorems (p. xxv).
- **Cramér-type conditions**, exponential moments, for the large-deviation
  results (p. xxiv).
- **No random vector fields.** The book does "not consider at all
  dynamical systems generated by random vector fields." (p. ix).
- **Weak dependence** of the perturbing process across distant time
  intervals, in the Introduction's argument for an exponential exit time
  (p. xx).

## Key results

As outlined in the front matter; exact statements and proofs were not read.

- **Three kinds of limit theorem (Introduction).** Law of large numbers
  (convergence to the unperturbed trajectory), central-limit (Gaussian
  fluctuations of order ε), and rough large deviations, the main subject.
- **The action functional (p. xx, eq. 16).** P{ρ(Xᵋ, ϕ) < δ} ≈
  exp{−ε⁻²S(ϕ)}; its value "characterizes the difficulty of the passage
  of" the process near ϕ, and as ε → 0 "only the summand with smallest
  S(ϕ) becomes essential." So the constant in a rare-event asymptotics is
  an infimum of S over the paths making up the event. For white noise S is
  ½∫ a_ij(ϕ)(ϕ̇ⁱ − bⁱ)(ϕ̇ʲ − bʲ) dt (p. xxiii). Precise form in Chapter 4
  §5, not read.
- **Exit time (p. xx).** Splitting time into intervals of length T, each
  carries almost the same small exit probability, so the first exit time
  "has an approximately exponential distribution", with its rate set by
  the infimum of S over exiting paths. Chapter 4, not read.
- **Which stable set (p. xxi).** With two stable sets K₁, K₂, passage
  probabilities of order exp{−V₁₂ε⁻²} and exp{−V₂₁ε⁻²}, and V₁₂ < V₂₁,
  "then it becomes plausible that for small ε the process spends most of
  the time in the neighborhood of" K₂, so the stationary distribution
  converges to a measure on K₂; the V's are infima of S over connecting
  paths. Without the large-deviation analysis the outcome is unclear: "it
  depends on the exact form of small perturbations." (p. xxi). Chapter 6,
  not read.
- **Metastability (Preface to the Third Edition, p. v).** "the notion of
  sub-limiting distribution for a given initial point and a time scale is
  identical to the notion of metastability." Stochastic resonance is
  called a manifestation of it.
- **Stability (p. xxviii).** Chapter 10 is said to "introduce a certain
  numerical characteristic of stability" tied to the action functional,
  and optimal stabilization. Not read.

## Concepts

- **Result of small random perturbations**: a family of processes
  converging in probability to the unperturbed solution as h ↓ 0 (p. xxiii).
- **Action functional S(ϕ)**: as above; it measures how hard it is for the
  process to pass near ϕ (p. xx).
- **Rough (logarithmic) equivalence**: A_h and B_h are equivalent when
  ln A_h ∼ ln B_h (p. xxv).
- **Quasi-potential, cycles, metastability**: named in the prefaces and
  contents (Chapter 4 §3; Chapter 6 §6); defined in chapters not read.

## Connections

The book places itself after Cramér's large deviations for sums of random
variables (Introduction) and after Pontrjagin, Andronov and Vitt's
Fokker–Planck approach to the stationary distribution, which it replaces
by the study of improbable events (p. xxi). Kuehn ([LIT-2048](../literature.d/LIT-2048.md)) cites the
1998 edition for its escape time. Berglund ([LIT-2037](../literature.d/LIT-2037.md)), read as the
stand-in, restates the exit-problem results.

## Limitations

- **Only the front matter was read.** No theorem statement was checked.
- **The authors' own caveats.** Some topics "have not yet taken their final
  form" and part of the material is given in survey form (p. x); the
  results are rough only (p. xxiv); there are no random vector fields
  (p. ix).
- **No prefactors.** By design the book gives logarithmic asymptotics, not
  sharp times; [LIT-2037](../literature.d/LIT-2037.md) records how much later work that took.
