# CLAIM-tmpa2l21: Wrongfully structuring another person's circumstances so that they are pressured, induced or conditioned to commit a wrong is a wrong of its own, committed against that person as well as against those they then harm, whether or not they comply; it does not take over their culpability for the act but stands beside it, and it is limited by how much control, knowledge and exploitation the structuring involved

## The claim

A9 §1 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b)
works through a manager and an employee asked to falsify records: ordinary
wrongdoing, coerced wrongdoing, and "Manufactured dependency", where the
manager has for years made the employee dependent and then asks. A
distinguishes "**First-order wrongdoing:** Doing something impermissible"
from "**Second-order wrongdoing:** Wrongfully structuring another person's
circumstances so that they are pressured, incentivized, or conditioned to
commit the first-order wrong", and says "I'm using these as analytical
labels, rather than as the names of a universally accepted philosophical
taxonomy."

Four parts:

- **It is wrong without success.** "**Creating a coercive moral trap can
  itself be wrong, whether or not the trap succeeds.**"
- **It is not zero-sum.** A9 §4: "responsibility isn't necessarily
  zero-sum." A salesperson lies to a customer under a manager's deceptive
  incentive scheme; the salesperson wrongs the customer, the manager wrongs
  the customer, and "The manager may also have wronged the salesperson".
  "They may be **several distinct wrongs connected through a single
  event**." The actor's culpability may be lessened by the circumstances
  but is not transferred.
- **It wrongs the person structured.** A9's principle separates a
  distributive claim from a relational one: "deliberately compromising
  another person's moral agency may be a wrong *against that person*,
  quite apart from whatever harms subsequently occur." A11 §6 states it
  for the reflexive-control case as A's responsibility "for appropriating
  B's agency". A9 §9: the arranger may owe "acknowledgment of the dilemma
  they created, restitution for resulting losses, and changes that restore
  the other person's ability to act independently."
- **It is limited.** A9 §4 grades what A does to B: "Influences B",
  "Creates foreseeable pressure", "Exploits B's vulnerability",
  "Deliberately engineers wrongdoing". A9 §7 weighs Control, Knowledge,
  Constraint, Connection, Exploitation and Proportionality, which stop the
  regress: "If all influence generated responsibility for subsequent
  wrongdoing, personal accountability would eventually dissolve into an
  unmanageable network of causes."

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)** also names a "second-order" wrong, an organization's breach
  of a duty to keep violations knowable, contestable and correctable. That
  is a duty about correction; this is a wrong of producing another's
  wrong. The two share a word and not a content.
- **[CASE-151](../cases.d/CASE-151.md)** (Wells Fargo) is a documented arrangement of the kind: a
  sales model and performance management that the board's investigation
  traced the misconduct to, with leadership blaming individual employees.
  The claim does not rest on it; whether the report shows the relational
  wrong, against the employees, is not settled here.
- **[QUESTION-449](../questions.d/QUESTION-449.md)** asks whether it matters that another installed a
  mechanism, or only who maintains it now. A9 §3 answers for persons that
  responsibility "for the formation of the dispositions that produced it"
  can lie with someone other than the actor: "These responsibilities can
  belong partly to different people." The claim bears on the question; it
  does not answer it for organizations.
- **[CLAIM-671](CLAIM-671.md)** strengthens a responsible agent's obligations where a
  transformation increases another's vulnerability to it; this names the
  wrong of using such vulnerability to produce wrongdoing.
- [CLAIM-tmpciy9q](CLAIM-tmpciy9q.md) uses the wrong this claim names as an instrument.
- The senses of responsibility are [TERM-tmp8pnnr](../terms.d/TERM-tmp8pnnr.md)'s.

## What it does not say

- Not that an explanation excuses. A9 §5: "We have to be especially careful
  about the phrase *You made me do it.*" "An explanation involving another
  person's conduct is not automatically an excuse." "we should judge their choices in light of the choices they actually had."
- Not that responsibility is fixed. A9 §5: "**A person's responsibility can
  change as their capacity for doing otherwise changes.**"
- Not that the second-order wrong is always the worse one. A9 §8: "Not
  everyone would agree that the second wrong is categorically worse, but
  there is good reason to regard it as qualitatively different."

## Note of 2026-11-17: Strawson, Watson through King, and Miller through Shaw-Young, read: qualifications at the actor's end
<!-- inactive-ok-file: NOTE-tmpnfib1 THEORY-tmpjhphq NOTE-tmplo4r7 THEORY-tmptax6l NOTE-tmps8s9k THEORY-tmp3xr2l LIT-tmpmsh0d LIT-tmpqsyr9 — new or Unavailable; the readings filed on 2026-11-18 and the unread works two of them stand in for -->

Strawson 1962, read (NOTE-tmpnfib1, THEORY-tmpjhphq); King 2013 for Watson 1996 (NOTE-tmplo4r7, THEORY-tmptax6l; LIT-tmpmsh0d unread); Shaw-Young 2008 for Miller (NOTE-tmps8s9k, THEORY-tmp3xr2l; LIT-tmpqsyr9 unread). None writes a relation to this claim; each qualifies the actor's side, and none treats the claim's core, the arranger's wrong against the person structured.

- **Strawson** gives coercion as his first kind of plea: "'He was pushed', 'He had to do it', 'It was the only way', 'They left him no alternative'". Such pleas "do not invite us to see the agent as other than a fully responsible agent. They invite us to see the injury as one for which he was not fully, or at all, responsible" (p. 193). That fits the claim's "does not take over their culpability" only in part: on Strawson's wording coercion can remove responsibility for the act altogether ("or at all"), not only lessen it. His second kind includes "His mind has been systematically perverted", which suspends the reactive attitudes toward the agent: conditioning carried far enough exempts, a step the claim's "pressured, induced or conditioned" does not mark. The claim's limits (A9 §§4, 7) could carry it.
- **Watson, as King quotes him**: "[i]t is unfair to impose sanctions upon people unless they had a reasonable opportunity to avoid incurring them" (237). This is a route by which structured conduct stays attributable while accountability-blame is reduced. It concerns the actor and the blamer, not the arranger.
- **Miller, as Shaw-Young reports him** (Miller 2007, p. 102): "Or consider someone who under coercion injures another. If the coercer himself disappears from the scene, causal responsibility falls on to the person who has been made to inflict the injury, and with it the responsibility to care for the injured party." Miller speaks of repair, not blame, so this does not contradict the claim; but it puts the remedial burden on the structured party once the arranger is gone, which the claim's repair clause (A9 §9) does not consider. If anything in the record cites Miller's remedial responsibility for this claim's wrong, Miller as reported carries less, and the lead should file an ARG saying so; the claim's own text does not cite him.

No status changes.

## Note of 2026-11-17: Keller and Eggert read: the wrong to the person placed in a predicament, without the induced wrongdoing
<!-- inactive-ok-file: NOTE-tmp0loqu THEORY-tmpg9i9l NOTE-tmp50e3o THEORY-tmpqwuyi LIT-tmp27gbk — new or Unavailable; the readings filed on 2026-11-18 and a registered source not reached -->

Keller 2016, read (NOTE-tmp0loqu, THEORY-tmpg9i9l, corroborates). The claim's relational part, that structuring another's circumstances wrongs "that person", is Keller's for moral blackmail: "I mistreat you by placing you in a difficult moral predicament" (p. 20), and the victim's complaint is coherent: "it is morally wrong that I have this moral obligation" (p. 26). But his victim is pressed to do what is right (mind the dog, keep teaching), not to commit a wrong. So he supports the wrong to the person placed in the predicament, not the claim's case of pressure "to commit a wrong".

Eggert 2023, read (NOTE-tmp50e3o, THEORY-tmpqwuyi, no relation). Registered for this claim because A9 says she "notes that such predicaments sometimes arise precisely because one person has wrongfully created circumstances". She does, in an aside: "Paradigmatic examples of dirty hands involve someone else creating a situation in which the agent must do wrong" (p. 18), and defers whether they always do ("a discussion for another day"). Nothing in the paper concerns the arranger's wrong. What it gives the claim is the agent's side: a remainder survives even a permissible act ("a wrong action ... may be permissible under certain circumstances, without this rendering it any less wrong", p. 14), which is consistent with "stands beside it". The claim does not cite her, so no ARG is needed.
