# THEORY-tmpukudp: In three Food for Peace projects in Kenya followed 2–3 years after exit, impact at exit did not predict sustained benefit: the activities that continued had, before the project withdrew, their own resource stream, sufficient technical and managerial capacity, motivated participants and substantive linkages formed early, and had operated independently for a time, while activities that relied on project-supplied inputs until the end declined

## Source

Coates, Kegode, Galante & Blau (2016), [LIT-tmp93nq2](../literature.d/LIT-tmp93nq2.md); read in [NOTE-tmp46avq](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp46avq.md).

## What was actually shown

- **Exit impact did not predict sustainability** (p. 2): "evidence of
  impact at the time of project exit did not necessarily predict
  sustainability 2–3 years later". No count of sustained against
  unsustained results is given.
- **Supplied inputs and decline** (p. 7): "practices that did not require
  continued external resources were more likely to be sustained than
  those that did".
- **The factors together** (p. 7): "sustainability will not be achieved if
  any one factor is missing"; linkages worked best when identified early
  and integrated.
- **Withdrawal** (pp. 2, 5): gradual exit with independent operation
  before closure made continuation more likely; CARE's graduated cost
  share against ADRA's subsidy until the end, with participation holding
  in CARE's producer associations and falling in ADRA's.
- **Cases** (pp. 3–7): savings groups grew after exit with no outside
  capital; paravets on fees largely held; water committees kept
  delivering services; health workers kept serving, but less, once the
  project's support ended and the ministry's did not replace it.
- The design could have come out otherwise: the replicated endline survey
  could have shown subsidised and weaned activities holding alike.

## What this does not say

- That the factors cause sustainability: there is no comparison group,
  and drought hit ADRA's and FH's areas, not CARE's.
- That the result holds outside Kenya, or across the study's four
  countries: the synthesis is unread.
- How large the differences were: the brief gives almost no figures.
- Anything beyond 2–3 years after exit.
