# LIT-493: The Grammar of Society: The Nature and Dynamics of Social Norms

Cristina Bicchieri (2006), *Cambridge University Press (print 12 December 2005, imprint 2006; online 5 June 2012; hardback ISBN 9780521574907, paperback 9780521573726)* — DOI-10.1017/CBO9780511616037

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, as part of filling out the
record's coverage of `social-ontology` ([ADR-017](../decisions.d/ADR-017.md)).

`Deferred` because no lawful full text was reachable, so it is not read
and no NOTE is filed. What follows is the publisher's description and
contents, and what the record's read works say about it.

**What the publisher says.** The book "examines social norms, such as
fairness, cooperation, and reciprocity, in an effort to understand their
nature and dynamics, the expectations that they generate, and how they
evolve and change". It draws on social psychology, experimental economics
and evolutionary game theory to give "an integrated account of how social
norms emerge, why and when we follow them, and the situations where we
are most likely to focus on relevant norms". It examines why inefficient
norms survive and how psychological dispositions can "naturally lead
individuals to evolve fairness norms that closely resemble those we
follow in most modern societies".

**Where the record's readings place it.**

- Hindriks & Guala ([LIT-481](LIT-481.md)) list it with Lewis, Sugden, Skyrms and
  Binmore as the equilibrium approach to institutions (n. 4). Their reply
  cites it, with Lewis and Sugden, for the view that the normativity of
  institutions comes from mutual expectations and the resentment people
  feel when expectations are frustrated ([NOTE-395](../notes.d/NOTE-395.md)). That is their
  summary, not the book's words.
- The SEP entry ([LIT-487](LIT-487.md), §4.2.2) lists it among convention-based
  theories of social phenomena.

**Topic.** `social-ontology` first: the book's stated subject is the
nature of social norms, which is a question about what a social fact of
that kind is. `social-science` holds its experimental and
game-theoretic methods. `ethics` holds its fairness norms.

**Neighbours.** Tomasello ([LIT-125](LIT-125.md)) explains the sense of obligation as
internalised pressure from a joint "we". This book, as its readers
describe it, explains norm-following from expectations. The two are
rival accounts of where social normativity comes from, and a reading
should say whether the book's account needs a "we" at all.

No instruction for machine-learning practice appears in what was seen,
so nothing here points to the Anthology of the SOTA.

**What a reading should check.** The book's definition of a social norm,
and whether it separates empirical expectations (what others do) from
normative expectations (what others think one should do); the precise
conditions are not quoted here because they were not seen. How chapter 4
(*Covenants Without Swords*) treats cooperation without enforcement, the
gap North ([LIT-489](LIT-489.md)) leaves open. Whether chapter 5's informational
cascades bear on how unpopular norms persist.
