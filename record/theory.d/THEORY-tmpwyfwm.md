# THEORY-tmpwyfwm: In human ventral temporal cortex the content of a clearly seen, unchanging image is carried for as long as it stays on by a stable population code while activity amplitude decays; prefrontal and parietal content is a transient event at onset

## Source

- Vishne, Gerber, Knight & Deouell (2023), [LIT-tmppqcvc](../literature.d/LIT-tmppqcvc.md), read in full in
  [NOTE-tmpdsg4z](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpdsg4z.md): Results (Figs. 1–5), Discussion, Limitations and the STAR
  Methods on stimuli, HFA, state-space analysis, decoding and statistics.

## What was actually shown

**The paradigm.** Vishne et al. ([LIT-tmppqcvc](../literature.d/LIT-tmppqcvc.md)) recorded subdural
electrodes in 10 patients with intractable epilepsy (8 right hemisphere
only) while they viewed clearly visible greyscale images of faces,
watches, other objects and animals for 300, 900 or 1,500 ms (three
patients also saw 600 and 1,200 ms).
Patients responded only to rare targets, about 10% of trials, which were
not analysed, so the analysed trials needed no report (though each image
had to be judged a non-target). The signal is high-frequency activity
(HFA, 70–150 Hz), a proxy for local firing. Of 907 noise-free electrodes,
430 were visually responsive by a criterion blind to category and timing.

**Amplitude decays.** With the image still on, HFA fell from peak by
81.8% ± 1.1% by 800–900 ms across occipital, ventral temporal, parietal
and prefrontal electrodes, and single-electrode category selectivity by
77.4% ± 1.1% (Fig. 1).

**Content does not.** Linear decoders on the pattern across electrodes
separated face from watch in ventral temporal cortex at a mean AUC of
99.8% over 100–900 ms (occipital 92.4%). A decoder trained at onset worked
at the end and the reverse: the temporal generalisation matrix in ventral
temporal cortex is a square block, so the discriminating direction in
population space is stable in time. Information ended about 450 ms after
offset for 300 ms images and 230 ms for the longer ones; the duration
contrasts were significant in ventral temporal and occipital cortex and
not in prefrontal or parietal cortex (Fig. 4). Exemplar identity and the
geometry of exemplars were sustained and stable in ventral temporal
cortex through 900 ms, also after category structure was removed, on 60
images seen by 5 patients (Fig. 5). Occipital results were in the same
direction and less robust.

**Prefrontal and parietal content is an onset burst.** Category decoding
there was real (peak AUC 84.1% and 86%) but "transient and mostly limited
to ~150–600 ms after stimulus onset", later than in visual cortex, did not
generalise across time and did not track duration. GNWT's predicted
offset ignition was not seen: the prefrontal event is at onset only, and
nothing marked the image going off.

What could have come out otherwise: ventral temporal decoding could have
decayed with amplitude, or survived as a changing sequence of codes (a
diagonal generalisation matrix), or ended at a fixed time after onset
rather than after offset. Prefrontal decoding could have lasted as long as
the image. None did.

## Bearing on IIT and the global workspace

The multi-duration paradigm was adopted by the COGITATE adversarial
collaboration, whose pre-registered predictions the paper tests. As the
paper reports them, IIT's proponents predicted a stable representation in
a posterior "hot zone" lasting as long as the experience, and were
"largely agnostic" about prefrontal cortex; the global neuronal
workspace's proponents predicted transient prefrontal onset and offset
representations with no sustained component, even without report. Both
held, except the offset ignition, and the authors conclude that "these
predictions of IIT and GNWT are, in fact, not adversarial".

That is a result about two sets of paradigm predictions, not about the
theories' formal content. IIT 3.0 ([LIT-445](../literature.d/LIT-445.md), read in [NOTE-365](../notes.d/NOTE-365.md)) derives
consciousness from integrated information in a system's cause–effect
structure, and nothing here computes Φ or tests a postulate; the
"posterior hot zone" prediction was made by Tononi et al. for COGITATE,
not derived in [LIT-445](../literature.d/LIT-445.md). The workspace side is the theory whose indicators
Butlin et al. ([LIT-056](../literature.d/LIT-056.md)) adopt, and its ignition prediction is what held.
So the paper does not move the record's standing on either theory. What it
shows is that a paradigm chosen to tell them apart did not, because each
predicted something about a different region and both were right.

**[THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md).** That account holds that current evidence cannot settle
whether an AI system is conscious, partly because architectural indicators
presuppose the disputed theory, so the dispute is over what counts as
evidence. Its claim is about AI and is neither supported nor contradicted
here. The bearing is narrower: even in humans, with intracranial
recordings and pre-registered predictions, a test designed to discriminate
two theories found them compatible. That is a reason to expect slow
progress from indicator-style evidence, not a test of [THEORY-023](THEORY-023.md)'s
argument, and no relation is declared.

The paper's own reading is conditional. If experience of a steady image is
continuous, the sustained ventral temporal code fits it; if experience is
discrete, the transient frontoparietal burst fits it better. Which region
is the correlate depends on the temporal nature of experience, which the
data do not settle. Dennett and Kinsbourne's *Time and the Observer*
([LIT-431](../literature.d/LIT-431.md), read in [NOTE-367](../notes.d/NOTE-367.md)) is the paper's second reference for that
question; nothing here tests their argument.

## What this does not say

- **It does not say the code is the percept.** Every image was clearly
  seen and stayed the same, so percept and input never came apart. The
  same code could carry an unseen image. The authors name binocular
  rivalry as the test.
- **It does not say prefrontal cortex holds no sustained content.**
  Prefrontal coverage was sparse and mostly one hemisphere, and only HFA
  was measured; low-frequency activity or synaptic states could hold it.
  The authors say the absence "should be taken with more caution than the
  presence of activity".
- **It does not say what the "experience subspace" does.** The authors use
  "representation" for patterns that correlate with stimulus
  descriptors, with no mechanistic role implied. Their proposal that
  position in a stable subspace fixes perceptual quality is speculative.
- **It is not single-neuron evidence.** HFA is a population proxy, and the
  main multivariate analyses pool electrodes across patients into one
  pseudo-population per region.
- **It does not reach beyond 1,500 ms**, or say how the code relates to
  representational drift over longer times, which the authors leave open.

## Connections

- **Verzhbinsky et al. ([LIT-tmp8o0fr](../literature.d/LIT-tmp8o0fr.md), [THEORY-tmpkyk0k](THEORY-tmpkyk0k.md)).** Also human
  intracranial high-frequency signals, but about co-occurrence across
  regions, not content within one. Prefrontal content arriving after
  sensory content and lasting a few hundred milliseconds is the kind of
  event a cross-region coordination mechanism would have to carry; neither
  paper tests the link.
- **[THEORY-tmprpyvx](THEORY-tmprpyvx.md).** Filed alongside: in crows reporting near-threshold
  stimuli, neurons in the prefrontal analogue followed the reported
  percept rather than the stimulus (second-hand, through [LIT-tmpulpg8](../literature.d/LIT-tmpulpg8.md)).
  That is the dissociation of percept from input this account lacks, but
  it was found in a report paradigm and in the frontal analogue, where
  here, without report, frontal content was only an onset burst. The two
  do not conflict; report may be what recruits sustained frontal coding.
  Neither tests that.
