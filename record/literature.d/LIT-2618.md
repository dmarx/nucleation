# LIT-2618: Information multiplexing from optical holography to multi-channel metaholography

Márquez, A., Li, C., Beléndez, A., Maier, S. A. & Ren, H. (2023). Information multiplexing from optical holography to multi-channel metaholography. *Nanophotonics* 12(24): 4415–4440. doi:10.1515/nanoph-2023-0605.

## Key takeaways

*From the skim, [NOTE-1968](../notes.d/NOTE-1968.md); the account is [THEORY-1882](../theory.d/THEORY-1882.md). The review's
summaries of the primary literature, not primary results.*

- **Bragg selectivity** (§2.1): "In the case of volume holograms, the
  multi-plane diffraction produced in the reconstruction step allows for
  Bragg selectivity and as a result, the reconstruction is very sensitive
  to the angle of incidence and/or the illumination wavelength".
- **Independent readout** (§2.1): "If the holograms are angularly or in
  wavelength detuned more than the Bragg selectivity range, then the
  different holograms are reconstructed independently", sharing the
  material's dynamic range.
- **Angle multiplexing** (§2.3, Table 1): ">1000 holograms; in
  transmission; most widespread", and in Bragg-based methods "only one
  object/data hologram is constructively generated for each specific
  reconstruction beam".
- **Crosstalk** (§2.2): "to diminish crosstalk between different
  channels/data pages, thick material layers are needed".

## Standing in the record

A cites an Optica paper and a doi.org link on angle-multiplexed storage
without titles; neither was identified, and this review stands in for
them. It carries A's two points: that angle multiplexing superposes
patterns in one medium and reads each by its reference angle, which holds;
and that Bragg selectivity in thick holograms makes the separation "quite
sharp", which holds qualitatively. The review gives no selectivity-width
formula, so "quite sharp" is not quantified here. The primary sources for
that are Kogelnik (1969) on coupled-wave theory and Mok (1993), the
5000-hologram demonstration; neither was read.

Its bearing on [CLAIM-182](../claims.d/CLAIM-182.md) is the clearest physical analogy in the batch:
pages superposed in one volume, nothing spatially separate, each recovered
by a key external to its content, and separable only when the medium makes
the keys selective. It shares that structure with Plate's holographic
reduced representations ([LIT-2644](LIT-2644.md)), and differs in readout: within the
selectivity condition holographic readout is near-exact, where HRR's needs
a content-addressed clean-up. The parallel is the record's to draw; the
sources do not draw it.
