# NOTE-tmpfi3s5: Shoemaker — Blameworthy but Unblamable: A Paradox of Corporate Responsibility

## Contribution

A paradox and its dissolution. Corporations have what blameworthy action
needs but lack the capacities for being angrily blamed. Pluralism about
responsibility dissolves it: corporations can be responsible in one sense
and not another.

## Key results

- **The theory is his earlier one** (p. 911): "I will draw from my
  previously published work on the topic"; the 2011 paper is cited as
  "121 ETHICS 602" (n. 39).
- **Answerability** (p. 912): "To have these capacities is to be
  answerable, to be able in principle to answer the question, “Why did you
  do that?”"
- **Accountability** (p. 912): "But this is just to have the capacities
  for emotions and empathy. To have these capacities is to be
  accountable."
- **Three types** (p. 913): "Attributability, answerability, and
  accountability are the labels for three distinct types of responsibility
  (drawn from labels already in the literature)." They are "radically
  independent" (n. 70).
- **Anger** (p. 913): "gratitude and anger aptly respond to empathic and
  emotional (dis)regard."
- **Others lack answerability** (n. 64), of views including Watson's
  *Agency and Answerability* (2004): "none of them have an independent
  notion of answerability."
- **The solution** (p. 915): "corporate agents may be blameworthy and
  blamable in answerability terms, but not blameworthy or blamable in
  accountability terms."

## Assumptions

That responsive attitudes come in families, each fitting a distinct
agential capacity; that anger requires a target capable of empathic
regard.

## Concepts

- **Quality of character / judgment / regard**, tracked by
  admiration-disdain, pride-regret and approval-disapproval, and
  gratitude-anger.
- **Answerability**: the capacity to answer for one's judgments, "Why did
  you do that?".
- **Angry blame** as against protest.

## Bearing on the record

- **[TERM-126](../terms.d/TERM-126.md)** (answerability): a homonym. Shoemaker's answerability
  is an agent's capacity, and a type of responsibility, for answering for
  its judgments. The term's answerability is a directed normative relation
  in which an actor owes a party an answer. The term should say that it
  does not mean Shoemaker's.
- **[TERM-127](../terms.d/TERM-127.md)**: its list of senses has attributability and
  culpability but no slot for Shoemaker's answerability; and its remark
  that A110 credits the split to Watson and Shoemaker jointly is
  confirmed: the third type is Shoemaker's.
- **[CLAIM-742](../claims.d/CLAIM-742.md)**: confirms its correction of A110's attribution.
- **[QUESTION-476](../questions.d/QUESTION-476.md)** (blameless post-incident practice): Shoemaker's
  answer for corporations, answerability and protest without angry blame,
  is a ready model of a practice that withholds one kind of blame while
  keeping another, one candidate reading of "keep accountability while
  giving up blame".
- **The exchange's characterization** (A110: Watson and Shoemaker "give the
  last category a closer relationship to blame"): partly right. Anger, the
  paradigm of angry blame, belongs to accountability; but corporations "may
  be blameworthy and blamable in answerability terms", so accountability is
  tied to one kind of blame, not to blame as such.
