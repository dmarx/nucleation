# LIT-958: How manipulable are prediction markets?

Itzhak Rasooly and Roberto Rozzi (2025), *arXiv:2503.03312*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-778](../notes.d/NOTE-778.md).*

- **Random shocks persist.** The yes–no gap was 0.0748 after a week,
  0.0686 after 30 days, and 0.0586 after 60 days in markets not yet
  resolved, from a starting gap of 0.10. All are significant at
  p < 0.01.
- **Reversion slows.** 25% of the gap reverted in week one and 6% in
  weeks two to four, the shape the model predicts.
- **Persistence without learning.** In the model every trader leans
  against a shock (Lemma 3), but finite, risk-averse traders lean only
  partly. With 11 traders and no learning, about 60% of a shock
  remains. Learning from the price makes it stick more, and more traders
  or a common prior make it stick less (Table A1).
- **The moderators are weak evidence.** Reversion is higher in markets
  with a Metaculus twin, more 24-hour volume, more comments or more
  traders. But the median splits have p between 0.04 and 0.19, and the
  traits were not randomised.
- **Robust within the platform.** Dropping possibly related markets
  changes nothing. A small real-money-convertible follow-up (110
  markets) shows the same pattern, with faster reversion.
- **What is not known.** Whether the persistence comes from traders
  updating on the price or from limited capacity to undo it.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 for [QUESTION-047](../questions.d/QUESTION-047.md) and read on 2026-10-14 in
[NOTE-778](../notes.d/NOTE-778.md). What it shows is held as [THEORY-267](../theory.d/THEORY-267.md). For the
question it is a measured case of a community-level estimate holding a
value that no member reasoned to. It is not a case of a community
belief: a price aggregates positions, and whether any trader holds the
persisting value is the open point.
