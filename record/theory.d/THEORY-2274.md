# THEORY-2274: Situated knowledge can still be realist

## Source

Massimi (2023), [LIT-3139](../literature.d/LIT-3139.md), read in [NOTE-2366](../notes.d/NOTE-2366.md); a summary of
Massimi (2022), [LIT-1228](../literature.d/LIT-1228.md), unread.

## The account

A scientific perspective is the situated practice of a community at a
time: its theories, instruments, techniques and methodological
principles. Perspectives "methodologically intersect" now, through models
that act as inferential blueprints between communities, and "historically
interlace", trading tools across cultures and centuries. "No particular
community can sanction the reliability of their own claims of knowledge
or justify such reliability by their own lights"; reliability is the
product of the plurality. What the plurality reaches is modally robust
phenomena, stable lawlike events re-identified across perspectival
inferences, and natural kinds are open-ended groupings of them.

## What was actually shown

Nothing, in the primer: it states the view, with examples (the bending of
cathode rays and the glass-blower and kelp-makers behind it, the
compass), and refers to the book for the arguments.

## What this does not say

- It does not say every perspective is as good as any other; its
  constraint is that reliability needs others' uptake.
- It does not say anything about norms of action or about which
  standards bind; it is an epistemology of science.
- It does not say how conflicts between intersecting perspectives are
  resolved when they persist.
