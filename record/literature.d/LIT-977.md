# LIT-977: Distributional hypothesis as isomorphism between word-word co-occurrence and analogical parallelograms

Takuma Torii, Akihiro Maeda and Shohei Hidaka (2024), *PLOS ONE* 19(10): e0312151.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-781](../notes.d/NOTE-781.md).*

- **Count models do most of word2vec's work on analogies.** The data are
  the English Wikipedia of 2017, with a window of 5 and d = 300, scored
  on the full Google set.

  | model | 1,487 words | 10,072 words |
  |---|---|---|
  | logfreq-SVD | above 61% | 50% |
  | ppmi-SVD | 55% | 51% |
  | word2vec, negative sampling, restricted vocabulary | 38% | 40% |
  | word2vec, full corpus | 75% | 69% |
  | raw rows | under 6% | under 5% |

- **Negative sampling is not needed.** word2vec trained by full-softmax
  cross-entropy on the co-occurrence probabilities reaches about 56–60%.
  The restricted negative-sampling baseline is trained on a dummy corpus
  whose context words are independent given the centre, so the
  comparison with it is confounded.
- **Theorem 1.** In a corpus of 24 subject–verb–object sentences, the
  eight subjects carry every combination of Sex, Royalty and Number, and
  each verb expresses one aspect through its object. The subjects' raw
  co-occurrence rows form a parallelepiped if and only if each object's
  four sentences are equally probable (Eq. 5). A random non-uniform
  distribution gives no parallelogram at all.
- **Why it holds.** Each sentence carries one aspect, so a subject's row
  is a sum of per-aspect contributions. Under Eq. 5 each contribution
  depends only on the aspect's value, and the row is affine in the ±1
  aspect vector.
- **Natural text.** One "parallelepiped-like" set of Family-category
  words appears in a 2D PCA, which "merely demonstrates the 'existence'"
  of one.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 for [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md), and read on 2026-10-14 in
[NOTE-781](../notes.d/NOTE-781.md). It was registered as "a derivation of why linear analogy
structure follows from co-occurrence statistics" and "a second account
of the premise that [THEORY-185](../theory.d/THEORY-185.md) derives". The reading corrects both. The
derivation covers one toy corpus, raw counts and exact shapes. Its
aspects combine additively, as a mixture over sentences, where
[THEORY-185](../theory.d/THEORY-185.md)'s multiply. So it is a rival account for a different
generative structure: there the PMI is linear in the attributes, here
the raw counts are. Theorem 1 is filed as [THEORY-271](../theory.d/THEORY-271.md). For
[QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md) its aspects are independent and its lattice Boolean, the
case the question says does not settle it. Its one bearing is that, with
the incidence held Boolean, any interaction in sentence probabilities
breaks the exact shape. It also connects to [THEORY-183](../theory.d/THEORY-183.md)'s reading of
geometry as a symmetry of the statistics. The analogy benchmarks are ML
practice on word embeddings, hence the `anthology-candidate` flag.
