  Abbott (2005). Treats professions, states and universities as linked ecologies, each defined by actors, locations and the relation between them and each an environment for the others. Two concepts link them: hinges, issues or strategies that work in both ecologies at once, around which alliances form; and avatars, attempts to institutionalize in one ecology a copy of an actor from another. Hinges are worked through medical licensing in 19th-century New York and England, avatars through four cases linking professions and universities.

# LIT-1366: Linked Ecologies: States and Universities as Environments for Professions

Abbott, A. (2005). Linked ecologies: States and universities as environments for professions. *Sociological Theory*, 23(3), 245–274. doi:10.1111/j.0735-2751.2005.00253.x.

Not reached. Skimmed on 2026-10-22 ([NOTE-1077](../notes.d/NOTE-1077.md)) through Abbott's French
precursor (2003) and the Bourdieu section cut from this article, both
posted by him. The summary now follows the article's abstract; what the
precursor shows is in the NOTE.

## Why this is here

A30 named linked ecologies as "even closer to the expanded domain" than its own "regime ecology". Bears on [CLAIM-423](../claims.d/CLAIM-423.md) and [CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md).

**From the precursor, 2026-10-22.** It bears on [CLAIM-423](../claims.d/CLAIM-423.md) more than the
exchange said. Its New York case already treats differences of tempo
between linked ecologies as explanatory: politics worked by urgency,
medicine sought a settlement once, and this "différence de rythme" changed
the doctors' allies across the decades; they won only by joining durable
causes such as public health. The claim's defeater names Pierson and
Putnam as accounts that may already state it; Abbott belongs on that
list, though he gives tempo as a variable, not an account of which
component gains authority. On [CLAIM-417](../claims.d/CLAIM-417.md) the precursor has little: it is
about jurisdictions and alliances, not perspectives.
