# NOTE-tmpm5bff: Davidson — Rational Animals

## Contribution

A short argument that only creatures with language have beliefs and so are
rational, built on the concept of belief and on triangulation.

## Key results

1. **Rationality.** "to be a rational animal is just to have
   propositional attitudes" (p. 318); "the intrinsically holistic
   character of the propositional attitudes makes the distinction between
   having any and having none dramatic." (p. 318).
2. **Irrationality.** "Irrationality is not mere lack of reason, but a
   disease or perturbation of reason." (p. 321).
3. **Thesis.** "My thesis is rather that a creature cannot have a
   thought unless it has language." (p. 322).
4. **Prediction is not possession.** Of the heat-seeking missile: "This
   uninformed observer might be justified in attributing a desire and
   beliefs to the missile; but he would be wrong." (p. 323).
5. **The two steps.** "First, I argue that in order to have a belief,
   it is necessary to have the concept of belief." "Secondly, I argue that
   in order to have the concept of belief one must have language." (p.
   324).
6. **Surprise and truth.** "Surprise requires that I be aware of a
   contrast between what I did believe and what I" come to believe (p.
   326); "To have the concept of belief is therefore to have the concept
   of objective truth." (p. 326).
7. **The gap.** "I confess I do not know how to show this." (p. 327);
   "Our sense of objectivity is the consequence of another sort of
   triangulation, one that requires two creatures." "The conclusion of
   these considerations is that rationality is a social trait." (p. 327).

## Assumptions

Opacity marks attitude attribution; every believer is liable to surprise;
holism of the attitudes.

## Concepts

Propositional attitude; holism; semantic opacity; concept of belief;
surprise; objective and intersubjective truth; triangulation.

## Bearing on the record

- **THEORY-596** and **THEORY-538** (Dennett), a contrast: attitude
  attribution may predict animal behaviour well and still be wrong.
- **THEORY-616**, a qualification: on Davidson's criterion whether a
  language model has attitudes turns on whether it interprets others'
  speech, not on how well attribution predicts it.
- **THEORY-495** and Tomasello's account of thinking, a contrast and
  parallel: both put the human difference in sociality, but Davidson
  denies non-linguistic creatures beliefs; triangulation is structurally
  parallel to joint attention.
- **THEORY-151** (objectivity as a community's practice), a parallel.

## Limitations

The argument is incomplete by its author's own account (p. 327); no
empirical content.
