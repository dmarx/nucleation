# QUESTION-tmpumkgh: Does the impossibility of robustly rational proposition-wise aggregation extend from categorical judgments to corporate attitudes in general: degrees of belief, desires, intentions or emotions?

## Why it is a question

[THEORY-127](../theory.d/THEORY-127.md) holds, for categorical judgments, a theorem with three
parts. Universal domain, robust rationality, proposition-wise
supervenience and unanimity together force a dictator. The proof is
written out in the promotion audit's decision file for it (batch
023).

List (2014, [LIT-700](../literature.d/LIT-700.md)) uses this to define a third kind of collective
attitude. On his account a group agent's corporate attitudes supervene
holistically, across a web of propositions. He names corporate fears and
hopes as well as judgments.

The theorem's proof uses the coherence conditions of binary judgment:
completeness, consistency and deductive closure. Credences, preferences,
intentions and emotions have other coherence conditions. For example,
proposition-wise linear pooling is said to preserve probabilistic
coherence. That is a standard result, but the record does not hold it.
If it holds, the impossibility fails for credences.

## What would answer it

One of the following, for some kind of attitude other than categorical
judgment, under conditions comparable to [THEORY-127](../theory.d/THEORY-127.md)'s:
- a proved impossibility;
- a proved possibility, a non-dictatorial proposition-wise rule that
  preserves that attitude's coherence.

Several attitude kinds with different answers would answer it piecewise.
More cases of discursive dilemmas in courts or committees cannot answer
it.
