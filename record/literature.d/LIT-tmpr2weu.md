# LIT-tmpr2weu: The Authentic Personality: A Theoretical and Empirical Conceptualization and the Development of the Authenticity Scale

Alex M. Wood, P. Alex Linley, John Maltby, Michael Baliousis and Stephen
Joseph, "The Authentic Personality: A Theoretical and Empirical
Conceptualization and the Development of the Authenticity Scale", *Journal
of Counseling Psychology* 55(3):385–399, July 2008 —
DOI-10.1037/0022-0167.55.3.385. `published:` is Crossref's online date,
15 July 2008; the issue is dated July 2008.

The brief's citation is correct; the full title is the one above.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from the abstract, not a reading.* The abstract, as the
Manchester portal gives it:

- "This article describes the development of a measure of dispositional
  authenticity and tests whether authenticity is related to well-being, as
  predicted by several counseling psychology perspectives."
- "Scales were designed to measure a tripartite conception of authenticity,
  comprising self-alienation, authentic living, and accepting external
  influence, which was supported with exploratory factor analysis.
  Multigroup confirmatory factor analysis showed that the factor loadings
  were invariant across sample, ethnicity, and gender."
- "The scale showed substantial discriminant validity from the Big Five
  personality traits, nonsignificant correlations with social desirability,
  and 2- and 4-week test-retest correlations ranging from r = .78 to .91.
  Each subscale was strongly related to self-esteem and aspects of both
  subjective and psychological well-being."
- It claims "the first direct test of several theoretical models that view
  authenticity as integral to well-being."

The scale's length (12 items, three subscales of four) and its grounding in
Rogers's person-centred theory are from scale-description pages found by
search, not from the article.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request, as the most used self-report
measure of authenticity and the person-centred alternative to Kernis and
Goldman. It is psychology with no instruction for machine-learning
practice, so no anthology topic holds it. The Anthology of the SOTA does not
hold it: a grep of its literature.d for "Authenticity Scale" and the DOI
found nothing.

Tagged `psychopathology-and-treatment` because the theory it measures is a
theory of distress and therapy: in the person-centred tradition, loss of
authenticity (self-alienation) is what goes wrong and its recovery is what
therapy does, and the abstract frames the scale as a test of the counseling
models that say so.

**Where it bears.**

- **Kernis and Goldman ([LIT-tmpc6vlg](LIT-tmpc6vlg.md)).** The two are the field's rival
  measures. Kernis and Goldman's construct includes self-awareness and
  unbiased processing; this one includes "accepting external influence", a
  measure of how far others' expectations steer the person. Whether the
  article compares itself with the AI-3 is not known from the abstract, so
  no relation is declared.
- **SDT ([LIT-559](LIT-559.md)).** "Self-determination" is one of the article's keywords.
  "Accepting external influence" sits close to SDT's external and
  introjected regulation, which [THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md) ties to the history of how a
  value was acquired. A reading should say whether the subscale measures
  present influence or acquired regulation.
- **Taylor ([LIT-tmprf69i](LIT-tmprf69i.md)).** The scale scores a person as authentic for living
  by his own values and resisting others' influence. Taylor's account, as
  commonly summarized, makes authenticity dialogical and dependent on
  shared horizons, so a high score on "accepting external influence"
  reversed is not obviously authenticity in his sense.

**Priority for a reading: medium.** It is a measurement paper; the record
needs it chiefly to say what the most-used authenticity score measures.
