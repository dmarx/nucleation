  one's category's prescriptions (Is) and when others' actions do (Io);

  little they earn. Evidence is illustrative. The authors call identity
  a nonpecuniary motive and never call it self-interest.

# NOTE-tmpwgpmb: Economics and Identity

## Contribution

It brings identity into economic analysis as an argument of the utility
function. Identity is tied to social categories, each with prescriptions
for behaviour and an ideal of physical and other attributes, and with
higher or lower status. The paper builds a small game around it and
applies it to three settings: sex segregation in jobs, poverty and social
exclusion, and the division of housework. In each, it says, identity
"substantively changes conclusions of previous economic analysis"
(p. 715).

## Key insight

Behaviour that looks self-destructive from outside can be utility
maximizing once self-image is counted. People "behave in ways that would
be considered maladaptive or even self-destructive by those with other
identities", to "bolster a sense of self or to salve a diminished
self-image" (p. 717). And because one's identity depends on what others
in one's category do, there is a new kind of externality: a man in a
dress threatens other men's identity, and they may respond (p. 717).

## Assumptions

- **The utility function** (p. 719). U_j = U_j(a_j, a_−j, I_j), with
  I_j = I_j(a_j, a_−j; c_j, ε_j, P). c_j is j's assignment of everyone,
  herself included, to categories; ε_j her given characteristics; P the
  prescriptions. "Identity" names both the self-image and the assigned
  categories (p. 718).
- **Prescriptions, not norms** (n. 2, p. 716). Norms in economics are
  obeyed for fear of punishment. Prescriptions are followed "for the most
  part, to maintain their self-concepts".
- **As-if maximization** (p. 719 and n. 6). People need not be aware of
  their motives; motives may be unconscious. Friedman's methodology is
  invoked.
- **Psychodynamic psychology** (p. 728). Breaking internalized rules
  causes anxiety (the superego); identity must be "defended against
  anxiety". Identification is internalizing values "such that her actions
  should conform with the behavior of some people and contrast with that
  of others". Cognitive dissonance is offered as an alternative basis
  (n. 28). Self-categorization theory is not used; Tajfel and Turner
  (1979) are cited only for a review.
- **Identity is partly chosen** (pp. 719–720, 725–726). Categories are
  more or less ascriptive; choice "may be proscribed for others".

## Key results

- **The prototype game** (pp. 727–730, Figure I). All see themselves as
  Green; Greens should do Activity One. Each person has a taste for one
  activity, worth V. Person Two, with a taste for Two, loses Is by doing
  it. Person One then loses Io and may respond, at cost c, inflicting L.
  Payoffs (One, Two): Activity One (V, 0); Two, no response (V − Io,
  V − Is); Two, response (V − c, V − Is − L).
  - Four subgame-perfect outcomes (p. 730): deterrence when c < Io and
    Is < V < Is + L; response without deterrence when c < Io and
    Is + L < V; no response when c > Io and Is < V; no Activity Two at
    all when Is > V.
  - Comparative statics (pp. 730–731). A tax T > Io − c on responding
    ends deterrence; total utility moves from V to 2V − Is − Io. A
    rhetorical campaign can raise Is and Io. The conflict is Sen's
    Paretian liberal: one cannot protect both the deviant and those
    disturbed by the deviance.
- **Evidence that identity enters payoffs** (pp. 720–727).
  - Minimal-group and Robbers Cave findings; the authors grant these "do
    not show the existence of a 'self' or this identification per se"
    (p. 720).
  - A telephone vignette survey, 70 households from the Fremont, CA
    phone book (Table I, p. 724). A carpenter teases the first female
    carpenter. 84% rated "he feels less masculine" at least somewhat
    likely; 84% "he and his friends will not be able to joke around";
    36% fear of a lower wage; 13% unfairness of her lower wage.
  - Southern men insulted in Nisbett and Cohen's experiments feared the
    experimenter thought less of their masculinity, though they would
    never meet again (p. 725).
  - Alumni giving to one's own college, against giving where the
    marginal return is highest (p. 722).
- **Gender and work** (pp. 732–737). A woman in a "man's job" loses Is;
  male coworkers lose Io and may act against her. Firms may create a
  parallel "woman's job"; competitive firms underinvest in new job
  categories when associations are economy-wide.
  - Two-thirds of the labour force would have had to switch jobs for
    parity in 1970, unchanged since 1900; 53% by 1990 (p. 732).
  - 1970 shares female: secretaries 97.8%, nurses 97.3%, elementary
    teachers 83.9% against secondary 49.6% (p. 734).
  - After the women's movement: the median job-tenure gap fell from 3.3
    years (1968) to 0.4 (1998); of 45 occupations 0.0% female in 1970,
    one was under 1% female in 1990; women rose from 24.6% to 52.7% of
    accountants and 4.5% to 24.5% of lawyers (p. 735). With no change in
    technology or endowments, "tastes" are "the leading suspect"
    (p. 736).
- **Exclusion and poverty** (pp. 737–745, Figure II). Members of an
  excluded community choose Green (dominant culture, work) or Red
  (oppositional). Greens lose r, the degree of exclusion.
  - An All-Green equilibrium exists if and only if r < I_oR (p. 741).
  - Exclusion alone, r > 0 with no peer externalities, makes some choose
    Red (p. 741). Middle-class out-migration or lost jobs raise the Red
    share (pp. 742–743).
  - Red "self-destructive" behaviour "is not the result of individual
    'irrationality'" (p. 742).
  - Readings of residential Job Corps against the non-residential
    JOBSTART, of selective schools, and of affirmative action's rhetoric
    as raising or lowering r (pp. 743–744).
- **The household** (pp. 745–748, Figure III). Panel Study of Income
  Dynamics, 1983–1992, slightly over 29,000 couple-years (n. 63). A Tobit
  fit of husbands' share of housework on their share of outside hours:
  about 10% when they do all the outside work, rising to no more than
  37% as their share falls (p. 746). Equal-bargaining comparative
  advantage cannot fit the left side; adding the prescriptions that men
  not do "women's work" and earn more than their wives can.

## Concepts

- **identity**: "a person's sense of self" (p. 715); in the model, both a
  self-image and one's assigned categories (p. 718).
- **prescriptions (P)**: the behaviour and ideal attributes appropriate
  to each category in each situation; kept to maintain a self-concept,
  not for fear of sanction.
- **Is and Io**: the loss of identity from one's own, and from another's,
  violation of prescriptions.
- **identity externality**: a change in one person's identity utility
  caused by another's action.
- **situation**: who is matched with whom, and in what context; it may fix
  which categories are salient (n. 30).
- **Red / Green**: an oppositional identity that rejects the dominant
  culture, and one that adopts it at the cost of never being fully
  accepted (r).

## Connections

- **Sugden's objection to the device** ([NOTE-tmph49t5](../notes.d/NOTE-tmph49t5.md)). This paper is the
  standard example of what Sugden rejects: keeping individual
  maximization and folding the social motive into the payoffs. Sugden's
  point that a preference can always be posited to fit a choice applies
  here; the authors grant that utility theory is agnostic about motives
  (p. 719).
- **Self-verification** ([NOTE-tmp3g9n4](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp3g9n4.md)). Swann et al. explain willingness to
  die for the group by the motive to keep one's self-views true. This
  paper's identity utility is the same kind of motive: following
  prescriptions to "maintain their self-concepts" (p. 716).
- **Self-categorization theory** ([THEORY-tmpo85ke](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpo85ke.md)). Little overlap. Its
  categories and prescriptions resemble social identities and their
  norms, and its "situation" gestures at salience (n. 30). But the
  mechanism is internalized rules and anxiety, not depersonalization,
  and the self-image is the individual's.
- **Categories, not group entities** ([TERM-tmpko4rw](../terms.d/TERM-tmpko4rw.md)). Every identity here
  is membership in a category with a shared prescription. No group is
  modelled as a whole with a good of its own.
- **Sen (1985), "Goals, Commitment, and Identity"**, not held. The
  authors note that Sen discusses identity but does not put it into a
  utility function (n. 1). Sen's point that commitment drives a wedge
  between choice and welfare is the alternative they do not take up.

## Bearing on the record

- **The owner's thesis ([CLAIM-tmpjylq8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjylq8.md)).** The claim says this paper makes
  the thesis true of an economist's agent by construction. That is half
  right. These are inferences; the paper says nothing about selflessness.
  - What it makes true by construction is that identity-driven action
    maximizes the agent's utility. That is "every motive is my motive",
    the trivial reading [CLAIM-tmprudek](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmprudek.md) separates from the thesis.
  - The self in the utility is a self-image: how far one's own traits and
    acts, and others' acts, fit the prescriptions for one's categories. It
    is not a self that includes the group's good. No group's welfare
    enters anyone's utility. Io is a loss when a fellow member deviates,
    and the remedy is to harm that member.
  - So the paper's "larger self" yields conformity and policing, not
    benevolence toward the group. Its one example of giving to a group,
    alumni giving (p. 722), is explained as loyalty and identification,
    not modelled.
  - It agrees with Swann et al. on what the served self is: a
    self-concept to be maintained. If the thesis recruits this paper,
    "self-interest" means keeping a self-image, not furthering a welfare.
  - On whether identity utility is welfare, the paper is two-minded. It
    treats utility as an as-if representation of choice (n. 6), yet sums
    identity gains and losses in "total utility" (p. 730), with the usual
    caveats (n. 29). Only on the second reading is identity-driven
    sacrifice self-interest in a substantive sense.
- **The triviality objection ([CLAIM-tmprudek](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmprudek.md)).** The paper offers a
  criterion of the self's extent that is not caring, but no evidence that
  it works apart from caring. Inferences again.
  - The criterion is category assignment with shared prescriptions (c_j,
    P). It is fixed partly by others: categories are "more or less
    ascriptive", the excluded "could never be a 'true' Green" (p. 748),
    and a person's identity can depend on how others categorize her
    (n. 5). That can come apart from what she cares about.
  - But the force of identity is in Is and Io, free parameters. They are
    never measured; they are read off the behaviour they explain. The
    paper's content comes from structure: payoffs attach to categories,
    are shared by their members and move together when prescriptions
    change. The housework asymmetry is the one place where that structure
    predicts what a taste for housework would have to assume.
  - The authors grant the gap: the experiments they rely on "do not show
    the existence of a 'self' or this identification per se" (p. 720).
- **Batson's objection ([CLAIM-tmpybc4k](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpybc4k.md)).** Not addressed. Nothing here
  concerns empathy or helping individuals.
- **A dated note is proposed for [CLAIM-tmpjylq8](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpjylq8.md)** (section 3 below), to
  correct its line about this paper. No THEORY filed. No ML instruction.

## Limitations

- **Illustrative evidence.** Examples, ethnographies and one vignette
  survey of 70 households. No test sets the identity model against a
  rival on data it was not built to fit.
- **Derivations withheld.** The workplace model's full specification
  (n. 34), the poverty model's analysis (n. 57) and the household
  regression's equations (n. 63) are "available from the authors upon
  request".
- **Parameters unobserved.** Is, Io, r and the prescriptions are inferred
  from the behaviour they explain.
- **Elimination as evidence.** The women's-movement argument rests on
  ruling out technology and endowments, not on measuring changed
  prescriptions.
- **Typos.**
  - "from from" (n. 4, p. 719).
  - "how others interpret i's actions" for j's (n. 5, p. 719).
  - "Nisbett and Cohn" (p. 723); the reference list has Cohen.
  - "Levine [1997]" (n. 13, p. 723); the reference list has 1995.
  - "Pierson 1986" (p. 734); the reference list has 1989.
  - Notes 48 and 49 (p. 736) give the same citation, 442 F.2d 385 (5th
    Cir.), cert. denied 404 U.S. 950 (1971), for Phillips v.
    Martin-Marietta and for Diaz v. Pan American. It is Diaz's.
  - "Wetherell [1998]" (n. 70, p. 749); only Wetherell 1996 is listed.
  - Kandori's *Review of Economic Studies* volume is printed "LXIX"; for
    1992 it should be LIX (p. 751).
  - "This respect is no less diminished by" (n. 11, p. 721) means "no
    less" or "not diminished", not both.
  - "Peter Howett" in the acknowledgements is probably Peter Howitt.

## Open questions

- Can Is and Io be measured apart from the choices they explain, for
  instance by asking about anxiety at deviating? Only then does category
  membership become a criterion of the self that can be tested against
  caring.
- Does identity utility ever take in the group's welfare, rather than
  members' conformity? The later identity-economics literature (Akerlof
  and Kranton's 2010 book, work on identity in organizations) may say;
  the record holds none of it.
