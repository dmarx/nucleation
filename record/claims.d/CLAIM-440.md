# CLAIM-440: An organization's appraisal is the negotiation among its perspectives, a common currency is that negotiation's admitting an ordering of the options it faces, and goal-conduciveness valence is a regime in which an ordering of the organization's own states by their bearing on its maintenance prevails in the negotiation; so valence presupposes contested rankings, not frustrated demands, its sign is whether the organization runs ahead of or behind a reference course along the potential ordering, its intensity is how far the negotiation's decisions follow that ordering, and ambivalence, where no ordering prevails, differs from neutrality, where one prevails and the organization keeps to its course

## The claim

The owner, 2026-10-23: "what if the 'appraisal' is the process by which
competing perspectives negotiate amongst themselves? I think our earlier
results equip us to reframe 'common currency' as 'admits an (at least
instantaneous) ordering'. we could then formalize a hedonic valence as a
circumstance in which a particular kind of ordering dominates the
dynamics." The definitions are [TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md)'s. The claim has three
parts and five consequences.

**1. Appraisal is negotiation.** Frijda's appraisal is an event's
pertinence to a concern, eliciting action readiness with control
precedence ([THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md)). Read with the record's distributed agent, the
concerns are perspectives, each with its own ranking of what to do next,
and control precedence is not a property an emotion brings with it but
the outcome of their negotiation: which perspective's ranking wins
authority over action. This is how Frijda, Ridderinkhof and Rietveld
already describe regulation, one motive state checking another with no
regulator above them ([THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md)), and it is [CLAIM-283](CLAIM-283.md)'s distinction
between the precedence a motive claims and the authority it is granted.

**2. A common currency is the negotiation admitting an ordering.**
Veit's "common currency of sharing evaluative information across
multiple control systems" ([THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md)) does two jobs: comparability, so
that the options faced can be traded off, and sharing, so that one
evaluation governs many control systems. The first is the negotiation
having an ordinal potential on a short window: ordinal extendibility,
the weakest of the four senses "currency" can bear, short of observed
comparability of every pair, of a scalar currency and of fixed exchange
rates ([TERM-077](../terms.d/TERM-077.md)). It needs the wins only to be acyclic, so that some
complete ordering agrees with them all; how options never contested
compare is not observed, and is open across the class of such
orderings. The second is part
3's potential. This is the owner's correction of 2026-10-17 carried
over ([CLAIM-347](CLAIM-347.md): "We can do quite a lot with access to an ordering, even
if it doesn't give us a true measure"). Two facts keep it from being a
relabelling. A negotiation need not produce an ordering: Sen's theorem
([THEORY-027](../theory.d/THEORY-027.md)) and [CLAIM-353](CLAIM-353.md) show that decisive jurisdictions, unanimity
and an unrestricted domain cannot always yield a best element. And an
ordering need not be a currency ([CLAIM-436](CLAIM-436.md)).

**3. Goal-conduciveness valence is the negotiation having a potential of a particular kind.**
The ordering ranks the system's own states by their bearing on its
maintenance, and it prevails: in the formal sense of [TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md), the
negotiation has it, approximately, as a potential, so that the moves that
win its conflicts are improvements along it, and it persists and is
climbed again after interruption, a persistent potential ([TERM-078](../terms.d/TERM-078.md)).
How coherent the negotiation is, is ordinal: the least fraction of its
wins whose reversal leaves them acyclic. How far this ordering prevails
is the share of contested wins that agree with it ([CLAIM-434](CLAIM-434.md),
parts 1 and 3). The Hodge decomposition measures something else, the
cardinal inconsistency of margins, and its gradient can reverse what the
wins settle (part 4). A record of wins fixes a class of orderings that
fit it, not one (part 7), so that the potential is of this kind is
established by intervention, not read off. The record of wins is
also only the record of the contests admitted to it. Where a legibility
regime selects which contests are admitted or recorded, an acyclic record,
even a persistent potential, can be the product of that selection, so a
coherent record is not by itself evidence that the perspectives are
integrated ([CLAIM-448](CLAIM-448.md)). A potential is read as the organization's, and its
valence with it, only on a record shown to admit the contests that bear on
it, as [CLAIM-403](CLAIM-403.md)'s ladder of access, recognition, credibility and uptake
would require; otherwise it is the valence of the admitted negotiation.

**What is not new here.** The dynamics of an ordering coming to prevail are Lewis's ([LIT-1455](../literature.d/LIT-1455.md),
[NOTE-1119](../notes.d/NOTE-1119.md)): an emotional interpretation must "take over" the system,
entrained elements "lose their independence and become embedded in a
larger regime", and of competing attractors "only one of these converges
at a time" (pp. 174–176). Part 1's competitive form is Moors's
([LIT-1434](../literature.d/LIT-1434.md), [NOTE-1117](../notes.d/NOTE-1117.md)): for her "most of the competition does not
occur between stimulus-driven and goal-directed processes, but instead
between different goal-directed processes", and control precedence is
what a tendency has when it ensues "from stimuli that are relevant for
highly valued goals" (pp. 11, 15), the outcome of a competition rather
than a property the emotion brings. Marsella and Gratch's EMA
([LIT-1447](../literature.d/LIT-1447.md), [NOTE-1100](../notes.d/NOTE-1100.md)) implements it, with appraisal frames "each
competing to determine the agent's coping response" (p. 81). On Moors's
typology the record's view is a response-evaluation theory rather than an
appraisal theory in the narrow sense, and "appraisal" here is meant in
the wider one. Her book ([LIT-1433](../literature.d/LIT-1433.md)) is unread, and the novelty of
anything below should not be claimed past it until it is. The component process model itself does not describe appraisal as a
negotiation: in Scherer and Moors's statement of it ([LIT-1457](../literature.d/LIT-1457.md),
[NOTE-1106](../notes.d/NOTE-1106.md)) appraisal is one evaluator's profile driving patterned
responses, and nothing there wins, persists or resumes; the nearest
precedent in that tradition is Shuman, Sander and Scherer's "perspectives
with which the same object may be approached" ([LIT-1458](../literature.d/LIT-1458.md), p. 13).
Scherer's
definition of emotion already holds
[CLAIM-283](CLAIM-283.md)'s distinction: "claiming (not always successfully) priority"
([LIT-1451](../literature.d/LIT-1451.md), [NOTE-1113](../notes.d/NOTE-1113.md), §1). What this claim adds is threefold. The competitors are perspectives,
each with its own ranking and authority, not options under one agent's
utility, and no regulator stands above them, where Moors settles goal
conflict by a higher-order goal. The competition is not assumed to
settle on a scale: Moors and EMA settle it by expected utility or
likelihood times utility, which is only the compensatory regime, and
neither allows a negotiation that produces no ordering. And what prevails
is an ordering, which makes the currency question askable
([CLAIM-436](CLAIM-436.md)), and an ordering of a particular kind, which is what
makes the regime valence. Neither Lewis nor Scherer gives the regime a
sign.

## What follows

1. **No contest, no valence.** Potential needs something to prevail over:
   contested pairs in the record of wins, where perspectives rank a pair
   differently and one prevails. Where no pair is contested, no ranking
   wins anything, and every ordering is trivially the negotiation's
   potential ([CLAIM-434](CLAIM-434.md), part 8), so valence is not a consequence of
   existence alone. [TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md) makes this a condition of the definition, and
   valence is undefined there, not zero. A thermostat, one loop with one
   ranking, has no valence on this account. This is the analogue of
   Frijda's "In the absence of concern pertinence, there is no motive"
   ([THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md)), and it does the work of Veit's restriction to animals of
   high pathological complexity, without a threshold. What the condition
   needs is contest, not frustration ([TERM-060](../terms.d/TERM-060.md)): rankings can contest
   while every demand stays jointly satisfiable, the contest being over
   which satisfying outcome, and demands can be jointly infeasible, a
   non-trivial compatibility complex ([CLAIM-347](CLAIM-347.md)), before any contest is
   recorded ([CLAIM-449](CLAIM-449.md)). So valence is not a consequence of the record's
   frustration line ([CLAIM-347](CLAIM-347.md), [CLAIM-368](CLAIM-368.md)); the two are about different
   relations. The absorbed writer ([CASE-051](../cases.d/CASE-051.md)), with nothing contested, has
   no goal-conduciveness valence while absorbed: the account leaves it
   undefined. Whether the writing is positive during the episode, and if
   so at which level of valence, is [CLAIM-441](CLAIM-441.md)'s case, and open.
2. **Sign is progress against a reference course, and needs only the
   ordering.** Positive when the system runs ahead of where it was
   expected to be on the potential ranking, negative when behind, neutral
   on course. This clause is Carver and Scheier's ([LIT-1441](../literature.d/LIT-1441.md), read
   through Carver 2018, [LIT-1449](../literature.d/LIT-1449.md), [NOTE-1102](../notes.d/NOTE-1102.md)): affect as the
   velocity of progress against a reference value "at which affective
   valence would be neutral". Their neutral point is a reference rate,
   not rest, and the claim takes it: a system standing still where it
   expected to advance is behind. Comparing positions on an ordering
   needs no measure, so the clause stays ordinal. What is the record's
   and not theirs is that the ordering progress is read on is the one
   that prevails. Carver gives each goal loop its own valence; here
   each perspective has its own, and the system's is the potential
   ordering's ([CLAIM-437](CLAIM-437.md), the owner's revision of the same day: an
   earlier draft said "there is one valence").
3. **Intensity is the potential's strength.** How much of what the other
   perspectives would decide the potential ranking overrides. [THEORY-055](../theory.d/THEORY-055.md)
   cites evidence that rated control precedence tracks rated intensity
   (Sonnemans and Frijda 1994, not read here); on this account that is
   because they are one quantity. The clause is the record's, and it has
   a known difficulty for positive valence: Carver (2003, [LIT-1445](../literature.d/LIT-1445.md),
   unread) is reported to hold that positive affect lowers a goal's
   priority, freeing attention for something else. If so, strong
   positive affect would go with weakening potential, not strong. Until
   that paper is read, this clause is held for negative valence only.
   Hesp et al. ([LIT-1468](../literature.d/LIT-1468.md), [NOTE-1111](../notes.d/NOTE-1111.md)) cut against it there too:
   in their model negative valence is a weakening of the preference
   ordering's precision, with a fall back to habit (p. 420), though their
   fear case, confident flight, fits this clause better. The clause is
   open for both signs.
4. **Ambivalence is not neutrality.** The distinction is the evaluative
   space model's ([LIT-1453](../literature.d/LIT-1453.md), [LIT-1456](../literature.d/LIT-1456.md), read through Norris et
   al. 2010, [LIT-1438](../literature.d/LIT-1438.md), [NOTE-1101](../notes.d/NOTE-1101.md)): positivity and negativity
   "can be reciprocally activated, independently activated or
   co-activated", and coactivation, ambivalence, yields "the same middling
   expression on the affective response surface" as neutrality. The
   record's reading differs. Neutral is potential with the system on its
   course. Ambivalence is either no ordering at all, when the negotiation
   fails as Sen's theorem allows, shown in circular triads within a
   window or in the same pair's outcome reversing across windows, or
   split potential, one ranking winning
   among some perspectives and another among the rest. Two things are
   owed: an argument that the model's two channels are perspectives in
   this sense, and, since both readings agree that ambivalent and
   neutral systems can choose alike, a record of which perspective's
   ranking each decision followed ([QUESTION-268](../questions.d/QUESTION-268.md)); the model's marks
   of ambivalence, arousal and approach–avoidance conflict, are candidate
   independent markers. The model's asymmetries, a higher gain for
   negativity and a positivity offset, are not predicted by anything
   here.
5. **Two potential regimes.** Potential can be compensatory, the
   potential ranking trading off against others in proportion, or
   lexicographic, the others deciding only its ties. [CLAIM-436](CLAIM-436.md)
   shows the first has a currency and the second none, and
   [CLAIM-435](CLAIM-435.md) reads Veit and ALARM as these two regimes.

## Revision of 2026-10-23: goal-conduciveness valence, not hedonic

The owner's word was "hedonic valence". The objection below showed that
the regime defined here tracks 'wanting', not 'liking', and the reply
showed where the claim falls on the component process model's levels of
valence: on goal conduciveness. At the owner's direction the claim and
[TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md) were revised before merge from "hedonic" to
"goal-conduciveness" valence, and the objection is conceded. The
owner's words are quoted above as given.

## Revision of 2026-10-23: the review

A review of the pull request found that the claim's measures confused
ordinal and cardinal structure, and the claim and [TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md) were
revised before merge. "Common currency" now means only ordinal
comparability (part 2). Coherence and intensity are counted on wins, and
the Hodge decomposition is kept for margins (part 3). Consequence 1 is a
condition of the definition. Ambivalence is read in the sequence of
windows, not in a netted record (consequence 4). And the potential is
reported as a class, identified by intervention. The review's hierarchy
of ordinal coherence, persistence, goal-conduciveness valence and felt
valence is now carried by [TERM-077](../terms.d/TERM-077.md), [TERM-078](../terms.d/TERM-078.md), [TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md) and
[CLAIM-439](CLAIM-439.md).

## The objection it faces, and the reply

[CLAIM-438](CLAIM-438.md): potential as defined tracks 'wanting', not 'liking'.
Sensitized incentive salience meets all three marks of potential while
hedonic impact, measured by taste reactivity, stays flat or falls
([THEORY-893](../theory.d/THEORY-893.md)). That is this claim's own second defeater. The regime defined here is motivational valence, and "hedonic" in this
claim and in [TERM-076](../terms.d/TERM-076.md) was the wrong word; the owner conceded the
objection and the revision above was made.

[CLAIM-433](CLAIM-433.md) replies by conceding in a principled form. On the
component process model's levels of valence, this claim's ordering is
goal conduciveness, the potential's strength tracks 'wanting', and
'liking' is intrinsic pleasantness, a separate appraisal. The account
is of goal-conduciveness valence, not hedonic valence.

## What it does not say

- **That a coherent record shows an integrated organization.** A potential
  describes the relation of wins it is computed on, and what was not
  admitted is not in it ([CLAIM-448](CLAIM-448.md)).
- **That the regime is felt.** The claim is functional. Veit identifies
  felt valence with the currency ([THEORY-101](../theory.d/THEORY-101.md)); adopting that identity for
  this regime is a further step the record has not taken, and the
  owner's constitutive thesis ([CLAIM-412](CLAIM-412.md)) would bear on it. The
  counterfactual eraser ([THEORY-252](../theory.d/THEORY-252.md)) bears on it too: priority,
  persistence and resumption are dispositional, so an identity of feeling
  with this regime would be an identity with a functional role, unless
  potential is defined by conflicts actually won over an interval.
- **How perspectives are individuated.** The claim takes them as given,
  as [TERM-072](../terms.d/TERM-072.md) does; individuating them without circularity is not this
  claim's business.
- **How potential is measured.** [QUESTION-268](../questions.d/QUESTION-268.md).
- **What happens when the potential ordering ranks a proxy for
  maintenance rather than maintenance.** [QUESTION-267](../questions.d/QUESTION-267.md).

## Note of 2026-10-23: cycles as a regime, and two objections

The owner asked whether, "rather than asserting potentials always have to
exist", cycles could be read as internal tension (workbench entry of
2026-10-24, U13). This claim never said potentials must exist; the reading
of the cyclic case is [CLAIM-451](CLAIM-451.md): potentials and nonintegrable cycles
are regimes, and a cycle is not a pathology by default. It narrows
consequence 4. Ambivalence, opposed evaluations both strong, can occur
where one perspective consistently prevails, and a cycle need not involve
opposed valences, so "ambivalence is no ordering" overstates the link.
Two objections are filed: [CLAIM-449](CLAIM-449.md), that consequence 1 runs together
contested rankings and infeasible demands, with the absorbed writer
([CASE-051](../cases.d/CASE-051.md)) as its test; and [CLAIM-448](CLAIM-448.md) (2026-10-24), that a potential
read off a record whose contests a legibility regime selected can be
coherent for the wrong reason. The same exchange (U5) suggested setting
Minsky's emotion machine ([THEORY-570](../theory.d/THEORY-570.md)), resources selected by critics and
selectors, beside this claim as a mechanistic alternative rather than
support: it has a more explicit mechanism for switching regimes and weaker
evidence.
