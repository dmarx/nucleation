# LIT-764: A Mathematical Theory of Communication

C. E. Shannon (1948), *Bell System Technical Journal* 27(3):379–423 and
27(4):623–656 — DOI-10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x

## Key takeaways

- **Entropy as a rate** (Part I). A discrete source is an ergodic Markov
  process; its entropy H = −Σ p log p per symbol is fixed by the fact that
  long sequences fall into about 2^{HN} typical ones carrying nearly all
  the probability (Theorems 3–4). A source can be sent over a noiseless
  channel of capacity C at C/H − ε symbols per second and not faster
  (Theorem 9). The three axioms that single out −Σ p log p (Theorem 2) are
  offered, Shannon says, for plausibility only; the coding theorems are the
  justification.
- **Capacity of a noisy channel** (Part II). The rate of transmission is
  R = H(x) − H_y(x), where the equivocation H_y(x) is exactly what a
  correction channel would have to supply (Theorem 10); capacity is its
  maximum over input sources. Below C, a code with arbitrarily small error
  frequency exists, shown by averaging over random codes; above C the
  equivocation is at least H − C (Theorem 11). The 1% error example: 1000
  bits per second carry about 919, not 990.
- **What it does not do.** The proofs are sketches; no explicit code near
  capacity is given beyond Hamming's seven-bit example, which Shannon
  connects to the difficulty of constructing near-random sequences.
  "Relative entropy" here means H over its maximum on the alphabet, not
  the Kullback–Leibler divergence.
- **Continuous parts** (III–V, skimmed). Sampling at 1/(2W) (Theorem 13,
  proof referred to the 1949 paper, [LIT-317](LIT-317.md)), C = W log(1 + P/N) for
  white noise (Theorem 17), and a minimum rate for a source at a fidelity
  criterion (Theorems 21–22), the first form of rate-distortion theory,
  which the information bottleneck ([LIT-338](LIT-338.md)) inherits.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-09 at the owner's request, as one of the works in the
reference list of the owner's working manuscript (October 2026) that the
record did not yet hold. See the curation entry of that day.

It is the record's primary source for entropy, equivocation and capacity.
[LIT-317](LIT-317.md) (Shannon 1949, Deferred) is credited in its summary
with the sampling theorem and W log(1 + P/N); both are stated here first,
and this paper defers only the sampling theorem's proof to the 1949 one.
The information bottleneck ([LIT-338](LIT-338.md)) starts from this
paper's exclusion of meaning and from Part V's minimum rate at a fidelity.
