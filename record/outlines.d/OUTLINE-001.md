# OUTLINE-001: The Individual and Its Conditions

## Abstract

What makes an organization one individual rather than an aggregate of interacting components? Dynamical integration, organizational closure, and relational constitution offer distinct criteria, but present organization does not generally reveal the processes maintaining it. A constructed system can remain strongly coupled while its maintenance regime approaches collapse, and internally and externally maintained systems can exhibit identical reduced state dynamics under specified interventions. These results establish limits on inference from observable unity to maintenance stability or provenance, not a new universal criterion of individuality. I distinguish genetic individuation from descriptive, dynamical, constitutive, and autonomous determinations; argue that productive provenance constrains but does not independently establish metaphysical constitution; and defend objective, criterion-relative organizational individuation without claiming that every warranted decomposition is a numerically distinct individual. Recursive individuation is understood as a distinctive organization of processes that participate in producing or transforming the constraints under which they continue.
