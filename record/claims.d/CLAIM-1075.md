# CLAIM-1075: For an adaptive inquiry the achievement is not flow as a state but flow-regulation as an organizational capability: to enter, sustain, interrupt and recover fluent coordination without losing the history of what was learned, so that a coordination system should remove the interruptions that rediscover what was settled while keeping those that expose an unaddressed objection or an inadequate premise

## The claim

A20 §6 of the exchange "Explain First Contact RL"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6b037165-3a1c-83ea-9d7c-311c7807acd3). A record
that removes coordination overhead can also make it too easy to continue
smoothly past a consequential mismatch; [CLAIM-181](CLAIM-181.md)'s danger is that
unfamiliar distinctions get forced into existing categories. "Luria must
sometimes make it harder to continue smoothly when continuing would conceal
a consequential mismatch." A's table:

| Unproductive interruption | Productive interruption |
|---|---|
| rediscovering a previously answered objection | discovering an unaddressed objection |
| reconstructing forgotten context | revising an inadequate premise |
| repeatedly resolving the same ambiguity | identifying a genuinely ambiguous concept |
| repairing incidental formatting mistakes | changing the grammar to represent something new |

"A good coordination system should minimize the first kind without
suppressing the second." The aim is not uninterrupted activity but less
unnecessary interruption with sensitivity kept to changes that warrant
reconsideration.

The closing proposition of A20 states the capability: "The crucial
achievement is not uninterrupted convergence, but the capacity to move
between fluent coordination and deliberate reorganization when the existing
constraints become inadequate", "to enter, sustain, interrupt, and recover
fluent coordination without losing the history of what has been learned."
A said this "identifies something the original Nucleation account of flow
doesn't yet fully capture": [CLAIM-162](CLAIM-162.md) describes the state, not its
regulation.

## Its relation to the record

[CLAIM-710](CLAIM-710.md) holds that procedural friction is where an opportunity to
intervene is exposed, so removing friction can remove those points;
[CLAIM-711](CLAIM-711.md) that standing constraints set slowly and enforced fast keep a
frictionless process interruptible. This claim says which friction a record
should remove and which it should keep, sorted by what the interruption is
about. A20 ties it back to the account of a self: a self of overlapping
perspectives might be unified not "by permanent harmony" but by maintained
processes that let its perspectives "coordinate fluently when possible,
contest one another when necessary, and reorganize".

## Where the gap is

The table sorts interruptions by their content after the fact, but a
coordination system must act before it knows whether an objection is
new. A filled step, in the assistant's voice: the record already sorts some
of them by address. An objection that matches a held entry (by code or by
search) is the first kind and can be routed to it; one that matches nothing
is a candidate for the second, and the cost of letting it interrupt is
bounded by how often that happens. Formatting repair is the first kind by
construction, which is why this record leaves it to CI ([ADR-054](../decisions.d/ADR-054.md)). This
sorting fails where an objection is held under another name, which is the
case step 2 of the read-and-file skill exists for. The wording of this
paragraph is the assistant's (Claude, in this session); adopting it is the
owner's call.
