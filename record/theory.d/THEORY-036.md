# THEORY-036: Dennett's real-pattern criterion, compressibility against the bit map, admits almost every non-random pattern; the projectibility, perspective and scale-relativity that rainforest realism needs are later additions that do the ontological work

## Source

- Dennett, [LIT-220](../literature.d/LIT-220.md), pp. 32–51, read in [NOTE-190](../notes.d/NOTE-190.md).
- Ladyman, *Patterns All the Way Up*, [LIT-219](../literature.d/LIT-219.md), theses 2, 12, 14 and 15
  and fns. 5–6, read in [NOTE-191](../notes.d/NOTE-191.md).
- Ladyman & Lorenzetti, [LIT-184](../literature.d/LIT-184.md) ([NOTE-129](../notes.d/NOTE-129.md)).
- Wallace, [LIT-151](../literature.d/LIT-151.md) ([NOTE-142](../notes.d/NOTE-142.md)).
- The Stanford Encyclopedia entry, [LIT-045](../literature.d/LIT-045.md) §4.4 ([NOTE-038](../notes.d/NOTE-038.md)).

## What was actually shown

**Dennett's criterion.** Dennett turns Chaitin's incompressibility account
of randomness around: "A pattern exists in some data – is real – if there
is a description of the data that is more efficient than the bit map,
whether or not anyone can concoct it" (p. 34). Three features follow from
the text.

- *Observer-free.* The criterion is an existence claim about a shorter
  description, not about any perspective actually having one. The "loose
  but unbreakable link to observers" (p. 32) is the intuition the test
  makes precise, not a clause of it.
- *Lossless baseline.* The frames and the Game of Life are finite data with
  a fixed encoding. The Game of Life was chosen because "nothing is hidden
  from view" (p. 38).
- *Pluralist.* Patterns trade compactness against noise. Two
  interpretations can be "different, but equally real, patterns", with
  "nothing deeper" to settle between them (p. 49), and the choice is
  pragmatic (pp. 35–37).

So any non-random data set contains real patterns, and the criterion ranks
them only by pragmatic trade-off ([NOTE-190](../notes.d/NOTE-190.md), Limitations). This is by
design: it is what makes the view "mild" realism.

**Ladyman's criterion is different.** "To be is to be a real pattern, where
a real pattern is something that indispensibly figures in projectible
generalisations" ([LIT-219](../literature.d/LIT-219.md) p. 2). It differs from Dennett's in three ways.

- Real patterns are scale-relative, and ontology is "effective" (thesis 12).
- Scientific real patterns are *lossy*, where Game-of-Life patterns are
  lossless (thesis 14).
- The book's definition "refers to perspectives", because no agent can
  model the whole universe (thesis 15).

Ladyman lists five ways the Game of Life misleads as an exemplar. The
first is that it has a privileged bit-map level (p. 3). He also says the
definition can be developed information-theoretically, dynamically or
statistically (fn. 5), and that the book "defines real patterns modally"
(fn. 6).

**Downstream, the added clauses carry the weight.** Two uses depend on
them. Ladyman & Lorenzetti make Newtonian forces and curved spacetime
"both exist at different scales" ([LIT-184](../literature.d/LIT-184.md)), which needs scale-relativity.
Wallace defines real patterns by instantiation plus the best
precisification ([LIT-151](../literature.d/LIT-151.md)). That is a selection criterion Dennett's
pluralism lacks, and it disagrees with Ladyman about whether the
fundamental level is itself a pattern ([NOTE-191](../notes.d/NOTE-191.md)). The record's glosses
"compressibility" ([NOTE-038](../notes.d/NOTE-038.md)) and "compression relative to physically
possible perspectives" ([NOTE-129](../notes.d/NOTE-129.md)) blur the two criteria. [NOTE-190](../notes.d/NOTE-190.md) corrects
them.

## What this does not say

- That Dennett's criterion is wrong. It does what he wanted, which is a
  mild realism about beliefs within Fine's natural ontological attitude.
- That compressibility is irrelevant to rainforest realism. The book's
  definition is reported to be "articulated in information theoretic
  terms" (fn. 5), but it was not read.
- That the several formulations of real patterns (information-theoretic,
  dynamical, statistical) agree. Ladyman leaves that open.
- That real patterns say anything about learned representations or
  minimum description length. No source here draws that connection
  ([NOTE-129](../notes.d/NOTE-129.md), [NOTE-191](../notes.d/NOTE-191.md)).

## Connections
