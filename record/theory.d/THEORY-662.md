# THEORY-662: The feeling of fluency is an inference about the precision of one's own processing, triggered by a change in precision against expectation; because its cause is internal and not given to introspection, a felt discrepancy is attributed to the most salient external source, as familiarity, liking or a sense of error

## Source

Brouillet and Friston (2023), [LIT-957](../literature.d/LIT-957.md), read in full in [NOTE-785](../notes.d/NOTE-785.md) from the
authors' manuscript; page numbers are the manuscript's. The experiments it
reviews were not read and are reported as the paper states them.

## What was actually shown

**The reviewed findings (pp. 8–13), secondhand.**

- Relative fluency, ease against expectation, is distinguished from
  absolute fluency (Whittlesea and Leboe 2003).
- In the hension effect (Whittlesea and Williams 1998, Exp. 3), new
  orthographically regular non-words drew more false recognitions than new
  real words or irregular non-words, although real words were read
  fastest. It is read as surprise: easy to pronounce against hard to
  identify, attributed to the stimulus.
- Motor fluency of an unrelated preceding gesture, or of a colour
  associated with a fluent gesture, raised "old" responses to new items
  (Brouillet et al. 2017, 2022). In typing, the sense of having erred came
  from a gap between expected and experienced fluency (Wilbert and Haider
  2012).

**The proposal (pp. 14–18).** Unfelt fluency is the subpersonal recognition
of a change in precision, which orients attention, likened to exogenous
attention. Felt fluency is "a hierarchically deeper representation of
fluency", made opaque by mental action, that is, endogenous attention.
Because felt fluency "arises from internal inferences about the precision
of predictive processing, not the sensorium", it "cannot be attributed to
its subpersonal cause—and is therefore plausibly attributed to the
sensorium" (p. 18).

**What could have come out otherwise.** In the reviewed experiments,
judgements could have tracked absolute ease. They tracked discrepancy. The
precision reading itself was not exposed to any test.

## What this does not say

- **That fluency is precision.** That identification is proposed by analogy;
  nothing is simulated or derived, and felt and unfelt fluency are not
  variables in any model (C2–C4, weak).
- **That fluency is affect.** The paper treats fluency as a metacognitive
  feeling of ease. It touches valence only by citing Hesp et al. (2021) and
  the hedonic-fluency literature.
- **That "felt" means phenomenally felt.** Felt fluency is the state
  endogenous attention makes reportable. The paper gives no argument that
  unfelt fluency lacks experience, and speaks of "a phenomenal sense of
  non-felt fluency" (p. 11). It cannot separate felt from unfelt in the
  sense [QUESTION-059](../questions.d/QUESTION-059.md) asks about ([NOTE-785](../notes.d/NOTE-785.md)).
- **That the neuromodulatory story is shown.** The link to dopamine and
  noradrenaline is by citation.
- **What would refute it.** The paper does not say.

## Connections

- Barrett's categorisation of affect by context ([THEORY-057](THEORY-057.md)) has the same
  structure: a feeling with an internal cause is attributed to the most
  salient source. The paper does not draw the parallel.
- If both this and [THEORY-045](THEORY-045.md) hold, some felt states are about the brain's
  own processing and not about the body. Felt fluency here involves no body
  in the loop, unlike Damasio's hybrid feelings ([THEORY-093](THEORY-093.md)). Neither is a
  contradiction, since fluency is not offered as a homeostatic feeling.
- Overt somatic markers ([THEORY-500](THEORY-500.md)) are a felt quality entering a
  judgement about its source, with a body state as the feeling.
- Cross-modal fluency ([LIT-190](../literature.d/LIT-190.md), [NOTE-146](../notes.d/NOTE-146.md)) is the record's other reading of
  fluency misattribution.
