# NOTE-tmpk2qt7: Wallace — Stating Structural Realism

## Contribution

Wallace answers the charge that structural realism never clearly states an alternative to object/property/relation metaphysics done in predicate logic. He proposes a math-first view of theories, close to the semantic view, on which a theory's content is its mathematical structure and its representational relations to systems. Adopting this view is already a form of structural realism. The ESR/OSR choice becomes a choice between language-first and math-first metaphysics. He then examines the status of objects in fundamental and non-fundamental physics on both versions.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- §1 (pp. 1–3): frames the problem as metaphysicians (Dorr, Hawley, Sider) calling OSR unclear, while French and Ladyman say set theory and logic are ill-suited to stating it.
- §§2–5 (pp. 4–16): the language-first and math-first views of theories, and "why it matters" for empirical prediction, theoretical equivalence (AdS/CFT among the examples) and inter-theoretic reduction. Equivalence and reduction are much coarser-grained on the math-first view.
- §§6–7 (pp. 17–21): language-first ESR and OSR ("relations without relata") fail the original goals. Their "structure" is as liable to be discarded under theory change as non-structural content.
- §8 (pp. 21–24): math-first scientific realism realizes Worrall's goals, and the move from truth to successful representation "is itself sufficient to make this form of realism 'structural'".
- §§10–12 (pp. 27–36): math-first OSR puts a "Model of the World" in place of a "Book of the World". Objects become derivative, following Saunders (2003), and higher-level ontology is grounded as real patterns in higher-level theories and is scale-dependent (Figs. 6–7).
- §13 (pp. 36–37): "structural realism" names two different projects. The second, a priority or anti-individualist metaphysics, has drifted from the first's motivations, and the underlying divide is syntactic versus semantic views of theories.

## Open questions

- Probably the sharpest statement of what SR claims, and a direct challenge to the relation-priority readings in Floridi ([LIT-tmph5kb9](../literature.d/LIT-tmph5kb9.md)), Morganti ([LIT-tmpmqxjp](../literature.d/LIT-tmpmqxjp.md)) and Chakravartty ([LIT-tmpvdlpp](../literature.d/LIT-tmpvdlpp.md)).
- Its coarse-grained equivalence meshes with De Haro & Butterfield ([LIT-tmpvlpmx](../literature.d/LIT-tmpvlpmx.md)) on duality. Check whether the two accounts of theoretical equivalence agree.
- Check whether math-first metaphysics avoids smuggling predication back in when it states the representation relation.
