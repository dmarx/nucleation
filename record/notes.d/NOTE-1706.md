# NOTE-1706: Shah, Hart and Kaltsakas — Prolonged weaning

## Contribution

A review for clinicians of the whole course of weaning, from the first
readiness check to the management of patients who cannot be weaned,
organized around the 2007 consensus classification and updated with the
large cohorts (WIND, WEAN SAFE) and trials published since.

## Key insight

Whether a patient can do without the ventilator is found out by taking it
away briefly and watching, not by predicting: predictors of readiness have
low power, clinicians underestimate readiness, and waiting harms. The
patients who fail repeated trials form a group whose outcomes are much
worse, and for some of them the support never becomes optional.

## Assumptions

- **A short trial predicts permanent withdrawal.** The trial is read as
  the outcome of extubation, checked by a window afterwards.
- **Failure is an imbalance** of respiratory muscle load, capacity and
  drive (pathophysiology), to be corrected while support continues.
- **The classification is the consensus'**, which Jeong et al. (2015) note
  "was based on expert opinion rather than rigorous analysis of a cohort of
  ventilated patients".

## Key results

- **The groups (weaning classification).** "Simple weaning patients (group
  1) are those who proceed from initiation of weaning to successful
  extubation on the first attempt without difficulty." "Difficult weaning
  patients (group 2) are those who fail initial weaning and require up to
  three spontaneous breathing trials (SBTs) or up to 7 days from the first
  SBT"; prolonged patients "fail at least three weaning attempts or require
  >7 days of weaning after the first SBT".
- **Failure defined (weaning classification).** Citing the consensus:
  "either a failed SBT, reintubation and/or resumption of ventilator
  support following successful extubation, or death within 48 h following
  extubation", and "an aggregate of several studies reported a weaning
  failure rate of 31% [9]".
- **Later frequencies (weaning classification).** WIND: "24% did not have a
  weaning attempt, 57% weaned in <24 h (group 1), 10% weaned between 1 and
  7 days (group 2), while 9% required >7 days". WEAN SAFE: "35% of patients
  remained on mechanical ventilation at 90 days" and "15% had weaning
  failure".
- **Readiness (assessing readiness).** "clinicians frequently
  underestimate the ability of patients to successfully wean";
  "approximately half of self-extubations do not require reintubation";
  "mortality was more than double when extubation was delayed". The
  Weaning Task Force reviewed "462 weaning predictors in 65 observational
  studies", whose "pooled likelihood ratios of the predictors were all
  found to have a low predictive power".
- **The trial (response to a reduction in support).** The trial lasts "at
  least 30 min, increasing up to 120 min" for those at high risk; "If it is
  not clear that the patient has passed at 120 min, the SBT should be
  considered a failure." Delay from eligibility to a liberation attempt
  "was associated with subsequent weaning failure". A trial of 30 min on
  pressure support beat 120 min on T-piece, "82% versus 74%, p=0.001";
  "synchronised intermittent mandatory ventilation should be avoided";
  "reconnection to a ventilator for 60 min following a successful SBT
  reduces the risk of reintubation within 48 h".
- **Why extubation fails (prevention of post-extubation failure;
  extubation).** "excessive secretion load, fatigue, respiratory muscle
  weakness or fluid overload"; after a passed trial, "Poor cough strength
  and excessive endotracheal secretions".
- **Outcomes of failure (pathophysiology).** Of patients whose weaning
  fails, "approximately 50% remain dependent on invasive ventilation at 1
  year", with "40–75% 1-year mortality". Of those liberated after
  prolonged weaning, "42% required reinstitution of mechanical
  ventilation, and the longer the time spent on ventilation, the more
  likely".
- **Management (management; prediction).** "Of patients who require
  prolonged mechanical ventilation, 55–65% are successfully weaned"; in
  difficult-to-wean patients "a successful SBT may not be a useful marker of
  weaning success".
- **Jeong et al. (2015), read in part.** In one Korean medical ICU (n =
  680; Table 3), 67% simple, 20% difficult, 13% prolonged; reintubation
  within 48 h 0%, 20.6% and 32.2%; hospital mortality 21.1%, 28.7% and
  58.6%. Prolonged weaning carried a crude odds ratio of 5.327 for hospital
  mortality, 5.528 after adjustment (Table 5); difficult weaning was not
  significantly associated with it.

## Concepts

- **Weaning**: the whole process of withdrawing ventilatory support and
  the endotracheal tube.
- **Spontaneous breathing trial (SBT)**: a timed, observed period on
  T-piece or low support, read as a test of withdrawal.
- **Simple, difficult and prolonged weaning**: groups by the number of
  failed trials or days of weaning after the first trial.
- **Post-extubation respiratory failure (PERF)**: respiratory failure
  needing reintubation within seven days of extubation.

## Connections

It restates from Boles et al. (2007), [LIT-2213](../literature.d/LIT-2213.md), the three groups, the
definition of weaning failure, the 31% aggregate failure rate and the trial
as the readiness test. It does not restate the consensus' grading of
evidence, its epidemiology tables or, beyond the abstract, its
recommendations on modes after a failed trial; its frequencies are from
WIND and WEAN SAFE, and its trial evidence mostly postdates 2007. Alía and
Esteban (2000), [LIT-2215](../literature.d/LIT-2215.md), carry the earlier trials on abrupt and
gradual withdrawal. Its invocation of respiratory muscle weakness connects
to the disuse loss in Jaber et al. (2011), [LIT-2211](../literature.d/LIT-2211.md) ([THEORY-1511](../theory.d/THEORY-1511.md)),
which it does not cite.

## Bearing on the record

- **[QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md).** The trial is a repeated, matched probe of withdrawal at
  the present state, so the withdrawal measure is estimated daily and
  reversibly; the finding that delayed testing goes with failure says the
  probe should be used early. The classification records how many probes
  failed before withdrawal held.
- **[CLAIM-600](../claims.d/CLAIM-600.md).** Supports the requirement that persistence after
  withdrawal be observed: success is judged only after a window (48 h to 7
  days). The 42% who need ventilation again after prolonged weaning are the
  clinic's case of slow reversion mimicking persistence. Qualifies it on
  method: medicine tests readiness by short, reversible trial withdrawals
  before the real one.
- **[CLAIM-597](../claims.d/CLAIM-597.md).** About half of those whose weaning fails remain dependent
  at a year: support that has not become optional.
- **[CLAIM-602](../claims.d/CLAIM-602.md), [CLAIM-558](../claims.d/CLAIM-558.md).** By analogy only: longer ventilation goes with
  more weakness, more failure and more relapse, an association confounded
  by severity.
- **[CLAIM-604](../claims.d/CLAIM-604.md).** None; no threshold model.
- Filed: [THEORY-1515](../theory.d/THEORY-1515.md). No instruction for machine-learning practice.

## Limitations

- A narrative review; its evidence is second-hand.
- Its frequencies are not the consensus' own.
- The outcome gradients across groups are partly definitional: simple
  weaning has no reintubation by construction (Jeong).
- Longer ventilation and worse outcomes are confounded by severity.

## Open questions

- Does testing readiness earlier, by protocol, raise the share weaned at
  the first trial without raising reintubation? Ely's trial (in Alía and
  Esteban) bears on it.
- How much of prolonged weaning is disuse loss acquired on the ventilator?
  Serial diaphragm measures across weaning groups would tell.
