# ADR-001: Nucleation holds what the anthology's topics cannot: LIT, NOTE and THEORY, with a seeded closed vocabulary

## Context

The anthology's reading-time triage of 2026-09-25 ([ANTH-ADR-052](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-052.md) governs its
topics) found that 47% of the reading time in the ten-minutes-or-more group
went to 34 works no anthology topic could hold. The heaviest clusters were
quantum foundations with category theory, network science, and complex
systems. The owner kept [ANTH-ADR-052](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-052.md)'s decision not to add topics for them.
The owner also decided that "out of scope there" should not mean
"unrecorded", and named this repository as the catchall.

## Decision

- **Schemes: LIT, NOTE and THEORY**, copied from the anthology's `luria.yaml`,
  with ADR, DP, a changelog and a curation journal. The copy is deliberate. A
  work that turns out to belong in the anthology, or an anthology note that
  turns out to belong here, moves as a file and needs no reshaping.
- **No SOTA practice scheme, and no `consensus` vocabulary.** The practice
  scheme exists to record what someone should do in ML. Material that
  carries such an instruction belongs in the anthology by definition.
  THEORY loses its `explains:` relation, which pointed at practices.
- **A closed topic vocabulary, seeded from the triage's clusters:**
  quantum-foundations, mathematics, network-science, complex-systems,
  probabilistic-modeling, neuroscience, natural-sciences, operations-research,
  society-and-governance. Closed for the anthology's reason: every tag is
  one somebody chose and blurbed. The alert says so. A work that needs a word
  the list lacks gets the word added by decision, not the nearest wrong one.
- **The boundary.** A work goes here if no anthology topic can hold it. If
  one can, it goes there. The test is the anthology's own (its [ANTH-ADR-059](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-059.md)):
  the topics follow the content.
- **Cross-reference, not duplication.** An `ANTH` remote lets a document here
  cite the anthology ([ANTH-LIT-285](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-285.md)), and codes are not shared. Nothing is
  filed in both.

## Alternatives

- **Add the topics to the anthology.** [ANTH-ADR-052](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/decisions.d/ADR-052.md) counted the candidates and
  rejected them. The owner kept that decision, and this record is the
  alternative it leaves room for.
- **A plain markdown catalogue.** Lower ceremony, but no lint, no relations, no
  generated indexes, and no path for a work to move between the records.
- **A full mirror including SOTA.** It would sit mostly empty, and it would
  invite filing ML practice in the wrong place.

## Consequences

- The seeded vocabulary is provisional in the way every closed list is. The
  first works filed will test it, and a misfit is a finding about the list.
- **Do not run `luria remotes --refresh` for `ANTH`.** Under luria 0.31.0,
  discovery reads the anthology's `luria.yaml` and indexes only its first
  scheme (practices). After that, every LIT, THEORY and ADR citation reads
  as "NO SUCH DOCUMENT". Without a lockfile, the code-only filename
  convention resolves `ANTH-LIT-285` to the right file. That is a luria
  limitation, and it is worth an upstream issue.
- The depth of each filing is left to the curation log to state per batch.
  NOTE's `Read` and `Skimmed` statuses already say how deeply something was
  read, so no new mechanism is needed.
