# THEORY-tmpdftev: Perceiving people as a group entity, rather than as members of a common category, requires detecting or inferring collective intentionality among them; similarity, and categorization into an equivalence class, yields interchangeable members but not a group

## Source

- Skorich (2025, version 2 2026), [LIT-tmpmzum3](../literature.d/LIT-tmpmzum3.md), read in full in
  [NOTE-tmplcvy8](../notes.d/NOTE-tmplcvy8.md) from the PsyArXiv preprint. The supplementary materials
  were not read.

## What the account says

Self-categorization theory (Turner et al. 1987, as quoted in the source)
has groups perceived "as one whole entity" through categorization into
an equivalence class, made salient by meta-contrast and accentuated by
depersonalization. The account here splits that in two:

- **Category membership**: oneself and others share a property. Under
  accentuation they look interchangeable. Out-group homogeneity is a
  category effect.
- **Group entity**: oneself and others are bound into a unit, perceived
  from cues to collective intentionality: mutual gaze, synchronous or
  complementary motion, joint attention, explicit shared mental states.
  Crowd behaviour is a group effect.

The first is neither sufficient nor necessary for the second.

## What was actually shown

Nothing at first hand. The source gives a conceptual argument (sets
versus things; orchestras and families) and summarizes four experiments
under review (Skorich et al.). In minimal-group and who-said-what
paradigms, groups defined by mental states drew more in-group
favouritism and more tracking than groups defined by physical properties.
That could have come out otherwise: physical-property groups could have
done as well. The record has not read the experiments.

## What this does not say

- **Not that categorization does nothing.** Category effects such as
  self-stereotyping and out-group homogeneity remain, and the source
  keeps them as SCT1.0's special case.
- **Not that collective intentionality requires a group subject.** It is
  "intentionality spread across what are otherwise individual
  intentional agents", perceived by the individual. The account is about
  perception, not ontology.
- **Not the rest of SCT2.0.** Conceptual atomism, the language of thought
  and the intractability of meta-contrast are argued in the same paper
  but are not part of this claim, which stands or falls with the
  category/entity distinction and the mental-state evidence.

## Bearing

- **Rival to [THEORY-tmpo85ke](THEORY-tmpo85ke.md)** on the mechanism of group perception and
  of salience. They agree that self-perception varies with context; they
  disagree on whether meta-contrast is what forms the group.
- **[THEORY-126](THEORY-126.md)** uses collective intentionality to mark We-mode
  collective emotion. This account uses it to mark perceiving a group at
  all. If both hold, We-mode emotion presupposes a perceived group
  entity.
- **[CASE-104](../cases.d/CASE-104.md) and [QUESTION-119](../questions.d/QUESTION-119.md).** It distinguishes the fanbase as a
  category (people who support the club) from the fanbase as a group
  entity (fans seen to share an intention: a chant, a boycott). Only the
  second is a candidate holder of a stance.
