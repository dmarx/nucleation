# NOTE-004: US v. Google remedies opinion

## Contribution

This is the remedies-phase memorandum opinion that followed the court's 2024 liability ruling that Google unlawfully maintained monopolies in general search and search text ads through exclusive default-distribution agreements. The court says generative AI changed the remedies proceedings: GenAI chatbots are a nascent competitive threat, and part of the court's aim is to keep Google's search dominance from carrying over into GenAI. The court accepts Google's proposed remedies with modifications and adopts some of the plaintiffs' proposals. It rejects structural divestitures and several behavioral remedies as overreaching or unconnected to the violation. It orders the parties to submit a revised final judgment by September 10, 2025.

## Skim

*Abstract, figures and selected sections, read when the work was seeded. Not enough to state its assumptions or results exactly; a `Read` note replaces this one.*

- Introduction (pp. 3-6): the court lists its top-line rulings. It bars exclusive contracts and certain conditioning and tying arrangements for Search, Chrome, Assistant and Gemini. It does not require divestiture of Chrome or Android ("Plaintiffs overreached"). It imposes no payment ban, citing harm to distribution partners. It requires Google to share certain index and user-interaction data (not ads data) and to offer search and search text ads syndication on largely ordinary commercial terms. It orders no choice screens, requires public disclosure of material ad-auction changes, and imposes no public-education fund, publisher-choice remedy, investment reporting, anti-retaliation or anti-circumvention provisions, or self-preferencing provisions.
- Introduction (p. 6): the court will set up a Technical Committee. The judgment runs six years and takes effect 60 days after entry, except that the Technical Committee provisions take effect immediately.
- Conclusions of Law §IV (pp. 99-101): the court rejects Google's argument that GenAI products fall outside the remedy. It relies on New York v. Microsoft's extension of that remedy to technologies beyond middleware, and on Apple executive Eddy Cue's testimony that Safari search queries fell for the first time in 22 years.
- Term (pp. 219-221): the court calls Google's proposed three years too short and the plaintiffs' ten years at risk of going stale given GenAI's pace. It adopts five years plus one year of ramp-up, by analogy to Microsoft.
- Findings of Fact §I (TOC pp. 17-36) covers GenAI key terms, how LLMs work, grounding, and GenAI market participants. I saw these sections in the table of contents but did not read them.

## Open questions

- It is one of the first major judicial treatments of LLMs and GenAI as a competitive substitute for search. The findings of fact on grounding and on the role of search indexes for LLMs (pp. 28-36) are directly relevant to ML practice.
- The data-sharing and syndication remedies (Qualified Competitors, privacy-enhancing anonymization of user-side data) should be checked in the later final judgment for exact scope.
- The appeal status and any later modifications are unverified here.
