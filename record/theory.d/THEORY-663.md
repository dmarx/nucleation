# THEORY-663: Altruism between non-relatives can be selected when each act costs the giver less than it benefits the recipient, the same partners meet often and symmetrically, and help is withheld from those who do not return it; in humans, moralistic aggression, gratitude, sympathy and guilt are adaptations that regulate such exchange, and selection then favours sham versions of each and the ability to detect them

## Source

Trivers (1971), "The Evolution of Reciprocal Altruism", [LIT-510](../literature.d/LIT-510.md) (*The
Quarterly Review of Biology* 46: 35–57), read in full in [NOTE-402](../notes.d/NOTE-402.md).

## What was actually shown

**The model (pp. 35–39).** Altruism dispensed at random is eliminated.
Altruism dispensed by reference to the recipient's own altruism spreads if
altruists who are cut off by cheaters gain more than non-altruists do.
Conditions (p. 37): many altruistic situations in a lifetime, repeated
interaction with a small set of individuals, and symmetric exposure.
Hamilton's sketch: over 2n situations two altruists each get nR, and an
altruist who switches after being cheated gets S + (n − 1)P. A rare
non-altruist cannot spread unless T ≫ R. A rare altruist faces a barrier
that weakens as n grows.

**One case that fits.** Cleaning symbioses: hosts do not eat their
cleaners, signal before leaving and chase off threats. The evidence is
natural history for each precondition. [NOTE-402](../notes.d/NOTE-402.md) rates it moderate.

**One case that does not.** Bird warning calls are explained by the
caller's own interest, not by reciprocity: a predator that eats a neighbour
is more likely to learn and stay near the caller's species and place. The
abstract lists warning calls among the cases the model explains; the
section rejects that ([NOTE-402](../notes.d/NOTE-402.md), corrections).

**The human system (pp. 47–54).** Friendship and liking motivate giving.
Moralistic aggression, provoked by "injustice, unfairness, and lack of
reciprocity", protects the giver against cheaters. Gratitude and sympathy
are sensitive to the cost and benefit of an act. Guilt motivates reparation
once a breach is found, and more after a public transgression than a
private one. Then: "once moralistic aggression has been selected for to
protect against cheating, selection favors sham moralistic aggression as a
new form of cheating" (p. 54), and likewise for the others, with suspicion
to catch them. With language, "selection may favor the elaboration of
norms of reciprocal conduct" (p. 52).

**What could have come out otherwise.** The social-psychology literature
could have shown these emotions indifferent to cost, benefit and
reciprocation. The findings Trivers cites fit, but they were gathered
before the account and for other purposes.

## What this does not say

- **That the emotions were tested as adaptations.** The account is fitted
  to existing findings. No study was designed for it ([NOTE-402](../notes.d/NOTE-402.md), C4,
  weak–moderate).
- **That sham emotions are detected.** Asserted, with no studies (C5).
- **That the model is stable against invasion.** That was shown later, in
  the form and with the limits of [THEORY-672](THEORY-672.md).
- **That warning calls are reciprocal altruism.** The paper argues they are
  not.
- **Anything about fair division.** The "unfairness" here is cheating in an
  exchange. Morality-as-cooperation's division domain is not grounded here.
- **That the dispositions are heritable.** Assumed (p. 48).

## Connections

- Axelrod & Hamilton ([LIT-498](../literature.d/LIT-498.md), [THEORY-672](THEORY-672.md)) formalize the model with a
  continuation probability w. Hamilton's Part I ([LIT-497](../literature.d/LIT-497.md), [THEORY-408](THEORY-408.md))
  is the foil: this model is built for cases where kin selection "can be
  ruled out".
- The multiparty passage anticipates indirect reciprocity, which Alexander
  ([LIT-495](../literature.d/LIT-495.md)) made the basis of moral systems and Nowak ([LIT-501](../literature.d/LIT-501.md),
  [THEORY-567](THEORY-567.md)) gave a rule.
- Tomasello ([LIT-125](../literature.d/LIT-125.md), [NOTE-185](../notes.d/NOTE-185.md); [THEORY-495](THEORY-495.md)) argues that tit-for-tat reciprocity yields
  gratitude, not obligation. Here guilt is the regulating emotion of exactly
  that reciprocity. The two disagree on whether reciprocity can be a source
  of moral emotion beyond liking and gratitude.
- Morality-as-cooperation takes its exchange domain from here ([LIT-503](../literature.d/LIT-503.md)).
  Curry's list for that domain (trust, gratitude, punishment, revenge,
  forgiveness) is close to Trivers's.
- Moral disciplining ([LIT-101](../literature.d/LIT-101.md), [NOTE-132](../notes.d/NOTE-132.md), A1) rests on the premise that
  reciprocal cooperation requires delaying gratification, which is the time
  lag between giving and return here.
