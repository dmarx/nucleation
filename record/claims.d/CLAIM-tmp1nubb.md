# CLAIM-tmp1nubb: An implicit contract cannot be read off consistent behaviour: arrangements with the same ordinary interaction differ in how they respond to interruption, a substitute provider, competing demands, removal of an enforcer, withdrawal of a discretionary contribution and a request to renegotiate, and only those responses show whether a dependence is regulated, by what, and on whose terms

## The claim

Where a contract is implicit ([CLAIM-tmp0d1fq](CLAIM-tmp0d1fq.md)), there is no text to read its
terms from, and the ordinary run of the relationship does not show them
either: "Two systems might exhibit identical ordinary interaction while
differing significantly in how their relationships are organized" (A20
§14). What shows them is how the arrangement answers a perturbation. A20
§14 and A22 §15 list the probes; together:

| Probe | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Interrupt the expected service | "Whether reliance or dependency exists" |
| Restore the service | "Whether the dependent capacity can recover" |
| Substitute another provider | "Whether reliance concerns the function or its particular provider" |
| Introduce competing demands | "Whether a precedence arrangement governs conflict" |
| Vary the severity of a deviation | "Whether responses reveal differentiated tolerance" |
| Disable feedback between participants | "Whether the relationship is maintained through their interaction" |
| Remove an external enforcer | "Whether maintenance is endogenous or externally supplied" |
| Withdraw discretionary assistance | "Whether an informal contribution has become required" |
| Request renegotiation | "Whether revision is genuinely available" |
| Measure performance independently | "Whether existing indicators conceal transferred burdens" |

Two consequences the turns draw:

1. **Breach is the natural probe** (A20 §9). "An implicit contract may
   become legible only when its expected regularity fails"; the parties may
   then discover "That it depended upon a service it had never
   represented" or "That the contract's conditions of suspension or
   revision had never been established". Exploitation is one such event,
   which is the owner's U17 on reading (b) ([CLAIM-tmppx64t](CLAIM-tmppx64t.md)).
2. **Compliance underdetermines the mechanism** (A22 §16). The same
   compliance can come from internalization, mutual accommodation,
   strategic compliance, dependency, enforcement, constitutive
   stabilization or suppression, and "These mechanisms can coexist". So
   "Productive institutionalization" and "Exploitative
   institutionalization" can both be stable, legitimate in the culture and
   quiet, and "Their difference may lie primarily in the counterfactual
   organization of the relationship."

## Relation to the record

This is the record's method applied to contracts. [CLAIM-364](CLAIM-364.md) holds that
processes conditioned never to object and processes that negotiate part
"only under intervention, when a constituent dissents, or in their
history"; [CLAIM-718](CLAIM-718.md) takes a normative verdict as a premise and looks for
the organizational difference between relationships with the same
behaviour and outcome. A20 cites [CLAIM-522](CLAIM-522.md) for "observing a maintained
organization" versus "identifying the processes through which it is
maintained". That claim adds a limit the probes should respect:
interventions on state, recovery included, cannot show where maintenance is
realized, so "Restore the service" shows whether capacity recovers but not
whose process does the maintaining; removing an enforcer, an intervention
on a mechanism, can.

## What it does not say

- Not that the probes are to be run on people. A22 §15: "They need not
  involve deliberately exploiting or harming people."
- Not that every probe applies to every arrangement. Which ones apply is
  fixed by what the arrangement is claimed to be.

A test of the claim, the matched-behaviour comparison A22 §21 and A24 §23
propose, is [LEAD-tmp5l71z](../leads.d/LEAD-tmp5l71z.md).

say which achievement sustains it. The earlier entries are the idea's
