# NOTE-tmpi3yt0: Simon — The Architecture of Complexity

## Contribution

A lecture to the American Philosophical Society proposing that one road to
a non-trivial theory of complex systems is a theory of hierarchy. It joins
four arguments: hierarchy is common; hierarchies evolve faster than
non-hierarchic systems of the same size; hierarchies are nearly
decomposable; and near decomposability makes them describable. It is not a
technical paper. Proofs and data are in cited work.

## Key insight

Complex systems are built, and persist, as wholes of stable parts whose
inside runs faster than their outside. The two consequences, faster
evolution and simpler description, both come from that one fact.

## Assumptions

- **Pragmatic holism.** The whole is more than the sum of its parts "not in
  an ultimate, metaphysical sense, but in the important pragmatic sense
  that, given the properties of the parts and the laws of their
  interaction, it is not a trivial matter to infer the properties of the
  whole".
- **Arbitrary floor.** Where partitioning stops, and what counts as
  elementary, "is somewhat arbitrary".
- **Random assembly with stable intermediates.** The evolution model assumes
  parts arrive at a constant rate and disperse with constant probability
  unless a stable state is reached. Simon calls the assumptions "not
  particularly realistic" and says they understate the advantage of
  hierarchy.

## Key results

- **Hierarchy, widened (Hierarchic systems).**
  - Defined as a system "composed of interrelated subsystems, each of the
    latter being, in turn, hierarchic in structure until we reach some
    lowest level of elementary subsystem".
  - It includes "systems in which there is no relation of subordination
    among subsystems". "In fact, even in human organizations, the formal
    hierarchy exists only on paper".
  - **Span** is the number of subsystems at a level. A diamond or a gas is a
    "flat" hierarchy.
  - Social hierarchies are identified "by observing who interacts with
    whom". The spatial and social views "can be reconciled by defining
    hierarchy in terms of intensity of interaction". "One of the
    interesting characteristics of nerve cells and telephone wires is that
    they permit very specific strong interactions at great distances."
  - Books and music are hierarchies too.
- **The watchmakers (Evolution of complex systems).**
  - Hora builds from subassemblies of about ten parts; Tempus builds each
    watch of about 1,000 parts in one piece, and an interruption scatters
    it. With an interruption probability of .01 per part, Tempus takes
    about 4,000 times as long.
  - "The time required for the evolution of a complex form from simple
    elements depends critically on the numbers and distribution of
    potential intermediate stable forms."
  - No teleology: "Direction is provided to the scheme by the stability of
    the complex forms, once these come into existence. But this is nothing
    more than survival of the fittest—i.e., of the stable."
  - Problem solving is the same process. Cues that signal progress are the
    stable subassemblies (the clicking safe: 10 × 50 tries instead of
    about 10^20).
  - Empires: "if one would be Alexander, one should be born into a world
    where large stable political systems already exist."
- **Near decomposability.**
  - Two propositions: "(a) in a nearly decomposable system, the short-run
    behavior of each of the component subsystems is approximately
    independent of the short-run behavior of the other components; (b) in
    the long run, the behavior of any one of the components depends in
    only an aggregate way on the behavior of the other components."
  - The worked example is a building of rooms divided into cubicles. Within
    hours each room equalizes; over days the building does. "After the
    intraroom short-run equilibria have been reached, a single thermometer
    in each room will be adequate".
  - The matrix of interaction coefficients is block-diagonal, with
    off-diagonal entries below some small ε. The theorem is from Simon &
    Ando (1961), cited, not reproduced.
  - "In the dynamics of social systems, where members of a system
    communicate with and influence other members, near decomposability is
    generally very prominent."
  - Limits on span in social systems: "a human being is more nearly a
    serial than a parallel information-processing system".
  - **Frequency separation in organizations:** "It is probably true that in
    social as in physical systems, the higher frequency dynamics are
    associated with the subsystems, the lower frequency dynamics with the
    larger systems." Executives' planning horizon is "generally believed" to
    lengthen with rank, and "It is probably also true that both the average
    duration of an interaction between executives and the average interval
    between interactions is greater at higher than at lower levels."
- **Description.**
  - Hierarchic systems are redundant and so describable briefly. Few kinds of
    subsystem, near decomposability (the "empty world hypothesis": "most
    things are only weakly connected with most other things"), and recoding
    a time path as the law that generates it.
  - "If there are important systems in the world that are complex without
    being hierarchic, they may to a considerable extent escape our
    observation and our understanding." Simon leaves open which way the
    dependence runs.
  - **State against process descriptions:** blueprints against recipes.
    "While RNA is a blueprint for protein, protein is a recipe for
    metabolism." Problem solving is translating between the two.
  - Ontogeny partially recapitulates phylogeny where descriptions are stored
    as processes, and so does teaching.

## Concepts

- **Hierarchy (Simon's sense).** Successive sets of interrelated
  subsystems, with or without authority between them.
- **Span.** The number of subsystems at a level; a flat hierarchy has a
  wide span.
- **Stable intermediate form.** A subassembly that persists if the
  assembly process is interrupted.
- **Near decomposability.** Interactions within subsystems much stronger
  than interactions between them (a block-diagonal matrix with small
  off-diagonal terms).
- **Empty world hypothesis.** Most things are only weakly connected with
  most other things.
- **State and process description.** A description of what a thing is, and
  one of how to make it.
