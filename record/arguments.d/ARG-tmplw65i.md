# ARG-tmplw65i: Inbound references per code and turns per filed entry show which structures anchor and that drift falls as anchoring keeps up with arrival

## The inference

CASE-tmp4hpm7 holds the two measures. Inbound references per code would
tell an anchor from a structure that only persists, which bears on
CLAIM-185: a persistent structure is available as an anchor. Turns per
filed entry, set against the re-derivations and unannounced changes of
thesis the workbench records, would show whether drift tracks the ratio
of arrival to anchoring, which is CLAIM-186. If the counts move as the
theses say, the theses are supported.

## Critical questions

- **Would the evidence be expected if the hypotheses were false?** In
  part, yes. The record's grammar rewards coding, linking and announcing
  changes, and "A measure built from the same grammar will register more
  coding, linking and announcing, whether or not the argument drifted
  less by the participants' own standards."
- **Does it tell the hypotheses apart from their rivals?** Not by itself.
  CLAIM-186's own rival, that pace alone and not anchoring reduces drift,
  needs a slow exchange with nothing anchored, which CASE-tmp4hpm7 does
  not have.
- **Does the measure measure what it is said to?** This is the question
  that is open. CLAIM-190: "The anchoring measures are computed in the
  grammar that shapes the argument, so by themselves they cannot certify
  that drift fell: drift must also be judged under standards the record
  did not supply." The general forms are CLAIM-264 (equivalence under a
  probe family is coarser than identity unless the family is rich enough)
  and CLAIM-266 (a probe family that supplies a system's training signal
  cannot also certify it). The record first argued the point for
  translation in CLAIM-149 and CLAIM-142. It is distinct from CLAIM-181,
  which says the record shapes what the argument becomes. This says it
  also shapes what its success looks like.

CASE-071 is the documented instance, made by selection rather than
training: translations picked by a neural metric are rated better by that
metric and its neighbours, and human raters find them no better. Its
limit is that it is machine translation scored for accuracy, not an
argument scored for drift. CASE-084, a community whose record traces
every commitment and whose vocabulary leaves out the right explanation,
illustrates the same worry.

## What would settle it

Inbound-reference counts and turns per anchor that agree with drift judged
blind, by raters using the exchange's own standards, on stretches held out
from the grammar's influence: for example, re-derivations and unannounced
changes of thesis coded before the record existed. That remedy treats
raters as the criterion. That is CLAIM-217's position, CLAIM-206 holds that
human judgements are one probe family among others, and the dispute is
open. So the argument stays Proposed.
