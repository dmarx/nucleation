# THEORY-1522: Ahn reads Bainbridge's ironies of automation as holding that automation takes the routine work and leaves the operator the atypical cases, recalls the operator at its failure, when the system is least predictable, and leaves the operator's manual and cognitive skills degraded from disuse; and, as his own remedies for medical education, he proposes building core skills in phases free of AI before AI is introduced, and simulations in which AI failures are injected

## Source

Ahn (2025), [LIT-2222](../literature.d/LIT-2222.md); read in [NOTE-1713](../notes.d/NOTE-1713.md).

## What was actually shown

Nothing was tested. The article states its reading of Bainbridge and its
remedies.

- **The atypical cases** (§4): automation "often takes over the more
  straightforward, routine tasks, leaving human operators primarily
  responsible for managing complex, atypical situations, and edge cases".
- **The hand-back** (§5): it "positions the human to intervene during
  unexpected or complex failures, precisely when the system is least
  predictable and intervention is most critical".
- **Skill degraded from disuse** (§5): an operator "out-of-the-loop" may
  "find their manual or cognitive skills degraded from disuse".
- **Ahn's remedies**: “AI-free” learning phases, after which "AI tools can
  then be introduced" (§2); simulations with deliberately injected flaws, to
  "prepare students for these high-stakes, low-frequency events" (§5).

## What this does not say

- What Bainbridge wrote. Her text was not reached; this is Ahn's paraphrase,
  and his quoted phrases "deskilling" and "worst possible time" are not
  located in it.
- That Bainbridge proposed these remedies. Ahn does not say she did.
- That the remedies work, or that the ironies of industrial control hold in
  medicine. Both are assumed.
