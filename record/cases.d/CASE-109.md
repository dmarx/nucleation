# CASE-109: Two people with the same demanding commitment, the same output and the same effort in the work, who differ in how their intrapersonal conflict is settled: one escalates it to deliberate override, the other's processes accommodate one another

## The case

A44 of the exchange of 2026-10-17: "A person can exert enormous effort
pursuing an important commitment without that effort indicating
alienation or failure of self-governance." The owner, at U45: "I think
this is an interesting example, especially if we assume that the effort
is due to intra-personal strain, and contrast with another person who
experiences less intrapersonal conflict in pursuit of the same
commitment."

A45 worked it on "the same difficult writing project": "Both care about
finishing it. Both experience competing impulses. Both produce work of
equal quality, and both exert substantial intellectual effort."

- **Person A, repeated escalation.** A "repeatedly uses higher-level
  inhibition or deliberation to resolve the conflict."
- **Person B, local coordination.** B's "constituent processes have
  developed ways of accommodating each other: perhaps novelty-seeking
  finds expression through the creative task, or an impulse for rest is
  accommodated by a locally maintained work–break rhythm." B "need not
  value every alternative identically across all internal processes.
  What matters is that **the processes can coexist within a workable
  policy without persistent coercive override**."

Held fixed: the commitment, the task and its difficulty, the quality of
the output, and the effort spent on the work itself. Varied: whether the
recurring conflict is settled by a higher-level process or among the
processes in conflict. In B the higher level "remains available but is
not routinely required" (A45's figure).

## The variation that discriminates

Tax or interrupt the higher-level process, for example with a demanding
second task, and leave the writing unchanged. The claim the case serves
predicts that only Person A's work degrades, since only A's commitment is
held up by repeated recruitment of that process. The variation is built
from A45's own test, "Remove the higher-level supervisor temporarily", for
which it predicts that "Learned coordination often persists", and from its
requirement of evidence on "the causal contribution of supervisory
intervention", compared "with similar execution costs".

## Beside the writer cases

[CASE-013](CASE-013.md) and [CASE-051](CASE-051.md) are one writer on three afternoons: avoidance
wins, a device enforces, and an absorbed afternoon on which the writer
reports "no struggle and no rule". Effort varies with the route. Here two
people both work hard, so effort in the work is held high in both and
only the cost of settling the conflict differs. That separates execution
effort from conflict-resolution effort, which the three afternoons
cannot. Person B is not [CASE-051](CASE-051.md)'s absorbed writer: B's processes still
disagree, and B is not synchronized in [TERM-050](../terms.d/TERM-050.md)'s sense. B is
synchronized in the broader sense of [TERM-061](../terms.d/TERM-061.md). Person A is the
divided agent of [CLAIM-261](../claims.d/CLAIM-261.md), who acts as one and pays for it.

## What it can show

That "effort" in the record's effort claims names two costs, and that a
case can hold one fixed while the other varies. It is what
[CLAIM-358](../claims.d/CLAIM-358.md) needs: the claim is about the second cost only.

## What it cannot show

- That Person B exists, or that people differ along this axis rather
  than in execution effort. It is stipulated.
- That B's arrangement is B's own. The case is the same from outside if
  B's dissenting processes were conditioned never to object
  ([CLAIM-364](../claims.d/CLAIM-364.md)). The owner's authenticity criterion
  ([CLAIM-362](../claims.d/CLAIM-362.md)) needs more than this case gives.
- That the processes are separable modules. A45's caption: "Conceptual
  comparison, not a claim about identifiable neural modules."
