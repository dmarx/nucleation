# CLAIM-022: Explaining practical efficacy does not settle whose the will is

## The claim

"A manipulator could alter an agent's regulatory architecture. A coercive
institution could impose durable governing relations." Velleman's objection to
Frankfurt ([LIT-292](../literature.d/LIT-292.md)) applies to higher-order organizations as much as to
higher-order desires.

## Note of 2026-10-27: grounded in the objection it applies

The status review of this date ([ADR-057](../decisions.d/ADR-057.md)) found this Active claim grounded
in [THEORY-120](../theory.d/THEORY-120.md), Frankfurt's account of ownership, which is Proposed. A
disclaimer does not rest on the account it declines to settle. What the
body applies is Velleman's objection to Frankfurt ([LIT-292](../literature.d/LIT-292.md), read in full),
generalized from desires to organizations, so that is now its ground.

## Note of 2026-10-31: the same limit in a process account of authorship

Bencherki (2017; [LIT-1901](../literature.d/LIT-1901.md), read in [NOTE-1482](../notes.d/NOTE-1482.md); [THEORY-1285](../theory.d/THEORY-1285.md))
gives an account of how an action comes to count as a person's or an
organization's. "A being only possesses its components from a
particular perspective: their contribution to its individuation process;
possession is never complete or univocal" (p. 782). "Deciding on an
author is the outcome of individuation, not its starting point" (p.
786). That explains attribution as this claim's account explains
efficacy: by what an action is allowed to contribute to. Bencherki meets
this claim's limit. His verdict that the army's taking of the daycare
work is "usurpation" (pp. 791–792) has force only if the spouses'
authorship was real before the army's attribution denied it. The account
does not supply that standard ([THEORY-1285](../theory.d/THEORY-1285.md), "What this does not
say"). This claim's disclaimer holds, in another literature: explaining
how action comes to be counted as someone's does not settle whose it is.
