summary: >-
  Wells (2012), <https://iep.utm.edu/sen-cap/>. A Sen-centred overview. It
  presents the approach as Sen's answer to four concerns: conversion
  variation, adaptive preferences, the value of options not taken, and
  informational exclusion. It sets out his critiques of the three pillars
  of utilitarianism (act-consequentialism, welfarism, sum-ranking) and of
  Rawlsian resourcism, and his two-stage structure, valuation then
  evaluation. In that structure selection is a social-choice exercise of
  public reasoning, able to reach partial agreement through "intersection"
  and "ranges of weights" (§3b). It reports four criticisms (illiberalism,
  under-theorisation, individualism, information gaps) with Sen's replies,
  and closes on Nussbaum and on how she differs from Sen. It never
  mentions the well-being/agency distinction, and it cites none of Sen's
  human-rights papers.

# LIT-tmp7j8wd: Sen's Capability Approach

Wells (2012), *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy (ISSN 2161-0002); originally published 24 Apr 2012, last updated 25 Apr 2012 (per the IEP page as archived 1 May 2012); text unchanged since* — <https://iep.utm.edu/sen-cap/>

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-09-28 at the owner's request, as one of nine texts assembled to represent Amartya Sen's
capability approach without a full reading of *The Idea of Justice*. `published:` is the first appearance
([ADR-002](../decisions.d/ADR-002.md)).

It was filed `Deferred`, unread. [NOTE-tmpd74t0](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpd74t0.md) is the close reading of 2026-09-28, and it placed the work: **Active** — worth reading as the most Sen-specific short account in the batch, especially §3b's partial-agreement mechanism and §5's criticisms with Sen's replies. It dates from 2012 and was never revised, and it omits agency freedom entirely, so for concepts it needs the SEP entry beside it.
