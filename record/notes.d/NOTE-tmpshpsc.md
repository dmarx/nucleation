# NOTE-tmpshpsc: Block — Can only meat machines be conscious?

## Contribution

A framing that turns the attribution of consciousness to AI and to simple
animals into a forced trade-off, the label meat-neutral for the major
theories, and an evolutionary pointer to where the realizer may lie.

## Key results

1. **Theories.** "Current theories of consciousness are 'meat-neutral'"
   (abstract, p. 298).
2. **Roles and realizers.** "The meat hypothesis is that our
   consciousness does depend directly on the subcomputational properties"
   (p. 299); "the problem with functionalism is that it focuses on the
   roles, neglecting the realizers" (p. 300); "The role and the realizer
   hypotheses are equally plausible" (p. 301); "From the point of view of
   the meat hypothesis, functionalism has it backwards" (p. 301).
3. **First animals.** "electrochemical processing has a functional
   advantage over purely electrical processing" (p. 304); "We should be
   investigating what is special about electrochemical computation." (p.
   298, margin).
4. **Conclusions.** "there is an opposition between AI and simple
   animals" (p. 306); "There is little doubt that some aspect of our
   biology is necessary for consciousness in us" (p. 307); "suffering may
   not require consciousness" (p. 307).

## Assumptions

Phenomenal consciousness is real; attribution must extrapolate from the
human case; a realizer is a mechanism, not a stuff; gradualism in the
evolution of consciousness.

## Concepts

Computational functionalism; meat hypothesis; role and realizer;
meat-neutral theory; extrapolation base; electrochemical and electrical
nervous systems; ctenophores.

## Limitations

An opinion piece; argues only that the meat hypothesis is as plausible as
functionalism; the electrochemical realizer offered on weak reason by its
own account; the phylogeny rests on two 2023 papers.
