# ADR-075: A dated note's heading says what job it does: the jobs tools read have reserved headings, a note doing one of those jobs takes its heading, and the worklist lists notes that do one under another

## Context

A dated note is a section in the body of a CLAIM, THEORY, TERM, QUESTION,
CASE or ARG, headed `## Note of YYYY-MM-DD: ...`. It records something about
the entry that is not a revision: a reading that bears on it, what holds it
back, a concession it was filed with. A revision goes in `history:`. Dated
notes are the record's commonest form of after-the-fact reasoning, and two
of their jobs are machine-read.

- **`what holds it back`** ([ADR-057](ADR-057.md)). A pass that keeps an entry Proposed
  writes the blocker under this heading. The worklist lists the entry as
  held for 14 days and brings it back sooner when something the note names
  moves.
- **`filed with its concessions`** ([ADR-057](ADR-057.md), [ADR-059](ADR-059.md)). A target filed with
  an objection already in its text says so under this heading, naming the
  objection. The worklist then counts the objection as written in.

Measured on main at 172d170a5:

| Heading | Dated notes |
|---|---|
| `what holds it back` | 310 |
| `filed with its concessions` | 8 |
| anything else | 1,648 |
| total, in 1,029 entries | 1,966 |

Of the 1,648 free-text notes, 62 contain blocker language: "holds it back",
"waits on", "kept Proposed", "what would promote", "until … is read". This
is a heuristic match, and some of the 62 will be history, not a standing
wait. Three failure modes show up.

- **A compound heading.** [CLAIM-082](../claims.d/CLAIM-082.md) and [CLAIM-162](../claims.d/CLAIM-162.md) head a note "narrowed;
  what holds it back". The worklist's pattern needs the reserved phrase as
  the whole heading, so it misses both. Their narrowing belongs in
  `history:`.
- **A note with two jobs.** A note headed for a reading ("the two Flack 2006
  papers, not reached", "Edmondson 1996, located and not fetched") goes on
  to say what the entry now waits on. The worklist sees the reading only if
  a NOTE records it, and it never sees the wait.
- **A blocker under a topical heading.** The twenty-sixth pass found
  [CLAIM-465](../claims.d/CLAIM-465.md)'s note of 2026-11-04 and the 2026-11-03 notes on [THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md),
  [THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md) and [THEORY-054](../theory.d/THEORY-054.md) doing a blocker's work under headings such as
  "the evidence its promote_when asks for". They surfaced only because those
  entries came up in the pass for other reasons.

In every case the entry looks to the worklist like something it is not:
ready when it is held, or held on an old note when a newer one says
something else. The status-review README already says "the heading is
exact, because the worklist reads it", but nothing checks it, and the
read-and-file skill does not say it at all. A rule that only a careful
writer follows is the kind of guard CLAUDE.md calls a bug report about the
workflow.

## Decision

1. **Reserved headings.**
   - **The reserved list.** A dated note's heading is free text, except for
     the reserved headings that tools read: today `what holds it back` and
     `filed with its concessions`.
   - **Exact match.** A reserved heading is the whole text after the date,
     in lower case, with nothing before or after it.
   - **One list, closed.** The reserved headings are a single closed list,
     kept in two places that must agree: `record/status-review/worklist.py`
     and the status-review README, which gives a table with each heading,
     its job and the tool that reads it. A new one is added by decision,
     the way a topic is added to the `topics` vocabulary.
2. **A note doing a reserved job takes the reserved heading.**
   - **What counts as the blocker job.** A note that says what holds the
     entry back, or what would promote, settle or answer it, is a blocker
     note, whatever else it says.
   - **What counts as the concession job.** A note on a target that says it
     was filed with an objection's concession already in its text is a
     concession note.
   - **Topical detail goes in the body.** A heading that needs to say more,
     such as which reading prompted the note, says it in the body's first
     sentence, not in the heading.
3. **One note, one job.**
   - **Split two jobs.** A note that records a reading and also states a
     wait is two notes on the same date: a free-text one for the reading,
     and a blocker note for the wait.
   - **Revisions go in history.** A revision's account goes in `history:`,
     never in a dated note's heading.
4. **The worklist reads the headings, and lists likely misses.**
   - **Tolerance.** The worklist matches the reserved headings ignoring case
     and surrounding white space. It does not match a compound heading.
   - **A new section.** It gains a section, "Free-text notes that may do a
     reserved job". It lists every free-text dated note whose text has
     blocker or concession language and is dated on or after the entry's
     latest reserved note of that kind.
   - **Not a must-be-empty section.** The match is a heuristic, so a pass
     reviews the list rather than clearing it to zero. The section sits with
     the work, after the moved-since blockers.
5. **Fixing a heading.**
   - **No version bump.** Retitling a note to its reserved heading, or
     splitting one note into two, does not change what the entry says, so
     it takes no version bump.
   - **An audit trail for splits.** A split leaves the original date on
     both halves and a one-line comment under the blocker note,
     `<!-- split from the note of YYYY-MM-DD headed "..." (ADR-075) -->`.
6. **The writers' instructions carry the rule.**
   - **Where.** The read-and-file skill's filing steps and the
     status-review README's "Kept Proposed" and "Concessions" rules state
     the reserved headings and the one-job rule.
   - **Briefs inherit it.** A brief that sends a reader or reviewer to write
     dated notes inherits the rule from those instructions, as [ADR-074](ADR-074.md) does
     for lawful copies.

## Scope

Only dated notes in the bodies of CLAIM, THEORY, TERM, QUESTION, CASE and
ARG entries are covered. NOTEs, the NOTE scheme's per-reading entries, are a
different thing: the worklist reads them through their frontmatter (`paper:`,
`status:`), which this decision leaves alone. Dated notes in decisions and
principles are not read by any tool and are not covered.

## Alternatives

- **A frontmatter field.** For example, `blockers:` with a date and text for
  each entry. This is the most machine-friendly option. It was rejected for
  now for three reasons:
  - it duplicates prose that has to stay readable in the body;
  - it needs a schema change in luria rather than in this record;
  - two reserved jobs do not yet justify a field.

  If the reserved list grows past a handful, this should be reconsidered.
- **A directive comment** such as `<!-- blocker: ... -->`. Rejected: the
  directive vocabulary is luria's (`inactive-ok:` and friends), not the
  record's to extend.
- **Keep the convention and nothing else.** Rejected. It is what produced the
  62, and the twenty-sixth pass found its four only by accident.
- **Make the worklist read any note with blocker language as a blocker.**
  Rejected. A heuristic deciding an entry's standing would hold entries on
  history ("was held on X until …") and release none. It is used only to list
  candidates for a person to sort.

## Consequences

- **The worklist change.** The change in point 4 is made in
  `record/status-review/worklist.py` with tests. It lands after
  dmarx/nucleation#414, which touches the same functions.
- **A sweep of the 62.** One sweep, its own pull request ([ADR-071](ADR-071.md)), retitles
  or splits the 62 notes, starting with [CLAIM-082](../claims.d/CLAIM-082.md) and [CLAIM-162](../claims.d/CLAIM-162.md). A note that
  turns out to be history keeps its free-text heading.
- **Instructions updated.** The read-and-file skill and the status-review
  README are updated in the same change as the worklist.
- **[ADR-057](ADR-057.md).** Its two reserved headings are unchanged. [ADR-057](ADR-057.md) gets a dated
  note pointing here for the list and the one-job rule.
