# CLAIM-017: What survives translation is the invariants, and approximately preserved relations, of communicative transformation

## The claim

A110, "The final thesis": "**What Survives Translation? The invariants—and
approximately preserved relations—of communicative transformation.** A
communicative realization is identified through an overlapping system of
observational distinctions. Translation and reconstruction act on that system
through directed stochastic transformations. What survives depends on the
structural invariants of those transformations, the information needed to recover
them, and the interpretive capacities of the receiving system. **Meaning is not
simply carried across representations; its defining relationships are selectively
conserved, reconstructed, and sometimes transformed.**"

## How it was replaced

U39 ("draft a manuscript", 21:33) produced C7, *What Survives Translation?
Symmetry, Observational Context, and Information Transport Across
Representations*. Its last sentence answers differently: "What survives
translation is not necessarily its wording or even its proposition, but the
communicative distinctions and relationships that a receiving system can still
reconstruct and act upon" ([CLAIM-115](CLAIM-115.md)). The answer moved from invariants of the
transformation to what the receiver can recover and use. The subtitle kept
"Symmetry", and the argument's own thesis had already named "the interpretive
capacities of the receiving system" third. The move is a change of emphasis, but
it decides which of the two is the criterion, and nothing in the transcript
argues it. A100's conclusion ("for particular observers and communicative
purposes") is its nearest precedent.
