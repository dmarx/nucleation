# THEORY-tmpreimy: Moral judgments are made relative to the social relationship an act is taken to occur within: each of four relational models (communal sharing, authority ranking, equality matching, market pricing) carries a moral motive (Unity, Hierarchy, Equality, Proportionality), so the same act can be required under one and wrong under another

## Source

Rai & Fiske (2011), [LIT-507](../literature.d/LIT-507.md) (*Psychological Review* 118(1)), read in
[NOTE-405](../notes.d/NOTE-405.md).

## What was actually shown

**The four motives (pp. 61–64).** Unity (communal sharing): care for and
protect the in-group, give by need, avoid contamination. Hierarchy
(authority ranking): subordinates defer, superiors lead and protect. Equality
(equality matching): even balance, in-kind reciprocity, turn-taking.
Proportionality (market pricing): reward and punishment in proportion to
merit, contribution or guilt.

**Fairness is relative (pp. 65–66).** Equal treatment is fair only under
Equality. It is wrong under Hierarchy or Proportionality, and stealing may be
"nonsensical" under Unity or Hierarchy.

**Violence (p. 65).** Harm can be praiseworthy: against out-group threats
(Unity), under orders (Hierarchy), in retaliation (Equality), or as a
necessary cost (Proportionality). Cited cross-cultural and vignette work
(Cohen et al. 2006; Kelly et al. 2007; Cikara et al. 2010) supports
acceptability of violence following the motive.

**Purity (pp. 66–67).** "The foundations of in-group/loyalty and
purity/sanctity are actually variants of the same social-relational
regulation motive" (p. 66).

**What could have come out otherwise.** The cited framing studies (Clark &
Waddell 1985; Fiske & Tetlock 1997 on taboo trade-offs) could have found the
same act judged alike under every framing. The paper reports that they did
not; it runs none itself.

## What this does not say

- **That the model in force can be identified in advance.** The paper gives
  no way to fix it before observing the judgment, so the central claim
  risks being unfalsifiable.
- **That purity is Unity.** It is argued from cases. Food taboos with no group
  boundary, most of Haidt, Koller & Dias's harmless wrongs, fit less easily.
- **That there are exactly four universal moral motives.** The moral extension
  of relational models theory is asserted, resting on that theory's prior
  validation.
- **That where no relationship exists there is moral indifference.** Two
  ethnographic anecdotes support it (p. 64).
- **What ought to be done.** The ethical-implications section mixes
  description and prescription, and the authors flag the is–ought question
  (fn. 4).
