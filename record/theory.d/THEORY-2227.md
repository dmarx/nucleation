# THEORY-2227: Frontoparietal rivalry activity tracks report

Brascamp, Blake and Knapen (2015), [LIT-3089](../literature.d/LIT-3089.md), read in [NOTE-2317](../notes.d/NOTE-2317.md).

## The account

In the authors' words, "perceptual switches do not always rely on
executive brain areas", and "noticing the change may rely on brain regions
dedicated to behavioral responses." The evidence is a rivalry condition in
which switches occur, inferred from each observer's alternation
statistics, while "detection of switches in the same color condition could
not be distinguished from chance", and in which the fronto-parietal switch
response matched a prediction from reportability alone.

## Its limits

- It measured a switch-locked BOLD transient, not which percept is held.
- It separates a perceptual change from awareness of that change. Both
  dominant fields are seen, so it is not a case of experience without
  access, and the matched fields may leave no experienced change at all.
- Six observers in the scanner, two of them authors.

## Bearing

On [QUESTION-048](../questions.d/QUESTION-048.md), the no-report kind: removing awareness of the switch
removes the executive signal, which supports [THEORY-094](THEORY-094.md)'s diagnosis but
does not meet its promote_when. On [QUESTION-061](../questions.d/QUESTION-061.md), background only: a
perceptual event absent, by BOLD, from executive regions, with the
content itself reported at every probe.
