# LIT-tmpozu5y: Demand characteristics confound the rubber hand illusion

Lush, P. (2020). *Collabra: Psychology* 6(1): 22. doi:10.1525/collabra.325. Preregistration osf.io/89m7j; data, materials and output osf.io/9c8mq.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmptyehi](../notes.d/NOTE-tmptyehi.md).*

- **The assumption tested.** Rubber-hand studies treat higher agreement
  with "illusion" than "control" statements, or with synchronous than
  asynchronous stroking, as experience not owed to suggestion. That
  holds only if expectancies are matched across statements and
  conditions. Riemer et al. (2019) are quoted: no psychometric work
  supports the control items.
- **Quasi-control.** Following Orne (1969), participants were told about
  the procedure and shown it, but not given it, and rated how much they
  expected each statement's effect.
- **Result.** Synchronous illusion expectancy 1.7, synchronous control
  0.0, asynchronous illusion 0.4, with dz 1.40 and 1.05. Every reported
  t, mean and SD was reproduced from the posted data.
- **The claim, and its limit.** "RHI control methods are not fit for
  purpose". But "These results do not provide evidence that the RHI is a
  suggestion effect, but rather that existing claims to the contrary are
  invalid."
- **The motivating account.** The illusion as "phenomenological control":
  top-down control of experience to meet expectancies, felt as
  involuntary, as in hypnotic suggestion. Its support is a separate
  preprint (Lush et al. 2019), cited and not shown here.
- **Implicit measures.** Proprioceptive drift and skin conductance are
  argued to be open to the same confound, and to get their link to
  ownership only through the reports.
