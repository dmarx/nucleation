# CASE-184: The central defeat test as stated: human judgements of fidelity or shared kind, predicted by the theory's judged measures against a text-similarity baseline

## The case

Four thesis-level defeat conditions in the pragmatic-transport line state
one test.

- [CLAIM-115](../claims.d/CLAIM-115.md): "predicted as well by propositional or embedding similarity
  as by measured preservation of pragmatic observables (stance, footing,
  alignment, expected uptake)".
- [CLAIM-139](../claims.d/CLAIM-139.md): "predicted as well by topic-factor, embedding or surface
  similarity as by independently measured relational profiles".
- [CLAIM-005](../claims.d/CLAIM-005.md): "Embedding or propositional similarity between source and
  rendering predicts held-out human communicative-fidelity judgements as
  well as the context-indexed distribution distance does."
- [CLAIM-078](../claims.d/CLAIM-078.md): "Structural and decision-theoretic measures add no
  prediction of communicative-identity changes beyond text similarity".

So the test has three parts.

- **The criterion**: human judgements of communicative fidelity, or of
  shared kind, for a set of renderings.
- **The predictor**: the theory's measure, mostly human judgements of
  stance, footing and the like for the same renderings ([CASE-040](CASE-040.md)'s
  interpretive judgements are of "tone, stance, solidarity, and
  authority").
- **The rival**: a machine score of propositional, embedding, topic or
  surface similarity.

The theses survive if the predictor beats the rival.

## What it can show

That judged pragmatic observables predict fidelity judgements better than
text similarity does. Failing that would defeat the theses.

## What it cannot show

It has not been run. As stated, its rival is one that no account the
record names holds ([CLAIM-233](../claims.d/CLAIM-233.md)), and its predictor and criterion share a
method that the rival does not ([CLAIM-232](../claims.d/CLAIM-232.md)). So a pass does not separate
the theses from functional, relevance-theoretic or speech-act accounts.
That is [ARG-026](../arguments.d/ARG-026.md), Rejected. [CASE-055](CASE-055.md) is the repair: a behavioural
criterion, predictors rated by other raters, and rivals that hold static
stance or proposition with force.

Filed 2026-10-28 by the [ADR-061](../decisions.d/ADR-061.md) migration, as the premise [CLAIM-232](../claims.d/CLAIM-232.md)'s and
[CLAIM-233](../claims.d/CLAIM-233.md)'s argument needed.

## Note of 2026-10-29: the conditions as revised

The quotations above are the conditions as stated when this case was filed.
At the seventh status review CLAIM-115, CLAIM-139 and CLAIM-005 were revised
for CLAIM-238: each test now fixes its observables, their weights, an
equivalence margin and a power before the data, and CLAIM-005's distance is
computed with one outcome correspondence fixed across a class of items and
scored on held-out ones (CLAIM-207). That repairs the null with no margin.
It does not repair what this case says it cannot show: the rival is still
one no account the record names holds (CLAIM-233), and predictor and
criterion still share a method (CLAIM-232). CASE-055 remains the repair for
those.
