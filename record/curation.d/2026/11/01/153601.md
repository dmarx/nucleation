**What the readings do to the record.**
- **Will-organization.** Mind-wandering, on Metzinger's account, is a
  recurring loss of the ability to suspend one's own mental processes. The
  ability is kept and its representation lost, which in structure is
  will-organization. It does not yet support CLAIM-027 (ARG-tmpmibc8,
  Proposed):
  - he attributes each episode's onset to cued goal states;
  - his 2017 chapter leaves open whether the self-model's representation
    causes, enables or only accompanies control (QUESTION-tmp7czq0).
- **His figure.** His "roughly two thirds" of waking life does not follow
  from his own figures, which give 52%.
- **Evidence for experience.** The minimal case is a "nonegoic"
  self-model with no world and no felt oneness, which narrows THEORY-063.
  - The book calls selfless consciousness empirically established, while
    its methods chapter calls remembered reports of selfless states
    "highly dubious" evidence (THEORY-tmp8nd9o).
  - QUESTION-tmpwit4l asks whether experience without self-model or
    valence would ground moral standing.
- **Dated notes.**
  - Will line: TERM-023 and nine claims and questions.
  - Experience line: QUESTION-255, CLAIM-412, CLAIM-355, QUESTION-046,
    QUESTION-048, QUESTION-254, TERM-045, TERM-082 and TERM-083.
  - Readings: THEORY-063, THEORY-064 and NOTE-1058. The two THEORYs now
    record *Being No One* as Unavailable, not Deferred.

**Inconsistencies in the book.** It gives 841 usable reports with more
than 500 printed (p. xvi), and also "1,183 written ... reports presented
in this book" (p. 17).

**For the owner.** "Artificial suffering" needs only a browser:
philarchive.org/archive/METASA-4. The readers suggest two works for later
reading: Pezzulo's 2018 commentary on the 2017 chapter, and Axelrod et
al. 2015, the first test of Metzinger's H1.
