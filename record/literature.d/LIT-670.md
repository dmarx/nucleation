# LIT-670: On Linear Mode Connectivity of Mixture-of-Experts Architectures

Viet-Hoang Tran, Van-Hoan Trinh, Khanh Vinh Bui and Tan M. Nguyen (2025),
39th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2025) —
[ARXIV-2509.11348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11348)

## Key takeaways

- **The gate's symmetry group.** G(n) = ℝ^d × ℝ × S_n acts by permuting
  experts with their gating rows and adding a common (c_W, c_b) to every
  gating row. Softmax is translation-invariant and the sum is
  permutation-invariant, so both dense and top-k MoEs are invariant
  (Propositions 3.1–3.2, on the dense open set where gate scores are
  distinct).
- **It is the whole of the gate's symmetry, generically.** For dense gating,
  if experts are pairwise distinct functions and the gate differences
  W_i − W_j are pairwise distinct, equal functions imply equal expert counts
  and parameters related by G(n) (Theorem 4.1). For top-k with k ≥ 2, the
  same holds under stronger conditions: strongly distinct experts and
  linearly independent consecutive gate differences (Theorem 4.2). Top-1
  has an extra positive-scaling symmetry and is set aside.
- **Only the permutation matters for barriers.** Adding a common
  translation to the gate leaves the loss barrier unchanged (Eq. 9,
  Proposition D.1).
- **Algorithm 1.** Order the experts by a linear assignment on centred gate
  weights or on Gram-matrix representations of each expert's weights. Then
  weight-match the hidden units inside each matched pair, as in Git
  Re-Basin. Cost O(n³ + nh³), with no data.
- **Barriers near zero.** On One Billion Word with a 12-layer GPT-2 and 4 or
  8 experts, the barrier after full matching is 0.0044–0.0066. Skipping the
  expert-order step leaves 0.14–0.86 (Table 10). On CIFAR-10 ViTs, matching
  leaves barriers at about 4–11% of naive interpolation's (Table 3).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 with the owner's mode-connectivity batch ([ADR-027](../decisions.d/ADR-027.md)). It
is the batch's mixture-of-experts case and its most careful statement of
which symmetries an architecture component has. The theorems go further
than the experiments.

**What is actually connected.** Every experiment takes a pretrained ViT or
GPT-2, replaces one feed-forward layer (first, last, or all) with a
randomly initialised MoE, freezes everything else, and fine-tunes only the
MoE from different seeds (§6.1, Appendix F). The models compared share all
weights outside the MoE. That is LMC of one inserted layer inside a fixed
network, a much easier setting than Git Re-Basin's or Theus et al.'s
([LIT-664](LIT-664.md)) independently trained networks. The paper's conclusion,
that permutation symmetry suffices for LMC in MoE models, should be read
with that scope.

It extends Git Re-Basin ([LIT-661](LIT-661.md)). Step 2 of its algorithm is Git
Re-Basin's weight matching, run per expert, and its expert-ordering step
is the same linear-assignment construction applied one level up. The
anthology reads Git Re-Basin as [ANTH-LIT-333](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/literature.d/LIT-333.md), and its practice [ANTH-SOTA-217](https://github.com/dmarx/anthology-of-the-sota/blob/main/record/practices.d/SOTA-217.md)
(align hidden-unit permutations before averaging) would need the expert
order aligned first for MoE layers. The paper takes its barrier definition
and the term "winning permutation" from Entezari et al. ([LIT-652](LIT-652.md)), and
cites Ferbach et al. ([LIT-680](LIT-680.md)) for formal guarantees of LMC under
optimal-transport alignment and Sonthalia et al. ([LIT-659](LIT-659.md)) for
star-shaped regions. Its §5.1 rests on that prior work for the step from
"the gate's symmetries are permutations" to "permutations suffice for
LMC".

It is flagged for the anthology: it carries a procedure for aligning MoE
layers before averaging.
