# LIT-2039: Rigid Foldings

Hull, T. C. (2020). Rigid Foldings. Chapter 13 of *Origametry: Mathematical Methods in Paper Folding*, Cambridge University Press, pp. 256–289. doi:10.1017/9781108778633.018.

Not read: the chapter is behind Cambridge Core's paywall (see the status note). What follows is the publisher's summary, quoted from the chapter page.

## Key takeaways

*From the publisher's summary only; unverified until read.*

- **Tools**: "the tools of reciprocal-parallel and reciprocal diagrams are
  used to establish conditions for infinitesimal and second-order rigid
  foldability".
- **Main theorem**: "a single-vertex origami crease pattern has a rigid
  folding from the unfolded state if and only if it has a nontrivial
  zero-area reciprocal diagram".
- **Degree 4**: fold-angle equations "linear when parameterized by the
  tangent of half the folding angles, also known as the Weierstrass
  transformation".
- **Intrinsic condition**: "An intrinsic condition for an origami vertex
  crease pattern to be rigidly foldable from the unfolded state is also
  given."

## Standing in the record

The pasted synthesis on constraint architectures cites it for
configuration spaces, rigidity and fold-angle equations, which matches the
summary; A omits the chapter's main theorem and its intrinsic condition.
Two neighbours were read in its place. Abel et al. (2016), [LIT-2093](LIT-2093.md),
co-authored by Hull, stands in for the intrinsic condition, fully: a
vertex with a mountain–valley assignment folds rigidly from flat exactly
when it contains a bird's foot. Foschi, Hull and Ku (2022), [LIT-2055](LIT-2055.md),
stands in for the flat-foldable degree-4 equations, which it restates
citing this book, and goes beyond them. Chen and Santangelo (2018),
[LIT-2021](LIT-2021.md), covers second-order rigidity at the flat state by another
route, self stresses rather than reciprocal diagrams, and is not a stand-in.
Nothing read covers the reciprocal-diagram machinery or the zero-area
theorem; for those the chapter itself is needed.
