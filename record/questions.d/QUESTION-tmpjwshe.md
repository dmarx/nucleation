# QUESTION-tmpjwshe: Can a population of operators whose demands are jointly infeasible under one functional organization develop a differentiated or compositional repertoire that makes them jointly feasible, without suppressing, weakening or deleting any of those demands?

## Why it is a question

CLAIM-347 separates two ways an organization can deal with incompatible
demands. It can choose among them, which leaves the complex unchanged. Or
it can change what it can attain, which enlarges the complex.
CASE-tmpuuf3p shows the second is possible. CASE-115 shows a continuous
version: splitting a shared parameter removes a two-demand frustration.
CLAIM-360 separates a larger realizable set from better choice within a
fixed one in the deep-against-shallow results.

The synthesis offered a candidate thesis, not filed as a claim:

> Some organizational conflicts are not best understood as problems of
> choosing among competing demands. They are problems of
> representational or functional organization: the existing repertoire
> cannot realize certain jointly demanded outcomes, while a
> differentiated or recomposed repertoire can.

That is stronger than saying specialization makes a decision process more
efficient. It says an organization can change its own attainable set.
Ecological niche differentiation is the analogy. Two organisms competing
for one scarce resource may be unable to grow at the same time, and
differentiated acquisition strategies can make joint growth feasible.
Differentiation can also fail, by adding costs or dependencies.

## What would answer it

A model that:

- fixes the acceptance sets of the operators' demands throughout
  (CLAIM-347);
- lets learning, differentiation or recomposition change the attainable
  outcome set ℛ(𝒪);
- shows a strict enlargement of 𝒦 with no compatible collection lost, or
  shows why the class of models considered cannot produce one.

Higher-order structure should be checked, not assumed. Pairwise
compatibility does not fix collective compatibility (CASE-tmpuuf3p), but
some apparent higher-order effects reduce to pairwise ones (THEORY-598).
An answer should show when the complex carries information its
1-skeleton does not.

## What would not answer it

Apparent relief that comes from deleting a demand, lowering its weight
or redefining success (CLAIM-361). An improvement in aggregate loss that
does not change 𝒦.
