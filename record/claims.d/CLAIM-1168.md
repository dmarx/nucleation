# CLAIM-1168: Regenerated variables defeat the structural answer

## The claim

The owner's examples ([CLAIM-1172](CLAIM-1172.md)) are of two kinds.

- **Common drivers.** Tides, day and night, lunar cycles, seasons, and
  cadences that human activity imposes on an ecology. Members read them and
  condition what they do on them, but do not regenerate them: the ecosystem
  does not make the seasons. They fail the third of [CLAIM-327](CLAIM-327.md)'s conditions
  ("by doing so regenerate it"). They coordinate by entrainment, and the
  record keeps coordination by a common driver apart from coordination by
  mutual regulation: the two "come apart under interventions on coupling"
  ([CLAIM-1175](CLAIM-1175.md)'s admission; [LEAD-144](../leads.d/LEAD-144.md) would test it), and a form constituted
  by mutual entrainment "is not thereby a self" ([CLAIM-165](CLAIM-165.md)).
- **Regenerated variables.** Predator–prey oscillations: predators read prey
  abundance, condition what they do on it, and by doing so regenerate the
  cycle. All three of [CLAIM-327](CLAIM-327.md)'s conditions hold. Boundary effects at an
  interface can be of either kind: a gradient a neighbouring ecology imposes
  is a driver; an edge the two communities' activity keeps up is regenerated.

The second kind defeats [CLAIM-1074](CLAIM-1074.md) as worded: an ecosystem with a
predator–prey cycle has a whole-level slow variable its members read,
condition on and regenerate, which the claim said makes a plurality
integrated. Either such an ecosystem is integrated at that regime, or the
criterion needs more ([CLAIM-1126](CLAIM-1126.md) offers what).

## What it does not say

- Not that common drivers are unimportant: they set the timescales on which
  regenerated variables run.
- Not which of [CLAIM-1126](CLAIM-1126.md) or [CLAIM-1153](CLAIM-1153.md) follows; that is the
  owner's choice.

## Note of 2026-11-23: Yellowstone, on the record

The owner's example ([CLAIM-1172](CLAIM-1172.md), its second addition): the
reintroduction of wolves to Yellowstone. The record holds it through
Kadowaki and colleagues ([NOTE-1677](../notes.d/NOTE-1677.md), Read; [THEORY-1468](../theory.d/THEORY-1468.md), Active), citing Wolf
et al. 2007 (not read by the record): "Although wolves were experimentally
reintroduced in 1995, the willows did not initially recover", because beaver
decline had removed dam sites through erosion, "and such hydrological shift
is irreversible". [CLAIM-463](CLAIM-463.md) (Active) already uses it: restoring the
conditions under which a capacity was lost need not restore it. For this
claim it is the regenerated kind, documented: the hydrological regime is a
slow variable beavers read, condition on and regenerate by building, and its
loss was hysteretic, so restoring an upstream condition (the predator) did
not restore it. The case is the narrow finding the review reports, not the
wider trophic-cascade account often told of Yellowstone, which the record
does not hold.
