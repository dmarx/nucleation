# NOTE-1987: Lu et al. — When are concepts erased from diffusion models?

## Contribution

Two models of what erasure does, and a probe suite that tells them apart.
**Guidance-based avoidance** redirects the conditional guidance away from
the concept and leaves the knowledge intact; **destruction-based removal**
lowers the unconditional likelihood of the concept. The probes:
optimization (Textual Inversion, UnlearnDiffAtk), in-context (inpainting,
diffusion completion), training-free noise-based trajectory expansion,
latent classifier guidance, and dynamic concept tracing. Seven methods on
SD 1.4: UCE, ESD-u, ESD-x, Task Vector, GA, STEREO and RECE.

## Key insight

Whether a concept is erased depends on the resources of whoever looks for
it. Text attacks are one observer; visual context, noise and classifier
gradients are others, and many methods hide a concept from the first
while leaving it for the rest.

## Assumptions

- SD 1.4 only.
- Concept presence judged by CLIP and an ImageNet classifier.
- 13 concepts: 10 objects and 3 styles (§5).

## Key results

- **§3.6**: "Methods such as GA, TaskVectors, STEREO, and RECE, appear
  robust to text-based attacks ... Yet TaskVectors and RECE resurfaced the
  erased concept under inpainting and diffusion-completion probes";
  "concepts erased by UCE, ESD-x, and ESD-u could be recovered with minor
  stochastic perturbations"; under classifier guidance "many models,
  including STEREO, could regenerate erased concepts".
- **§3.6**: "most current erasure methods operate through guidance-based
  avoidance rather than true destruction of underlying representations".
- **§6**: "knowledge undetectable through one technique can often be
  recovered through others."
- **§5**: "destruction-based removal tends to impact related concepts more
  significantly than guidance-based avoidance."
- **§5, discovery vs induction**: an extensive enough optimization-based
  probe "may be able to induce generation of a concept the model did not
  even encounter during training."

## Concepts

- **Guidance-based avoidance vs destruction-based removal**: above.
- **In-context probing**: recovering a concept from visual context.
- **Discovery vs induction**: whether a probe recovers what the model
  holds or supplies it.

## Bearing on the record

- **[TERM-064](../terms.d/TERM-064.md)**: each probe is an observer with different resources, and
  "erased" is illegibility to one of them. The discovery-versus-induction
  limit is the converse worry: a powerful enough observer can supply the
  distinction rather than recover it.
- **[THEORY-281](../theory.d/THEORY-281.md)**: a different setting, generative likelihood rather than
  independence of a representation; the cost to related concepts of
  destruction echoes the cost of erasure there, by analogy only.
- **[CLAIM-407](../claims.d/CLAIM-407.md)**: by analogy only. Guidance-based erasure is a behaviour
  under ordinary prompting that fails under an intervention on the
  generation process.
- **[QUESTION-010](../questions.d/QUESTION-010.md)**: weak. Dynamic concept tracing follows what a run of
  increasing erasure strengths keeps, but not for two task families.

## Connections

The discovery-versus-induction limit applies equally to Reisizadeh et
al.'s forget-trained probes ([LIT-2628](../literature.d/LIT-2628.md)).
