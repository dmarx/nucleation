### Summary
CogLoadBench holds a target problem and its answer fixed and changes only presentation, one factor at a time, across three synthetic families (Context: 5-step entity chains; Math: 4-step word problems; Analogy: infer one of three symbolic rules from example pairs), N=200 base problems per axis per family, five open instruction-tuned models (Qwen3-4B/8B/14B, Gemma3-12B, Llama3.1-8B), greedy decoding, JSON answers, target question repeated at the end. Each prompt has K instances, one target and K-1 distractors.

The five factors (§3; App. B.4):
1. Distractor quantity: K in {1,5,10,20,50} (Analogy to 20), nested distractors, target relative position fixed.
2. Distractor similarity: at fixed K, high-similarity distractors share the target's domain with a different structure (Math: same topic, different operation; Context: similar entities, different relations; Analogy: same semantic relation, different symbolic rule).
3. Task interference: a prepended secondary task with its own JSON field: remember three words, count "the", or compute 34 x 26 in the head.
4. Discontinuity: ordered vs local shuffle (within instance) vs global shuffle (interleaved across instances); statements keep their [i.j] labels.
5. Underspecification: an explicit element becomes inferable ("the first person mentioned", "the latter"; in Analogy "all but one example output is hidden").

How intrinsic load is held fixed: by design only, matched variants of the same base problem "preserving the target question and answer" (B.1). No independent measure of intrinsic difficulty for the variants. The paper concedes the issue for real documents: discontinuity and underspecification were not run on QASPER/CUAD "because they are difficult to manipulate without affecting intrinsic task difficulty" (§5).

The two mechanisms. They are a grouping of axes stated before the results, not found by them: "Three axes (distractor quantity, distractor similarity, and task interference) add competing information around the target. The remaining two axes (discontinuity and underspecification) make the target reasoning path more complicated" (§3). "Competition" means content that offers an alternative operation, rule or task ("distractors are most costly when they overlap with the information needed to solve the target", §3.1); "complicating the reasoning path" means the needed information is all present but its connection must be reconstructed ("extraneous load can arise solely from requiring the model to spend effort understanding how relevant information connects"). Nothing tests the division: no experiment separates the groups, and the activation signatures (Table D7) do not split along it. Discontinuity patterns with quantity ("possibly because its effects overlap with those of distractor quantity", §4), and interference stands alone on alignment.

ELS (§4; App. D.1-D.3). For each prompt, one forward pass. 30 hand-built statistics of residual-stream activations over context, question and full-prompt regions, averaged over early/mid/late/all layers plus the last layer: mean token norm (5), Gini sparsity and effective rank (11), cross-layer drift/angle/step length of mean activation (12), context-question cosine (2). Chosen from a pool of 50 by dropping correlated and least-important features. "Explicit length features" are excluded. The scorer is a per-model MLP (30 -> 64 -> 32 -> 1, ReLU), trained with weighted BCE on matched pairs labelled low=0 and high=1, ELS = sigmoid output. So it is a supervised nonlinear probe on summary statistics, not a linear probe or a difference of means. The supervision is the manipulation label, not accuracy. "Behavior-calibrated ELS" is a separate linear regression from the same features to the setting-level accuracy drop, per family and model (D.4).

Steering (§5; App. F). Behavior-calibrated ELS is fit on QASPER/CUAD with F1 drop as target. At each of three layers (16/20/24 for Qwen3-14B; 24/32/40 for Qwen3-32B), a ridge regression from hidden states to that calibrated score gives a unit direction. During context prefill the component along it is scaled by (1+alpha), alpha<0, with norm rescaling. Alpha is chosen per axis on 200 validation examples from 9 values; direction fit on 150; test on 300. Baselines: random direction (same layers, rule and strengths), CAA (mean difference high-low), RepE-PCA. Only Qwen models are steered, on only the three "competition" axes.

### Contribution
A single matched benchmark comparing five presentation factors under shared scoring; an activation-statistics classifier of the presentation condition, validated in construct-validity terms (convergent: held-out problems, families, levels; discriminant: two intrinsic-difficulty manipulations); and a small inference-time steering gain. The CLT frame supplies the factor list and the vocabulary. App. B.3 states human hypotheses from Harp & Mayer 1998, Marsh et al. 2009, Tombu et al. 2011, Chandler & Sweller 1991 and Miyake et al. 1994, and leaves them untested "without assuming shared underlying mechanisms".

### Key results
1. Discontinuity is the largest effect (Table 2a): global shuffle Math 89.5 -> 55.1 (-34.4), Analogy 49.7 -> 18.5 (-31.2), Context 97.9 -> 83.7. "models rely not only on having the right information, but also on seeing it in an order that preserves the reasoning chain" (§3.1).
2. Underspecification (Table 2b): Context 91.3 -> 54.4 (-36.9) from "Rodipu" -> "the latter"; Math -7.9; Analogy -11.3.
3. Quantity (Table C1a): Math and Context flat to K=20, then about -14 at K=50; Analogy -24.1 already at K=5 (75.5 -> 51.4). "in Analogy, each distractor provides examples of another possible rule" (§3.1).
4. Similarity (Table C1b): Analogy 52.3 -> 21.0, Math -8.1, but Context rises +4.0 (93.8 -> 97.8), with Llama +10.0 (Table C3). The main text says only "but not in Context, where the question's entities identify the target chain".
5. Interference (Table 1): modest, -1.3 to -6.5. Per model it is non-uniform: Llama3.1-8B Math rises +14.5 with the memory task and +11.5 with arithmetic (Table C4). Not discussed.
6. ELS on held-out base problems (Table 3), AUC by axis Quantity/Similarity/Interference/Discontinuity/Underspecification: 1.000/0.924/0.999/0.998/0.705. Logistic-regression probe on PCA'd final-query-token states: 1.000/0.777/1.000/1.000/0.641. Prompt length: 1.000/0.488/0.787/0.500/0.487. Output entropy and logit margin 0.49-0.62 everywhere: "uncertainty metrics like entropy and logit margin do not recover any axis well".
7. Transfer (Table 4): leave-one-family-out 70.8-80.5 AUC; held-out levels 93.2-100. Leave-one-axis-out (Table D1): Quantity 0.989, Discontinuity 0.754, Similarity 0.579, Interference 0.458, Underspecification 0.470. "extraneous load is not captured by a single direction shared across all load factors" (§4). Four calibration pairs raise the mean 0.650 -> 0.850; underspecification stays 0.54-0.72.
8. Intrinsic-difficulty control (Table D4): difficult Math (quantities as products) costs 16.9 accuracy points and difficult Analogy (letter-shift rule) 56.6 points, with Analogy difficult accuracy 0.8% on average. ELS shifts +6.1% and -6.2% "of the extraneous load gap". "ELS changes little and does not systematically increase with intrinsic difficulty".
9. Feature signatures (Table D7, Cohen's d high-low): Quantity magnitude -9.36, sparsity +2.61, stability +2.37; every other axis |d| <= 0.50. Underspecification is the weakest.
10. Behavior-calibrated ELS ranks settings by accuracy impact 86.8% vs raw 70.6% (Table D2).
11. Real-world (Table 5a; E1-E2; GPT-5, Sonnet 4.5, Qwen3-14B/32B): CUAD quantity K=1 -> 50 gives 74.3 -> 47.5 F1; QASPER K=1 -> 7 gives 45.7 -> 23.7; multi-question interference is up to -17.8 (Qwen CUAD).
12. Steering (Table 5b): macro +0.42 F1 vs random -0.03, CAA +0.05, RepE-PCA -0.17. Best cells: CUAD Qwen3-32B similarity +1.76 +/- 0.71 and CUAD Qwen3-14B interference +1.39 +/- 0.98. "These results provide initial evidence that ELS-guided steering can improve average model performance" (§5). Gains "come mainly from prompts the model answers poorly without steering; on prompts it already answers correctly, it loses less than a random direction".

### Assumptions
- Matched variants have equal intrinsic difficulty because answer and target are unchanged.
- Presentation-induced load is encoded at prompt time ("load arises while the input is being encoded", §4).
- A classifier of the manipulation label measures an internal quantity "load", on the strength of convergent and discriminant behaviour (Cronbach & Meehl).
- Steering along a direction predictive of setting-level F1 drop reduces load.
- The CLT categories carry over to LLMs as categories of manipulation, with the mechanism explicitly not assumed shared (B.3).

### Concepts
Intrinsic load and extraneous load (taken from CLT, operationalized only as task-preserving presentation change); competition vs reasoning-path complication; ELS (raw and behavior-calibrated); load profile (a model's per-factor sensitivity, Conclusion); ELS-guided latent steering; construct validity (convergent and discriminant).

### Bearing on named codes
- CLAIM-1269 (spurious sensitivity/invariance): supports, as an instance of the method. The design is the claim's schema: a transformation the standard (answer invariance) treats as irrelevant, and a response that varies under it. Table 2's discontinuity and underspecification drops are spurious sensitivity relative to that standard. The Conclusion's per-model profile ("whether a model is broadly sensitive to inefficient presentation or mainly to one factor") is a "family of response contrasts" used as a signature of a model. It also qualifies the claim. Whether global shuffle is irrelevant depends on the declared standard: for a bounded reasoner reconstructing interleaved chains, it adds real computation. The paper's own refusal to run discontinuity on real documents concedes this, which bears out CLAIM-1269's point that the classification needs a declared standard. Spurious invariance is not tested.
- CLAIM-tmp7oycz / THEORY-662 (fluency as inferred precision): mostly silent, with one qualifying datum. Output-level uncertainty, the nearest model-side analogue of felt (dis)fluency, does not track extraneous load (Table 3: entropy 0.49-0.62, logit margin 0.52-0.60), although accuracy drops by up to 37 points. If extraneous load is the model analogue of disfluency, the model's output confidence does not register it. Reading entropy as the analogue is my inference, not the paper's. The paper does not mention fluency.
- CLAIM-1086 (grounding as reliability under interventions): supports, as instance. Each axis is an intervention that keeps the truthmaker (target content) and changes what accompanies it (order, neighbours, phrasing). The failures are failures of reliability over a family so closed. Analogy similarity (52.3 -> 21.0) is the cleanest case: a rule is drawn from surface-similar context, not from the target's examples.
- CLAIM-tmpgmvhl (shortcut = earliest-learnable reliable correlate): silent on its learning-order thesis, since there is no training study. My reading: the order dependence ("seeing it in an order that preserves the reasoning chain") is consistent with adjacency as a cheap correlate reliable over natural text. The paper offers no such account.
- CLAIM-1100 (decodable is stored, not known): bears directly, supporting the distinction. ELS is a probe: a supervised MLP on activation statistics, benchmarked against a logistic-regression probe that matches it on three axes. What it decodes is which presentation the input had. The paper calls this "estimates a model's internal extraneous load". Three findings press CLAIM-1100's point. (a) Under the Analogy intrinsic manipulation, accuracy falls to 0.8% and ELS moves -6.2%, so ELS reads the input's form, not the model's processing difficulty. (b) No single direction is shared across axes (D1). (c) The only test of use is steering, and its effects are small. Decodable here means stored; that the model uses it as "load" is not shown.
- CLAIM-029 (response as pragmatic frame): qualifies, toward its defeater. The defeater is "prompt effects ... fail paraphrase invariance ... a function ... of its surface form". Underspecification is close to a paraphrase ("Rodipu" -> "the latter") and costs 23.5-46.0 points per model in Context (Table C6). This is reasoning accuracy, not interpretation, so it presses the defeater without meeting it.
- THEORY-1240 (chat models follow narrative): silent. Truth tracking across frames is not studied. At most, distractors with competing rules draw the answer despite the instruction to "answer ONLY the question that we repeat", a weak case of context content overriding the instruction frame.
- THEORY-1334 / THEORY-1313 (persona vectors, difference of means): qualifies the method's reach. CAA, the difference-of-means direction persona vectors use, gives +0.05 macro here, near nothing, and the ridge-fit direction gives +0.42. For this construct a contrastive mean direction barely steers. Silent on personas.
- THEORY-1330 / THEORY-1339 (Assistant Axis, drift, capping): silent. The intervention (attenuate one direction's component during prefill, with norm rescale) resembles projection capping, but no persona or multi-turn measure is taken.
- CLAIM-266 (optimization games the probes that train it): mostly consistent and silent, with a caution. Steering is not optimization of weights. It is certified on held-out F1, not on ELS, so the paper avoids self-certification. But §4 proposes using ELS features "to test whether it moves these features toward their low-load patterns". Certifying a mitigation by the probe that chose its direction is what CLAIM-266 warns against. The paper reports no post-steering ELS.

### Limitations and gaps
Stated by the authors (§6): one factor at a time; only long-context QA tested in the wild; no matched human study; ELS is "a diagnostic rather than a complete model of task difficulty".

Gaps in the argument:
1. The CLT analogy works as a label and a source of factors. Its distinctive content is a limited working-memory capacity that intrinsic and extraneous load share, so they trade off additively. That is never tested: no crossing of intrinsic and extraneous factors, no capacity notion, no interaction. B.3 disclaims shared mechanism. "Load" in the models is defined by the manipulation.
2. The two mechanisms are an a priori grouping (§3), not a finding. The activation signatures put discontinuity with quantity, not with underspecification (D7, §4).
3. Intrinsic difficulty is held fixed by assertion. Global shuffle across instances, hiding Analogy example outputs, and adding a secondary task all plausibly add task computation. The paper's own reason for skipping discontinuity and underspecification on real data concedes this. The intrinsic controls are only two (Math products, Analogy letter-shift), and the Analogy one floors accuracy.
4. Circularity. Raw ELS is not circular with accuracy, since it is trained on manipulation labels. But for the same reason it is a classifier of the manipulation, and it is never shown to predict which prompts a model fails within a condition. Behavior-calibrated ELS is fit to setting-level accuracy drops, and its 86.8% test reproduces the calibration-set ranking of the same settings on held-out examples. That shows the setting means are stable; it does not show prediction of unseen settings' severity. Near-circular.
5. Length. Explicit length features are excluded, but mean norms, effective rank and Gini over T x d entries depend on token count. Quantity is separated perfectly by every family, alignment's two features included (99.8, D6a), and prompt length alone gives 1.000. Interference adds a fixed prepended block, and discontinuity adds no tokens but is detected at 0.998-1.000. A length-matched control (e.g., neutral padding) is absent.
6. "Domain-general notion of extraneous load" (§4) is in tension with leave-one-axis-out being at chance on three of five axes (D1). The paper softens this to "reusable feature representation".
7. Normalization of the intrinsic shift. It is reported as a percentage of "the average score gap between low- and high-load prompts". If that gap pools axes, quantity's very large separation (d = -9.36) would make any shift look small. The text does not say.
8. Steering is small and noisy. +0.42 F1 against load drops of 10-27 F1 recovers roughly 1-5%. Most cells are within 1-1.5 SE, with only CUAD/32B/similarity above 2 SE. Random direction gives +0.78 and +1.33 in two cells. Alpha is selected per axis from 9 values on validation. Only Qwen and only three axes are steered. No test that steering lowers ELS or moves the features. Side effects are reported only as "loses less than a random direction" on already-correct prompts, so there is a loss.
9. Non-monotone and positive effects go unexplained: Context similarity +4.0; Context K=5 above K=1 (93.3 -> 96.2); Llama Math interference +14.5. These are relevant to "desirable difficulty" readings.
10. Small open models (4-14B) on synthetic tasks; frontier models only in §5, and not probed.

### Candidate THEORY
Statement: Gupta, Liu and Zou find that in five open instruction-tuned models of 4-14B parameters, on synthetic contextual, arithmetic and analogy problems whose target and answer are held fixed, changing only presentation (more or more similar distractors, a secondary task, shuffled statements, or an implicit reference) usually lowers accuracy, by up to about 37 points averaged over models, and that a per-model classifier on 30 prompt-time activation statistics tells high- from low-load variants of unseen problems apart (ROC AUC 0.71-1.00 by factor) while moving little under two manipulations of intrinsic difficulty; they read this as extraneous cognitive load arising from competing content or from a harder reasoning path.

promote_when: An independent replication, on other models or with the released benchmark, that finds the same per-factor accuracy drops with target and answer held fixed, and a prompt-time activation-statistic classifier that separates high- from low-load variants of held-out problems at comparable AUC by factor and shifts little, relative to its load separation, under intrinsic-difficulty changes that lower accuracy. It is refuted for the classifier claim if that separation vanishes once prompts are matched in length. The cognitive-load reading is the authors' and is not what this asks to confirm.

A second, smaller reading could hold the steering result: "+0.42 F1 macro over 12 settings, vs -0.03 random, +0.05 CAA, -0.17 RepE-PCA, with most cells within about one SE".
