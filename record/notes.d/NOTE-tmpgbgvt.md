# NOTE-tmpgbgvt: Lemmer and Wagner — Can we really reduce ethnic prejudice outside the lab?

## Contribution

The first meta-analysis restricted to contact interventions, that is,
programmes deliberately run in schools, universities and other field
settings, as against the mostly correlational surveys pooled by
Pettigrew and Tropp (85% of its 73 documents were not in theirs). It adds
delayed posttests, a comparison of direct with indirect programmes, and
a comparison of regions with and without protracted conflict.

## Key insight

For attitudes measured soon after a programme, it did not matter much
whether participants met the other group or only met it at one remove.
Extended contact programmes, in which children or students read or
watch stories of friendship between a member of their own group and a
member of the other, did at least as well as programmes that brought the
groups together.

## Assumptions

- **Inclusion.** A field programme based on contact theory that ensured
  contact actually occurred (desegregation, housing, exchange and
  tourism studies, and unstructured mixed camps excluded); imagined and
  parasocial (mass-media) contact excluded by definition; at least one
  measure of ethnic prejudice, cognitive, affective or conative
  (behavioural intention); English or German; documents to mid-2012.
- **Designs.** A primary cluster of randomized posttest-only and
  pretest–posttest designs with control groups (Hedges's g, Morris's g),
  and a secondary cluster of single-group pre–post designs (Becker's g),
  analysed apart because they mix the intervention with time effects.
- **Types.** Direct: contact meetings (structured dialogue about the
  intergroup relation) and cooperative learning (joint work on a task not
  about the relation). Indirect: extended contact ("books, picture
  stories, radio plays, or films that explicitly display friendships or
  positive relations" across the line) and virtual contact (exchange
  between members of the groups by computer).
- **Model.** Random effects with REML for the means; mixed-effects
  meta-regression, one moderator at a time, for the hypotheses; the unit
  is the comparison, with dependent effects averaged within it.

## Key results

- **Overall, immediate (Table 2).** Primary 0.28 [0.21, 0.35], k = 79,
  N = 9,212, I² = 71%; secondary 0.39 [0.31, 0.47], k = 44, N = 2,159.
- **Overall, delayed (1–12 months).** Primary 0.23 [0.13, 0.34], k = 8,
  N = 1,186, I² = 10%; secondary 0.35 [0.21, 0.50], k = 17, N = 464. No
  study measured beyond a year.
- **Direct against indirect (Table 3), immediate only.** Primary: direct
  0.29 [0.21, 0.37], k = 63; indirect 0.23 [0.07, 0.38], k = 16; Q = 0.47,
  p = .49. Among majorities only, still no difference (p = .98).
  Secondary: direct 0.41, k = 37; indirect, all virtual, 0.33, k = 5.
- **Subtypes, primary cluster.** Contact meetings 0.31 [0.21, 0.41],
  k = 39; cooperative learning 0.25 [0.13, 0.38], k = 24; extended
  contact 0.42 [0.19, 0.65], k = 8; virtual 0.08 [−0.13, 0.28], k = 8.
  The subtype moderator is not significant (Q = 5.82, df = 3, p = .12).
- **Conflict regions.** Primary 0.31 (k = 11) against 0.27 (k = 68),
  no difference.
- **Status.** Primary: majorities 0.38 (k = 39), minorities 0.20
  (k = 20), difference significant only one-tailed (two-tailed p = .0546,
  footnote 10). Only one indirect comparison concerns a minority.
- **Generalization.** Primary: individuals met 0.26 (k = 12), the whole
  target outgroup 0.32, unspecified "other ethnic groups" 0.21; no
  difference. No comparison measured attitudes toward a specific
  uninvolved outgroup.
- **Quality.** Randomized comparisons gave larger effects (0.43, k = 19)
  than non-randomized (0.24, k = 60), and lower attrition larger
  effects. No sign of publication bias by three tests.

## Concepts

- **Interethnic contact intervention**: a programme run under natural
  conditions to improve intergroup relations by establishing direct or
  indirect contact, which ensures the contact occurs.
- **Extended contact**: knowing, or here being shown, that a member of
  one's own group has a close relationship with a member of the
  outgroup (Wright et al. 1997).
- **Virtual contact**: the authors' term for interaction between
  members of the groups through a computer system, without meeting.
- **Comparison**: an intervention–control contrast for one sample, the
  unit of analysis.

## Connections

It takes Pettigrew and Tropp's 2006 meta-analysis ([LIT-1413](../literature.d/LIT-1413.md)) from
mostly correlational contact to field programmes and borrows its
categories of generalization. It cites the 2008 mediator meta-analysis
([LIT-1405](../literature.d/LIT-1405.md)) for anxiety and empathy and says the intervention literature
is too thin to test mediators. Paluck, Green and Green ([LIT-1420](../literature.d/LIT-1420.md),
[NOTE-1393](NOTE-1393.md)) draw studies from it and are stricter: randomized designs
only, outcomes at least a day later. Its extended-contact arm rests on
Wright et al. 1997, the triage's fallback if this paper had not
separated the arms. Scacco and Warren ([LIT-tmpi9wm8](../literature.d/LIT-tmpi9wm8.md)) is the kind of
behavioural study it found none of: cooperative learning with no peace
content, measured by games.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-430](../claims.d/CLAIM-430.md), second defeater.** The triage expected this paper to
  supply the nearest test and to correct the claim's note that the
  defeater "has no test in the randomized contact literature". It
  supplies something nearer than the note allowed, and weaker than a
  test. Nearer: it does estimate programmes with no taking part, the
  eight extended-contact comparisons, in which participants read or
  watch cross-group friendships, beside programmes in which they take
  part, and the first are no less effective (0.42 against 0.31 for
  meetings and 0.25 for cooperative learning). If anything, that weighs
  toward the defeater. Weaker, on each of the defeater's terms:
  - *equal favourable content*: nothing matches content across arms;
    the comparison is between different studies of different
    programmes, populations and measures, and the subtype moderator is
    not significant;
  - *as large*: immediate posttests only, self-reported attitudes only;
  - *as durable*: the delayed analyses are not broken down by type, so
    nothing is known about extended contact's persistence;
  - *taking part*: "indirect" is not "without taking part". The virtual
    subtype is interaction by computer, which is taking part at a
    distance, and it is the subtype that did not work in the stronger
    designs. Pooling it with extended contact as "indirect" hides the
    contrast the claim cares about.
  The note's "no test" should become: no within-study test; between
  studies, observed friendships do about as well as taking part, on
  immediate self-reports.
- **[QUESTION-262](../questions.d/QUESTION-262.md).** On the measurement demand, it is no help: "Due to a
  lack of appropriate research", no behavioural outcome could be
  included, and the authors' sixth direction for research is behaviour.
  On "local against general", its outcomes reach the whole target
  outgroup and unspecified ethnic outgroups, but no comparison measured
  a specific outgroup not involved, so the question's generalization to
  other members of the category is supported and generalization beyond
  it is untested, as [NOTE-1393](NOTE-1393.md) found for its sample.
- **[CLAIM-275](../claims.d/CLAIM-275.md).** Nothing: no included study orders conduct and
  interpretation.

No instruction for machine-learning practice; nothing for the anthology.

## Limitations

- **Between-study comparisons.** Every moderator result compares
  different studies; type of contact is confounded with age, setting
  and design (the extended-contact studies the text names, Cameron et
  al. 2006 and 2007 and Derbaum 1981, are with children). The
  authors run one moderator at a time and say why (footnote 5).
- **Self-report only**, and mostly short-term.
- **Most comparisons are not randomized** (60 of 79 in the primary
  cluster), and the authors' reading of the randomization moderator as
  "higher quality, larger impact" is one of several.
- **Discrepancies in the manuscript.** These may be corrected in the
  typeset article, which was not read.
  - Table 1 gives indirect programmes in the primary cluster as 16 =
    22.3%; 16 of 79 is 20.3%.
  - Table 1's secondary cluster lists 6 indirect comparisons (12.0%) but
    5 virtual comparisons (also 12.0%), the only indirect subtype there.
  - Table 2 marks the delayed primary-cluster Q = 9.11 with ***, while
    the text reports it as p = .25, homogeneous; and gives the
    secondary immediate Q as 150.60 against the text's 150.85.
  - Table 3's secondary-cluster conflict estimates (0.47 [0.35, 0.60];
    0.34 [0.22, 0.46]) differ from the text's (0.48 [0.36, 0.59]; 0.32
    [0.20, 0.43]), and its primary "target outgroup" k = 38 from the
    text's 40.
  - The reference list gives Cernat (2011) and Ertmer et al. (2011) the
    same journal, volume and pages, Internet and Higher Education 14,
    251–261; one of them is wrong. Connolly (1992) and Cook (2000) are
    listed twice; "Miles & Crips, 2014" is Crisp; "outgoups" in the
    abstract.

## Open questions

- Does extended contact's advantage survive a within-study comparison
  with cooperative contact on matched content, on behaviour, after a
  delay? That is the test [CLAIM-430](../claims.d/CLAIM-430.md)'s defeater asks for.
- Is the virtual null about the medium or about the studies (k = 8)?

## Note of 2026-10-29: which of the two references is wrong

Limitations says the reference list gives Cernat (2011) and Ertmer et al.
(2011) the same journal, volume and pages, Internet and Higher Education
14, 251–261, and that one of them is wrong. It is Cernat's. Checked
against Crossref and PubMed (LIT-tmph031e's history), Cernat's article is
in The Journal of Social Psychology 151(6), 737–753,
doi:10.1080/00224545.2010.522622, and Crossref gives Internet and Higher
Education 14, 251–261 to Ertmer, Newby, Yu et al.,
doi:10.1016/j.iheduc.2011.05.005. So Cernat's entry carries Ertmer et
al.'s venue. The typeset article's list was not checked, so whether the
error survives there is not known.

Open questions names a within-study comparison of extended contact with
cooperative contact as the test CLAIM-430's defeater asks for. Cernat, the
candidate this reading named, is registered as LIT-tmph031e, Unfetched. It
is unread, and from its abstract it is not that test: all three arms read
a story and none takes part; it compares a Roma exemplar's friendship with
a member of the reader's own group against one with a member of another
outgroup. Whether it is among the eight extended-contact comparisons was
not checked, since the Supporting Information, which lists the
comparisons, was not read.
