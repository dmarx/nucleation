  caused by fast affective intuitions; moral reasoning mostly comes after,
  to justify the judgment to others, and changes minds chiefly by
  triggering new intuitions in listeners. Four reasons are offered: two
  processing systems, motivated reasoning, post-hoc confabulation, and the
  weak link between moral reasoning and moral action. Intuitions are
  partly innate and are tuned by culture through custom complexes and peers
  in late childhood. Harmless taboo violations are condemned anyway, and
  people are "dumbfounded" when their reasons run out.

# LIT-511: The emotional dog and its rational tail: A social intuitionist approach to moral judgment

Jonathan Haidt (2001), *Psychological Review* 108(4), 814–834 —
DOI-10.1037/0033-295X.108.4.814

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 in the morality-as-cooperation batch ([ADR-018](../decisions.d/ADR-018.md)). It
is the oldest of the rivals and the prequel to Moral Foundations Theory,
whose founding essay, [LIT-502](LIT-502.md), cites it for the claim that moral
judgment is intuitive. No anthology topic holds it, and it carries no
instruction for machine-learning practice.

Its bearing on morality-as-cooperation is in two places, both set out in
[NOTE-403](../notes.d/NOTE-403.md).

- **It is not a rival on function.** The paper locates morality "in a
  group's efforts to solve cooperation and commitment problems" (p. 18),
  and maps three of Fiske's relational models onto kin care, rank and
  Trivers' reciprocity. Bloom's commentary on [LIT-047](LIT-047.md) (read in [NOTE-034](../notes.d/NOTE-034.md))
  says morality-as-cooperation says nothing about proximate mechanisms; this
  paper is a proximate account and is compatible with a cooperative
  ultimate one.
- **It supplies the evidence against cooperation's exclusivity.** The
  harmless-but-condemned cases it reports (incest with contraception, sex
  with a dead chicken, eating the family dog) are the cases Bloom and the
  Reply argue over. Baumard, André and Sperber ([LIT-508](LIT-508.md)) answer the
  chicken case with a fairness account of sexual morality; Rai and Fiske
  ([LIT-507](LIT-507.md)) answer the incest case relationally; Schein and Gray
  ([LIT-494](LIT-494.md)) deny that such wrongs are intuitively harmless.

Its gut-feeling mechanism leans on Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis,
which the record holds and has read ([LIT-384](LIT-384.md)). The Wheatley and Haidt
hypnotic-disgust study it reports is one of the findings whose
generality the meta-analysis cited in [NOTE-132](../notes.d/NOTE-132.md) disputes.

[NOTE-403](../notes.d/NOTE-403.md) is the close reading of 2026-10-02, and it placed the work:
**Active**. It is the reference for intuitionism in moral psychology. Its
own evidence is mostly borrowed from social cognition, and it says so.
