# LIT-1359: A systems identification approach using Bayes factors to deconstruct the brain bases of emotion regulation

Bo, K., Kraynak, T. E., Kwon, M., Sun, M., Gianaros, P. J. & Wager, T. D. (2024). A systems identification approach using Bayes factors to deconstruct the brain bases of emotion regulation. *Nature Neuroscience* 27(5): 975–987. doi:10.1038/s41593-024-01605-7. Preprint: bioRxiv, doi:10.1101/2023.04.26.538485, as "Deconstructing the brain bases of emotion regulation: A systems-identification approach using Bayes factors".

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-1061](../notes.d/NOTE-1061.md). Page numbers are the preprint's.*

- **Nulls made the question answerable.** A region is "Reappraisal only"
  if it responds to reappraisal and shows 10:1 evidence of no response to
  negative against neutral pictures without it. A null at that strength
  needs at least 120 participants (p. 5).
- **Reported affect falls.** Reappraisal lowered negative ratings
  (d = 0.92); "81% of individual participants showed regulation effects in
  the same direction as the group findings" (p. 7).
- **Mostly shared, partly selective.** "The majority of the regions
  activated by reappraisal were also activated during negative
  emotion-generation without explicit reappraisal instructions" (p. 18).
  Sixteen regions, anterior prefrontal, temporo-parietal and cerebellar,
  were selective for reappraisal (p. 8), and they best predicted its
  success (r = 0.2; pp. 11–12).
- **Subcortical responses unchanged.** "Most regions responsive to
  emotional stimuli were non-modifiable, including all subcortical
  emotion-related regions" (p. 10). Amygdala subregions and PAG showed
  evidence of no reduction (all BF < 0.1), some moving the other way
  (p. 11).
- **What reappraisal reduces is cortical.** The eight modifiable regions
  were "located exclusively in the cortex", mainly higher visual and
  parietal areas, read by the authors as semantic and conceptual
  representation of the picture (pp. 10, 21).
- **Limits the authors state.** The component definitions assume that the
  two contrasts capture appraisal and that appraisal produces no decreases;
  "emotion appraisal may not be sufficiently defined by current contrast"
  (p. 23).

## Why this is here

The owner's exchange of 2026-10-21, "Experience and interpretation", asked whether experience is constituted by interpretation or produced by it. The exchange cited this study as a limit on the constitutive reading of affect: some emotion systems were modulated by reappraisal and a largely subcortical one was not. On the exchange's reading, not every component of an emotion is open to reinterpretation. Bears on [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md), [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md) and [CLAIM-412](../claims.d/CLAIM-412.md).

**Corrected 2026-10-22, from the reading.** The description is right about
the subcortical regions and too narrow about the rest. The unmodulated set
is most of the emotion-responsive brain, cortex included (lateral OFC,
medial prefrontal, midcingulate, parietal); the subcortical regions are
those where nothing was modulated. And the limit holds only if the
unchanged subcortical response is a component of the experience. The study
measured BOLD and one rating per trial, and the rating did fall; the
subcortical response did not predict how far (r = 0.015). Reading the
unchanged response as the emotion behind a changed feeling is the split
Barrett et al. ([LIT-1353](LIT-1353.md)) reject. The study does bear directly on the
dispute between [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) and [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md): most of the reappraisal network
is shared with generation, as [THEORY-056](../theory.d/THEORY-056.md) expects, and a selective set is
not, as [THEORY-680](../theory.d/THEORY-680.md) expects. Filed from it: [THEORY-878](../theory.d/THEORY-878.md) (the shared and
selective sets) and [THEORY-871](../theory.d/THEORY-871.md) (what reappraisal changes and does not).
