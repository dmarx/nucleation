# THEORY-tmpzdtc8: AI decision-support tools can open an attributability gap, on Zeiser's account: a decision made by a human can express value-judgements embedded in the tool that she has not endorsed, which is a failure of decision ownership distinct from accountability, and it reaches descriptive tools too, so that attributability comes in degrees

Zeiser (2024), LIT-tmp3iik8, read in NOTE-tmpnpm7l.

## The account

The gap occurs "when a human decision-maker uses a decision-tool that
presupposes values that have a significant influence on the outcome of
the decision and that they haven't endorsed as their own values". Of the
recruiter: "Despite being technically made by a human the decision doesn't
reflect characteristics of Susan's judgement." Even "descriptive AI
systems also don't provide neutral, value-free information", so
"attributability might not be a binary property, but a gradual one."
Accountability can be kept without it, through "blank check
responsibility".

## What it does not show

- **That users' values in fact diverge** from their tools'; the paper is
  conceptual.
- **That endorsement suffices** for ownership: endorsement can itself be
  shaped, which the paper does not treat.
