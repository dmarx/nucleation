# NOTE-2362: Bourrat et al. — Individuality through ecology (2024)

## Contribution

A programmatic statement, not a result. It sets the externalist mode of
explanation of transitions in individuality beside the internalist one,
says what each takes as its problem (the free-rider problem against the
origin of Darwinian properties), and reformulates the endogenization
puzzle so that it has stated conditions.

## Key insight

Internalist accounts take higher-level Darwinian properties for granted,
and collective-level selection "cannot be invoked to explain the
emergence of Darwinian properties at the same level" ("Introduction").
An environmental structure can impose those properties first. The
transition is then complete only if, when the scaffold goes, the
collective does not fall back to independent cells: the scaffolding
framework holds that higher-level individuality "has only truly occurred
if a return to an independent mode of existence for cells is hindered
upon the removal of the scaffolding conditions" ("Endogenization as a
Puzzle").

## Assumptions

- Lewontin's three conditions, read as three Darwinian properties:
  discreteness, reproduction, heredity (Box 1, after Godfrey-Smith).
- An "operative cause" is the variable whose intervened change explains
  the phenomenon, taken furthest up a causal chain "assuming there are no
  feedbacks" (Box 1). The internal/external distinction is "relative to
  the respective explanations being contrasted" (Box 2).
- Endogenization "requires the existence of at least two types of
  evolutionary equilibria in the non‐scaffolding environment"
  ("Endogenization as a Puzzle"), separated by a valley the scaffold
  removes (Figure 2, after Doulcier et al. 2024).

## Key results

- **The model of [LIT-1940](../literature.d/LIT-1940.md), restated** ("Ecological Scaffolding and the
  Value of Externalism"): bounded patches of resources; cells die when
  resources are depleted; periodic dispersal of propagules; the number of
  propagules depends on population size at dispersal, set by consumption
  rate against dispersal timing. Selection then acts within and between
  patches and can favour slower growth that avoids over-exploitation.
  Internal changes "are merely “follower” factors in the causal chain".
- **Endogenization defined** (Box 1): "An evolutionary process during
  which higher‐level collectives gain the capacity to persist even in the
  absence of an ecological scaffold", by "developing properties that make
  the ecological scaffold unnecessary for their maintenance"; the
  extracellular matrix is the example.
- **Scaffolds are hard to observe**: "a scaffold is a transient feature
  that disappears over time", the outcome of endogenization; candidate
  settings are tidal pools, reeds in a pond, and obligate pathogens with
  infection bottlenecks.
- **Two peaks** ("Endogenization as a Puzzle", Figure 2): with the
  scaffold, only the collective peak exists; after it, the valley
  returns, and "Once a multicellular mode of living has evolved, there is
  no going back".
- **Constructive scaffolding** ("Combining Changes"): "the origin of the
  scaffold is assumed to be internal" when cells' waste accumulates at the
  edges of resources and makes the patches; "Future formal models will
  elaborate on this idea". What is internal and external shifts: "a
  boundary shift between what is considered internal and external occurs
  during the emergence of new levels of individuality". A scaffold may be
  biotic, a partner in an egalitarian transition.

## Concepts

- **Ecological scaffold**: "An environmental structure that enables
  individuals at one level of organization to realize Darwinian
  properties at a higher level of organization" (Box 1).
- **Endogenization**: as above.
- **Operative cause**; **internalist/externalist mode of explanation**:
  Boxes 1 and 2.

## Connections

It stands in for [LIT-1940](../literature.d/LIT-1940.md) (Black, Bourrat and Rainey 2020) as far as the
model's setting and framing go. It restates: patches, depletion,
periodic propagule dispersal, propagule number set by population size,
within- and between-patch selection, the scaffold as the operative cause,
the Darwinian properties imposed exogenously. It does not restate: the
model's equations, the timescale conditions under which slow growers
win, the result on single-cell founding and a reproductive division of
labour (which bioRxiv's abstract of the preprint names), or any numbers.
It develops the niche-construction route that [LIT-1927](../literature.d/LIT-1927.md) ([NOTE-2361](NOTE-2361.md))
sketches in its §7, and replaces [LIT-1927](../literature.d/LIT-1927.md)'s pleiotropy mechanism with
Doulcier et al.'s two-peaked landscape as the general condition.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md)** (a parallel, with a twist): the paper's constructive
  route is a boundary that cells produce and that then works as an
  imposed one, a scaffold, before being taken into the collective. It
  treats the distinction [CLAIM-194](../claims.d/CLAIM-194.md) draws as one that moves during the
  transition rather than one a test settles once.
- **[CLAIM-529](../claims.d/CLAIM-529.md)** (corroborated): the criterion for completion is stated as
  what happens to a grouping when the scaffold is removed, and can come
  out false ("a return to an independent mode of existence").
- **[QUESTION-398](../questions.d/QUESTION-398.md)**: a stated condition, two equilibria in the
  unscaffolded environment with the population near the collective peak
  when the scaffold goes, under which an organization becomes
  independent of the intervention that established it.
- **[CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md)** (background): its "operative cause" is defined by
  intervention and assumes no feedback, which is the case [CLAIM-522](../claims.d/CLAIM-522.md)
  leaves open; a constructed scaffold is a feedback.

## Limitations

- No model, data or computation; the two-peaks condition is borrowed
  from Doulcier et al. (2024), not shown here.
- The definition of operative cause excludes feedback, and the
  constructive route is a feedback, so the paper's own account of which
  cause is operative does not apply to the case it proposes.
- "Once ... there is no going back" overstates the landscape picture,
  which needs the population near the collective peak when the scaffold
  goes (Figure 2c caption).
- A citation is garbled: "(... Rose et al. 2020; Rainey 1872)" in the
  Introduction.

## Open questions

What is a scaffold that the collective itself makes, once made: inside
or outside? The paper says the line shifts but gives no criterion for
when it has.
