  anaesthetised mice. A whisker-evoked wave spreads at about 0.1 mm/ms in
  every direction, with direction-dependent intensity. A 2-D

  coupling map is inferred from each animal's data. The model then
  predicted, and the experiment confirmed, the main result: two waves

# LIT-tmpzhtw7: Nonlinear collision between propagating waves in mouse somatosensory cortex

di Volo, M. & Férézou, I. (2021). Nonlinear collision between propagating waves in mouse somatosensory cortex. *Scientific Reports* 11: 19630. doi:10.1038/s41598-021-99057-7.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpm4s2r](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpm4s2r.md).*

- **Sub-linear collision, the main result.** "the activity evoked by the two
  stimuli presented sequentially is lower than the linear summation of the
  responses to the two stimuli presented separately". Across all six mice
  there is "a mean peak at around -30%", and the deficit travels as a
  "suppressive wave".
- **An excitable front, not a linear wave.** A local input "can potentially
  trigger a chain of jumps to high activity states that spreads in space".
- **Anisotropy in intensity, not speed.** The speed "is approximately the
  same in all directions", and the inferred couplings show "a heterogeneous
  and non-symmetric distribution".

## Standing in the record

A's description is accurate. The record should carry the result A's
"collisions" leaves unstated: cortical waves here do not superpose. Two
fronts sum to less than their sum. The linear interference of
[LIT-tmphayso](LIT-tmphayso.md) therefore holds for one model's stationary, low-contrast
regime, not as a general description of how cortical waves interact. The
data are from anaesthetised animals; Muller et al. ([LIT-tmp0aok6](LIT-tmp0aok6.md)) is the
awake contrast. There are no spirals, phase singularities or
annihilation, so [THEORY-088](../theory.d/THEORY-088.md) is untouched.
