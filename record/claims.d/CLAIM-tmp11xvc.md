# CLAIM-tmp11xvc: Automation can be viewed as the effort to reduce procedural friction, yet procedural friction exposes, and may even characterize, the opportunities for intervention in a process, so reducing it can remove the places where a process could be stopped, questioned or redirected

## The claim

The owner, U11 of the exchange
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b): "we can
view automation as the effort to reduce procedural friction, but
paradoxically procedural friction exposes (or perhaps even characterizes)
opportunity for intervention."

The steps automation removes as delay are also where something can be
noticed, objected to or stopped. A21 §1's caseworker, applicant and
supervisor each had such a step: "the procedure doesn't just implement a
decision. **It provides opportunities for the decision to become
responsive to objections that couldn't have been fully specified
beforehand.**" A21 §5: "**Automation can preserve the formal right to
object while eliminating the practical opportunity to prevent the harm
that makes objection necessary.**"

## Its scope, from A21

- **Friction is not delay.** A21 §2: meaningful intervention needs
  visibility, deliberation, contestability and control, and "a delay alone
  guarantees none of them." "**Politically and morally valuable friction
  is not delay itself, but the preservation of meaningful points at which
  an unfolding process remains open to intervention.**" The claim is about
  that kind of friction.
- **Two kinds.** A21 §4: "**Extractive friction** consumes people's time,
  attention, or resources without giving them meaningful influence over
  the decision." "**Deliberative friction** creates opportunities to
  introduce reasons, recognize exceptions, challenge assumptions, or alter
  an outcome." Only the second is what the owner's "opportunity for
  intervention" names. The two coexist in one process, and an
  administrative burden can be the first without the second.
- **Three strengths.** A21 §9: weak, "Friction exposes intervention
  opportunities"; stronger, "Friction can constitute intervention
  opportunities"; strongest, "**The distribution of friction partly
  constitutes the distribution of political power.**" The claim is the
  first two, which are the owner's "exposes (or perhaps even
  characterizes)". The third is A's and is not filed; its closing question
  is "**Who should possess the power to make whom stop and give
  reasons?**"

**Why it rests on CLAIM-523.** A30 §2, setting the claim beside CLAIM-523's
three achievements: "**procedural friction is one possible institutional
mechanism for maintaining the three achievements across time**", and "not
every interruption provides all three", so "It's whether a procedure
preserves the *causal pathway through which a justified challenge can
change what happens*." Removing a step removes an intervention point only
if the step was where legibility, efficacy or correctability was had.

## How it sits with the record

- **CLAIM-523** (Active, the owner's synthesis) says what an objection needs
  to change what an organization does; this says where in a process those
  needs are met, and what automation does to them.
- **CLAIM-410** (Active, the owner's) holds that removing a constraint
  redistributes governing positions rather than reducing governance. A21
  §2 says the same of friction: "**What looks like a reduction in friction
  from the organization's perspective may actually be a transfer of
  discretion from one group of people to another.**"
- Developed from it, in the same exchange: CLAIM-tmph28l6 (execution and
  contestation friction), CLAIM-tmpohafo (explanation, contestation,
  interruption), CLAIM-tmp238zy (cognitive and political friction),
  CLAIM-tmpgh3v2 (a slow rule over a fast process) and the study
  CASE-tmpdn372.
- CASE-250's "interface friction" is a cost put in a user's way to steer
  them; it is extractive friction in A21's sense, not this.

## Attribution

A21: "I'd sharpen your observation slightly. Friction doesn't merely
*expose* opportunities for intervention. In many institutional settings,
it actually **constitutes the opportunity for intervention**". The owner's
parenthesis, "(or perhaps even characterizes)", had already made that
move, and A21 §2 then credits it to "your phrasing". The stronger form is
the owner's. A26 §VI, the synthesis the owner asked for at U14, credits
it: "Your observation about procedural friction gave the framework a more
general political dimension." A26 tells the rest of the framework as
joint ("we called"), though its coinages are A's.

## What it does not say

- Not that more friction is better. A23 §4: "**The aim is not more friction
  or less friction in general. It's a more just distribution of
  friction.**"
- Not that older, slower procedures were fairer. A21 §4: "Procedural
  friction can protect arbitrary power just as easily as it can constrain
  it."
