# THEORY-tmp316ih: Meyer argues that a threshold notion of harm lets a weakly person-affecting view state harms to future people despite the non-identity problem, best specified as weakly sufficientarian, and that such a view gives no reason to prefer a flourishing future to none, a gap he fills with concerns that rest on what present and past people value and an obligation owed to them

## The account

Future people's existence, number and identity depend on our choices, so
harm understood as making someone worse off than they were, or would
otherwise have been, cannot reach them. On a threshold notion, a person is
harmed if she is brought into existence, or kept, below a level she is owed;
she is then "worse off than she ought to be" (§3.1). A disjunctive notion
joins that to the subjunctive-historical one (§3.2).

The threshold is best specified as weakly sufficientarian, in basic needs or
central capabilities, after egalitarian and prioritarian rivals are set
aside (§§4.1–4.4).

The cost, stated plainly: such an account "may specify no reason for
preferring a future with people all of whom have lives far above the level
of a sufficiency threshold to a future with no people" (§4.6). Concern that
humanity continue needs another ground: Scheffler's afterlife conjecture,
and Meyer's own obligation with no correlative right not to destroy
inherited goods or what future-oriented projects depend on. "While future
people belong to the beneficiaries, the obligation is owed to both present
and past people."

## Evidence

Argument, on the Interest Theory of rights and a weak person-affecting view,
both assumed (§1). The threshold is normative, not found. Impersonal views
are reported, not examined ([NOTE-1304](../notes.d/NOTE-1304.md), Limitations).

## What it bears on

positions." Its note of 2026-10-29 reads it as showing that "the grounds
divide the labour": a weak person-affecting view states harms to actual
future people and fills its gap "with Scheffler's grounding in what the
