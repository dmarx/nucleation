# NOTE-2229: Jafarpour, Cisneros-Velarde and Bullo — Weak and semi-contraction

## Contribution

A coordinate-free theory of contraction with respect to seminorms (semi-contraction) and of non-expansion (weak contraction), their combination (doubly contracting systems), and applications to affine averaging, flow systems, primal–dual dynamics and synchronization of diffusively coupled oscillators.

## Key results

1. **What semi-contraction allows.** It "allows the distance between trajectories to increase in certain directions, thus requiring the dynamical system to be contractive only on a certain subspace" (§I).
2. **Definition 12.** Semi-contracting means the matrix semi-measure of the Jacobian is at most −c; Theorem 13(ii) gives convergence "to x∗ + Ker|||·||| with exponential rate c", a translate of the kernel, not a point.
3. **The kernel coordinate is not controlled.** Theorem 13(ii) "does not state that the asymptotic behavior of generic trajectories is the same" as that of trajectories in the invariant set; in Example 16 a trajectory reaches the kernel while one coordinate diverges, and "this trajectory does not converge to any equilibrium point" (p. 7).
4. **Neither property is enough alone.** "semi-contractivity or weak-contractivity alone do not guarantee the convergence of trajectories to equilibrium points" (§V); Theorem 24 gives convergence to some equilibrium in the kernel for doubly contracting systems, without saying which.
5. **What fixes the limit.** For ẋ = −Lx + b with vᵀb = 0 the limit is L†b + (vᵀx₀)1, because "Since vTL = 0, we have vTx(t) = vTx0" (Theorem 26); with vᵀb ≠ 0 every trajectory is unbounded. The limit is set by the conserved functional vᵀx, v the dominant left eigenvector, not by the seminorm.

## Assumptions and concepts

Continuous time, deterministic, f twice differentiable; convex invariant domains; shifted-invariant kernel for Theorem 13(ii); time invariance for convergence results; globally reachable node or connected undirected graph for the network results. Concepts: seminorm and kernel, matrix semi-measure, infinitesimal and shifted invariance, weak, semi- and doubly contracting systems.

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-772](../claims.d/CLAIM-772.md)** (qualifies). The paper supports one framework for global and partial contraction (a seminorm with trivial kernel is a norm, p. 3), but semi-contraction alone keeps nothing: the kernel is where the system does not contract, which is not a part it conserves. What a limit keeps is fixed by an exact conserved functional (vᵀx), DeGroot's mechanism ([THEORY-960](../theory.d/THEORY-960.md)). The claim is revised to say so (version 3).
- **[TERM-132](../terms.d/TERM-132.md)** (qualifies). The kernel of a seminorm may hold neutral or expansive directions; the term's conserved part is the exactly conserved functionals, not the kernel. Revised (version 2).
- **[ARG-124](../arguments.d/ARG-124.md)** (qualifies). ODEs only; strengthens the argument's reliance on an exact invariant.
- **[THEORY-960](../theory.d/THEORY-960.md)** (supports, continuous-time counterpart): the consensus value is fixed by the invariant vᵀx as DeGroot's by πF.
- **[THEORY-2044](../theory.d/THEORY-2044.md)** (supports): Remark 14 presents Theorem 13(ii) as a generalization of partial contraction.
- **[THEORY-1161](../theory.d/THEORY-1161.md)** (Friedkin–Johnsen, held; the reader's recheck): "anchoring on initial positions" re-injects the start as a persistent input, the b term here, a different sense of anchoring from keeping a conserved part; named in [CLAIM-772](../claims.d/CLAIM-772.md)'s limits.

## Limitations

Sufficient conditions; the seminorm is found case by case; continuous-time and deterministic; the limit is identified only in linear examples; a small slip in Theorem 28's statement of ν∗.
