# NOTE-1532: Juarrero — Context Changes Everything

## Contribution

A book-length synthesis, not a result. It restates the constraint vocabulary
of *Dynamics in Action* (1999, [LIT-1990](../literature.d/LIT-1990.md), which it cites for enabling
constraints, second-order context-dependent constraints and hermeneutics)
and extends it from intentional action to "complex systems, living and
nonliving" (p. 23): convection, lasers, homeostasis, ecosystems, economies,
cultures. Its claim is ontological: coherent dynamics generated by
constraints are "real and novel, interactional types of entities" (p. 72)
with top-down powers. The author calls it "a work of speculative
metaphysics" (p. 32). There are no models, theorems or data of the author's
own; the empirical chapter (15) interprets two published neuroscience
studies.

## Key insight

Causation by constraint. A constraint changes what can happen and how
likely it is without transferring energy: "Constraints are entities,
processes, events, relations, or conditions that raise or lower barriers to
energy flow without directly transferring kinetic energy" (p. 40). Timing a
swing's kick "does not impart additional energy to the swing over and
beyond the kick itself" (p. 46). On that basis the book claims that a
whole can act on its parts without overdetermination or a breach of causal
closure (pp. 25, 46, 186): bottom-up enabling constraints make a coherent
dynamic, and its constitutive constraints then "do double duty as
governing constraints" (p. 79), confining components "to a subregion of
the system's overall possibility space" (p. 80).

## Assumptions

- **Open systems far from equilibrium** throughout: "The one prerequisite:
  conditions must be open and away from thermal equilibrium" (p. 233).
- **Probability as the measure of constraint.** Constraints are "encoded as
  probabilities" (p. 46); context-dependent constraints are "measured with
  conditional probability: X is more or less likely to happen, given the
  presence of constraint Y, in context Z" (p. 67). "Selection" and
  "filtering" are to be "translated into the language of conditional
  probability" (p. 82).
- **Realism about possibility spaces and order parameters.** Possibility
  spaces "are not solely epistemic; they are real" (p. 47); Pattee's
  "statistical" loss of detail tracks real coherence, not only epistemic
  simplification (pp. 96–97).
- **Wholes are not other than parts.** Governing constraints "are not
  spatiotemporally other than the components and constraints from which
  they were formed and which they control" (p. 81; again pp. 84, 95, 233).
- **Constraints transfer no energy**, so their effects escape conservation
  and closure arguments (p. 46). The book does not argue for this beyond the
  examples.

## Key results

### The kinds of constraint (chs. 3, 4, 6)

- **Context-independent** constraints (gradients, fields, buffers, isolation)
  "take or keep conditions away from equiprobability" (p. 67).
- **Context-dependent** constraints "take conditions away from independence
  (Gatlin 1972)" by making processes "mutually conditional on one another
  and on the history and context in which they formed" (p. 67). Kauffman's
  buttons, Huygens's clocks and Bénard cells are the examples (pp. 67–69).
- **Enabling** constraints (Pattee 1973; Salthe 1985; Juarrero 1999)
  "irreversibly link and couple previously separate ... entities at the same
  scale as the constraints" and "drive parts-to-whole phase transitions"
  (p. 71).
- **Constitutive/governing** constraints (Mossio 2013; Moreno and Mossio
  2016) hold the result together, and act top down as "second-order
  context-dependent constraints (Juarrero 1999)" (p. 81), "often as
  negative feedback loops" (p. 79), as "distributed mechanisms of control"
  embodied in order parameters (p. 80).
- **Temporal** constraints make one event conditional on an earlier one;
  sequences become units ("chunked into a context-independent constraint, a
  ritual or protocol", p. 74), and complex systems become historical: "They
  carry their history on their backs" (p. 75).

### Time scales and levels (chs. 6, 12)

- **Coherence makes slower patterns.** Entrained pendulums' "overarching wave
  patterns ... are longer and slower than the swings of the individual
  pendulums", and "Different cycling rates at different scales of
  organization will play a key role in refashioning a new understanding of
  the boundaries of identity" (p. 68).
- **Inside a coherent dynamic, coupling is faster.** Its interactions become
  "more tightly coupled, their feedback loops and relaxation times shorter,
  faster, and more regular" than the environment's or the elements' (p. 72).
- **Boundaries are rate differences, not material.** "Rate differences
  between full predator–prey cycles and the life span of the individual
  animals entrained in those cycles constitute the boundaries between parts
  and wholes" (p. 77).
- **Levels are rate-demarcated** (Salthe 1985, 72): "Boundaries between
  levels of organization and constraint are therefore typically marked by
  sharp discontinuities in order parameters and average process and
  relaxation rates", and "overarching hierarchical levels of organization
  are usually slower than their components" (p. 183).
- **Why levels interact by constraint**, quoting Salthe: processes at
  different levels cannot interact energetically "because [faster]
  lower-level dynamics would have been completed before feedback from the
  [slower] upper level arrived" (p. 187). Whole-to-part relations are "rate
  independent" (Salthe 1985, 72): governing constraints "inform and
  influence" components "without participating in them dynamically" (p. 188).
- **Control is lost when the rates converge.** The asymmetry "can be
  destroyed by either excessively accelerating the dynamics of an embedding
  environment or slowing down the rates of the focal-level system beyond its
  tolerance range" (p. 189); "Lack of asymmetry in cycling rates ... reflects
  the absence of control constraint by one on the other, regardless of
  signal amplitude" (p. 190). A component sped up becomes "invisible" to
  its context; the tumour is the example (p. 190).
- **Control can also be lost without a change of rates.** When faculty
  can threaten to take their grants elsewhere, "indirect enabling
  constraints ... blur hierarchical levels and top-down control is muted
  or lost—all without any extra energy having been expended" (p. 188).
  Rate asymmetry is a condition of control in the book, not what suffices
  for it. The same picture recurs at p. 107: "Earth 'treats' those slower
  cycling rates as 'constants' (Allen and Starr 1982; Salthe 1985, 2001)".
- **Attribution.** The convergence clause (p. 189) is from Allen, O'Neill
  and Hoekstra 1984; the "invisible" component and the tumour (pp. 185,
  190) from Allen and Starr 1982.
- **Modularity and near-decomposability** come from Simon, but the book holds
  that "this matryoshka doll image ... is misleading", since hypercycles and
  closure "confound the clean material separability of layers", so
  decomposability is analysed "dynamically, as differences ... in phases,
  rates, scales" (p. 183).

### Closure (ch. 7)

- Montévil and Mossio's closure of constraints is quoted from Moreno and
  Mossio 2015 (pp. 20–21) and their figure reproduced (pp. 93–95, fig. 7.1).
  The book adds that the closed loop C₂–C₄ "spans (and is conserved
  throughout) a longer interval, τ2 through τ4" and "persists despite
  turnover in its components" (p. 94), and glosses it as "a virtual
  governor ... a hypercatalyst" (p. 93).
- It reports their view that physical and chemical dissipative structures
  "still depend on the externally set ... boundary conditions" and are "not
  yet self-constraining" (p. 92), and accepts it: closure of constraints is
  what makes a system "self-determining" (p. 94). Yet it also says
  "Dissipative processes like the Rayleigh–Bénard cells and BZ reactions
  described earlier are autopoietic" (p. 91), which neither Maturana and
  Varela nor Montévil and Mossio say.

### Many-to-one transitions and analog control (ch. 11)

- Coherence-making is "the generation of such Many-to-One functions" (p.
  158): different physical inputs recoded as the same "effective input"
  relative to order parameters (Patten and Auble 1980; pp. 156–157).
- Top-down control is analog: order parameters take continuous values, so
  control "in reference to continuous values supports a wider range of
  behavior" (p. 168) and is "more tolerant of error" (p. 169). Ashby's law
  of requisite variety is invoked (p. 168). The examples are fuzzy-logic
  thermostats and anti-lock brakes (pp. 168–169).
- The analog layer "supervenes on precise and accurate digital input–output
  relations" (p. 175).
- Qualia are hypothesized as tags on recoded integration that speed action
  (pp. 163–166). Stated as a hypothesis.

### The neuroscience (ch. 15)

- Churchland et al. 2012 and Shenoy et al. 2011: preparatory activity
  occupies a low-dimensional subregion whose end state predicts reaction
  time and movement; population activity rotates (pp. 224–227).
- Mante, Sussillo et al. 2013: in PFC a line attractor ("axis of choice")
  and a context-dependent "selection vector" integrate only the cued
  evidence (pp. 227–232).
- The book reads the subspace as "top-down governing constraints for the
  correct response" (p. 230) and its persistence as what "can be
  interpreted as the standing cause of the response" (p. 230). The axis of
  choice "might be considered the neural correlate of a prior intention"
  (p. 229) and the selection vector "the neural correlate of a proximate
  intention of action" (p. 231). It says the second study "proves the
  construction of an organized neural space" (p. 229) and that the first
  subspace "structures, initializes, and controls" the response (p. 223).
  The studies' own evidence, as the chapter reports it, is dimensionality
  reduction, prediction ("in the sense that it is predictive of actual
  reaction times and movement", p. 225) and a trained-network simulation
  for the selection vector ("In a simulation", p. 230). No intervention on
  the population pattern is described.

## Concepts

- **Constraint** — anything that raises or lowers barriers to energy flow
  without transferring kinetic energy (p. 40). No time-scale condition.
- **Context-independent / context-dependent constraint** — away from
  equiprobability / away from independence (Gatlin; p. 67).
- **Enabling constraint** — couples separate entities at the same scale,
  bottom up (p. 71).
- **Constitutive / governing constraint** — the constraint regime of the
  coherent whole, acting top down as a "second-order context-dependent
  constraint" (p. 81).
- **Interactional type** — a coherent, multiply realizable dynamic
  generated by constraints; "Types are not things" (p. 27).
- **Effective input / output** — physical signals as recoded relative to a
  system's order parameters (Patten and Auble; pp. 156–158).
- **Analog control** — control by continuous order parameters (pp. 167–175).
- **Profile** — a possibility space's "probability contour" or "dynamic
  signature" (p. 46).

## Bearing on the record

- **[CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md): philosophical prior art for both directions, without the
  mechanism.** Organization makes separation: entrained pendulums produce a
  pattern "longer and slower" than their swings (p. 68), and higher levels
  are "usually slower than their components" (p. 183). Separation makes
  control: inter-level influence is by constraint because the faster level
  completes before the slower one's feedback arrives (p. 187), and "Lack of
  asymmetry in cycling rates ... reflects the absence of control constraint"
  (p. 190). That is close to the claim's thesis and to its defeater's
  negation. But the book has no slow manifold, no singular perturbation and
  no critical slowing; its source is hierarchy theory (Salthe), and its
  constraint (p. 40) is not relative to a time scale, unlike [LIT-582](../literature.d/LIT-582.md)'s.
  It also says that within a coherent dynamic the internal loops get
  faster (p. 72), which fits the claim (fast internal relaxation is what
  settles a relation) but which the book does not connect to the slow
  whole. Bousquet et al.'s critical separation ([LIT-1292](../literature.d/LIT-1292.md)) is the formal
  counterpart of its p. 189: an asymmetry that fails when the rates come
  together. [THEORY-1306](../theory.d/THEORY-1306.md) states what the book holds here. Rate separation is,
  for the book, a condition of control and not sufficient for it: p. 188
  has control "muted or lost" by an enabling constraint with no change of
  rates.
- **[CLAIM-533](../claims.d/CLAIM-533.md): the 2023 book has nothing on sensitivity or chaos, and
  credits control to confinement.** Its control is governing constraints
  holding components to a subregion "through cascades of negative feedback"
  (p. 101; pp. 79–80, 167), measured against Ashby's requisite variety
  (p. 168). Amplification appears only as the bottom-up route to a
  phase transition (p. 25). That fits the claim's "sensitivity is not
  control", and Jennings's emphasis on constraint over amplification, but it
  is not an argument for either.
- **Froese's objection ([NOTE-1521](NOTE-1521.md)), read against the 2023 book.** Froese
  says it makes no difference to the subject's efficacy whether one credits
  an order parameter or the neural substrate. The 2023 book grants his
  premise: governing constraints "are not spatiotemporally other than the
  components" (p. 81). Its answer to overdetermination is that constraints
  transfer no energy (pp. 25, 46). That answers the closure worry, not
  Froese's question about whose efficacy it is. And the book is not
  libertarian: "The price of such creativity and uniqueness is
  indetermination and uncertainty" (p. 235), with no account of an
  agent's control over that indetermination. So Froese's diagnosis fits
  the 2023 book; whether it fits the 1999 book is unread.
- **[TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md): Juarrero's context-dependent constraint is not the record's
  constraint.** Hers is Gatlin's: a departure from independence, a
  conditional dependence among processes (p. 67), with no condition that it
  be conserved at the time scale of the process it acts on. [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)'s
  constraint is Montévil and Mossio's, which has that condition. The two
  meet only in chapter 7, where the book adopts [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)'s closure word for
  word (pp. 93–94) and adds the time-scale reading of the loop (p. 94). Her
  "governing constraint" is the nearer match: it is a whole's regime acting
  on parts, and her chapter 12 makes it slower. A reader citing Juarrero
  for "constraint" in [TERM-070](../terms.d/TERM-070.md)'s sense should cite chapter 7 only.
- **[TERM-100](../terms.d/TERM-100.md) and [CLAIM-532](../claims.d/CLAIM-532.md): self-determination here is closure,
  so maintenance, and the book's claim about history is the opposite of
  [CASE-191](../cases.d/CASE-191.md)'s result.** Self-determination is "self-constraint" by
  closure (pp. 93–94), a present organization; the book offers no
  formation condition. But it holds that coherent dynamics "carry their
  history on their backs" (p. 75) and that constrained trajectories "make
  manifest the context and the moment in which they were created" (p. 76),
  with graphite and diamond as the case. [CASE-191](../cases.d/CASE-191.md) builds two agents
  with different formation histories that reach the same organization,
  which no knockout separates afterwards. The book's own multiple
  realizability (one type, many routes) cuts the same way as the case.
  Its examples differ in kind (different attractors); the case is one
  attractor reached two ways.
- **[THEORY-1314](../theory.d/THEORY-1314.md) (Jennings, contextual emergence): a related view with a
  different boundary.** The book does not use "contextual emergence". It
  shares the Bénard case and says convection cells "depend on externally
  set boundary conditions" (p. 91). It even has the skull: "a central
  nervous system with a brain protected by a skull and capable of supporting
  context-dependent coordination might have evolved" (p. 168). But its
  boundaries are rate differences, not material ones ("boundaries need not
  be tangible, material structures", p. 77), and it holds that the governing
  regime is not other than the parts (p. 81). So it does not supply the
  irreducible boundary that [THEORY-1314](../theory.d/THEORY-1314.md)'s promote_when asks for, and its
  neural evidence (ch. 15) is of the same correlational and model kind.
- **As a stand-in for [LIT-1990](../literature.d/LIT-1990.md).** It restates the 1999 constraint
  vocabulary and cites the 1999 book for its key terms, so it is a fair
  source for what Juarrero means by enabling, governing and second-order
  constraints. It is not a source for the 1999 book's action theory
  (intentions as constraints on behavior), which chapters 13 and 15 touch
  and this reading did not cover in full.

## Limitations

- **Speculative and unformalized** by its author's account. Claims C1, C5
  and C6 are argued by example.
- **The energy-free claim** (C1) is not examined: a governing constraint is
  maintained by dissipating processes (p. 82: vasculature), so whether its
  influence involves no energy transfer depends on how the system is cut.
- **Inconsistency on autopoiesis** (p. 91 against p. 92).
- **The neuroscience is read as top-down causation** where the studies as
  reported give prediction and model fits.
- **Seven chapters were searched, not read** (1, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10, 14; see
  history), including the philosophical case study (ch. 14, Shapiro,
  Gillett and Lange) and the persistence and entrenchment chapters (9,
  10). None bears on the THEORYs filed from this reading.

## Open questions

- What, in the book's terms, separates a constraint that is slower than
  the process it constrains from one that is not? Its swing-timing example
  (p. 46) is a phase relation, not a slow variable; does it fall under
  [CLAIM-478](../claims.d/CLAIM-478.md) or against it?
- Does the 1999 book's account of intentions as context-sensitive
  constraints say more about control against noise than this one does?
- Chapters 9 and 10 (persistence, entrenchment) may bear on [CLAIM-331](../claims.d/CLAIM-331.md) and
  [CLAIM-477](../claims.d/CLAIM-477.md); unread here.
