The owner asked for contemporary work in conversation with Lessig's
*Remix* ([LIT-tmpreyek](../../record/literature.d/LIT-tmpreyek.md), entry of 01:56 today), "at the intersection of
property/ownership and expression/communication". Ten works were
proposed, and the owner said to file them all. Each was checked against
Crossref (or the court's or agency's own page), then searched for a
lawful full text. Seven were read in full and three were not reached.

**How they were read.** Seven sub-agents of this session each took one
or two works under a shared brief. The brief told them to read every
line, file by `luria new`, and check every quotation against the
extracted text by script. The coordinator re-ran that check on all
thirteen files, and every quotation matched. Two gaps were closed by
hand:

- *The Line* was first filed Unavailable, because the Duke repository
  and MIT Press Direct both returned bot checks. The publisher's own PDF
  was then found in MIT Press's open-access collection on the Internet
  Archive, and the book was read from it.
- The authors' posting of *The End of Ownership* lacks p. 193. That page
  was read from the same MIT Press collection.

Drassinower is the one to get first. Balganesh's review reports his
thesis that a work is a communicative act. It does not mention the
publisher's further claim, that an author's rights reach only as far as
"the structure of the dialogue" in which the work takes part allows.
Only the book can settle that.

**The boundary.** The anthology holds nothing on copyright (clone of
2026-10-20, commit d1573d2). The Copyright Office report carries
`anthology-candidate` last ([ADR-005](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-005.md)), because the anthology's
deployment-and-society topic could hold it. It is filed here for this
record's question: whether training is copying in Lessig's sense. *The
Line* is about AI but carries no flag. It is legal and moral theory of
personhood, as [LIT-166](../../record/literature.d/LIT-166.md) is.

**What the batch found, across readings.**

- **Lessig's copy trigger is confirmed from three sides.** *The End of
  Ownership* shows it from the buyer's side: digital transfer and use
  make copies, so first sale and lending fail. The Copyright Office
  accepts it for training's inputs, down to temporary copies. Boyle
  describes the same thing as triggers on "individual footsteps". The
  Office does not extend it to the trained model: the weights infringe
  only where a work can be recovered from them.
- **"Remix needs the original" is narrowed in law.** Boyle and Tushnet
  agree with Lessig that critique and sampling need the actual artifact.
  The *Warhol* majority accepts that only where the original is the
  target, as with parody. Commentary aimed elsewhere counts as satire,
  and it must justify the borrowing. The Court measures purpose of use;
  Kagan's dissent measures meaning.
- **[CLAIM-210](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-210.md) gets independent legal instances.** Copyright's
  independent-creation rule (Balganesh) means sharing a kind without
  being a rendering is no wrong. In *Warhol*, sharing the photograph's
  purpose mattered only once there was copying. The Office's new
  "market dilution" harm is the one place the law reaches outputs that
  share a kind with their sources without copying any one of them.
- **Tushnet adds verbal overshadowing.** Describing an image changes how
  a receiver later sees it. That bears on how [CLAIM-220](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-220.md) names its
  receivers, and it is evidence the translation line lacked.
- **Personhood.** *The Line* keeps human, legal person and moral person
  apart, as [ADR-024](../../record/decisions.d/ADR-024.md) does. But it also takes autonomy as evidence of
  consciousness. Its "inscrutability paradox" predicts that
  understanding a mechanism leads people to deny it agency, so public
  attributions would diverge from [CLAIM-174](../../record/claims.d/CLAIM-174.md)'s test.
