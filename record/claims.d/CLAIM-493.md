# CLAIM-493: When a fanbase is set against an outside party such as its club's owner, its more extreme members become more prototypical of it, so a vocal minority can fix the fanbase's public stance through a group process and not only through biased sampling of who speaks

## The claim

[QUESTION-119](../questions.d/QUESTION-119.md) asks when a stance credited to a diffuse population is the
population's, and when it is a biased sample of what some members said.
This claim offers a third possibility for [CASE-104](../cases.d/CASE-104.md): a minority stance that
becomes the group's through how the group perceives itself.

Self-categorization theory holds that a category's prototype is set by
metacontrast with the salient out-group. As a group becomes more extreme
in an intergroup context, its more extreme members gain prototypicality
over moderates (Turner et al. 1994, p. 458; [THEORY-1114](../theory.d/THEORY-1114.md)). A dispute
with an owner is such a context. Turner (2005, [LIT-1791](../literature.d/LIT-1791.md), unread)
argues that influence follows prototypicality. Hornsey (2008, p. 211;
NOTE-1253) reports the step at second hand, and that in the SCT tests
prototypicality affected influence only under high salience or
identification. The fans most hostile to
the owner would then be taken, by fans themselves, as speaking for the
fans, and their stance would set the norm (A2's third reading in [CASE-104](../cases.d/CASE-104.md))
without any procedure.

## What it does not say

- **Not that the private majority holds the stance.** The point is that a
  stance can be the group's public stance without that.
- **Not that sampling bias is absent.** The two mechanisms can add up.
- **Not tested.** It is a derivation from studies of laboratory and
  student groups, applied to fandom.

## Note of 2026-10-30: what holds it back

Its premise THEORY-1114 is Proposed: no study that varies only the
comparative frame and measures who is judged typical has been read, and
the two primary studies the record names are Unavailable. Neither step is
tested in a fanbase: no measurement shows judged typicality shifting
toward the extreme while a dispute with the owner is salient, and the step
from prototypicality to influence rests on Turner 2005, unread, reported
only by Hornsey.
