# THEORY-912: In a picture-based avoidance task, treatment-seeking patients with OCD or PTSD avoided a safety cue more than healthy controls and, after effective avoidance, still expected the aversive picture more; patients with panic disorder with agoraphobia or PTSD reported more relief when it was omitted; the groups did not differ in avoiding the danger cues

## Source

De Kleine, Hutschemaekers, Hendriks, Kampman, Papalini, Van Minnen &
Vervliet, [LIT-1490](../literature.d/LIT-1490.md), read in full in [NOTE-1123](../notes.d/NOTE-1123.md) from the
publisher's version of record (CC BY 4.0): pp. 3–8, Table 1 and
Figures 2–3.

## What was actually shown

- **Avoidance.** A Group × CS interaction, driven by OCD (p = .004) and
  PTSD (p = .028) patients pressing the avoidance button more than
  controls to the safety cue. "No significant differences between groups
  were found in reaction to the CS+ ’s" (p. 5).
- **Expectancy.** OCD and PTSD patients rated the aversive picture as
  more expected than controls did after the safety cue and the avoidable
  danger cue. They also did so on trials where they had pressed the
  button to the avoidable cue (OCD p = .045, PTSD p < .001; p. 6).
- **Relief.** PDA and PTSD patients reported more relief at omissions
  than controls, and PTSD patients more than the other two patient
  groups. This held with rated aversiveness as a covariate (p. 7).
- **Before avoidance.** In the Pavlovian phase the groups did not differ
  in expectancy, but relief to the safety cue already differed in the
  same direction (p. 5).

What could have come out otherwise: patients could have avoided the
danger cues more, as the authors predicted for the unavoidable one. Or
they could have learned the action-safety contingency as well as
controls, with any difference confined to avoidance rates.

## What this does not say

- **That excess relief reinforces avoidance.** That is the authors'
  account (p. 7). Relief was rated, and reinforcement was not measured.
  Relief was elevated before avoidance existed in the task.
- **That the pattern maintains the disorders.** The design is
  cross-sectional. The authors: it "does not allow testing whether the
  observed effects and proposed mechanism are causal to the disorder"
  (p. 8).
- **That it is diagnosis-specific.** Two of three groups showed each
  effect, and comorbidity crossed the groups.
- **That learning, rather than threat appraisal, differs.** Patients
  rated the pictures as more aversive, and the authors raise this as a
  rival explanation (p. 7).
- **Anything about costly avoidance.** Pressing the button cost nothing.

## Connections

- **[CLAIM-446](../claims.d/CLAIM-446.md).** It supports the clause that relief reinforces
  the defence without being evidence of danger, weakly and only through
  the authors' interpretation. It complicates the clause about
  identifiability: patients avoided without trusting the avoidance,
  which is a different failure from learning that avoidance works while
  learning nothing about the world without it.
- **[THEORY-285](THEORY-285.md).** A different failure from the model's. There the agent
  stops sampling. Here patients sampled as often as controls and drew
  less from the outcomes.
