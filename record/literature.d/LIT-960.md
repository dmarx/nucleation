  from the group's irreducible representations block-diagonalizes the

# LIT-960: Symmetries, Cluster Synchronization, and Isolated Desynchronization in Complex Networks

Louis M. Pecora, Francesco Sorrentino, Aaron M. Hagerstrom, Thomas E. Murphy and Rajarshi Roy (2014), *Nature Communications* 5, 4079.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-775](../notes.d/NOTE-775.md).*

- **The wiring fixes which clusters can synchronize.** For
  ẋᵢ = F(xᵢ) + σ Σⱼ Aᵢⱼ H(xⱼ), with identical units and a symmetric 0/1
  adjacency matrix, each symmetry of the graph commutes with A. So the
  orbits of the symmetry group can move identically.
- **The dynamics fix which do.** The IRR transformation makes TAT⁻¹
  block-diagonal. Each cluster is stable where its own transverse block's
  maximum Lyapunov exponent is negative, for any node dynamics.
- **Isolated desynchronization.**
  - If the group splits into commuting factors, nodes outside a cluster
    see the same summed input from it whether or not it is synchronized.
  - So it can desynchronize alone.
  - Clusters moved by one non-decomposable factor are "intertwined" and
    desynchronize together.
- **Observed.** In an 11-node, 32-symmetry electro-optic network, the
  four-node cluster split while the others held (β = 1.4π). Two
  intertwined clusters lost synchrony together. Simulated exponents
  matched both.
- **Common.** Almost all of 10,000 random, Barabási–Albert and bipartite
  100-node graphs of each kind had several nontrivial clusters and
  several factors. So did two power grids.
- **Limits stated in the paper.** Symmetry is sufficient, not necessary:
  clusters also arise from matched input and output couplings without
  symmetry. Nonidentical units and weighted or directed coupling "can all
  be handled in much the same way", but are not worked through.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 for [QUESTION-063](../questions.d/QUESTION-063.md) and read on 2026-10-14 in
[NOTE-775](../notes.d/NOTE-775.md). Its finding is filed as [THEORY-276](../theory.d/THEORY-276.md). The registration
said its clusters are "a coupling signature that a shared external drive
does not predict". The paper never considers external drive. The
inference is the record's, and it is sound only within the paper's class:
identical units on a known graph. [THEORY-276](../theory.d/THEORY-276.md) states it in that form.
It also bears on [CLAIM-172](../claims.d/CLAIM-172.md) and [CLAIM-157](../claims.d/CLAIM-157.md). A mutually coupled network
yields several synchronized sub-collectives whose members need not be
linked, and their invariance comes from equal input, not from weak
coupling across a block's edge.
