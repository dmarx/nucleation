# NOTE-tmpgvphv: Alfrink, Keller, Kortuem and Doorn (2022) — Contestable AI by Design: Towards a Framework

## Contribution

A synthesis of the contestable-AI literature (Scopus 2016–2021, snowballed, about twenty included papers) into a framework that places each proposal by actor and by lifecycle stage.

## Key results

1. §1: contestable systems are "open and responsive to human intervention throughout their lifecycle: not only after an automated decision has been made, but also during its design and development."
2. §2, after Hirsch et al.: contestability works "by allowing human controllers to intervene before machine decisions are put into force."
3. §2, after Almada: intervention can "take place not only post-hoc, in response to an individual decision, but also ex-ante, as part of AI system development processes"; ex-ante contestability "allows for an 'agonistic debate'" over data and modelling choices.
4. §6.1.1: built-in safeguards limit what systems can do unilaterally, e.g. a second, external system every decision passes through: "If disagreement between both systems occurs, decision can be flagged for human review"; also formal constraints on behaviour.
5. §6.1.2: interactive control "enables direct intervention in machine decisions".
6. §6.1.4: "Requests for human intervention are necessarily post-hoc, since they happen in response to discrete decisions"; "Reliance on out-of-system mechanisms for contestation is insufficient"; a human "at the very end of the chain" without authority or information "may not be a sufficient safeguard"; reviewers need "the authority and capability to actually change previous decisions".
7. §7: the adversarial approach "should be an effective way to curb the administrative logic of efficiency".
8. §7: "a small sample of academic papers"; "Many of these papers are not based on empirically validated interventions"; validation "was not part of this paper".

## Assumptions

Contestability serves fairness, legitimacy and self-determination; automated decisions are sociotechnical and organizational; literature proposals are a sound base for design knowledge.

## Concepts

Contestability; ex-ante against post-hoc contestation; contestability by design; built-in safeguards; interactive control; intervention requests; agonistic ML development; third-party oversight; traceable decision chains.

## Bearing on the record

- **CLAIM-tmpuiaop**: the second-system safeguard is a designed step at which each automated decision is shown to a human, if flagged, before it takes effect. That is the "exposes" reading as a design pattern. Without such a step, the paper says, a subject's intervention is "necessarily post-hoc". The paper gives no evidence.
- **CLAIM-tmpwpvbz**: the paper's two routes to contestability before effect are the objection's two architectures. One is ex-ante contestation of design choices (slowly set standing constraints); the other is safeguards and formal constraints enforced on each decision. The paper offers them alongside post-hoc review, not as replacements for it.
- **TERM-tmp91lqy, CLAIM-tmpkx7ct**: the second-system safeguard is a step whose passage depends on a check another party runs and that delays only when it flags: the term's "friction without delay". The paper does not use the word friction.
- **CLAIM-523**: "authority and capability to actually change previous decisions" is volitional efficacy and correctability.
- **QUESTION-tmp9n1ac**: a second system that every decision passes through also yields "a record of all decisions made"; the framework reads onto the record's own checks.
- **As a stand-in for Harid (LIT-tmp7ftjn)**: it carries the concept of contestability before a decision takes effect. It does not carry Harid's deficit construct, the register shift, or the separation of contestability from friction.

## Limitations

A framework from about twenty papers, not validated; the ex-ante practice is a lifecycle stage, not a pause before each decision; nothing on delay, cost, or who bears them.
