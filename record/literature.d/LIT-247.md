# LIT-247: On Mutual Information Maximization for Representation Learning

Michael Tschannen, Josip Djolonga, Paul K. Rubenstein, Sylvain Gelly, Mario Lucic (2019), *International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2020)* — [ARXIV-1907.13625](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.13625)

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-tmpqr9lw](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpqr9lw.md).*

- **Setup.** Representations of the top half of MNIST digits, learned by
  maximizing I_NCE or I_NWJ against the bottom half, scored by a linear
  probe (pixel baseline about 85%, supervised about 94%); batch 128, so
  I_NCE ≤ log 128 ≈ 4.85. §§3.2–3.3 are repeated on CIFAR10 with the same
  conclusions (App. G).
- **True MI constant, accuracy rising.** With invertible RealNVP encoders,
  true MI is the same for every parameter value, yet the estimate and the
  probe accuracy both rise in training (Fig. 1a–b). An encoder of the same
  architecture, trained adversarially, stays MI-maximal and ends worse than
  raw pixels (Fig. 1c).
- **Estimators prefer ill-conditioned encoders.** From an identity
  initialization, the Jacobian's condition number grows as the estimate
  rises (Fig. 2).
- **Tighter is not better, shown for NWJ.** An MLP critic reaches a higher
  I_NWJ and worse features than bilinear or separable critics. For I_NCE all
  critics sit near log K, and only accuracy differs (§3.2, Fig. 3).
- **Encoder over estimator.** At matched bound values (t = 2, 4), ConvNet
  encoders beat MLP encoders for both estimators (§3.3, Fig. 4a–b).
- **Non-i.i.d. batches break the bound.** If batch pairs are identically
  distributed but dependent, I_NCE is "neither a lower nor an upper bound
  on the true MI" (App. D); in a Gaussian toy both I_NCE and I_NWJ exceed it
  (Fig. 4c). CPC with same-image negatives is cited as such a case.
- **InfoNCE as metric learning.** I_NCE = log K − E[(1/K)Σ_i log(1 +
  Σ_{j≠i} e^{f(x_i,y_j) − f(x_i,y_i)})]; with f = φ(x)ᵀφ(y) this is Sohn's
  multi-class K-pair loss (§4, App. C). The authors propose this view as the
  better explanation, by argument.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-09-26 while pursuing, at the owner's request, the connection
*Radon–Nikodym, density ratios and contrastive objectives* (see the curation
entry of that date), and read in full on 2026-10-14 in [NOTE-tmpqr9lw](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpqr9lw.md). It is
held as the record's evidence that the optimal-critic and MI-bound reading
of contrastive objectives does not explain which encoder features are good,
and as the statement of where the InfoNCE bound's i.i.d. assumption fails.

It is a supporting source of [THEORY-006](../theory.d/THEORY-006.md) and is named in [THEORY-001](../theory.d/THEORY-001.md)'s limits.
The reading finds that [THEORY-006](../theory.d/THEORY-006.md) inverts the first experiment: it is the
true MI that stays constant while the estimate rises, not the estimate. It
also finds that [THEORY-006](../theory.d/THEORY-006.md)'s tighter-bound claim holds as shown for NWJ,
not InfoNCE. Its i.i.d. analysis builds on Poole et al. ([LIT-246](LIT-246.md)), whose
proof it restates.
