# LIT-tmpazv5c: Psychological need frustration as a transdiagnostic process in associations of self-critical perfectionism with depressive symptoms and eating pathology

Campbell, R., Boone, L., Vansteenkiste, M., Soenens, B. (2018). Psychological need frustration as a transdiagnostic process in associations of self-critical perfectionism with depressive symptoms and eating pathology. *Journal of Clinical Psychology, 74(10), 1775-1790*. doi:10.1002/jclp.22628.

## Key takeaways

Read in full on 2026-11-03 ([NOTE-tmpxovm7](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpxovm7.md)). THEORY: [THEORY-tmpeqxmj](../theory.d/THEORY-tmpeqxmj.md)
(Active).

- Need frustration absorbs most of the depression–eating association,
  cross-sectionally (.50 to .18, ns) and in correlated change (.43 to .24).
- The "longitudinal" evidence is two waves of same-interval change: no
  temporal order, so it does not meet [THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md)'s promote_when.
- The measure is the BPNSFS frustration scale, which [LIT-tmpjcuzb](LIT-tmpjcuzb.md) argues is
  keyed low satisfaction.

## Standing in the record

The one longitudinal study [THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md) cites, through [LIT-561](LIT-561.md) p. 12, for need frustration as a transdiagnostic process. Now identified.
