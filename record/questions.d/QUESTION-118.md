# QUESTION-118: When a collective reproduces its organization through the replacement of all its members, what must stay causally continuous for the later organization to be the earlier one persisting, and not a similar one produced anew, and when several later collectives each continue it in a different respect, which successor relation, if any, is its persistence?

## Why it is a question

A5 §7: "**When a collective reproduces its organization through
replacement of constituents, what must remain causally continuous for the
later organization to count as persistence of the earlier collective
rather than production of a similar new one?**" A5 noted that it "does not
presume that a conserved scalar γ exists. It does not equate reproduction
with identity. And it is compatible with competing answers from
organizational closure, causal continuity, structural stability, and
participant recognition."

It sits between [QUESTION-035](QUESTION-035.md)'s "how long does it last" and
[QUESTION-043](QUESTION-043.md)'s coherence measure for a person whose life story changes.
A5: "Those two questions nearly meet at the fandom example."

The branching part comes from the Washington Senators ([CASE-105](../cases.d/CASE-105.md)):
franchise, city and supporters each have a different successor. A2: "This
suggests that fandom identity is not always an equivalence relation with
sharp boundaries." A4's weaker and safer form: "Multiple successors do not
imply that numerical identity itself branches. The weaker and more
defensible claim concerns plural successor relations." [CLAIM-002](../claims.d/CLAIM-002.md) holds
the textual analogue, identity across retellings as a family of relations
(artifact, transmission, function).

## A candidate answer for the branching part

[CLAIM-328](../claims.d/CLAIM-328.md), A4's weak form of plural succession, promoted at the
owner's word: when the respects are separated, several later collectives
can each be a successor in a different respect without identity
branching.

## What would count as an answer

A criterion that distinguishes, in stated cases, numerical continuation,
branching descent and independent reconstitution (A5 §6), and that is not
satisfied by every causal descendant. It should return verdicts on the
three Senators lineages and on the 2005 Nationals, whose claim on old
Washington baseball is narrative rather than organizational. Candidate
materials named in the exchange: organizational routines (Feldman and
Pentland, [LIT-1124](../literature.d/LIT-1124.md)), imprinting (Marquis and Tilcsik, [LIT-1144](../literature.d/LIT-1144.md)),
identity trajectories (Cloutier and Ravasi, [LIT-1141](../literature.d/LIT-1141.md)), all unread; and the record's own closure reading ([THEORY-221](../theory.d/THEORY-221.md)),
with A5's caution that "recruiting and cultural transmission may
regenerate characteristics while depending heavily on external causes".

## Note of 2026-10-23: the classic transmission study, not reached

Cited at A5 of the exchange of 2026-10-23, "Framework Overview Request": Jacobs and Campbell (1961, [LIT-1423](../literature.d/LIT-1423.md)) seeded
an exaggerated norm in Sherif's autokinetic task with confederates and
replaced members one at a time; as standardly reported, the norm outlasted
the confederates for several generations and decayed. A5: it "already
establishes a crucial possibility: **the persistence of a group's
characteristic behavior does not require persistence of its original
members**." No lawful copy was reached, so no THEORY is filed. A9 rightly
left this question unsplit: transmission of a norm is not what this
question asks, which is what must stay continuous for the later
organization to be the earlier one. The study's control groups, which
reach the unseeded level, are a natural comparison for [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md)'s "an
organization of the same kind that arises independently". [CASE-148](../cases.d/CASE-148.md)
proposes the transfer test.

## Note of 2026-10-27: continuation relations and the record's structural realism

A3 of the exchange of 2026-10-26, on the Chagossians, resisted a single
coherence measure: "The more appropriate initial object may be a
**structured family of continuation relations**." The owner, at U4: "this
sounds like a potential opportunity to connect to our broadly structural
realist account." A4 took it up.

- **A typed, directed family.** For an earlier realization A and a later B,
  R(A, B) = {genealogical, cultural, organizational, institutional,
  capability, ecological, …}, each with conditions of its own. A4: "the
  family itself is not yet a definition of identity. It is a way of
  describing **the respects in which one realization continues another**.
  Numerical identity, when relevant, remains a further question." That is
  [CLAIM-328](../claims.d/CLAIM-328.md)'s plural successors and [CLAIM-002](../claims.d/CLAIM-002.md)'s family of relations for
  texts, which A4 cited, with direction added: one predecessor can have a
  scientific successor (its methods and people) and an institutional one
  (its charter and authority), as USIP may ([CASE-176](../cases.d/CASE-176.md)).
- **Against isomorphism, both ways.** A4: A ≅ B "is too restrictive in one
  respect and too permissive in another": continuation can involve deep
  change, and two independently formed organizations can be isomorphic.
  That is [CLAIM-329](../claims.d/CLAIM-329.md) against resemblance and [CLAIM-210](../claims.d/CLAIM-210.md)'s kind-sharing apart
  from rendering.
- **When a category is earned.** A4: "A simple directed graph of
  relationships would suffice initially. A category becomes warranted only
  after identifying composition laws and identity morphisms that genuinely
  correspond to the phenomenon"; a chain of training "may preserve
  provenance even as capability or fidelity degrades", so composition need
  not preserve what matters.
- **The Newman constraint** ([CLAIM-138](../claims.d/CLAIM-138.md)). A4: "If we allow arbitrary
  relations to count as structural continuities, practically anything can
  be made to resemble or continue anything else." The relations need
  independent grounds, training histories, transferred techniques, assessed
  competences, transmitted instruments and data, and "The structural
  account's strength comes from specifying relations that could fail to
  hold." A4 left [CLAIM-136](../claims.d/CLAIM-136.md)'s question, whether relata have individuality
  beyond their relations, unsettled, and so does this note.
- **Responses under probes.** A4 and A8 proposed identifying a continuation
  partly by preserved responses to a declared family of interventions,
  with a correspondence τ between probes, citing [TERM-069](../terms.d/TERM-069.md)'s limit that
  observational equivalence is not identity, and the record's readings of
  causal abstraction ([LIT-1334](../literature.d/LIT-1334.md), [LIT-1310](../literature.d/LIT-1310.md)).

A4's reframing is the part the question lacks: "Under what conditions
should we recognize the loss of an organization or organizational
capability, even when its apparent constituents and aggregate outputs
remain?", which "may actually be more empirically tractable", since
"Destructive interventions can reveal causal dependencies that normal
functioning conceals." The cases are [CASE-169](../cases.d/CASE-169.md), [CASE-172](../cases.d/CASE-172.md) and
[CASE-176](../cases.d/CASE-176.md).

## Note of 2026-10-27: the possibility of plural succession split off

The status review of this date split this question under [ADR-049](../decisions.d/ADR-049.md). Whether
several later collectives can each be a genuine successor in a different
respect without numerical identity branching is [QUESTION-290](QUESTION-290.md),
Answered by [CLAIM-328](../claims.d/CLAIM-328.md) on the Senators ([CASE-105](../cases.d/CASE-105.md)). This question keeps
what is still open: what must stay causally continuous for persistence,
and, when the respects separate, which successor relation, if any, is the
earlier collective persisting.

## Note of 2026-10-29: routines and imprinting, read

Of the three candidate materials "What would count as an answer" lists as
unread, two are now read and the third is Unavailable, so "all unread" no
longer holds.

- **Feldman and Pentland**, [LIT-1124](../literature.d/LIT-1124.md) ([NOTE-1386](../notes.d/NOTE-1386.md)), offer one candidate
  for what must stay causally continuous: the ostensive aspect of a
  routine, as maintained by ongoing performance and by those with power to
  say which performances count. They do not discuss total turnover of
  performers, so they do not test whether a routine survives when every
  participant changes.
- **Marquis and Tilcsik**, [LIT-1144](../literature.d/LIT-1144.md) ([NOTE-1373](../notes.d/NOTE-1373.md)), offer layered
  imprints: a later sensitive period adds strata without removing earlier
  ones, so what stays continuous through change would be the older
  strata. The review does not test it, and every case it discusses fixes
  the bearer's identity by its legal or institutional existence.
- **Cloutier and Ravasi**, [LIT-1141](../literature.d/LIT-1141.md), is Unavailable.

Neither reading separates continuation from independent reconstitution.
The question is unchanged and stays Open.

## Note of 2026-11-01: Griesemer and Shavit on community reproduction, re-read
<!-- inactive-ok-block: LIT-188 LIT-1932 THEORY-418 — Deferred, unread; Proposed; Proposed: read in full; cited from the re-reading of LIT-188, not leaned on -->

[LIT-188](../literature.d/LIT-188.md) ([NOTE-173](../notes.d/NOTE-173.md), re-read in full on 2026-11-01) bears on this question
from the side of reproduction, not persistence, and [THEORY-418](../theory.d/THEORY-418.md) already
says so. Three points the earlier connection did not record:

- **A criterion that not every causal descendant meets.** On Griesemer's
  account of inheritance, which the paper adopts (§3; the source is
  registered unread as [LIT-1932](../literature.d/LIT-1932.md)), offspring develop from a "sample"
  of the parent's material parts, and "sampling propagates more than mere
  resemblance in virtue of the offspring propagule having originated from
  parts of the parents". A group founded by migrants drawn from the parent
  community meets it. A similar group assembled independently does not.
  That separates descent from independent reconstitution, one of the
  three verdicts "What would count as an answer" asks for. It does not
  separate numerical continuation from descent: the paper treats the
  founding of a new group as reproduction throughout.
- **What the sample must carry.** Not members but "the heterogeneous
  structure and dynamics of their interactions", because "no one
  individual migrant can carry even a symbolic representation of the
  whole community structure in their head" (§3). The offspring must
  "repeat the assembly" (after Caporael) of the organization by
  development. So the paper's answer to "what must stay causally
  continuous" is developmental capacity carried by an organized sample.
  It is stated, and the Neolithic case that illustrates it is
  conditional.
- **The converse case.** The paper also describes a group changing while
  its members stay: "reference groups themselves can move their trait
  boundaries", so that "a person can no longer 'recognize' their party"
  while remaining a member (§2). Members can also migrate among groups
  within a group generation. Neither is analysed for persistence.

No status, title or warrant changes.
