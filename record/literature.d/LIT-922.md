# LIT-922: The Organization of Behavior: A Neuropsychological Theory

D. O. Hebb (1949), *The Organization of Behavior: A Neuropsychological Theory*, Wiley, New York

## Key takeaways

*Filed, not read.* Known here by what it is standardly cited for.

- The neurophysiological postulate: when an axon of cell A repeatedly or
  persistently takes part in firing cell B, some growth or metabolic
  change increases A's efficiency in firing B.
- Repeated co-activity builds cell assemblies, groups of cells that can
  reverberate and so outlast the stimulus that formed them, and assemblies
  link into phase sequences.
- It is standardly cited as the origin of "cells that fire together wire
  together", a slogan that is not Hebb's wording.

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-11 at the owner's request, for the nucleating-argument
line. Read across, Hebb's postulate is a persistence trigger keyed to
recurrence ([CLAIM-185](../claims.d/CLAIM-185.md)): repeated co-activity is what makes a
connection last. It differs from the record's rule in being graded, not a
count, and in strengthening with use, which bears on what turns
persistence into anchoring ([QUESTION-038](../questions.d/QUESTION-038.md)). A reading should check how
Hebb states the postulate and what he says makes an assembly persist.
