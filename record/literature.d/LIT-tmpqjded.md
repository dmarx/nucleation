# LIT-tmpqjded: Emotion and Adaptation

Richard S. Lazarus, *Emotion and Adaptation*, New York: Oxford University
Press, 1991 (ISBN 978-0-19-506994-5; later printings 978-0-19-509266-0).
Crossref dates the Oxford Academic edition to 29 August 1991, which is
`published:` here. Open Library gives a median of 567 pages.

## Key takeaways

*Registered from Crossref's chapter records and from read works that cite it.
Nothing here is a reading.*

- **Chapters** (Crossref): 1 About Emotion; 2 Issues of Research,
  Classification, and Measurement; 3 The Person-Environment Relationship,
  Motivation, and Coping; 4 Cognition and Emotion; 5 Issues of Causality;
  6 Goal Incongruent (Negative) Emotions; 7 Goal Congruent (Positive) and
  Problematic Emotions; 8 Individual Development; 9 Social Influence;
  10 Emotions and Health; 11 Implications for Research, Assessment,
  Treatment, and Prevention.
- **As cited by Gross ([LIT-tmptr6ik](LIT-tmptr6ik.md))**, as "Lazarus, 1991a": the "adaptational
  encounter" as the unit of an emotion episode, emotions as having specific
  objects, and a source of the consensual "evaluation → response tendencies"
  model of emotion generation (p. 273 and footnote 1).
- **As cited by Barrett ([LIT-tmp7xlrj](LIT-tmp7xlrj.md))**: the first example of a "classical"
  causal appraisal theory, one that assumes "a response derives from a stimulus
  that is evaluated for its meaning". She calls such theories "highly
  doubtful".

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-03 at the owner's request. It is the work both read papers in
this batch cite for appraisal theory: Barrett as the theory she rejects, Gross
as part of the consensus he builds on. Without it, the record would hold the
rivalry with only one side named. It is `Deferred` because neither the book nor
a shorter statement by Lazarus could be read lawfully (see the status note).
Frijda's action-readiness theory ([LIT-tmp57opn](LIT-tmp57opn.md), read) is the appraisal account
the record does hold in its author's words. Both read papers class Lazarus's
theory with Frijda's as appraisal theory: an evaluation of the event's meaning
for the person elicits the emotion. What Lazarus takes an
emotion to *be*, and so where he and Frijda part, is exactly what an unread
entry cannot state; a reading of the 1991 paper would settle it.

The tag `psychopathology-and-treatment` rests on chapters 10 and 11, on health
and on treatment and prevention.

**Priority for a reading: high** for the appraisal side of the emotion-theory
rivalry. Start with the 1991 American Psychologist paper.
