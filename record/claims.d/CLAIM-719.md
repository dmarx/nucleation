# CLAIM-719: An institution can be reliably responsive to reasons within its governing mechanism and unresponsive to justified reasons for revising that mechanism, and because its own actions change the mechanism, responsiveness at the first order is no evidence of responsiveness at the second; identical decisions can come from different capacities to be governed by reasons

## The claim

A108 §6 of the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b) separates
"**Epistemic responsiveness:** The institution can detect normatively
significant differences", "**Practical responsiveness:** Those differences
can appropriately affect its decisions", and "**Reflexive
responsiveness:** Reasons concerning defects in the decision-making process
can appropriately affect that process itself", and says:

> A system can be responsive to reasons within a fixed framework while being entirely unresponsive to justified reasons for changing the framework.

"In our earlier vocabulary, it can be highly *correctable* at the first
order but inadequately governable at the second."

A110 §9–10 gives it a model. The institution's response runs through a
pipeline s →Λ_G→ q →Γ_G→ j →Θ_G→ u →F_G→ s′ (representation, evaluation,
allocation of authority, execution), and "An organization may fail at any
link." Response adequacy in a state is 𝟙{π_G(Λ_G(s)) ∈ R_N(s)}. One correct
decision is uninformative ("A mechanism that always approves applications
may occasionally produce the correct answer"), so A takes the worst case
over a declared family 𝒮_r of cases that vary the relevant circumstances:
ρ_N(G; 𝒮_r) = inf_{s ∈ 𝒮_r} Pr_G(a ∈ R_N(s) | s). Then the mechanism
itself moves: M_t = (Λ_t, Γ_t, Θ_t, C_t, K_t) and M_{t+1} = Ψ(M_t, u_t, ξ_t).

> **An organization can influence its future ability to recognize and act upon reasons that constrain it.**

So there are two responsivenesses, ρ^(1) "within a fixed governing
mechanism" and ρ^(2), "responsiveness to justified reasons for revising
the governing mechanism itself", and "An institution can score highly on
the first while being deficient on the second", for example by following
an eligibility rule consistently while refusing "to consider evidence that
the rule conflicts with a superior legal obligation". That is "**resistance
to justified revision of the criteria governing first-order decisions**".

**What is defined and what is not.** ρ_N is defined, and its worst-case
form is the right answer to the always-approve mechanism. ρ^(2) is named,
not defined: A gives no case family or response set for revisions of M.
The claim rests on the separation, not on a measure of the second.

## Identical decisions, different capacities (A106 §18, folded in)

A106 §18 makes the same point about one decision record. An organization
that "consistently makes the correct decision" may do so from commitment,
by accident, because incentives happen to align, or because it is
"configured so rigidly that it produces the correct outcome under present
conditions but cannot respond when the relevant circumstances change". "The
observed decisions may be identical. The organization's normative
capacities are not." And: "its willingness to correct some errors does not
establish that it can recognize errors arising from inadequacies in its own
categories."

## How it sits with the record

- **[CLAIM-462](CLAIM-462.md)** (Active): an arrangement can keep or increase its capacity
  for effective action while its capacity to be corrected by others
  degrades, because different relations maintain them. This claim has the
  same form and a different pair: correction within the mechanism, and
  revision of the mechanism, whether by others or by the institution
  itself.
- **[CLAIM-685](CLAIM-685.md)** (Proposed) predicts that institutions able to revise their
  own standards avoid persistent failures better than those that only
  monitor outcomes against fixed standards. That is an empirical
  comparison that presupposes this separation.
- **[CLAIM-523](CLAIM-523.md)** (Active): legibility, volitional efficacy and correctability
  are distinct achievements. The pipeline's Λ, Γ and Θ, F are close to
  them; reflexive responsiveness is correctability applied to the
  mechanism.
- **[CLAIM-535](CLAIM-535.md)** (Active) is the Λ link: a policy acting on an observation
  that merges circumstances with no common acceptable action cannot be
  adequate in all of them.
- **[CASE-226](../cases.d/CASE-226.md)** (Proposed) is a proposed study of the second order: two
  communities shown that a category conflates two phenomena, one revising
  it and one reinterpreting the evidence. A110 §11's Case C, identical
  compliance with and without a rule-revision detector, is close to it and
  to [CASE-229](../cases.d/CASE-229.md).
- The second-order capacity named here is what [TERM-128](../terms.d/TERM-128.md) calls
  normative governability.

## What it does not say

- Not that responsiveness means giving way. A108 §6: "An institution is not
  normatively governable because it obeys challengers." Objections can be
  mistaken or outweighed, and "legitimate finality prevents reopening a
  settled matter."
- Not that the lack of second-order responsiveness is a wrong. A110 §11:
  "A finding that the second institution lacks the relevant capability is
  descriptive. A finding that it violated a duty by eliminating that
  capability requires an additional normative premise." That premise is
  [CLAIM-624](CLAIM-624.md)'s duty.

## Note of 2026-11-18: postmortem action items as a measure of second-order responsiveness
<!-- inactive-ok-file: THEORY-2000 THEORY-1998 CASE-293 — new; the readings and study filed on 2026-11-18 -->

The lead filer's, from the case of blameless culture ([QUESTION-476](../questions.d/QUESTION-476.md)), accepted for filing by the owner on 2026-11-18. **A measurement proposal, not a new claim.**

A postmortem's action items are an organization's own statement of what it will change after a failure. Some fix the instance: a bug, a configuration, a missing alert. Others revise the mechanism that produced the decision: review or launch criteria, staffing, who may stop a release, what counts as an incident. The first are responsiveness within the mechanism; the second are this claim's responsiveness to reasons for revising it. **Proposed measure:** the share of mechanism-revising action items that are completed, counted separately from instance fixes. An organization that holds postmortems and closes its instance fixes but not its mechanism-revising items is responsive at the first order only, which is this claim's separation in a form that can be counted.

**What the readings say.** No reading on the record reports completion rates, or says that completion is a known weak spot of postmortem practice. That it is one is the lead's understanding, not a finding here. The SRE chapter ([THEORY-2000](../theory.d/THEORY-2000.md)) asks reviewers "Is the action plan appropriate and are resulting bug fixes at appropriate priority?" and says "An unreviewed postmortem might as well never have existed", but says nothing of completion. It does describe one second-order practice: "We regularly survey our teams on how the postmortem process is supporting their goals and how the process might be improved", a revision of the review mechanism itself.

**Two qualifications.**

- **Completion is not adequacy.** Cook ([THEORY-1998](../theory.d/THEORY-1998.md), point 15): post-accident remedies are usually "end-of-the-chain measures" that "do little" and "usually increase the coupling and complexity of the system." Closing many such items is no evidence of governability.
- **Declining is not failing.** This claim's "What it does not say": responsiveness is not giving way. An item closed as not to be done, with its reason recorded, is a response; an item left open with no decision is not.

[CASE-293](../cases.d/CASE-293.md), the proposed study of postmortems, would collect the same data. Relation: none.

No status changes.
