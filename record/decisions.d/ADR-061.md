# ADR-061: An ARG is any claim about support; a claim about what a source says is a THEORY

## Context

ADR-032 built ARG for two jobs: a second, independent line of support for
a claim, and an objection to an inference rather than to its conclusion,
which the will exchange showed was the most common real objection. To
keep capture from flooding, it filed an ARG "only when something must
point at the inference".

The record holds ten ARGs and nine `undercuts`. All of them are dated
2026-10-04 to 2026-10-10, the two reconstructions that demonstrated the
schemes. Since then about 280 claims have been filed, and no ARG. The
objections did not stop: 30 `counter` claims are dated on or before
2026-10-10 and 31 after it. What stopped was filing them against an
inference. CLAIM-163 says in its own summary that it is "the kind ADR-032
found most often real: same structure, not the same mechanism", and it is
filed as `objects_to` against CLAIM-162. CLAIM-254 says it "does not say
relational identity is false, only that Saussure, read correctly, does not
support its transport", which is an undercut by definition, and it is
filed as `objects_to` too.

The cause is the filing rule. The person who needs to point at the
inference is the objector, and at that moment there is no ARG to point
at. Undercutting means first writing out someone else's inference as an
ARG, then the objection. `objects_to` on the conclusion is one file and
passes the lint. Filed that way, an objection that succeeds reads as a
defeat of the conclusion, when what it shows is that one route to the
conclusion fails.

**The classification.** On 2026-10-26 three readers classified all 453
claims then filed (485 by 2026-10-28; the 32 since are unclassified), one third each, against one rubric. A claim was SUPPORT when its
subject is whether something supports, establishes or shows something
else in the record. It was SOURCE when its subject is what a named work
says, shows or proves, settled by reading the text. It was MIXED when it
bundled either with a claim about the subject matter. Readers marked
their own confidence. The calls were spot-checked, but not every one was
read twice, and the readers differed in strictness (the third marked no
SUPPORT as high), so the low-confidence counts are soft.

| Kind | High | Low | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| SUPPORT | 20 | 22 | 42 |
| SOURCE | 5 | 16 | 21 |
| MIXED | 4 | 13 | 17 |
| Neither | | | 373 |

SUPPORT falls into four groups:

- **Verdicts on an ARG already filed.** CLAIM-011, CLAIM-032, CLAIM-041,
  CLAIM-085, and the support halves of CLAIM-035 and CLAIM-253. These are
  the `undercuts` the demonstrations filed.
- **What a test or a body of evidence could show.** CLAIM-190, CLAIM-203,
  CLAIM-205, CLAIM-206, CLAIM-208, CLAIM-222, CLAIM-226, CLAIM-227,
  CLAIM-232, CLAIM-233, CLAIM-244, CLAIM-248, CLAIM-334, CLAIM-364,
  CLAIM-427 and CLAIM-448. Most come from the audit of the translation
  line on 2026-10-10. Each says that a design, a measure or a result
  would not bear on a thesis.
- **What an argument in the record does or does not establish.**
  CLAIM-022, CLAIM-053, CLAIM-071, CLAIM-075, CLAIM-093, CLAIM-116,
  CLAIM-138, CLAIM-145, CLAIM-150, CLAIM-163, CLAIM-168, CLAIM-200,
  CLAIM-229, CLAIM-247, CLAIM-252, CLAIM-254, CLAIM-278 and CLAIM-288.
- **Whether a defeat condition can be met.** CLAIM-216, CLAIM-238,
  CLAIM-239 and CLAIM-243. These are about a thesis's `defeated_if`, not
  about support, and this decision leaves them as claims.

SOURCE held a surprise. Of the five high-confidence cases, four ground a
THEORY that already reads the same source: CLAIM-066 and THEORY-155 on
Kirby, Cornish and Smith; CLAIM-069 and THEORY-168; CLAIM-072 and
THEORY-159 on Saussure; and CLAIM-169 and THEORY-067 on Ainslie. Three of
the four were `granted` objections when classified; CLAIM-069 has since
been ruled Proposed. CLAIM-072 is not a reading the record
lacks. It says that CLAIM-082 leans on Saussure for something THEORY-159's
reading does not say. That is a verdict on support. Only CLAIM-097, on
T2I-CompBench, reads a source with no THEORY behind it. Most of the
low-confidence SOURCE cases are prior-art reports (CLAIM-001, CLAIM-019,
CLAIM-024, CLAIM-088, CLAIM-146) or restate textbook results the record
applies (CLAIM-094, CLAIM-131, CLAIM-132, CLAIM-148, CLAIM-302).
CLAIM-028 reads the exchange's own drafts, not literature, and stays a
claim.

MIXED: CLAIM-035, CLAIM-140, CLAIM-234 and CLAIM-412 with high
confidence; CLAIM-130, CLAIM-134, CLAIM-211, CLAIM-219, CLAIM-225,
CLAIM-235, CLAIM-253, CLAIM-309, CLAIM-343, CLAIM-393, CLAIM-411,
CLAIM-432 and CLAIM-433 with low. The remaining SOURCE cases at low
confidence are CLAIM-013, CLAIM-018, CLAIM-159, CLAIM-325 and CLAIM-360.

## Decision

**The boundary between CLAIM and ARG is drawn by subject.**

- **CLAIM** holds what the record says about its subject matter.
- **ARG** holds what the record says about support between its entries:
  that these premises do, or do not, establish this conclusion. Its
  `status` is the verdict, which is what ADR-032 already made it ("whether
  the INFERENCE holds, which is not whether its conclusion is true"), and
  what ADR-059 since stated exactly: an ARG's status is credence in its
  inference, within the limits its granted undercutters set.

So a negative verdict is an ARG too. "X does not show Y" is filed as the
ARG whose premises are X and whose conclusion is Y. It is Rejected when
the record holds that the support fails, and Proposed while that is open.
Its body answers the form's critical questions, and the question that
fails is the objection. CLAIM-163 becomes the ARG from CLAIM-169 and
THEORY-070 to CLAIM-162, form `analogy`, Proposed. Its body records that
the critical question "is the mechanism the same?" is answered no. That
takes one file, and no one has to write out the inference first, because
stating the inference is the objection.

The rest follows from that:

- **The filing rule changes.** An ARG is filed when the record says
  something about a support relation: it argues one, doubts one, concedes
  one fails, or needs one as a second line. A claim's own `rests_on` still
  needs no ARG (ADR-032's throttle stands). The new rule moves the
  decision to where the objection is written, which is where it was being
  lost.
- **`undercuts` narrows.** It is kept for a claim about the subject matter
  that defeats an inference: the non-invertibility of translation
  operations in CLAIM-035, which defeats the relativity analogy of ARG-004.
  A claim whose content is only "that does not follow" is not an
  undercutting claim. It is the ARG's status and body.
- **One ARG per inference.** Doubts about one inference, and replies to
  them, are revisions of its ARG, recorded by version and `history:`. They
  are not a chain of claims about it. A doubt that needs a code of its own
  is a claim about the subject matter, and it `undercuts`.
- **A test design is a premise.** "A win on this test would not support
  the thesis" is the ARG from the CASE that describes the design to the
  thesis, Rejected. CLAIM-203 already grounds CASE-040, so its premise
  exists.
- **Either-or support is unchanged.** Two ARGs that conclude one claim are
  two independent lines (ARG-005 and ARG-009 for CLAIM-027).

**A claim whose subject is what a source says is a THEORY**, since THEORY
is the record's reading of the literature. Where the record already holds
a THEORY reading that source, any new content is a revision of that
THEORY. Where the claim objects that another claim misuses the reading,
the objection is the ARG from the THEORY to that claim, Rejected.
CLAIM-072 becomes a revision to THEORY-159, if it adds anything to the
reading, and the ARG from THEORY-159 to CLAIM-082, Rejected. A claim
that reads a source and finds the target false stays an objecting claim,
grounded in the THEORY. Prior-art reports and restated textbook results
stay claims. They are the record's own use of known results, and a THEORY
for the sheaf gluing property would be a reading nobody did.

This is a rule about a claim whose subject is the reading. It leaves
ADR-058 as it is: a reading that is evidence for a claim the claim does
not stand on is `corroborated_by`, not `grounds`, and that edge is
between a claim about the subject matter and a THEORY, which this
decision does not move.

**Two argument forms are added**, because the support claims need forms
the closed list lacks:

- **`evidence`**: an observation or result supports a hypothesis.
  Critical questions: would the evidence be expected if the hypothesis
  were false? Does it tell the hypothesis apart from its rivals? Does the
  measure measure what it is said to?
- **`source`**: a reading of a work grounds a claim. Critical questions:
  does the work say this, read in context? Is it said within the scope the
  claim needs? Does the claim lean on the reading for more than the reading
  holds?

**A MIXED claim is split.** The part about the subject matter stays a
claim. The part about support becomes an ARG, which the claim `undercuts`
when that part defeats it.

## Alternatives considered

- **Fold ARG into CLAIM** (the owner's first framing). An inference is a
  claim, and a bridge claim ("the analogy carries the mechanism") gives an
  undercut a target. But `rests_on` is one list of premises that are all
  needed, so it cannot say that a claim rests on either A and B or D and E
  without a node per route, and a CLAIM in `rests_on` reads as one more
  premise. Its `status` would also mean something different from its
  neighbours', which is ADR-032's own test for a separate scheme.
- **A voice field on ARG** (`role`, as on CLAIM). The status already says
  what the record holds about the one proposition an ARG states.
  `disclaimed`, an inference nobody draws, is the case that tempts it.
  ADR-032 found most "X does not establish Y" sentences were of this kind.
  Such an inference is filed only when the second-mention rule says so,
  and its body says nobody draws it. Add the field when a check needs
  it.
- **More CLAIM schemes, split by `role` or by `warrant`.** ADR-032 already
  rejected a split by role: a counter's status means what a thesis's does.
  Of the 88 `demonstrated` theses, 83 are Active, and their `defeated_if`
  amounts to "a counterexample or an error in the proof", so credence
  barely applies to them. But they have the same relations and required
  fields as other claims, and ADR-043 made warrant a field on purpose. A
  field still suffices.
- **Keep the boundary, and add a prompt to the counter template.** That
  treats the filing cost as a habit. The cost is structural: an undercut
  needs two files where a rebuttal needs one. The prompt is still worth
  adding under this decision, as the question that sends an objection to
  the right scheme.
- **Status quo.** About 60 claims would keep saying something about
  support in a scheme whose status and relations are about the subject
  matter, and an objection that succeeds would keep reading as a defeat
  of its target.

## Consequences

- **A Rejected ARG draws no step in the argument chain** (the
  `argued_from` pattern already excludes it), so a negative verdict
  never lengthens a line. It appears under its conclusion as `argued_by`
  and on the ARG index.
- **Undercuts join the dialectic page, through a pattern.** The page
  walked only `objects_to`, so a claim that undercuts an ARG was in no
  thread: CLAIM-032, CLAIM-035, CLAIM-085, CLAIM-141 and CLAIM-144 were
  on no line of it. The `undercuts_support_of` pattern in `luria.yaml`,
  `(u:CLAIM)-[:undercuts]->(g:ARG)-[:concludes]->(c:CLAIM)`, puts the
  undercutting claim in the thread of the claim the argument concludes,
  and names the argument it went through. It keeps Rejected arguments,
  since an undercut that succeeded is the history the thread is for. It
  was fired on 2026-10-27 by rendering the page: 46 threads became 48,
  and all nine undercuts appear under their conclusions, "through" their
  ARGs. This part does not wait on acceptance, and is in this
  contribution.
- **A bare verdict gets a page through luria's pattern pages, not a
  multi-scheme chain.** An ARG with no undercutting claim is on no
  dialectic thread, and that is most of what the migration files. The
  draft of 2026-10-26 waited for chains to list several schemes
  (dmarx/luria#380). Since then luria 1.5.0 renders a pattern page as an
  outline, nested like a chain, and lets patterns that share an output
  merge (dmarx/luria#383). A trial on this record on 2026-10-28, with a
  bare pattern `(g:ARG)-[:concludes]->(c:CLAIM)` and the undercut pattern
  sharing one page, listed all ten ARGs under their nine conclusions with
  each undercut nested beneath its argument. Adopting it is the pin bump
  from 1.3.0, in its own contribution, and #380 is not needed for it.
  Rejected on the way: a pattern nesting a premise under the conclusion
  through a non-Active ARG, which would show the premise's voice and
  credence as the objection's, and a separate view of contested
  arguments, which would split the thread across two pages.
- **ADR-032 takes a history note.** Its ARG paragraph and the filing rule
  are changed by this decision. So is its consequence "Lines of argument
  stop at an ARG", which the `argued_from` pattern in `luria.yaml` stopped
  being true of, though no decision recorded it.
- **The schemes need small changes.** The claim template's counter
  section asks the routing question: does this say the conclusion is
  false, or that this does not show it? The ARG template drops "only when
  something needs to point at an inference". The `undercuts` blurb narrows,
  and the arg-forms vocabulary gains its two forms. A CLAIM's
  `superseded_by` widens to ARG and THEORY, so a moved claim can be
  retired by status.

## Migration

To be done once this decision is accepted, in its own contribution, and
by reading each entry. The counts above come from a classifier, and the
low-confidence calls are expected to shrink when read closely.

- Each SUPPORT claim becomes an ARG, and the claim is retired by status,
  Superseded, never deleted. A CLAIM's `superseded_by` names only claims
  today, so it widens to name an ARG or a THEORY. Whether luria writes
  the converse across schemes is checked, and fired once, before the
  first move.
- **A SUPPORT claim that an Active claim rests on is split, not
  superseded.** `rests_on` names only claims, and `inverted_premise`
  (an Active claim resting on one not held) fails the lint since
  ADR-057's forcing functions. So a dependent cannot be repointed to the
  ARG, and superseding its premise would break it. The dependence is the
  sign that the claim also says something about the subject matter: that
  part stays a claim, revised, and the support verdict becomes the ARG,
  as for MIXED. On 2026-10-28 this covers CLAIM-011 (rested on by
  CLAIM-249 and CLAIM-381), CLAIM-085 (CLAIM-243, CLAIM-248, CLAIM-252),
  CLAIM-053 (CLAIM-229) and CLAIM-233 (CLAIM-232).
- Each SOURCE claim is read against the THEORY it grounds, and
  split as the Decision says.
- Each MIXED claim is split.
- The four claims about defeat conditions stay.
- References to a moved claim are repointed in the moving contribution;
  the pull request's docs-check repair spells their targets (ADR-054).
