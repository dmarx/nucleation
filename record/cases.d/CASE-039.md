# CASE-039: Who crosses the meeting point first? Two craft near light speed, and the relativity of simultaneity

## The case

U47, as typed: "the paradox I was thinking of was actually just "who
crossed the meeting point first?" of two near lightspeed objects headed
towards each other. my understanding is that due to dilation effects, the
local frame of each would respectively answer that it is the frame which
crosses first. They can't both be true at the same time, so the local
interpretations can be glued into a global section."

The last clause says "can be glued", and is read here as "can't be glued",
for four reasons. The sentence argues from a contradiction ("They can't both
be true ... so"), and only "can't" follows from one. U46 had asked for
evidence that no global realization exists. "Can" would make the example
support nothing. And A164 answers the "can't" reading throughout, without
remarking on the wording: the disagreement "does not prevent their
descriptions from being assembled into a globally consistent spacetime
model". The text says "can"; the record files it as "can't".

The scenario has four readings.

- **(a) They meet at the point.** That is one event, and there is nothing
  to order. A164: "if both spacecraft cross **the same meeting point at the
  same event**, there aren't two crossing events to order."
- **(b) Each passes a fixed marker, at different times.** Both passings lie
  on the marker's worldline, so they are timelike-separated, and every
  inertial observer agrees on their order. A164 does not say this.
- **(c) Spacelike-separated events,** such as passing two different markers,
  or the two launches in the midpoint frame. Here the order does depend on
  the frame: Δt′ = γ(Δt − vΔx/c²) changes sign for some sub-luminal v when
  c²Δt² − Δx² < 0. In the symmetric launch, each craft's frame dates the
  other's launch earlier, and at the meeting both agree on both clock
  readings. This is the scenario A164 substitutes, without the launch
  detail: "This reversal is due specifically to **relativity of
  simultaneity**, not merely time dilation."
- **(d) The textbook form** of the intuition is the pole-and-barn paradox.

Time dilation is symmetric and never reverses an order, so "due to dilation
effects" names the wrong mechanism. Neither craft crosses anything in its
own rest frame. A173 drew the lesson for the manuscript: "It must explicitly
distinguish two spatially separated events from two objects meeting at one
spacetime event."

## What it can show

That a disagreement between local descriptions is not an obstruction when a
coherent transition law relates them ([CLAIM-143](../claims.d/CLAIM-143.md)). Coincident
events have no order, events on one worldline have an invariant order, and
only spacelike-separated events change order. The frames are global charts
related by the Poincaré group, so there is no cover whose sections fail to
glue ([CLAIM-144](../claims.d/CLAIM-144.md)). A164: "**The relativistic example is
therefore not evidence that global gluing fails.**"

## What it cannot show

Anything about translation's own maps. Lorentz maps are invertible, form a
group and preserve a metric; translation correspondences do none of these
([CLAIM-035](../claims.d/CLAIM-035.md)). It cannot show that local interpretations fail to glue,
which is what U46 and U47 offered it for ([ARG-010](../arguments.d/ARG-010.md), Rejected). It is
not a closed-timelike-curve case, which was U46's, and nothing in it
depends on time dilation alone.

A178 and A218 use relativity of simultaneity as a calibration case, a
positive control in which local disagreement has no obstruction. A178:
"These reference cases test the formal distinctions; they are not treated
as direct evidence that linguistic judgments are quantum contextual." That
use claims nothing about translation's maps, so it does not revive
[ARG-004](../arguments.d/ARG-004.md), the U9/A18 analogy between frames and translation.

A164 corrected the owner politely ("Your intuition leads to something
important") but did not say that in the owner's own scenario the answer is
invariant.
