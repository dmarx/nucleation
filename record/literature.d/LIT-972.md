# LIT-972: On Linear Representations and Pretraining Data Frequency in Language Models

Jack Merullo, Noah A. Smith, Sarah Wiegreffe and Yanai Elazar (2025), *ICLR 2025*.

## Key takeaways

*From the reading, [NOTE-786](../notes.d/NOTE-786.md).*

- **What "linear" means here.** A linear relational embedding is
  F(s, c) ≈ W s + b, with W the mean Jacobian, from a subject's
  middle-layer state to the final state, fitted per relation on 8
  examples. Its *causality* is the share of edits through it that change
  the model's answer as intended. It is a map for one relation, not a
  direction for an attribute.
- **Frequency tracks it.** Across 24 relations, causality correlates with
  mean subject–object co-occurrence within training sequences at
  r = 0.82. That beats subject frequency (0.66), object frequency (0.59)
  and 5-shot accuracy (0.74).
- **Thresholds, at any step.** Mean causality exceeds 0.9 above 1,097
  (GPT-J), 1,998 (OLMo-7B) and 4,447 (OLMo-1B) mean co-occurrences,
  "regardless of pretraining step". It does so "consistently (but not
  exclusively)".
- **Recall without linearity.** Rare relations can be recalled with no
  consistent linear map. Star–constellation reaches 84% 5-shot accuracy
  at 44% causality, with about 21 co-occurrences.
- **Inverting it.** LRE features predict an object's training frequency
  within 10× about 70% of the time on held-out relations. Across models
  they give 0.65 ± 0.12 on GPT-J against 0.31 for the mean baseline.
  Subject–object counts are barely predicted.
- **Not causal.** "We can not draw causal claims" (Appendix A).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-12 for [QUESTION-025](../questions.d/QUESTION-025.md), and read on 2026-10-14 in
[NOTE-786](../notes.d/NOTE-786.md). It was registered as "an empirical test of the question's
premise that co-occurrence statistics give linear attribute directions".
The reading corrects that. The paper measures relation maps, not
attribute directions. It counts how often terms co-occur, not how
co-occurrence is structured across attributes. And its result is a
correlation. Its bearing on the question is indirect: in trained models
linearity is graded and depends on exposure, so a measurement of
attribute directions would need to control for frequency. The
frequency–linearity finding is filed as [THEORY-269](../theory.d/THEORY-269.md). It sits beside
[THEORY-185](../theory.d/THEORY-185.md), whose noise account a count threshold fits loosely. The
authors' proposals, to shape training data to meet thresholds and to
infer closed models' data, are ML practice, hence the
`anthology-candidate` flag.
