# CLAIM-tmp7fzb9: A fork can be the construction of a new organization through which a disposition that the parent's governance kept ineffective gains efficacy, and not only one of several successors of the parent

## The claim

A50 §6 of the exchange "Individuation, Possibility, and Disposition"
(https://chatgpt.com/share/6aee7a9b-f13c-83e9-a865-6a29c2c3ff7e):
"An open-source community can possess a disposition to reject unilateral
governance changes." "Contributors may object strongly without possessing
the practical capacity to change the decision." "They may then organize a
fork, creating a new channel through which those previously ineffective
dispositions can become consequential." "It can also be **the construction
of a new organization through which a previously constrained disposition
gains efficacy**." A: "That is a powerful bridge from disposition to
organizational nucleation."

It rests on held claims. [CLAIM-047](CLAIM-047.md): possessing a regime is not the
same as its being practically effective, so the dissenters' disposition
can be real while the parent grants it nothing ([CLAIM-283](CLAIM-283.md)). An organization can have several successors, each in a different respect
(CLAIM-328), and a self's continuity can branch ([CLAIM-287](CLAIM-287.md)), so the fork
can carry the parent's lineage and still be a different organization. What the claim
adds is that the two meet: when voice inside the whole is denied a channel,
building a new whole is a way to efficacy, and the new organization is
individuated partly by the disposition it lets govern.

It bears on [QUESTION-041](../questions.d/QUESTION-041.md), which asks what channels from the members keep a
whole's course correctable. A fork is the exit side of [THEORY-152](../theory.d/THEORY-152.md)'s exit
and voice, read as a channel that founds an organization rather than one
that signals to the old one.

## What it does not say

- **That every fork is of this kind.** A fork can be a division over
  resources, a takeover or a copy for convenience, with no disposition
  denied in the parent.
- **That the forking disposition is the community's.** In A's example the
  disposition is attributed to "An open-source community" and then
  exercised by some contributors against the rest. Which bearer holds it is
  what [TERM-tmp6ersv](../terms.d/TERM-tmp6ersv.md) requires a claim to declare; read as the dissenters'
  disposition, the parent's governance masked it.
- **That the new organization succeeds or is better.** Gaining efficacy is
  not gaining legitimacy or viability.

No case is filed for it. Hirschman's exit and voice is the obvious
literature, and the record holds it: [THEORY-152](../theory.d/THEORY-152.md), Hirschman's 1974 revisiting
of his 1970 book. A does not cite it.
