# THEORY-010: Grangier and Auffèves's contextual objectivity is an ontological postulate, not contextuality in either formal sense: its one argued consequence, unpredictability after a change of context, is reproduced by an epistemically restricted classical model

## Source

Grangier & Auffèves (2018), [LIT-077](../literature.d/LIT-077.md), pp. 3–5 ([NOTE-044](../notes.d/NOTE-044.md)). Spekkens (2014), [LIT-007](../literature.d/LIT-007.md), Table II. Abramsky & Brandenburger, [LIT-016](../literature.d/LIT-016.md). Schmid et al., [LIT-003](../literature.d/LIT-003.md).

## What was actually shown

[LIT-077](../literature.d/LIT-077.md) postulates that the real is relative to a context: modalities belong jointly to system and context, and are mutually exclusive within one (Postulates 0–2, p. 3). Its one argued result is informal: with N exclusive modalities per context, certainty on returning to a first context after an incompatible second one would give N² > N joint modalities (p. 4). It defines no measurement cover, empirical model or operational equivalence, derives no noncontextuality inequality, and uses the Kochen–Specker and Bell results only as motivation; its Born-rule claim is cited, not shown ([NOTE-044](../notes.d/NOTE-044.md)).

The phenomenon the argument uses — losing certainty about one question after asking an incompatible one — is reproduced by Spekkens's epistemically restricted classical bit, and [LIT-007](../literature.d/LIT-007.md) files it as only weakly nonclassical (Table II, asserted). And a single system probed context after context presents no overlapping contexts, so no global-section obstruction in the sense of [LIT-016](../literature.d/LIT-016.md) can arise. Both formal notions ask whether a context-independent model of the statistics exists; contextual objectivity is a claim about what exists.

## What this does not say

- That contextual objectivity is wrong, or that its authors' separate derivation of unistochastic transition matrices fails; that derivation is not held and has not been read.
- It says only that the work held here does not establish contextuality in the Kochen–Specker or Spekkens sense.
