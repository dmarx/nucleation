# THEORY-1308: In Chen et al.'s student samples from Belgium, China, the USA and Peru, need satisfaction related to life satisfaction and vitality and need frustration to depressive symptoms with no detectable difference across countries, and neither relation was moderated by how much a person valued or desired the need

## Source

Chen et al. (2015), [LIT-2000](../literature.d/LIT-2000.md), pp. 220–233; read in [NOTE-1527](../notes.d/NOTE-1527.md).

## What was actually shown

**Study 1** (Belgium 359, China 324): autonomy and competence satisfaction
predicted a latent well-being of self-esteem and depressive symptoms
(β = .36, .41), relatedness did not (.04); paths constrained equal across
the two countries fit as well (Δχ²(3) = 3.63); valuation × satisfaction
interactions were null (β = .01, .05, −.02).

**Study 2** (USA 298, China 309, Belgium 200, Peru 244): with health,
financial satisfaction, income, age and gender controlled, composite
satisfaction related to life satisfaction and vitality and composite
frustration to depressive symptoms (reconstructed paths .51, .73 and .60).
Per need, satisfaction of all three predicted vitality and autonomy and
relatedness satisfaction predicted life satisfaction; frustration of all
three predicted depressive symptoms. Constraining paths equal across four
countries did not worsen fit (Δχ²(18) = 27.74; 20.29; Δχ²(9) = 3.26). Desire
× satisfaction and desire × frustration interactions were null overall and
in each country.

Either test could have come out otherwise: a significant interaction, or a
worse fit under constraint, would have shown moderation.

## What this does not say

- **That the relations are the same size everywhere.** Non-significant
  difference tests with groups under 150 do not bound the difference.
- **That satisfying the needs causes well-being**: the data are
  cross-sectional self-reports.
- **That need strength never moderates.** Only explicit valuation and
  desire were tested; implicit motives are set aside (pp. 231–232), and
  desire is itself correlated with frustration.
- **That the needs are needs and not universal enhancers.** Non-moderation
  by preference is consistent with both ([QUESTION-369](../questions.d/QUESTION-369.md)).
- **That the frustration dimension is valid.** See [THEORY-1338](THEORY-1338.md).
