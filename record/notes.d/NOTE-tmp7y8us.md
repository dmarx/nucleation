# NOTE-tmp7y8us: Neural Mechanics

## Contribution

A unified account of the geometric constraints that architectural
symmetries put on gradients and Hessians, with conservation laws under
gradient flow derived from them. The paper then works out what weight
decay, momentum and finite learning rates do to those laws, giving exact
expressions for the dynamics of a large number of parameter combinations
in a real network.

## Key insight

The parameters of a network are chaotic, but some combinations of them,
picked out by symmetries, are simple. A symmetry direction is invisible to
the loss, so gradient flow cannot move along it, and the coordinate along
it is conserved. What does move it is everything gradient flow leaves out.
Weight decay pulls it toward zero. Finite steps push outward, because each
step is tangent to a curved orbit.

## Assumptions

- **Differentiable symmetries** of the training loss: translation, scale
  and rescale, generated by softmax, batch normalization and homogeneous
  activations.
- **Theorem 1** requires ⟨θ, [∂_α ∂_θ ψ]g⟩ = 0, which holds for the three
  symmetries treated.
- **The exact dynamics of §6** use a deterministic continuous model:
  weight decay plus the modified loss, without momentum or noise. Noise
  is treated separately (App. F).

## Key results

- **Constraints (§3).** Translation: ⟨g, 1_A⟩ = 0 and H1_A = 0. Scale:
  ⟨g, θ_A⟩ = 0 and Hθ_A + g_A = 0. Rescale: ⟨g, θ_A1 − θ_A2⟩ = 0.
- **Conservation (Theorem 1, Eqs. 11–13).** Under gradient flow, the
  parameter sum, the norm, and the difference of squared norms are
  conserved for the three symmetries.
- **Broken conservation (Eqs. 18–20).** Translation:
  ⟨θ(t), 1_A⟩ = e^{−λt}⟨θ(0), 1_A⟩, independent of η and the data. Scale:
  |θ_A(t)|² = e^{−2λt}|θ_A(0)|² + η∫e^{−2λ(t−τ)}|g_A|²dτ. Rescale: the same
  form, with the difference of gradient norms, whose sign is not fixed.
- **Scale balance (Eq. 21).** Weight decay's centripetal pull −2λ|θ_A|²
  against discretization's centrifugal push η|g_A|². At stationarity the
  angular speed is √(2λ/η), consistent with Wan et al. (2020).
- **Rescale.** "For large enough weight decay, the regularization
  dominates any complexity introduced by the gradient norms and the
  difference in parameter norms decays exponentially to zero."
- **Momentum (§6, App. G).** The quantities follow driven harmonic
  oscillators. They are overdamped for standard β, and visibly underdamped
  at β = 0.99 on VGG-16.
- **Noise (§5, App. F).** Batch gradients are orthogonal to the generators
  too, so Gᵀ∂_α ψ = 0: the noise is low-rank along symmetry directions.
  Under an Itô model, the scale-symmetric norm still gains a deterministic
  forcing term from the noise variance, (η/S)Tr[GᵀG].
- **Count.** A VGG-16 with batchnorm on Tiny ImageNet has 12,751 such
  combinations (App. J, Table 5).

## Claims

- The analytic expressions "match nearly perfectly with empirical results"
  on VGG-16. See Limitations for what that match tests.

## Concepts

- **Generator:** ∂_α ψ at the identity, the direction a symmetry moves the
  parameters.
- **Modified loss:** L̃ = L + (η/4)|∇L|², whose gradient flow tracks
  gradient descent at finite η (Barrett & Dherin 2020).

  makes the covariance independent of the parameters. That removes the Itô
  forcing term App. F derives.
- **[LIT-tmppdo96](../literature.d/LIT-tmppdo96.md) (Ziyin et al.)** builds that Itô term into a law of
  balance for rescale symmetry, where the noise drives the norm
  difference to a balanced value.

## Limitations

- **The match is with recorded gradients.** The "theoretical" curves in
  the figures compute the integral terms from the batch gradients recorded
  during training (App. J). The discrete versions of Eqs. 18–20 are exact
  identities given those gradients (App. I). So the agreement tests how
  well the continuous model approximates the discrete updates. It does not
  test a prediction made without the trajectory. The translation result,
  which needs no gradients, is the exception.
- **One architecture and dataset** for the experiments: VGG-16, with and
  without batchnorm, on Tiny ImageNet.
- **Noise in §6 enters only through the recorded batch gradients.** The
  Itô treatment is sketched in App. F for scale symmetry only.

## Open questions

- What is the stochastic counterpart of Eq. 20 for rescale symmetry? That
  is what Ziyin et al. answer, as a law of balance.
