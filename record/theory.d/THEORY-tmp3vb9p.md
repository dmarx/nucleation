# THEORY-tmp3vb9p: Thompson argues that the obligations of states and corporations persist through changes of membership and pass to successor group agents, not because the later agent is identical to the earlier, but because temporally trustworthy group agents serve their members', future members' and victims' interests in lasting commitments and just reparation; and that essentially intergenerational groups owe their members' children entitlements grounded in needs intrinsic to human life

## The account

Thompson argues that the obligations of states and corporations persist through changes of membership and pass to successor group agents, not because the later agent is identical to the earlier, but because temporally trustworthy group agents serve their members', future members' and victims' interests in lasting commitments and just reparation; and that essentially intergenerational groups owe their members' children entitlements grounded in needs intrinsic to human life

## Evidence

The reading in NOTE-tmpdkxlk; its key results are quoted there with pages.

## What it does not say

What fixes the content of members' entitlements, or which successor bears an obligation in a contested succession. It does not answer whether a later collective is the earlier one; it argues the question can be bypassed for responsibility.
