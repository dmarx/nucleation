# THEORY-1590: Markus and Kitayama propose that cultures construe the self as independent or interdependent, that for the interdependent self others are included within the self's boundaries without a merging of self and other, and that the construal shapes cognition, emotion and motivation

## The account

The two construals differ in "the role that is assigned to the other in
self-definition" (p. 245). For the interdependent self "others are
included *within* the boundaries of the self because relations with
others in specific contexts are the defining features of the self" (pp.
245–246), but this "does not result in a merging of self and other" (p.
228). The authors offer a third option: "there is an alternative to
selfishness (which implies the exclusion of others) besides selflessness
(which is to imply the exclusion of the self or self-sacrifice): There
is a self defined in relationship to others" (p. 247). Others' goals may
be "experienced as personal goals", or met as a requirement of meeting
one's own under watched reciprocity (p. 229). Other-focused emotions
"can motivate genuine, other-oriented, altruistic behaviors" (p. 248).

## Evidence

From [NOTE-1275](../notes.d/NOTE-1275.md): cross-national contrasts in person description,
attribution, self-description, emotion and self-enhancement (for
example, American students placed on average 30% of peers above
themselves, Japanese about 50%). Only Kitayama and Markus's emotion
study measures construal in individuals, and it gives no sample size.

## What it bears on

[QUESTION-300](../questions.d/QUESTION-300.md) cites this work for selves "construed
interdependently", one of the accounts on which selves are more or less
inclusive. The paper supports that premise in its own words, names the
middle position between selfishness and selflessness, and reads the
action as other-oriented rather than self-interested. [CLAIM-491](../claims.d/CLAIM-491.md)
grounds on the work itself; its note of 2026-10-29 records the
reading.
