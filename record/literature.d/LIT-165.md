# LIT-165: Coherence-based measures of explanatory power

Stephan Hartmann, Borut Trpin (2026), *Philosophical Studies, 183(6), 1817–1843* — DOI-10.1007/s11098-026-02517-x

## Key takeaways

- Probabilistic measures of explanatory power survive Lange's entailment problem and Roche & Sober's "Two Likelihood Inequalities" critique if explanatory power is measured as how well the whole set of explanantia coheres with the explanandum versus with its negation — in particular using the CohOG+ coherence measure.

*Seeded from the abstract and a skim, not a reading. What follows is what the work says about itself.*

Recent critiques have questioned whether probabilistic measures of explanatory power can work at all. The authors respond with a family of coherence-based measures that avoids those problems. Instead of scoring only how well one hypothesis accounts for the evidence, the measures compare how well the whole explanatory package, which may contain several explanantia, coheres with the explanandum against how well it coheres with the explanandum's negation. They show that this accommodates key scientific judgments while keeping the advantages of a probabilistic framework.
