# NOTE-tmpbntuj: Slemp et al. — Interpersonal supports for basic needs, a meta-analysis

## Contribution

The largest synthesis of SDT's contextual claim: that others' support for
autonomy, competence and relatedness goes with need satisfaction,
autonomous motivation, well-being and performance. It is the first to treat
competence and relatedness support alongside autonomy support across
domains, to estimate their incremental contribution from meta-analytic
matrices, and to compare lateral (peer) with vertical (authority) sources
(pp. 2–7).

## Key insight

The three supports are not three separate levers in the data. They
correlate .67–.71 with each other, and each correlates about as strongly
with every need as with its own. The specificity SDT's model draws
(Fig. 2: each support "primarily" feeds its own need) shows only in the
joint models, and most clearly for relatedness: with all three in,
relatedness support's weight on relatedness satisfaction is .56 and
autonomy support's is .007 (Table 2).

## Assumptions

- **Correlations, read as direct effects.** Only zero-order correlations
  were pooled; support is "treated as the predictors" on theoretical
  grounds, and where lagged correlations existed the most distant was used
  "consistent with the causal direction implied by SDT" (p. 10). Mediation
  through need satisfaction is not tested (p. 5).
- **Schmidt–Hunter psychometric meta-analysis**, correcting for
  unreliability by artifact distributions; Gignac and Szodorai benchmarks
  .15/.25/.35 for weak/moderate/strong (pp. 9–10).
- **Culture as Hofstede's individualism percentile of the sample's country**
  (p. 14), which the authors call an assumption of homogeneity (p. 20).
- **Structure and involvement counted as competence and relatedness
  support** (p. 8).
- **Incremental validity from pooled matrices**, with harmonic-mean N
  (32,933–113,363) as the sample size (p. 11).

## Key results

### The database (pp. 8–9)

- Seven databases to 27 November 2021, plus citation searches; 768
  records, 881 samples; 50 matrices added by emailing 173 authors.
  Screening by the first author, 20% double-screened (98% agreement,
  κ = .93). Grey literature not actively sought. Not preregistered; data
  and scripts at OSF (osf.io/2ht3s).

### Main associations (pp. 11–12; ρ, corrected)

- **Need satisfaction**: autonomy support with autonomy .57 (k = 235),
  competence .45 (k = 274), relatedness .44 (k = 251); competence support
  .50, .48, .48; relatedness support .53, .45, .62.
- **Need frustration** (k = 8 for competence and relatedness support):
  autonomy support −.30, −.20, −.35; competence support −.33, −.38, −.33;
  relatedness support −.30, −.30, −.55.
- **Motivation**: autonomous motivation .40–.44; controlled motivation
  .01–.10; amotivation −.17 to −.31.
- **Subjective well-being**: positive affect .39–.50; negative affect
  −.24 to −.44; life satisfaction .42–.50 (competence support k = 3).
- **Performance**: overall .19 (autonomy), .14 (competence), .25
  (relatedness); self-reported performance stronger than objective.
- Heterogeneity: "very high values for I² (typically exceeding 90%)"
  (p. 12).

### Moderators (pp. 13–14)

- Measures: domain-specific scales (TASC, POPS) gave weaker effects, e.g.
  autonomy support with intrinsic motivation in classrooms .17 (TASC)
  against .57 (climate questionnaires).
- Domain: physical education strongest; autonomy support with autonomy
  satisfaction in health care weakest (.23, k = 11).
- Source: vertical autonomy support stronger than lateral for competence
  satisfaction and autonomous motivation; lateral relatedness support
  stronger than vertical for relatedness satisfaction (difference −.20).
- Culture: 2 of 18 relations moderated by individualism, autonomy support →
  autonomous motivation weaker in more individualist samples (β = −.0010
  per percentile) and relatedness support → intrinsic motivation stronger
  (β = .0030). Age and time lag weakened some relations.

### Incremental validity (pp. 14–17, Tables 1–4)

- Table 1: supports intercorrelate .67, .68, .71; satisfactions .61–.72.
- Table 2, all three supports (Model 7): R² = .369 (autonomy
  satisfaction), .269 (competence), .388 (relatedness); ΔR² over autonomy
  support alone .044, .067, .194. Joint betas for relatedness
  satisfaction: autonomy support .007, competence support .078,
  relatedness support .560. Relative weights: each support at least 18%
  of R².
- Reproduced here from the Table 1 matrix: Models 1–7 for all three
  needs, to three decimals.
- Table 4: lateral and vertical supports add to each other; lateral
  relatedness support with relatedness satisfaction ρ = .78 (k = 4) leaves
  vertical relatedness support ΔR² = .008. Several cells rest on k = 2–4
  (Table 3).

### Robustness (pp. 14–15)

- Outliers rarely mattered; publication-bias analyses flagged 41% of
  distributions as possibly misestimated by more than 20%, but by the
  average-range estimate 90% were within 20%.

## Concepts

- **Interpersonal supports for basic needs** — "an umbrella term" for
  behaviours that nurture the three needs (Fig. 2): autonomy support
  (choice, rationale, acknowledging feelings, avoiding controls),
  competence support including structure (clear expectations, feedback,
  expressed confidence), relatedness support including involvement
  (interest, care, unconditional regard).
- **Vertical / lateral** — support from an authority (teacher, manager,
  parent) or from a peer (colleague, sibling, partner) (p. 6).

## Bearing on the record

- **"Effects" in the abstract and title wording are correlations.** The
  registration calls it evidence "for SDT's contextual claims"; it is
  evidence for the *association*. The authors say their "interpretation of
  need-supportive behaviors as the antecedent was based purely on
  theoretical grounds" and that reverse causation could not be ruled out
  (p. 20). Both sides are mostly the same person's report.
- **[QUESTION-tmpee04a](../questions.d/QUESTION-tmpee04a.md).** The three-way intercorrelation of the supports
  (~.7) and of the satisfactions (.61–.72) is a discriminant-validity
  question the record's question inherits: if the needs are distinct, their
  measures and their antecedents are hard to tell apart in the data. The
  specificity in the joint models (relatedness support → relatedness
  satisfaction) is the best evidence here that they are not one factor.
- **[THEORY-052](../theory.d/THEORY-052.md).** Support correlates with frustration at −.20 to −.55, on
  k = 8 for two of the three supports. Nothing about psychopathology,
  direction, or onset; the promote_when is not touched.
- **[THEORY-047](../theory.d/THEORY-047.md) / internalization.** Support's near-zero relation to
  controlled motivation (.01–.10) and strong one to autonomous motivation
  (.40–.44) is consistent with SDT's claim that support fosters
  internalization rather than less regulation.
- Filed from it: [THEORY-tmp4q289](../theory.d/THEORY-tmp4q289.md) (Active).
- **No instruction for machine-learning practice.** Its practical
  recommendations concern teaching, management and health care.

## Limitations

- Mostly cross-sectional, recipient-reported support and outcomes; time lag
  weakened some relations, "indicating a possible upward bias in
  cross-sectional effects" (p. 20).
- Heterogeneity is very high and largely unexplained; 60 distinct autonomy
  support measures (p. 19).
- Culture coded at country level from Hofstede.
- Some incremental and lateral analyses rest on very few studies (k = 2–4
  for some cells), which the authors flag as "more affected by
  second-order sampling error" (p. 20).
- Not preregistered; grey literature not sought; single first-pass
  screener.

## Open questions

- Do the supports predict need satisfaction prospectively, or does
  observed satisfaction elicit support (p. 20)?
- Is the ~.7 intercorrelation of the supports in the behaviour or in the
  measures (p. 20)?
- Do the cross-domain correlations hold up in a meta-analytic structural
  model with need satisfaction as mediator (p. 20)?
