# NOTE-tmp2juuo: Self-Deception

Ian Deweese-Boyd, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, revised 2023.

## Contribution

A survey with two branches:
- **intentionalist accounts** (§2), which model self-deception on deceiving another and divide the mind to escape the paradoxes;
- **revisionist accounts** (§3), which drop the intention requirement (deflationary, §3.1) or the belief requirement (§3.2).

It also covers twisted self-deception (§4), responsibility (§5), whether the capacity is adaptive (§6), and collective self-deception (§7). The author gives no verdict, though §5.1 leans to deflationary accounts keeping responsibility.

## Key results

- **Motive without intention** (§3.1): Allison's belief "fulfills her desire, quells her fear, or reduces her anxiety, and it's this function—not an intention—that explains why her belief formation process is biased. Allison's false belief is not an innocent mistake but a consequence of her motivational states."
- **Mele's conditions** (§3.1): the subject treats data relevant to the truth of p "in a motivationally biased way".
- **Which desires bias belief** (§3.1): on the error-cost account, "the minimization of costly errors is the central principle guiding hypothesis testing". The question is left open.
- **Unwelcome beliefs** (§4): the twisted case has "a belief that is neither warranted nor wanted in consequence of some desire, emotion, or combination of both".
- **Remedies** (§5.2): "the cultivation of one's epistemic virtues and the cultivation of one's epistemic community (Galeotti 2016b)".
- **Collective** (§7.1): "The collective entrenches self-deceptive beliefs by providing positive reinforcement through others sharing the same false belief".

## Assumptions

Motivation acts on one episode of belief formation at a time. Nothing in the entry says how a mind comes to self-deceive more over time. Empirical work is cited, not weighed.

## Concepts

Intentionalism and deflationism; the static and dynamic paradoxes; motivationally biased treatment of evidence; error-cost hypothesis testing; twisted self-deception; collective self-deception.

## Bearing on the record

- **The inside position** ([DISTINCTION-001](../distinctions.d/DISTINCTION-001.md)). Deflationism is a third option between the counter's two: something selects, a motive's function, with no one intending it. The counter ([CLAIM-1441](../claims.d/CLAIM-1441.md)) keeps its strongest line: a false belief with no motive is "an innocent mistake".
- **[CLAIM-tmpu6c7h](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpu6c7h.md)** fits the entry best of the three, though the entry favours none. Its line between sought and unsought insight matches the entry's line between motivated self-deception and innocent error, and §7.1's positive reinforcement matches the community case. Two limits:
  - what selects a belief in the entry is the comfort of its content, not a feeling of insight;
  - no account makes reinforcement over time the mechanism, which the claim's test needs.
- **[CLAIM-1393](../claims.d/CLAIM-1393.md)'s remedies.** §5.2's epistemic virtues and epistemic community are two of its three.

## Limitations

A survey that adjudicates little. Why only some desires bias belief is left open.
