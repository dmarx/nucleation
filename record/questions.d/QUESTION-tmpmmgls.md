# QUESTION-tmpmmgls: When the continuities bundled in a collective come apart, can several later collectives each be a genuine successor of it in a different respect without numerical identity branching?

## The question

[QUESTION-118](QUESTION-118.md) asks how persistence is settled when several later
collectives each continue an earlier one in a different respect. Before
that comes whether such plural succession is possible at all without
numerical identity branching. This question asks that.

## The answer

[CLAIM-328](../claims.d/CLAIM-328.md), Active, answers yes, as an existence claim grounded on [CASE-105](../cases.d/CASE-105.md).
When a collective's franchise, civic role, interpersonal transmission, and
narrative and place come apart, as at the Senators' relocation, several
later collectives each succeed it in one respect: the Twins by the league's
legal succession, the expansion Senators by the city's place in the
league, the 2005 Nationals by claim to the city's history. These are plural
successor relations. None of them, and not the earlier collective, has to
branch in numerical identity.

It rests on one documented case, with no data on how supporters divided.
Which successor relation, if any, is the earlier collective persisting,
and what criterion decides it, remain [QUESTION-118](QUESTION-118.md)'s.
