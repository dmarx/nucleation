# THEORY-tmpt34ma: Vallor holds that calling AI superhuman is an ideology that, by redefining intelligence as outperforming humans at economically valuable work, devalues human agency and autonomy and erases the difference between human minds and the tools built to mirror them, and that the poverty of such tools may instead prompt a reclamation of humane capacities

## The account

Vallor: the ideology "fosters the growing devaluation of human agency and autonomy and collapses the distinction between our conscious minds and the mechanical tools we’ve built to mirror them"; "OpenAI’s AGI bait-and-switch wipes anything that does not count as economically valuable work from the definition of intelligence"; after Borgmann, "it is precisely when a technology has nearly supplanted a vital domain of human meaning that we are able to feel and mourn what has been taken from us."

## Evidence

The reading in NOTE-tmpl6ia5, results 1–7.

## What it does not say

Moral deskilling, practical wisdom, or the mirror's backward orientation, which the book is reported to argue. It is a stance, argued by example, not a demonstration.
