  For random networks of binary threshold gates driven by a random binary
  input, an annealed mean-field theory gives the order-to-chaos line as
  K·P_bf = 1, a single flipped input bit flipping on average one node; a
  separation measure corrected for initial-condition and input-drive
  separation peaks near that line, and a trained linear readout does best

  assessed over many tasks rather than tuned to one. A local

# LIT-tmpdsowv: At the Edge of Chaos: Real-time Computations and Self-Organized Criticality in Recurrent Neural Networks

Natschläger, Bertschinger & Legenstein (2004). Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 17.

## Standing in the record

Registered and read on 2026-11-22 as a stand-in ([ADR-114](../decisions.d/ADR-114.md)) for
Bertschinger and Natschläger (2004), [LIT-2991](LIT-2991.md), whose text could not be
reached. It is a separate paper by the same group, not the Neural
Computation article: it says of that article "A similar analysis for a
restricted case (zero mean of synaptic weights) of the network model
considered in this paper can be found in [4]", and that its own model
"differs in two important aspects" (states in {0, 1}, and weights of
arbitrary mean µ). So it restates the programme of [LIT-2991](LIT-2991.md) (the
mean-field critical line of input-driven threshold networks, readout
performance peaking near it) and cites it for the experimental details,
and adds the NM-separation measure and the synaptic-scaling rule, which
the article's abstract does not mention. How far it stands in is set out
in [NOTE-tmpi5t3r](../notes.d/NOTE-tmpi5t3r.md).

These three authors are the ones the exchange "Rich Get Richer Power
Laws" (A15 §3) named for the 2004 edge-of-chaos result: A conflated the
two 2004 papers ([CLAIM-809](../claims.d/CLAIM-809.md), version 3). The reading qualifies
[CLAIM-809](../claims.d/CLAIM-809.md) ([ARG-tmp2zz6k](../arguments.d/ARG-tmp2zz6k.md)) and bears on [QUESTION-507](../questions.d/QUESTION-507.md) and [CLAIM-850](../claims.d/CLAIM-850.md).
