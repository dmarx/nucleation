# THEORY-tmpzuniv: Moen argues that Kaczynski's case against industrial technology reaches its conclusion from defensible empirical worries, that technological progress is hard to predict and as an arms race hard to restrain, only through unargued evaluative premises that count technology's harms and not its benefits, so that counted even-handedly the case fails

## The account

Moen argues that Kaczynski's argument rests on largely defensible empirical worries: technological progress is "an arms-race", and its control faces "both an epistemic problem and a coordination problem". Its conclusion needs evaluative premises Moen rejects, chief among them that "the benefits of technology have little or no real value".

## Evidence

The reading in [NOTE-tmp1h9te](../notes.d/NOTE-tmp1h9te.md). Corroborates [CLAIM-tmpbypt7](../claims.d/CLAIM-tmpbypt7.md): the car city and the arms race are its infrastructural and competitive mechanisms, from a source independent of the exchange.

## What it does not say

Anything in support of Kaczynski's conclusion or means, which Moen rejects.
