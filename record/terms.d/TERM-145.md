# TERM-145: Matthew effect

## Definition

**Matthew effect** has two senses in the record's sources. Say which is
meant.

1. **Merton's sense: allocation by standing.** Recognition for a *given*
   contribution goes disproportionately to a contributor who is already
   eminent and is withheld from one who is not: "the accruing of greater
   increments of recognition for particular scientific contributions to
   scientists of considerable repute and the withholding of such
   recognition from scientists who have not yet made their mark"
   ([LIT-3002](../literature.d/LIT-3002.md), p. 58; [NOTE-2268](../notes.d/NOTE-2268.md)). The contribution is held fixed and
   the reward varies with rank. Its mechanisms, in Merton, include a fixed
   number of places at the summit, a ratchet that keeps the eminent from
   falling back, and greater visibility, which he calls "a special
   application of the self-fulfilling prophecy" (p. 61). Its unit is a
   reward event, and its claim is about attribution.
2. **The formal sense: growth in proportion to size.** A unit's expected
   gain is proportional to what it holds (or to that plus a constant).
   Newman lists "Matthew effect" among the names of this rule, the Yule
   process, beside "cumulative advantage" and "preferential attachment"
   ([LIT-2976](../literature.d/LIT-2976.md), p. 18; [NOTE-2261](../notes.d/NOTE-2261.md)). Its unit is a holding, and its claim
   is about a rate.

## How the senses connect

Merton links them himself but does not identify them. Cumulative advantage
is "One institutional version of the Matthew effect", where "the rich get
richer at a rate that makes the poor become relatively poorer" (p. 62),
and he credits that implication to Price (note 46). Sense 1 can produce
sense 2: if credit goes by standing and credit raises standing, gains grow
with holdings. But sense 1 fixes no rate, so it does not give linear
attachment, and with fixed top places or a ratchet it can give
concentration of other shapes.

## What it is not

- **Not a synonym of preferential attachment** in Merton's sense. "The
  Matthew effect gives a power law" is a claim in sense 2 only.
- **Not a halo effect.** Merton: "This greater visibility is not merely
  the result of a halo effect" (p. 60).
- **Not a claim about any distribution.** Neither sense, by itself, says
  what tail results; that needs the kernel and entry ([CLAIM-817](../claims.d/CLAIM-817.md)).

## The same kind of split

[TERM-144](TERM-144.md) does the same for "Gibrat": one name for a rule, used for
different outcomes or settings. Here the split is between an attribution
effect and a growth rule; there it is between the outcomes of one rule.
