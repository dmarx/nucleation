## "Do we even need Riesz?"

The owner's note under the Riesz heading asked this. The dossier agent's
answer, from reading the four pages: **yes, as a lemma, not as a reading.**

- Riesz is what makes kernels exist. Evaluation at a point is a continuous
  functional, and Riesz turns it into the kernel vector k_x.
- It is why a linear probe or "concept direction" can be treated as a vector.
- It is why adjoints, and so the spectral arguments in the self-supervised
  learning papers, make sense.
- Adler's REU paper ends on Radon–Nikodym, the density result that KL
  divergence and mutual information, and so the bottlenecks, rest on.
- Bra–ket is notation.
- GNS is a suggestive analogy: a state determines its representation up to
  unitary equivalence. That echoes the Platonic hypothesis's kernel
  comparisons, but no representation-learning paper found uses it.

None of the four pages makes these connections itself. They are the dossier's
own and are marked as such.
