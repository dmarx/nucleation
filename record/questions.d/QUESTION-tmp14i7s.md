# QUESTION-tmp14i7s: Can a party's capacity to make remedies costly or unavailable change, over time, the standard those remedies are measured against, so that what correction is understood to require bends toward what correction can achieve?

## Why it is a question

A68 §4 of the exchange (https://chatgpt.com/share/6afcb46d-83a8-83e9-9c85-4a0c3604405b)
reads Fiss's account of structural reform litigation, through the record's
reading of it: a court "may **adapt its articulation of a right to the
remedy it believes it can achieve**." NOTE-1364 has Fiss's line: no judge
"is likely to decree more than he thinks he has the power to accomplish …
He will tailor the right to fit the remedy."

A68's step is to put a party's resistance behind the remedy's
difficulty. If a powerful party can make remedies costly (CLAIM-tmpyo35b),
and courts adapt rights to what they can remedy, then "**An
organization's practical resistance to correction may influence not only
the correction it receives, but the legal understanding of what
correction is required.**" A's schematic: the operative interpretation
N_{t+1} = Φ(N_t, 𝓡_t, E_t), with the feasible remedies 𝓡_t possibly
moving it ("The derivative is purely schematic; standards of law are not
ordinarily scalar variables").

A68's closing pairs this with Ahmed's account of complaint (THEORY-1824):
a challenge becomes costly, practical correction diminishes, and "The
institution's interpretation of what must be corrected may adapt to what
correction can achieve", a loop A labels "Hypothesized feedback
mechanism, not a universal account of institutional or judicial
behavior."

It bears directly on a premise of the record's claims. **CLAIM-464**
(Active) says an intervention can make a corrective outcome costlier or
unreachable "while the standards that govern" it stay the same. If
standards drift with remedial feasibility, that holds at a time and can
fail over time, and CLAIM-tmpa0wdv (F4, from the same exchange: making a
remedy unreachable gains no power over what is owed) would have a route
by which it gains that power after all, not by right but by changing what
is taken to be owed.

## What the record already has

- **CLAIM-464's note of 2026-10-29** lists Fiss among four works read:
  remedies depend on forces outside the court, "and Fiss adds that the
  standard itself then bends", with Cover's *Tiede* (LIT-1601, NOTE-1384)
  as the inverse case in which enforcement conditions changed a sentence.
- **LIT-1592**, Levinson, "Rights Essentialism and Remedial Equilibration",
  *Columbia Law Review* 99(4), 1999, is the standard source for the thesis
  that rights and remedies equilibrate. It is Unfetched: a lawful copy on
  SSRN (abstract 155564) returned 403 on 2026-10-29. NOTE-1364: Fiss's
  line "is the thesis Levinson later names remedial equilibration". It is
  directly relevant and unread; reading it is the first step toward an
  answer.

## What would count as an answer

- **A mechanism with evidence**: cases in which a standard was narrowed
  after its remedies proved costly, with the cost traceable to a party's
  resistance and not only to a court's limits. Levinson is the place to
  start; A68: "That is not a theorem from Fiss or a universal account of
  judicial behavior."
- **A separation**: whether a standard that bends to remedial feasibility
  is the same standard reinterpreted or a different one adopted. If the
  second, CLAIM-464's "standards that govern that outcome stay the same"
  is not threatened, only outflanked.
- **A negative**: an argument that rights essentialism holds, that the
  content of a right is fixed apart from its remedies, would answer it no.
