# NOTE-1642: Aksel et al. — Design principles for accurate folding of DNA origami

## Contribution

A modular, global optimizer for where to break staple precursors in a
DNA origami design, with a stated thermodynamic score, released as code
(douglaslab/pyOrigamiBreak). With it the authors show that, for a fixed
helix layout, the choice of staple breakpoints (and, in one case, the
scaffold seam) changes the measured yield of accurate folding by about an
order of magnitude, and they offer a per-staple folding-temperature map
for diagnosing existing designs.

## Key insight

Staple breakpoints are a design variable as consequential as the shape,
and they can be optimized globally once each candidate staple is given a
cost. The cost here is equilibrium stability of each staple in the
finished object, so the method improves yield by making the target's own
parts more stable, not by choosing a folding pathway.

## Assumptions

- **Independent staples at equilibrium (Eqs. 1-3).** "We adopted an
  equilibrium modeling approach by independently calculating each staple
  score in its fully bound state, and ignoring folding kinetics and
  interstaple cooperativity." P_origami is the product of staple
  probabilities; Q_origami is Σ ln P_staple per base pair.
- **One temperature stands for the anneal**: the score is evaluated "at
  T = 50 °C", with 12.5 mM Mg2+ assumed (SI constants, after Dunn et al.
  2015), while yields were quantified on 20 mM MgCl2 folds.
- **Loop entropy** of the intervening scaffold from a freely jointed or
  worm-like chain (SI: persistence length 0.9 nm, 0.6 nm per base),
  assumed to hold inside a dense 3D block.
- **Gel intensity in the leading mobility window measures accurate
  folding.**
- **The search is not an exact global optimum**: per-subgraph k-best paths
  (k = 10) are assembled at random into global solutions, with penalties
  for breaking both halves of a double crossover.
- The scaffold route is drawn by hand; only staples are routed.

## Key results

- **Reduction (Results, "Reduction to a known Problem").** The authors
  "reduced the problem of DNA strand routing to the known problem of
  shortest-path finding in a weighted graph": nodes are staple-precursor
  phosphates, edges candidate staples (21-60 nt), and "the shortest paths
  represent globally optimized solutions according to the scoring
  function."
- **Score (Eq. 4).** Total free energy is the sum of a bimolecular binding
  term, a loop-closure term for each scaffold loop and a nearest-neighbour
  hybridization term (SantaLucia and Hicks 2004, salt-corrected).
- **Yields (Results, Fig. 3).** "The 10 × 10 block redesign improved from
  2 to 61%" and "The 64-helix blocks with 4 × 16, 8 × 8, and 16 × 4
  configurations improved from 4 to 48%, 3.6 to 56%, and 6 to 48%". These
  are ratios of 30.5, 12, 15.6 and 8; the abstract's "6- to 30-fold" has a
  lower bound the reported numbers do not give. Yields correlate with
  Q_origami, R² = 0.8615 over 8 designs.
- **How yield was measured (Methods; Fig. 3 legend).** "We defined the
  Yield of accurate folding for each design as the integrated pixel
  intensity corresponding to the fastest moving Peak among the original
  and redesign conditions, which in all cases was the redesign"; the
  window was set by hand on an intensity histogram. So the originals are
  scored in the redesigns' window. TEM only of gel-extracted redesign
  bands; "Micrographs of the unimproved designs can be found in the
  original publications". No replicates or error bars are stated.
- **Same layout, different staples (SI Figs. S3-S5).** For the three
  64-helix blocks the redesigns "used identical scaffold routes and
  staple-passivation strategies".
- **The 10×10 is not the same molecule (SI Fig. S2).** The authors
  "rerouted the internal scaffold crossovers to a ‘middle-seam’ layout",
  moved the 110-nt insertion and added edge staples, "resulting in 2719
  added base pairs (6 of 7560 remain unpaired)". The largest gain is the
  case with the most changed target.
- **Hybridization against loops (Fig. 4B).** For the 64-helix blocks the
  redesigns improved hybridization by 8.5 to 12.7 kcal/mol "at the cost of
  slightly less favorable ∆Gloop terms" (+0.9 to +2.8 kcal/mol). The
  binding term was the same for every staple, concentrations being fixed
  (10 nM scaffold, 100 nM each staple).
- **Staple excess (Fig. 4C).** Tenfold more staple lowers the binding
  penalty by about 1.5 kcal/mol, but "Increasing the molar ratio of
  staples to scaffold above 10:1 is unlikely to significantly improve
  folding accuracy." Tuning a subset's concentration is suggested, not
  tested.
- **Magnesium (SI Fig. S6).** Folded at 6-20 mM MgCl2, redesigns pass from
  incomplete to mostly well folded above 8 mM; most originals do not
  complete the transition. The authors speculate that redesigns assemble
  more cooperatively at higher temperature "before the structure gets
  locked into irreversible kinetic traps": a speculation, not tested.
- **Smaller levers.** Scaffold permutation moves Q_origami by up to 0.03
  per base pair against redesign gains of 0.27 to 0.56; a staggered and a
  non-staggered seam on the 4×16 block gave comparable gel yields (SI
  Fig. S13).
- **Limits stated.** "The yields of our redesigns still show 39 to 52%
  misfolded populations"; the model "does not capture the thermal
  annealing process".

## Concepts

- **Staple precursor**: a continuous staple path before breaking; each is
  its own subgraph.
- **Breakpoint** and **breakpoint rule**: where a precursor may be cut
  (SI Fig. S1).
- **Q_origami**: the design's score per base pair, Σ ln P_staple / L.
- **Tfold**: a staple's folding temperature, ΔH/ΔS of its total free
  energy, mapped per staple for diagnosis.
- **Passivation; scaffold seam; scaffold permutation**: the other design
  choices the method holds fixed or tests.

## Connections

It builds on Dunn et al. (2015), [LIT-2135](../literature.d/LIT-2135.md), for its loop model and
binding term; Majikes et al. (2020), [LIT-2117](../literature.d/LIT-2117.md), measured the
fold-distance dependence of loop entropy that the loop term prices; the
64-helix originals descend from Douglas et al. (2009). Against Majikes et
al. (2021), where yield falls at high staple excess for one fold, the
binding term here only improves with excess. Wah et al. (2016) shifted
nucleation by non-uniform staple concentration, the subset tuning this
paper proposes and does not test. Schneider, Möritz and Dietz (2019),
[LIT-2122](../literature.d/LIT-2122.md), found folding order set more by topology than by binding
strength, which an independent-staple score cannot express; Dannenberg
et al. (2015), [LIT-2129](../literature.d/LIT-2129.md), model the loop-closure cooperativity and
hysteresis this score omits. The held simulation of DeLuca et al.
([LIT-2081](../literature.d/LIT-2081.md), [NOTE-1561](NOTE-1561.md), [THEORY-1398](../theory.d/THEORY-1398.md)) puts misfolding in kinetic traps,
which this model leaves out by construction. Wagenbauer et al. (2014),
[LIT-2128](../literature.d/LIT-2128.md), is the branch's reading on quantifying quality; this yield
is gel-only. Cumberworth, Frenkel and Reinhardt (Nano Letters 2022), a
simulation of design-dependent nucleation barriers, is the likely source
of A's first misattribution; it is not held.

## Bearing on the record

- **A's characterization.** The opening attribution to "Cumberworth and
  colleagues (2024)" is wrong, and A's own correction (Aksel, Navarro,
  Fong and Douglas, PNAS) is right. Treating strand routing as a
  weighted-graph problem is supported, though the routes are staple
  routes; "binding energetics and loop-closure costs" is supported but
  incomplete, since hybridization is the term that moved most for three
  blocks and binding was constant. "Redesigning several
  three-dimensional structures" (four blocks) and "approximately 6- to
  30-fold" (the abstract; the text's numbers give 8 to about 30) are
  supported, as is "an actual engineering method". That changing how
  components are connected changes formation probability without changing
  the final object is A's paraphrase: it holds for the three 64-helix
  blocks, not for the 10×10, and the paper's account is that the
  redesigned target is itself more stable. "optimizing a developmental
  mechanism" goes beyond the paper, whose model scores the finished state
  and explicitly ignores folding kinetics.
- **[CLAIM-567](../claims.d/CLAIM-567.md) (feasible, reachable, reliably reachable).** Supports,
  weakly: each original is feasible and reached at a few percent, the
  redesigns reach the nominally same layout at 48-61%. Reliability here is
  one gel window after one anneal.
- **[CLAIM-583](../claims.d/CLAIM-583.md) (same complex, different reach).** Does not instantiate it.
  Changing the staple set changes the compatibility complex and its costs,
  and the explanation offered is the stability of the parts, not a
  different path over the same complex. Dunn et al. (2015) is the reading
  that tried to hold final-fold stability fixed.
- **[CLAIM-569](../claims.d/CLAIM-569.md) (construct, maintain, transition).** Qualifies: a hold-type
  quantity, per-staple equilibrium stability at 50 °C, predicts
  construction yield (R² = 0.86 over 8 designs). In this design family the
  two co-vary; this is not a case that separates them.
- **[CLAIM-571](../claims.d/CLAIM-571.md)**: leaves open; no threshold or path is measured.
  **[CLAIM-566](../claims.d/CLAIM-566.md)**: consistent, assembly only.

## Limitations

- **The yield measure**: a gel window set by hand, the originals scored in
  the redesigns' window, no stated replicates or uncertainty; TEM
  qualitative and only of redesigns.
- **Four structures**, all multilayer lattice blocks; the R² is over 8
  points from those same structures.
- **The model** is equilibrium at one temperature and 12.5 mM Mg2+, while
  yields were read at 20 mM; one anneal protocol.
- **Confounded best case**: the 10×10 redesign also changes the scaffold
  route, insertion position and paired fraction.
- **The abstract's 6-fold lower bound** is not recoverable from the
  reported numbers.

## Open questions

- Does the gain survive a quantified count (TEM or AFM particles, with
  replicates) on designs whose scaffold route is held fixed?
- Would a score with kinetics or inter-staple cooperativity predict the
  originals' failures better than equilibrium stability does?
