# LIT-487: Social Ontology

Brian Epstein (2018), *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy; first published 21 March 2018, substantive revision 4 March 2024* — <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/social-ontology/>

## Key takeaways

- **Two inquiries.** One asks what a social entity is and what
  determines it: the conditions for something to be a group belief or a
  woman (§3). The other asks what sets up, constructs or "anchors" the
  category: what makes those the conditions (§4). The entry likens them
  to semantics and metasemantics.
- **Grounding and anchoring.** Recent work treats ontological
  determination as metaphysical grounding. Epstein argues that social
  construction, "anchoring", is a distinct relation that is neither
  grounding nor meta-grounding. Haslanger, Hawley and Schaffer accept the
  practical distinction and resist the sharp one (§2.2.2).
- **The field's maps.** Ontological individualism is widely assumed and
  recently contested (§3). Lewis's convention, Hart's accepted rules,
  Searle's status functions, Millikan's reproduced kinds, Boyd's and
  Hacking's causal loops and practice theories are all theories of
  anchoring (§4). Groups, group agency, institutions, race and gender,
  artifacts, money, law and language are the main domains (§5).

## Standing in the record

Filed on 2026-10-02 at the owner's request, as a readable survey for the
new `social-ontology` topic ([ADR-017](../decisions.d/ADR-017.md)), and as the source for what *The
Ant Trap*'s grounding–anchoring framework is, since the book itself is
not lawfully readable ([LIT-478](LIT-478.md)). [NOTE-393](../notes.d/NOTE-393.md) is the reading.

**What the framework is**, from §§2.2 and 4.5, with the author's précis
of the book (read for [LIT-478](LIT-478.md)) where it is fuller:

- A social fact is **grounded** by other facts: a pig in a place grounds
  the fact that there is an unkosher animal there.
- The conditions for grounding a fact of that kind are given by a
  **frame principle**, which holds across a frame, a set of possible
  worlds.
- What puts a frame principle in place is its **anchor**: perhaps the
  agreement of religious authorities, collective acceptance, practice or
  divine command. Anchors are heterogeneous. US legal categories are
  anchored by jury instructions, trial results, legislative enactments,
  judicial interpretation and environmental regularities (§4.5).
- Searle's constitutive rules are frame principles of one narrow form,
  and his collective acceptance is one theory of anchoring (précis).
  Lewis's convention, Hart's rule acceptance, Millikan, Boyd, Hacking and
  practice theories are other anchoring theories (§4).
- "Socially grounded" and "socially constructed" are two dimensions.
  Crowds are socially grounded but not constructed; phenotypic racial
  categories are constructed but not grounded; fiat money is both
  (§2.1). The entry cites this to an "Epstein 2016" that its bibliography
  does not list ([NOTE-393](../notes.d/NOTE-393.md), Corrections).

**How it places the other works filed with it.** Lewis ([LIT-473](LIT-473.md)) and
Bicchieri ([LIT-493](LIT-493.md)) are in the convention list of §4.2.2. Searle's
theory of status functions (§4.2.3) is the subject Hindriks & Guala
([LIT-481](LIT-481.md)) reduce to rules in equilibrium; the entry reports Searle's
view and the critics of collective acceptance in money (§5.6) but does
not discuss that reduction. North ([LIT-489](LIT-489.md)), Guala ([LIT-472](LIT-472.md))
and Guala & Hindriks 2020 are placed in new institutional economics
(§5.3).

**Group minds and [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md).** §5.2 is a short survey of the group-agent
literature the record already holds in part: List & Pettit's "groups with
minds of their own" ([LIT-391](LIT-391.md)), Huebner on complex groups ([LIT-426](LIT-426.md)), and
the functionalist model of group agency as a realised "system of
practical activity". It cites Schwitzgebel 2015 ([LIT-159](LIT-159.md)) and List 2018
([LIT-401](LIT-401.md)), the record's two anchors for [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md). It does not take a
side on group phenomenal consciousness. What the framework implies for
[THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md) is set out in [NOTE-393](../notes.d/NOTE-393.md); in brief, it says the whole
whose mindedness is in question is not fixed by its members, and that
both of [THEORY-043](../theory.d/THEORY-043.md)'s routes are stated as if it were.

**Realism about patterns.** §4.3 surveys "pattern theories" (functional
roles, Millikan's teleofunctions, Boyd's homeostatic clusters, Hacking's
looping kinds), on which social kinds are real because they support
induction. That is the social-kinds counterpart of Dennett's real
patterns ([LIT-220](LIT-220.md)), though the entry does not cite Dennett in that
section.
