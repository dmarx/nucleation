# Part C, continued. Diagnosis and lock-in

## 10. From modes to diagnosis

Section 9 studied one way an organization can fail to respond: it cannot afford the transition. The motivating draft (ChatGPT's article) lists four ways, which "can look similar in aggregate statistics, but they are causally different". A signal is detected too slowly. Or it is detected and no actor has authority to act on it. Or the action is authorized and the transition cannot be afforded. Or the transition succeeds once while the capacity to repeat it decays. The draft concludes that a regulator should identify "which part of its temporal response architecture failed". This section sets out what the record holds for each of these four failures and where the record keeps them apart. It also asks where the bottleneck of a response lies.

### 10.1 Four failures, each with its own entry

The record holds each of the draft's four failures, in entries from different lines. Laying them side by side as one diagnostic sequence was the first draft's join (join JC6: the four failures, from CLAIM-523, CLAIM-349, CLAIM-568, TERM-085, CLAIM-422 and CLAIM-556). The account of correcting a regulator lists it under "Not stated on the record" with the verdict "fails, a recap": "It is a map of held separations, and as a sequence it goes against CLAIM-523, which says the achievements 'form no necessary sequence'" (SYNTHESIS-tmp3x39i, Proposed). The nearest analogue was declined earlier, in the realizability account, SYNTHESIS-043 (Proposed): "**Capability as not one quantity.** That every part separates properties a single measure of capability would merge", as "a conjunction of the separations, which cannot fail while they hold". So JC6 is offered here as a list of held separations, in no fixed order, not as a sequence or a result.

**Detection and authority.** CLAIM-349 (Active, interpretive) separates them for an organization's own signals: "An organization's access to a signal, the signal's authority over action, and action on it are separable, and adaptive regulation keeps the first while making the second conditional on evidence". It adds that buying safety by cutting access, or by making inhibition unconditional, "is a maladaptive regime and not more control". CLAIM-523 (Active, interpretive) carries the split from signals to normative demands. For a discrepancy to change what an organization does, it needs "three distinct achievements ... and any can fail while the others hold":

- **legibility**, "by which the discrepancy becomes recognizable";
- **volitional efficacy**, "by which a corresponding demand can move action";
- **correctability**, "by which a fitting revision can be carried out within deadline and resources".

The draft's detection failure is a failure of legibility, and its authority failure is a failure of volitional efficacy. The draft states both and does not cite CLAIM-523 (a dated note on CLAIM-523 of 2026-11-23 records this). The same three achievements diagnose a dependent party's position as well as an organization's own failure. CLAIM-686 (Active, demonstrated, the assistant's) holds: "Where a dependent party's claims on an arrangement's controllers fail of legibility, volitional efficacy or correctability in the controlling organization, the dependent lacks effective means of influence and challenge". It keeps the scope that CLAIM-523 sets: the three are needed and not sufficient, so the converse does not follow.

Two cautions from CLAIM-523 bear on any diagnostic sequence. The three achievements come in no fixed order: "they form no necessary sequence. An organization can have executable corrective machinery for discrepancies it cannot see." And none of them shows that a demand is right: "A legible, effective, executable revision can be wrong."

**Affordability.** The third achievement, correctability, is TERM-085 (Active): "the chance that the arrangement, starting from the description of the problem it actually has, finds, authorizes, coordinates and carries out a plan achieving the objective before the deadline and within its resources". Its "within its resources" is the level §9 modelled. CLAIM-568 (Active, interpretive), ChatGPT's table, names it as one of four obstructions: "A transition can fail in four ways that call for different remedies: a feasible path exceeds a resource ceiling, no path exists in the fixed configuration space, a path exists that no available input can execute, or a path exists that cannot be reliably followed". The remedies differ. For a capacity bottleneck the table's repair is "Increase or reallocate resources". For geometric disconnection it is "Change the constraint architecture". The claim's point is that a remedy applied to the wrong obstruction buys nothing: "added actuation can overcome an energy barrier and cannot join disconnected configurations". A finding about intervention in general points the same way. CLAIM-562 (Active, empirical) holds: "Where an intervention acts in a system's constraint structure can predict whether it achieves a target better than how much intervention there is, and adding an intervention to a successful set can make it fail". Its evidence is in folding sheets, and its summary notes that A's wider claim, "greater intervention capacity need not monotonically improve success", "goes further than the paper and is not filed".

**Success once, without the capacity to repeat it.** CLAIM-422 (Active, interpretive) holds that "A successful corrective act, an executable procedure and a maintained organizational capacity are three different things, so an organization can record a correction, even rewrite its rules, without the corrected capacity being maintained". Its case is NASA's: a safety office set up after Challenger, and the Columbia board finding the capacity it was meant to maintain eroded.

### 10.2 The six levels, and their standing

CLAIM-556 (Active, interpretive) orders these failures into one diagnosis: "a diagnosis runs through six levels, each with its own repair". In ChatGPT's table:

| Level | Question | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Compatibility | "Does a valid state exist?" | "Change constraints or demands", corrected to a change of the constraints |
| Reachability | "Is there an admissible path?" | "Change transformation repertoire" |
| Affordability | "Does a path fit the budget?" | "Add or reallocate resources" |
| Controllability | "Can available inputs execute it?" | "Change actuation or policy" |
| Reliability | "Can it be realized robustly?" | "Improve feedback and stabilization" |
| Regeneration | "Can the capability persist through turnover?" | "Establish reproductive or maintenance mechanisms" |

The correction to the first repair is the record's. CLAIM-347 holds that "dropping or suppressing a demand is not relief", so changing the demands changes the question rather than answering it (§2). The table is "a sequence for diagnosis, not a chain of implications".

This is a stronger form of the draft's "causally different" failures with different remedies. But a reader should know its standing. Two synthesis reviews, on 2026-11-21 and 2026-11-23, each by a reader who did not know the other's verdict, found that CLAIM-556 "adds nothing to its premises" (CLAIM-568, CLAIM-566, CLAIM-347 and CLAIM-586 side by side). They also found that the first clause of its defeater is met by "a capability lost over a horizon with no turnover and no failure of information, such as creep or fatigue of a folded structure". Both recommend folding it into its line's account and setting it Superseded. That waits on the owner. Until then the paper uses the six levels as a list of distinct repairs held by the claims beneath it, CLAIM-568 above all, and not as an Active result in its own right.

**Where §9's loss falls.** §9's model gives a capability that is reached once and then lost. Under the peak rule, a transition made once leaves the budget at the transition's peak for one period. After the next period spent holding, the transition is lost again (CLAIM-577, Active, demonstrated). So the capability is reached, affordably and reliably, and then lost with no turnover of its carriers and no failure of information. That is the draft's fourth failure, by one mechanism among others. It is not a failure outside CLAIM-556's six levels. It is CLAIM-568's first obstruction, at the affordability level, with the ceiling lowered by the budget rule. CLAIM-556's note of 2026-11-23 (the assistant's, filed for this manuscript's gap GC9) says so: "It does not: it is CLAIM-568's first obstruction, 'A feasible path exceeds a resource ceiling', with the ceiling set by the rule itself, which is this claim's affordability level ('Does a path fit the budget?'), repaired by adding or reallocating resources (outside money, a floor, a reserve)." So the case is not a second instance of the creep or fatigue that the two reviews found to meet the defeater, and it leaves their verdict where it was. What it does add is an instance of CLAIM-422's separation. CLAIM-422's note of 2026-11-23 (the assistant's) reads: "The act succeeded; the capacity to repeat it was not maintained. That is this claim's separation, by a mechanism other than the decay with disuse of the notes above: nothing is forgotten, and the loss is CLAIM-568's first obstruction, a resource ceiling, here lowered by the allocation rule itself."

The first draft of this paper stated more: that the loss falls under none of the six levels, with its home in CLAIM-422 alone (join JC7: success once, then loss by the budget rule, from §9's model, CLAIM-577 and CLAIM-422). The accounts decline that, chiefly SYNTHESIS-043, and the corrected content is the two notes just quoted, on CLAIM-556 and CLAIM-422. SYNTHESIS-043 (Proposed) declines a link of this kind as "a free parameter, which level a failure is assigned to". SYNTHESIS-024 (Proposed) leaves open "Whether CLAIM-556's two added levels are complete", and says QUESTION-404 "holds the open case". And SYNTHESIS-112 (Proposed) declines the general form, that "Budgets set by recent activity lose that ability silently", because "CLAIM-577 holds it only for the pure spend-matching rule, with no raise from outside". So the paper states only what the two notes hold: one mechanism, a ceiling lowered by the budget rule, by which an organization can act once and not keep the capacity to act again. The question that mechanism bears on is QUESTION-386 (Open; A's proposal, in the exchange on emergency readiness): "Under what funding and incentive rules does allocating resources according to observed activity cause an organization to lose capabilities whose value is concentrated in rarely encountered circumstances?" CLAIM-577 answers it for the pure spend-matching rule only.

Three further limits bear on diagnosis.

- **The list may be incomplete.** CLAIM-556 says no level is given for information, where "the system cannot tell which state it is in or which response the context calls for". QUESTION-404 (Open) asks whether that is "a fifth obstruction".
- **Diagnosis can itself be hard.** CLAIM-575 (Active, demonstrated): "diagnosing the obstruction is a problem with its own complexity".
- **Aggregate measures do not separate the failures.** CLAIM-357 (Active, interpretive): "aggregate performance does not tell infeasibility from realized violation". CLAIM-414 (Active, demonstrated) gives the method that does separate them, an intervention specified in advance, which is TERM-115's do(c) (§6 and §9).

The draft's list of remedies follows the levels:

- better monitoring for legibility;
- preauthorized authority for volitional efficacy;
- reserves for affordability;
- a change of infrastructure or standards for reachability;
- "restructuring the institution that evaluates and revises the regulations themselves".

The last remedy is second-order. CLAIM-719 (Proposed, interpretive) holds it in the record: an institution "can be reliably responsive to reasons within its governing mechanism and unresponsive to justified reasons for revising that mechanism". It is §13's subject.

### 10.3 The bottleneck lies along the whole route

The draft warns against "the mistaken inference that the slowest organizational component always constitutes the governing bottleneck". It says instead that "The effective response time depends on the entire causal route from detection to action". The record holds parts of this.

**The time to correct is a sum of stages that compete.** TERM-085's definition splits the time to correct into "discovery, verification, coordination and execution, which overlap and compete". It draws a consequence: "A sufficiently long search can make an otherwise available remedy unusable", and further search can have negative value. Its note on Hutchins's cockpit adds that preparation "moves the cost of search ahead of the deadline". In other words, a stage done beforehand drops off the route that the deadline measures.

**The route's time, where its stages do not compete.** The simple part of the draft's sentence is now filed as CLAIM-tmpiwxla (Proposed, demonstrated, thesis, the assistant's, filed for this manuscript's gap GC8): "Where a response runs through stages that do not compete for a shared resource, its time is a property of the route, stages in sequence adding their times, stages that overlap contributing the longest of theirs and a stage completed before the contingency contributing nothing, so the slowest component bounds the response time only when it lies on the route and is still to be done". It is the critical path, applied to TERM-085's stages. Its scope condition matters. It says "Nothing where stages compete": "stages sharing reviewers or a budget can take longer than the longest of them, up to their sum on one server, as CASE-316's saturated reviewers show". TERM-085's own stages "overlap and compete", so the claim covers them only where they do not. With random durations it holds sample by sample, so "the readiness curve of TERM-115 is the distribution of that longest chain".

**Where stages share capacity, throughput sets another bound.** The route sets how long one response takes. How many responses get through in time is set by the slowest stage's rate. CLAIM-705 (Proposed, demonstrated; ChatGPT's statement in the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse", the derivation the filer's) holds: "When an institution produces challengeable decisions faster than its corrective process can resolve them, the formal right to challenge stays with everyone while its preventive effect cannot: if valid challenges arrive at rate λ and are resolved at rate μ < λ, at most a share μ/λ can ever be resolved in time, whatever the order of review, and under first-come review none filed after a finite time is". CLAIM-704 (Proposed, demonstrated) adds that the order of review then decides whose challenges still count: "under insufficient review capacity, scheduling distributes preventive rights". So the draft's warning is right for latency and does not carry to throughput. A slowest stage that is overloaded bounds the share of cases met in time, whatever the route.

**A slow commitment can be applied fast.** CLAIM-711 (Proposed, interpretive, counter) is ChatGPT's, from another exchange. It objects to the owner's CLAIM-710 (Proposed) on procedural friction: "standing constraints set slowly in advance and enforced by mechanisms faster than the process can foreclose alternatives keep it open to intervention without delaying it". What carries the opportunity to intervene, on this account, is a relation of speeds: the enforcing mechanism must act before the process forecloses the alternatives, "whatever the speed of the deliberation that set the limit". The claim states its own limit: standing constraints "interrupt only the transitions anticipated when they were set". A novel failure still needs a channel through which someone can stop the process. The draft's "A slow institutional commitment can enable fast behavior" is near this claim. The claim is Proposed, and its promotion waits on the owner's ruling on the sense of friction (TERM-125, Proposed).

**Speed does not settle authority.** CLAIM-423 (Proposed, interpretive) holds that in a political collective, components that change at different speeds differ in "which forms of influence each can accumulate, exercise and keep, and so which of them holds authority over the others". It also says what it does not claim: "Not that timescale is a source of authority. Speed confers nothing by itself." Its note of 2026-10-30 adds: "No case in the record shows a component's temporal horizon, rather than the formal rule that gives an event its consequence, deciding which component holds authority." The draft's "A technically flexible production system can be locked into an institutional arrangement that prevents its capabilities from being exercised" is a case of this kind: the bottleneck lies at the authority stage, not at the slowest stage.

Putting these together gives the draft's sentence. The response time is a property of the route, set by the order of its stages, by which stages overlap and by which are done in advance, and not by the slowest component; and where stages share capacity, the slowest stage's rate bounds how many responses arrive in time. That sentence was the first draft's join (join JC8: the bottleneck along the route, from TERM-085's split of time, CLAIM-711 and CLAIM-423). Its core is now stated by CLAIM-tmpiwxla, for stages that do not compete, and the account of the timing of response and revision says so: "The core of JC8 is stated by CLAIM-tmpiwxla" (SYNTHESIS-tmp8j9h0, Proposed). CLAIM-tmpiwxla itself excludes the rest: it is "Not the manuscript's join JC8 as a whole: that a commitment set slowly can be applied fast (CLAIM-711), or that speed does not settle authority (CLAIM-423), which is more than this construction". The account lists two parts under "Not stated on the record". Reading CLAIM-711 and CLAIM-423 as instances of the route, a slow commitment as a stage done in advance and authority as a stage that can be the bottleneck, gets the verdict "fails, a recap by relabelling": "Each reading places a held entry under CLAIM-tmpiwxla's construction and adds no inference", and neither has a case. Setting the route's latency beside CLAIM-705's bound on throughput gets "fails, a recap": "Each half is stated, and the conjunction adds no link" (ibid.). So the paper states the core with CLAIM-tmpiwxla, the throughput bound with CLAIM-705, and the readings of CLAIM-711 and CLAIM-423 as readings. CLAIM-711 and CLAIM-423 are Proposed, and CLAIM-423 has no case.

## 11. Overconsolidation and lock-in

The draft's §11 makes a general observation. "As an organization becomes more efficient at its current activities, it may become increasingly dependent on the arrangements supporting those activities", so that "An ecosystem can become better at producing what it currently produces while becoming worse at becoming something else." It then draws a regulatory distinction between "productive specialization" and "destructive lock-in". This section separates what the record holds firmly beneath that distinction from what it only proposes. The classic statement of the trade is a reading the record holds: Holling (1973), THEORY-132 (Active), "Stability, a system's speed of return to equilibrium after disturbance, and resilience, its capacity to absorb change and persist within its domain of attraction, are distinct properties that can trade off, so a system can be highly unstable and highly resilient, and managing it for constancy can erode its resilience".

The record holds two different mechanisms beneath the draft's observation, and the draft runs them together. One is a budget or allocation rule that follows use (§11.1). The other is consolidation in a learner (§§11.2–11.3). The budget rule of CLAIM-577 is not consolidation. An ecosystem that closes plants and lets suppliers go is consolidating, which is the second route if it is anything the record holds. CLAIM-577's mechanism is the first.

### 11.1 Lock-in by allocation is held, and needs no learner

Four Active, demonstrated claims carry a mechanism of allocation.

- **CLAIM-569:** "A form has at least three resource requirements, to construct it, to maintain it, and to move into or out of it, and none fixes another: a capability can be cheap to keep and costly to acquire, so having enough to sustain it is not having enough to acquire it".
- **CLAIM-577:** under a budget set to last period's peak, any transition whose threshold exceeds the holding cost "is lost after one period spent holding it", "unless the budget is raised from outside by at least the difference".
- **CLAIM-679**, ChatGPT's sentence in the record: cutting the resource held toward the cost of holding "removes exactly the transitions whose threshold lies between the old holding and the new, so that at the limit the organization can still hold where it is and can reach no configuration whose transition carries any reconfiguration slack". Its source in ChatGPT's earlier exchange put it in words close to the draft's: "A society can become more efficient at reproducing its current organization while becoming less capable of transforming that organization."
- **CLAIM-675:** where capacity is learned by doing and decays with disuse, "support that does the work shrinks what the party can reach unaided", so support "has to be assessed by the capability trajectory it produces".

The first three need no learning at all. An organization that changes nothing about itself, under a budget rule that tracks its use, loses its transitions (§9). CLAIM-679's "efficient" is fixed to one sense, cutting the resource held toward what holding requires. CLAIM-675 needs capacity that grows with practice and decays without it, which is a learning process in a weak sense.

Whether the efficiency was real is a further question. CLAIM-760 (Active, interpretive; ChatGPT's in the exchange "Topological protection explained") holds: "That an organization makes an activity cheaper for those who perform it does not show that work was saved: the work may have been transferred, to an earlier time, to upkeep, or to other parties ... work saved, work transferred and valuable capability expanded are three separate achievements". And a consolidated structure can expand what is possible as well as narrow it. CLAIM-761 (Active, interpretive; the owner's thesis, in ChatGPT's wording) holds that "A stabilized constraint can be stored work", which "changes which activities are realizable within a fixed per-task budget", on conditions that include "the distribution of later demands", "and the persistence that buys it can entrench".

### 11.2 Overconsolidation is Proposed and untested

The draft cites CLAIM-758 (Proposed, interpretive) for the general form. The record files it as "The owner's generalization, in A's wording". The owner made the point in another exchange: "sounds like you've landed on a generalization of overfitting". The claim itself reads: "Overconsolidation, a generalization of overfitting: relative to a declared family of later acquisitions lying outside the training task distribution, continued stabilization of a learner can keep improving its present objective while raising the budget it needs to acquire that family to a declared reliability with its held capabilities kept above a declared floor, so collateral forgetting counts as part of the cost". It rests on CLAIM-675 and CLAIM-569. It is "a failure only relative to that family and horizon". Its section "What it is not" states the draft's caution in its own words: "Not a failure in itself", and "A specialist in a stable environment is not overconsolidated."

It has not been tested. Its "Evidence wanted" names CASE-294 (Proposed), a study designed at the owner's request across Pythia checkpoints, to "see whether later checkpoints are better now but cost more to teach new tasks". CASE-294 has not been run; "whether to run it is the owner's call". CLAIM-757 (Proposed, interpretive) adds that whether the loss can be reversed, and by when, depends on how the capacity to acquire is lost: by disuse, which renewed practice restores before a threshold point, or by use, which practice does not restore.

Two neighbouring entries from the same exchange say why present competence is the wrong measure of a learner. CLAIM-754 (Proposed, interpretive; ChatGPT's) holds that "A learner's acquired organization fixes three resource-indexed frontiers that move separately: the operational (what it can reliably do now within an operating budget), the developmental (what it can come to do reliably within a further acquisition budget, given a declared interface for what it may learn from) and the inquiry frontier", so "no scalar measure of competence or plasticity ranks learners or learning processes independently of context". CLAIM-756 (Proposed, demonstrated; ChatGPT's, with the filer's numbers) holds that "An acquisition can be worth making for what it does to the cost of other acquisitions". The owner's question behind both is open, QUESTION-495: "What would make a learning process optimal, when some of the most valuable consequences of learning are changes in what the learner can later learn, ask and afford, which cannot be specified in advance; and when is investing in future learnability better than improving present competence?"

### 11.3 From a learner to an ecosystem

CLAIM-758 is about "a learner", and the record's term for learning sets conditions. TERM-131 (Proposed) defines learning as "an experience-dependent change in the system's retained organization that alters what it can later do, at what cost and with what reliability, judged against a counterfactual in which the experience did not occur", of "a declared system over a declared horizon". It excludes relaxation: "A cooling material can relax toward equilibrium without learning in the usual sense." So to read an industrial ecosystem's consolidation as CLAIM-758's overconsolidation, one must declare which system learns, and show a change in its retained organization caused by experience. A settling of fast variables does not count, and neither does a budget rule's ratchet.

The nearest industrial reading the record holds is at the level of a firm. Cohen and Levinthal (1990), THEORY-1518 (Active), report the argument "that a firm's ability to recognize, assimilate and apply outside knowledge rests on its prior related knowledge, built largely as a byproduct of its own R&D and not quickly bought, and that because this capacity accumulates and shapes what the firm expects of a field, a firm that stops investing in a fast-moving field may be locked out of it even after major advances". Whether that thesis is true is THEORY-1543's question (Proposed). The reading's caveat: "It does not show that any firm that stopped R&D was locked out, or how long recovery takes", and its one test is of "only the static part". The record does not link it to CLAIM-758, and this paper does not either.

This yields two routes to lock-in, and the draft's §11 runs them together (join JC9: two routes to lock-in, CLAIM-577 and CLAIM-679 without a learner against CLAIM-758 with one, under TERM-131). The distinction is now on the record, as a note of 2026-11-23 on TERM-tmpu5k0o (the assistant's, filed for this manuscript's gap GC10): "The sense above joins two routes the record holds apart, with different remedies." JC9 is therefore a restatement of that note, as the account of keeping a route under a budget rule records ("JC9 is stated by that note", SYNTHESIS-tmpfxv00, Proposed); whether to lift the note into a claim is the owner's call (ibid.).

- **Lock-in by allocation.** A budget or allocation rule follows use, and the transitions that need slack are priced out (CLAIM-577, CLAIM-679). This is demonstrated, needs no learner, and is reversed by outside money of at least the slack (CLAIM-577's step 4).
- **Lock-in by consolidation.** A declared learner's retained organization changes with experience so that acquiring a declared family of capabilities costs more (CLAIM-758). This is Proposed, needs a learner in TERM-131's sense, and may not be reversible by money at all (CLAIM-757).

The two call for different remedies, as the obstructions of §10 do. A reserve or a commitment not to cut (§9; CASE-200) answers the first. Varied practice before the threshold point, or an intervention that renews what training used up, answers the second (CLAIM-757). The note draws the consequence for the term: "The term names CLAIM-758 as its sense and CLAIM-679 as its mechanism, so it covers both without saying which a case is. A diagnosis under it should say which route it found."

A third sense of lock-in is in the readings the record holds, and fits neither route. Arthur's model, THEORY-863 (Active), finds that with increasing returns "one technology takes the whole market with probability one", "the winner need not be the better technology, and once it has locked in no small change in returns alters later choices". The reading's summary: "Its lock-in is absorption in accumulated adoptions, which a small reversal of returns cannot undo." Pierson, THEORY-885 (Proposed), argues that "Politics is more prone than markets to increasing returns, and so to path dependence", and North, THEORY-429 (Proposed), that "increasing returns to the institutional matrix make frameworks path dependent". This is lock-in by the choices of many adopters, with no budget rule and no learner. The record does not relate it to the two routes above, and neither does this paper.

### 11.4 Productive specialization and destructive lock-in

The draft's distinction is filed as ChatGPT's TERM-tmpu5k0o (Proposed): "destructive lock-in, of an industrial ecosystem against a declared family of future demands: specialization that removes or makes unaffordable the pathways to alternatives those demands need while current operation stays successful, and whose loss the benefits of consolidation do not justify; its opposite is productive specialization". The term names CLAIM-758's overconsolidation as its sense, "with CLAIM-679's transition loss as its mechanism and CLAIM-079's stable-but-unreorganizable system as its result". It therefore takes the learner route and the allocation route together, as §11.3 and the term's own note of 2026-11-23 warn.

Its own limit is the open part: "The justification clause has no standard: whether consolidation's benefits 'justify' a loss is the open part, near CLAIM-684's rising burden of justification." The draft's version of the test is "whether its specialization has foreclosed valuable, attainable alternatives whose future absence cannot be justified by the benefits of consolidation". Without a standard for "justified", the test classifies nothing. Every loss of a pathway can be called productive or destructive at will. The gap is now filed as QUESTION-tmpvkgc0 (Open, the assistant's, for this manuscript's gap GC11): "What standard makes the loss of a pathway to alternatives justified by the benefits of consolidation, so that destructive lock-in can be told from productive specialization?" It counts as an answer "A standard that classifies a stated loss of a pathway, says who judges it, and either is cost-benefit with its trade-offs declared or says what it adds to cost-benefit."

**Two partial standards are on file. Neither is adopted, and neither is complete.**

*A burden that rises with irreversibility.* CLAIM-684 (Proposed, interpretive) holds that "When a choice about a technology or an institution could substantially and durably narrow the possibilities later open to those it affects, the burden of justifying it should rise with the size and the irreversibility of the narrowing". Its reason is uncertainty with learning, and it says where that reason runs out: "Where nothing will be learned, irreversibility adds no reason of its own". It says who should bear the burden only in part, and who judges not at all. The audit of the motivating draft left it out of the work ("near §11, not argued"). A scaling rule of the same shape is Active: CLAIM-687 (Active, interpretive; ChatGPT's in the exchange "Analyze technological civilization trap") holds that "The accountability that those who control an arrangement owe to those who depend on it should rise with how indispensable the arrangement has become to them, so that an arrangement that has become effectively compulsory owes public justification of its purposes and governing rules". Its summary calls it "of the same shape as CLAIM-684's burden that rises with irreversibility". It concerns what controllers owe as dependence grows, not which losses of a pathway are justified.

*The break-even of §9.6.* Within §9's model, a reserve that keeps a pathway pays when the contingency's probability times its loss exceeds pL*. One could read "justified" in TERM-tmpu5k0o as this inequality reversed: consolidation that prices a pathway out is justified when keeping the pathway would cost more than the expected late losses it prevents, over the declared family and deadlines (join JC10: §9's break-even as one part of the justification standard, with CLAIM-684 and TERM-tmpu5k0o). QUESTION-tmpvkgc0 holds this reading as a partial candidate, not as held, and the account of keeping a route under a budget rule lists the join under "Not stated on the record" with the verdict "fails, a free parameter": "'Justified' still classifies nothing until the trade-offs among demands are declared" (SYNTHESIS-tmpfxv00, Proposed). It stays in QUESTION-tmpvkgc0. The reading has two limits, and QUESTION-tmpvkgc0 records both. It is a cost-benefit standard, and so it inherits CLAIM-tmphjm0q's objection (Proposed, counter; §14) that the trade-offs among demands must first be declared. And it needs the readiness it compares to be known, which is QUESTION-395's identification problem (Open; §9.6). Whether "justified" also needs the conditions of answerability that §13 discusses is outside both standards. QUESTION-tmpvkgc0 puts it so: "Neither says whether 'justified' also needs justification owed to, and answerable to, those whose alternatives are lost (QUESTION-480)."

Any such standard has a baseline problem. CLAIM-747 (Proposed, interpretive; ChatGPT's in the exchange "Assess Depp Heard Abuse") holds that "The alternatives against which a governing relationship or principle is assessed can be products of that relationship, so the present distribution of options is not a neutral baseline, and an actor cannot justify its terms by the narrowness of options it helped narrow without justification". A consolidation that has already priced out the alternatives cannot be justified by their absence.

Not every concentration is entrenchment, either. CLAIM-531 (Active, interpretive) holds that "Generative entrenchment does not by itself give organizational closure: an entrenched core, which later interactions depend on so that perturbing it is catastrophic and perturbing them tolerable, can sit at the root of an acyclic dependency order, and where interactions turn over such an order persists only while something outside supplies its core". And CLAIM-875 (Proposed, empirical, the assistant's) finds, in one network model, a core that "is a regime the dynamics regenerate, not an asymmetry entrenched in its first holders: removed, it is refilled from the periphery, and made rare, it is recovered by its own members". A concentrated industry may therefore be a role that its dynamics refill rather than a structure held by its first holders. Which it is, is a question about a case, and the record holds no industrial one.

Regulation can lock itself in too. CLAIM-445 (Proposed, interpretive; the owner's) holds that "A protective regime ... becomes self-maintaining when the interventions it authorizes shift the evidence used to judge its necessity", and that "it is a failure only where the regime has become less responsive to evidence that it is no longer needed". A reading of one laboratory study, Schram and Ule (2024), THEORY-858 (Proposed), finds that "Insulating a regulator from future political revision fixes in place the interests of whoever designs the insulated regime", which "entrenches capture" "when they serve the regulated industry". Its caveat: "it says nothing yet about real independent agencies". Section 13 takes up who revises the regulator.

### 11.5 Lock-in can look like success for a long time

The draft says that destructive lock-in preserves "the appearance of successful current operation". The record has a reason why this appearance can last. Flack (2017), in the reading THEORY-1073 (Proposed), holds that an aggregate variable much slower than the behaviour producing it "becomes an environment the components tune to whether or not it summarizes the underlying regularities well, which helps coordination; if it summarizes them poorly the system likely fails in the end, later the larger the separation". In Flack's words: "failure may just take longer if the time scale separation is very large". THEORY-1073 notes that "This is an informal argument. No model is given". CLAIM-478 (Proposed) records the limit as one "the claim lacks".

The first draft joined this to §11's distinction: the slower the consolidated structure, the longer a lock-in it causes can go unnoticed in current operation, so that strong identity, which §§3–5 treat as an achievement, is also what delays the evidence of lock-in (join JC11: Flack's limit applied to lock-in, from THEORY-1073 and TERM-tmpu5k0o). No entry draws that link, and the account of keeping a route under a budget rule lists it under "Not stated on the record" with the verdict "fails" (SYNTHESIS-tmpfxv00, Proposed). It carries an informal, hedged reading ("No model is given") to a different object. And the record holds a case in which the reading's premise holds and the join fails: in the allocation route the loss "leaves no trace in output" (CLAIM-577) after one holding period, with no separation of timescales at all, so how long it goes unnoticed is the time until the contingency or an imposed test (CLAIM-414, TERM-115), "not how slow anything is" (SYNTHESIS-tmpfxv00). The account calls the join "false as a general statement about lock-in"; it would need a model of the consolidation route. This paper does not state it. What survives is the point both routes share: the evidence that would show a lock-in has to be produced by an intervention specified in advance (CLAIM-414; TERM-115's do(c)), because ordinary operation need not produce it.
