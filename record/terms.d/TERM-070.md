# TERM-070: organizational closure, as closure of constraints: a set of constraints each of which depends directly on another in the set and has another depending on it (Montévil and Mossio 2015), distinct from informational closure and from a flame's maintenance of the conditions of its own combustion

## Definition

Montévil and Mossio ([LIT-582](../literature.d/LIT-582.md)), as [CLAIM-395](../claims.d/CLAIM-395.md) gives them. A **constraint** C
acts on a process at a time scale τs(C) at which its relevant aspects are
conserved. C₁ **depends** on C₂ when C₂ constrains, at τs(C₂), a process
that produces aspects of C₁. A set of constraints realises
**organizational closure** when each member "depends directly on at least
one other constraint belonging to" the set and at least one other member
depends on it. Strict closure adds that the set cannot be split into two
closed sets.

What the record has proved of it ([CLAIM-395](../claims.d/CLAIM-395.md)): a closed set has at least two
members, and under two stated readings of the paper's time scales it
includes at least one slow and one fast dependence, so a system with a
single constraint of its own cannot realise closure.

## What it is not

- **Not informational closure** ([TERM-068](TERM-068.md), sense 1). That is a
  condition on a variable's law. This is a relation among constraints. A11:
  "organizational constraint closure is not a variant spelling of
  conditional mutual information being zero."
- **Not "being a living individual".** [THEORY-072](../theory.d/THEORY-072.md), which reads closure as
  building differentiation in, is Proposed: "No paper analyses a flame
  under the 2015 definitions. Neither paper says closure defines an
  organism." A12's condition holds here: the proved constraints of
  [CLAIM-395](../claims.d/CLAIM-395.md) are kept apart from the demarcation.
- **Not Mossio, Saborido and Moreno's 2009 closure** ([LIT-570](../literature.d/LIT-570.md)), which grants
  a candle flame closure: its one pattern keeps up the conditions of the
  combustion that makes it. That weaker sense appears only inside [THEORY-072](../theory.d/THEORY-072.md)
  and has no code of its own.
- **Not a self in any of [TERM-045](TERM-045.md)'s senses,** though [THEORY-071](../theory.d/THEORY-071.md) calls it
  "autonomy" and "self" ("Here "autonomy" and "self" mean organisational
  closure. They do not mean a subject or self-governance.").
- **Not agency.** [THEORY-071](../theory.d/THEORY-071.md) makes individuality, of which this is the
  organizational form, a precondition of agency that does not suffice.

## Note of 2026-11-01: closure of interactions, and entrenchment
<!-- inactive-ok-block: LIT-188 CLAIM-tmpr242r — Proposed, filed 2026-11-01; Proposed: read in full; cited from the re-reading of LIT-188, not leaned on -->

Griesemer and Shavit (LIT-188, §3; NOTE-173) adopt this term from Moreno
and Mossio for social groups: "community requires 'organizational
closure' of the system of social interactions". Two things a reader
citing them should know:

- **The members are interactions, not constraints.** The paper does not
  say which interactions constrain which, or what each one's production
  is. The term's definition is about constraints, so applying it needs a
  reading of an interaction as a constraint, which the paper leaves
  implicit.
- **The test it gives shows dependence, not closure.** "Integration means
  that removal of node or interaction types disrupts social
  organization", and the paper's mechanism is generative entrenchment, in
  which later interactions depend on core ones. Both are met by a one-way
  dependency order whose core depends on nothing in the set. The term
  needs every member to be depended on and to depend on another member.
  CLAIM-tmpr242r states the gap, and what an order that is both
  entrenched and closed would look like.
